### Palworld Beginner Guide — Where to Build Your First Base, First Pals to Catch & the Level 10 Decision (2026)

**Where's the best place to build my first base in Palworld?**

The plateau near the Fort Ruins (coordinates roughly 200, -350). It has 5-8 ore nodes, flat land for building, cliff walls on three sides for natural defense, and is near multiple fast travel points. The spawn area looks safe but has almost no ore — you'll spend hours walking to mine and walking back. Move your base to Fort Ruins plateau as soon as you have a flying mount (around level 15). The Palbox relocation cost is minimal compared to the hundreds of hours of walking you'll save.

**What are the first 5 Pals I should catch?**

1. Foxparks (Kindling + ranged fire attack — early combat and smelting). 2. Rushoar (Mining 3 — your first real miner, and it charges at enemies for early combat). 3. Cattiva (Transporting + Handiwork — helps move items in base). 4. Lamball (Handiwork + produces Wool — helps build and provides resources). 5. Lifmunk (Planting + SMG Partner Skill — solid early combat and farming). These 5 cover Mining, Kindling, Handiwork, Transporting, and Planting — all the work suitabilities your first base needs.

### 10 Palworld Beginner Mistakes That Waste Hours — And How to Fix Them (2026)

**What's the biggest mistake new Palworld players make?**

Building their first base in the starting area. The initial spawn zone has poor resource density, no ore nodes, and is far from mid-game content. The best early base location is the plateau near the Fort Ruins (coordinates 200, -350) — it's flat, has 5+ ore nodes, is near a fast travel point, and has natural cliff barriers on three sides. Moving your base early costs minimal resources compared to the weeks of walking you'll save.

**Should I catch every Pal I see in Palworld?**

No. Catch 1 of each new Pal species for the XP bonus (catching 10 of a species gives a huge XP bonus), then focus on catching Pals with good work suitability traits (Artisan, Serious, Work Slave) for your base, and combat traits (Musclehead, Ferocious, Lucky) for your team. Catching random Pals past the 10-catch bonus is wasted time and Pal Spheres.

### Best Food Recipes & Pal Feeding Guide — Palworld Cooking Optimization (2026)

**What's the best food for combat in Palworld?**

Mammorest Curry is the best combat food — it gives +25% Attack for 10 minutes, which is the highest Attack buff in the game. Mozzarina Hamburger (+20% Defense, +15% Attack) is the best all-around combat food if you need survivability too. For early game, Eikthyrdeer Stew (+10% Attack) is easy to make with starter-zone ingredients.

**How do I stop my Pals from starving at base?**

Build a Feed Box and keep it stocked with at least 100 units of food at all times. Use Jam-Filled Buns (made from 2 Berries + 1 Flour) for the best food-to-fullness ratio for workers. DO NOT put raw Berries in the Feed Box — they give almost no nutrition, forcing Pals to eat constantly and wasting work time. One Jam-Filled Bun fills a Pal for 10+ minutes vs 2-3 minutes for raw Berries.

**Does food quality affect breeding in Palworld?**

Yes — Pals with higher SAN (Sanity) breed faster and pass down better IVs. Feeding breeding Pals high-quality food like Salad (restores SAN) or Mozzarina Hamburger keeps their SAN high, which improves egg quality. However, there is no food that directly increases breeding speed — only Cake in the Breeding Farm starts the breeding process. Regular food only affects the parents' condition, not the breeding timer.

### Palworld Pal Management — When to Keep, Condense, Sell or Butcher Every Pal (2026)

**Should I keep Pals with bad traits like Slacker or Destructive?**

Almost never. A Pal with Slacker (-30% Work Speed) assigned to your base is actively slowing your production more than an empty slot would. The only exception: if it's literally the only Pal you own with a required work suitability for your base — for example, your sole Kindling 3+ Pal. In that case, keep it temporarily but prioritize catching or breeding a replacement. The moment you have a better Pal of the same species, condense the bad-trait one into the good one. The Palbox space you free up is worth more than a bad Pal you'll never deploy.

**How many copies of a Pal species should I keep?**

Keep exactly ONE of each species as your 'best copy' — the one with the best traits you've found or bred. Condense all other copies into that best copy. The only exception: if a species has multiple roles (e.g., Anubis is both the best Handiwork Pal AND a strong combat Pal), you might keep two copies — one bred for work traits (Artisan, Serious, Work Slave) and one bred for combat traits (Musclehead, Ferocious, Lucky). Never keep more than two copies of any species.
