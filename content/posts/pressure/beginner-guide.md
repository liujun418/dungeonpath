Your first five Pressure runs will end the same way, so let me save you the 45 minutes. Run one dies at floor 2: the lights flicker, a sound you have never heard cuts through the room hum, and you spin in place trying to find whatever made it. You die without ever seeing it. Run three dies at floor 4: the room goes dark, someone whispers "psst," and you stand perfectly still, because standing still is what every other game taught you to do. Three seconds later the screen goes black. Run five is the worst — it ends at floor 8 with a clean high-pitched screech, a closet three steps away, and you frozen in the doorway because you were too busy staring at loot to notice where the closet was. The game never tells you that Rush announces itself 3.5 seconds before it arrives, and 3.5 seconds is a lifetime of time if you already know where to go. It never tells you that Ambush sounds like Rush played through broken speakers — distorted and glitchy — and that Ambush passes multiple times while Rush passes once. It never tells you that Screech's "psst" means look at it, not hide from it.

This guide is the tutorial the game should have had: your first 20 floors, room by room. What you will hear. What it means. What to do about it. Plus the Credit-earning protocol that builds your first real loadout without grinding for 10 hours.

## Floors 1-5: The Unspoken Tutorial Zone

Floors 1 through 5 are Pressure's unspoken tutorial zone. Rush can spawn before floor 5, but it rarely does. Use these floors to practice the room entry protocol, because it is the most valuable skill in the game and it costs nothing.

The scene repeats every room: you step through the door and your instinct is to walk straight in and look at the loot. Do not. Pause for half a second. Identify the nearest closet or hiding spot. Identify the exit. Then proceed to explore. Do this for every single room until it is automatic — by floor 5 you should be doing it without thinking. This single habit, knowing where the nearest closet is before you need it, saves more runs than any item in the game.

Sometime between floor 3 and floor 5 you will meet your first Screech. The room goes dark. You hear a whisper: "psst." Do not panic, and do not stand still. Spin a full 360 degrees smoothly — no jerky mouse flicks — and watch your peripheral vision. Screech's white glowing eyes are visible at the edge of your screen. The moment your crosshair crosses them, it disappears and you take zero damage. The spin works every single time. Practice it in every dark room, because Screech does not stop appearing at floor 5.

## Floors 6-10: Rush Becomes Real

Floors 6 through 10 are where Rush starts showing up. The screech is clean, high-pitched, and rises in pitch. The moment you hear it, you have 3.5 seconds. Here is the whole sequence, step by step.

You hear the screech while exploring a room you entered 8 seconds ago. Because you pre-identified the closet when you walked in, you know exactly where it is. Walk to it — do not sprint; walking is fast enough when you know the direction. Enter. Close the door. Wait 1 second after the screech stops, then exit and continue.

Three rules keep beginners alive in this stretch. Do not exit during the screech — Rush can still be in the room. Do not enter a closet with the red occupancy light on — it is taken, and the door will not close properly. Do not sprint — arriving calm means you close the door on time, while panic speed gets you to the wrong door faster.

## Floors 11-15: Ambush and the Three-Count

Starting around floor 10, Ambush has a roughly 15% chance to replace Rush — and it is the number one reason players who mastered Rush start dying again. The screech is distorted, lower-pitched, and glitchy — like Rush played through broken speakers. If the screech sounds wrong, it is Ambush.

The difference in response is one sentence: Rush passes once and you leave; Ambush passes 2 to 6 times and you stay until the silence is real. After the first pass, count to three. If another screech comes within 3 seconds, stay in the closet and count to three again after that pass. If 3 seconds pass with silence, exit. The most common Ambush death is exiting after pass 2 when pass 3 is already on its way — the three-count exists to stop exactly that. ## Floors 16-20: Eyes Rooms

The screen tints purple and your crosshair suddenly feels heavy? Eyes is in the room. Drop your gaze to the floor immediately — eye contact drains roughly 8% HP per second, so 3 seconds of looking costs a quarter of your health bar. Navigate with peripheral vision and wall-following: keep a wall on one side, keep your camera low, and cross the room without ever letting the center of your screen rise. Most Eyes rooms keep the same layout run after run — the second time you see one, you can walk through it from memory.

## The 3-Minute Sound Drill: Die Ten Times On Purpose

The fastest way to learn Pressure's sounds is not careful play. It is deliberate death. Start a run. Do not buy items. Sprint from room to room. When Rush or Ambush screeches, let it kill you. Restart immediately. Do this 10 times.

In 30 minutes you will have heard Rush's screech 15 to 25 times — more exposure than you would get in 5 hours of careful play where you hide and survive. The drill burns each sound into your brain so that in a real run, your hands already know the response while your brain is still catching up. ## Failure Analysis: The Six Deaths You Will Actually Die

Every beginner dies the same six ways. Here is each one, what it costs you, and the fix.

**1. Freezing in a dark room.** You hear "psst" and you stand still to figure out what it is. Three seconds later Screech eats you. The fix: the instant the room goes dark, start the spin. You identify and kill Screech in the same motion.

**2. Sprinting to the closet.** Rush screeches, you panic-sprint, overshoot the closet, and arrive too hot to get the door closed in time. Walking is fast enough — you have 3.5 seconds and you already know the way. The fix: pre-identify closets, then walk.

**3. Exiting after Ambush's first pass.** You heard one pass, assumed it was Rush, and stepped out right into pass 2. The fix: the three-count. No screech for 3 full seconds means safe, and the count is non-negotiable.

**4. Taking a closet with a red light.** The occupancy light is on, you open the door anyway, and the door does not close — or a teammate gets dragged out with you. The fix: check the light before you touch the handle, and know where the room's second closet is.

**5. Looking at Eyes.** You walk into a purple-tinted room and your first instinct is to look up to see what is wrong. You lose about 8% HP per second. The fix: entry protocol — the moment the tint registers, your camera goes to the floor before your brain finishes saying "purple."

**6. Looting before learning.** You play loot runs — grab every chest, die to an entity you do not recognize. The fix: run discovery runs first (sprint, die, restart) so the sounds are in your head before the loot matters.

## Decision Framework: Two-Choice Calls That Decide Your Run

In the first 20 floors, nearly every death comes down to a decision you made in 2 seconds. When time is short, use this card instead of thinking.

| Sound heard | What it is | Your move | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean rising screech | Rush | Walk to the pre-identified closet, close the door | One pass; 3.5 seconds is plenty when you know the way |
| Distorted glitchy screech | Ambush | Closet, then three-count after every pass | 2-6 passes; pass 2 is usually not the last |
| "psst" whisper, dark room | Screech | Spin 360, stare down the glowing eyes | The stare is the kill button, not the danger |
| Purple screen tint | Eyes | Look at the floor, wall-follow out | ~8% HP/s; you have about 12 seconds total |

Four two-choice calls follow from the same logic:

**Loot or closet?** A chest sits 3 steps ahead and the closet is 5 steps back. Take the closet. No item is worth a death, and the entity is gone in seconds — the loot will still be there when you return.

**Heal or hoard?** You are at 50 HP with 2 Medkits in a safe room. Heal now. Every dead player's inventory is full of unused Medkits; the player who heals at 60 HP is the one who still has items at floor 20.

**Spin or search?** The room goes dark and you think "where is it?" Do not search with your crosshair — search with your spin. One smooth 360 finds Screech's eyes in under 2 seconds no matter where it spawned.

**Careful play or the drill?** You have died 6 times before floor 10 and you are considering even more careful play. The data says the opposite: the drill gives you more sound exposure in 30 minutes than careful play gives in 5 hours. Die ten times on purpose, and the careful play finally works.

## Counter-Intuitive: The Advice That Sounds Wrong

Four pieces of Pressure advice look backwards until you try them.

**1. Stare at Screech.** Every survival instinct says avoid the eyes. In Pressure, eye contact is the weapon: the moment your crosshair crosses its glowing eyes, it disappears and deals zero damage.

**2. Die on purpose.** Dying 10 times deliberately teaches you more than 5 hours of careful survival. Deliberate death is the fastest teacher in the game, because entity sounds appear at full frequency only when you stop hiding from them.

**3. Walk, don't sprint.** Sprinting to safety feels like urgency. Walking is actually faster in practice — you arrive calm, at the right door, with time to close it. Panic speed gets you to the wrong place faster.

**4. Do not buy items yet.** It feels wrong to run with nothing, but every credit spent before you can identify sounds by ear is a credit wasted on a run you will die in. Discovery runs first — the gear is the reward for learning, not the substitute for it.

## The Credit-Earning Protocol: From Zero to Full Loadout

Once the sounds are in your head, the protocol is simple, and it does not involve grinding for 10 hours.

**Discovery runs (your first 5-10 runs).** Do not try to survive. Sprint through rooms, let entities kill you, restart. Each death teaches one sound cue and earns 15-30 Credits. After 10 discovery runs you will have 200-300 Credits and recognize Rush, Ambush, and Screech by sound alone.

**Partial clear runs (the next 10).** Now try to reach floor 20, bringing only the Gummy Flashlight. Each run to floor 15-20 earns 40-60 Credits. After 10 partial clears you will have 500-700 Credits — enough for the full Essential loadout.

**Loadout runs.** Gummy Flashlight, 2 Medkits, 1 Sprint Potion, 1 Code Breacher. Target floor 30+. You now have the gear and the knowledge, earned in the order that makes both stick. ## The Bottom Line

Pressure is a game that rewards preparation over reaction. Every entity gives you a warning. Every threat has a counter. The players who survive consistently are not faster. They already knew what they were going to do before the threat appeared.
