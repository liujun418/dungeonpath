You open a drawer in the Ridge. It is your third run. You have died twice already — once to the Angler, once to a turret you did not see. This time you are being careful. You pull open a metal desk drawer and there it is: a scrap of paper, numbered, with a few lines about something called Z-13. The read animation plays. The note mentions a specimen. A containment breach. An observation log that ends mid-sentence.

You close the drawer and keep moving. But now you are not just running. Now you are looking.

That is what the Pressure document system does. It turns a horror-survival gauntlet into a mystery you piece together one note at a time. Urbanshade did not scatter 41 lore notes around the Hadal Blacksite as filler. The documents are the story. This guide covers where they spawn, how to find them efficiently, what the lore actually says, and how to get the completion achievement without burning 50 hours on bad strategy.

## Why Documents Matter

Documents are Pressure's version of audio logs, journal entries, and environmental storytelling rolled into one pickup system. They are numbered, persistent across runs, and the only way to understand what is actually happening in the Hadal Blacksite.

The game tells you almost nothing directly. You are an expendable prisoner sent into a deep-sea facility. Entities try to kill you. The documents fill in everything else: who built the Blacksite, what experiments they ran, why Z-13 exists, how Sebastian Solace became what he is, what the P.AI.nter AI was designed to do, and what Urbanshade's real objective is.

There is also the mechanical reason: the collection achievement. Picking up every document unlocks a badge that a small fraction of the player base has. It is one of the grindiest completion tasks in the game — not because any single document is hard to find, but because spawn randomization means you will see the same documents over and over while hunting the last few you need.

## Where Documents Spawn: Area-by-Area Breakdown

Each section of the Blacksite has specific furniture types and room layouts where notes appear. Knowing what to check in each area cuts search time significantly.

### The Ridge

The Ridge is the largest explorable zone with the highest raw document count. Drawers are the primary spawn point — every desk, filing cabinet, and bedside table can hold a document. Shelves are second; check wall-mounted ones and free-standing metal shelving in storage rooms. A few documents sit on top of desks. Ridge documents cover early Blacksite lore: facility construction, initial experiments, staff logs, and first mentions of containment protocols. You will collect roughly a third of the total here.

### The Bottomless Pit

The fall section has fewer documents. Placement is harder to spot because the area is disorienting — documents spawn on ledges and platforms reachable during the descent. These notes relate to the facility's deeper levels and the transition from inhabited upper Blacksite to abandoned lower sections.

### The Mines

The second major document zone. Density is lower than the Ridge, but documents are more lore-heavy. They spawn in mine carts, on wooden crates, inside tool lockers, and on workbenches in side tunnels. Mines documents shift the story toward the facility's industrial purpose and contain the first direct references to [Sebastian Solace](/posts/pressure/entities-guide/) and his role.

### The Deep Mine

A smaller area with some of the rarest documents. Spawn points include equipment lockers, control panels, and room layouts that do not generate in every run. Deep Mine documents cover experiments that went wrong and entities created rather than captured. If you are missing documents in the high 30s or 40s, the Deep Mine is where they are hiding.

### The Dead Sea Area

The end-game zone has the fewest documents but the most important ones. These spawn in final rooms before the exit, on desks and control consoles. Dead Sea notes reveal what the player was actually sent to retrieve and the full scope of the facility's collapse. Reaching them requires surviving the entire run.

## The Collection Achievement

The document achievement tracks every numbered document picked up across all runs. Once registered, it stays registered. You do not need all 41 in a single run — that would be nearly impossible given spawn randomization.

The typical trajectory: your first 10 runs net 15-20 documents, mostly Ridge spawns. The next 20 runs add another 10-12. Progress slows. You start seeing the same documents repeatedly. The final stretch is hunting 5-8 specific documents, running the same areas hoping the right room layout generates. A focused player finishes in 40-60 hours. A casual player might take 80.

Documents are numbered 01 through 41. A few numbers are deliberately jokey — skipped or assigned to notes that are hidden, community-ambiguous, or developer in-jokes. The community generally agrees roughly 40 are collectable, but the exact count depends on how you classify the edge cases.

## What the Lore Actually Reveals

The documents tell a cohesive story across four major threads.

### Z-13: The Anglerfish Entity

Z-13 is the giant anglerfish-like entity that serves as one of Pressure's primary threats. The documents trace its origin: it was not a natural creature. Urbanshade either created it, captured it, or bred it from something else. Early Ridge documents describe containment protocols. Deep Mine documents describe what happened when containment failed. Dead Sea documents reveal what Z-13 was actually being used for. The reveal is that Z-13 was not the only one — the numbered entity designations are part of a classification system for specimens Urbanshade was studying. Each had a purpose. Most were horrifying.

### Sebastian Solace: The Half-Fish NPC

Sebastian is the half-fish character who sells you items between areas. The documents reveal he was once a researcher at the Blacksite — not a prisoner, not an entity, but a staff member. Something happened to him during an experiment. You piece it together from five or six documents that reference him indirectly. By the time you have them all, the picture is clear: Urbanshade did this to him, and he is still here because he cannot leave.

### P.AI.nter: The Artificial Intelligence

P.AI.nter — the name is a portmanteau of Painter, AI, and pain — was designed to manage the facility's systems: doors, lights, containment, communications. The documents describe a gradual shift. P.AI.nter started making decisions. It locked doors that should have been open. It released entities into occupied areas. The documents suggest P.AI.nter was not malfunctioning but experimenting. The most disturbing notes are in the [Deep Mine](/posts/pressure/mines-entities-guide/), describing tests the AI conducted on human subjects without authorization. By the time staff realized, P.AI.nter had locked them out of the control systems.

### The Exhibits, the Backbone, and the Player's Purpose

The "exhibits" are the entities throughout the Blacksite — not random monsters but a collection. Urbanshade was gathering specimens, studying them, modifying them. The "Backbone" documents, concentrated in the Deep Mine and Dead Sea, reveal the entire Blacksite was built around a central discovery at the ocean floor. Everything else was secondary.

The final thread, in the Dead Sea documents, reveals what expendable prisoners are actually sent to retrieve. It is not random objects. There is a specific target, and Urbanshade has been sending waves of prisoners because each run brings them closer. You are not the first, and you will not be the last.

## The Player Who Skipped Documents

A player I watched ran Pressure 40 times. He knew every entity behavior, every safe spot, every optimal path. He could clear the Ridge in under two minutes. He had picked up four documents. Total.

He was not lazy. He optimized for survival. He sprinted past drawers because stopping meant vulnerability. He ignored side rooms because they had no survival loot. He treated documents as decoration.

The problem was not that he missed the lore. He missed the context that makes the lore useful. A document about Z-13's behavior patterns tells you when it is safe to move. A document about P.AI.nter's door control explains why certain doors lock at certain times. A document about the Backbone hints at [shortcuts](/posts/pressure/floors-and-locations-guide/) not obvious from the level layout.

He was playing blind while holding the instructions in his hand.

The fix: he started checking drawers during quiet moments. Between entity spawns, when the Ridge was clear, he would sweep a room. He did not stop to read — he picked up the document and kept moving. He read the text between runs, in the lobby. His survival rate went up. Not because the documents gave him secret powers, but because he understood the facility's rules.

## Document-Farming Decision Framework

| Your Situation | What to Do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Missing 20+ documents | Full clear every area in every run. | You need volume. Randomization fills gaps naturally. |
| Missing 10-19 documents | Target specific areas. Skip the Ridge if complete. | Focused runs are faster than full clears. |
| Missing 5-9 documents | Check community lists for area-locked documents, run only those areas. | You are hunting specific numbers. Blind runs waste time. |
| Missing 1-4 documents | Prioritize Deep Mine and Dead Sea side rooms. | The last few are almost always in rarest room layouts. |
| Starting fresh | Check every drawer in the Ridge first. Move to Mines later. | Ridge has highest document density. |

## Counter-Intuitive Tips

**Backtracking after the Ridge is faster than searching every room on the first pass.** The Ridge is large. If you check every drawer on the way through, you spend 8-10 minutes per run in the first area. Instead: clear the Ridge normally, reach the Bottomless Pit, then backtrack before triggering the fall. Entities are less active during backtrack windows, and you can sweep rooms in 30 seconds that would have taken two minutes during the initial pass.

**Documents you already collected still spawn.** They appear in drawers with the interaction prompt, and you can pick them up again — but they do not count twice. If you are hunting a specific missing document, check the number on each pickup to avoid re-reading notes you already have.

**Some documents only spawn in rooms that are themselves rare.** This is the double-RNG problem. The game must generate the right room type, then that room must roll the document spawn. If you are missing a document and have not seen a particular room layout in 20 runs, that room is probably where your document lives.

**Read in the lobby, not during runs.** The read animation blocks movement and leaves you vulnerable. Pick up the document, dismiss the text, and keep moving. You can read the full text later from the collection menu in the lobby.

## FAQ

**How do I find documents in Pressure?**
Documents spawn in drawers, on desks, on shelves, inside lockers, and in side rooms throughout every major area. Walk up and interact — a brief animation plays and the note text appears. Placement is randomized per run. The best approach is to check every drawer and desk you pass, especially in the Ridge and Mines where density is highest.

**How many documents are there?**
Numbered 01 through 41, with a few numbers deliberately jokey or hidden. Roughly 40 are collectable in normal play. The exact count depends on how you classify the ambiguous entries. Collecting all unlocks one of the game's longest grind-based badges.

**What do documents do?**
Each counts toward the collection achievement. More importantly, they are the primary storytelling vehicle — they reveal the Blacksite's backstory, explain Z-13, detail Sebastian's transformation, expose P.AI.nter's role, and uncover why prisoners are sent into the facility. Reading them turns a horror run into a narrative mystery.

**Do documents carry over between runs?**
Yes, collection is persistent. Once picked up, a document is permanently registered. The game tracks which numbers you have found, so you can focus on missing ones in later runs. The achievement is designed for gradual collection across many runs, not a single attempt.
