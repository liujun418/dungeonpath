You are in a dark corridor. Room 37. The lights flicker. A screech tears through your headphones — high-pitched, rising, clean. You have 3.5 seconds to live. Your brain says run. Your hands say freeze. You do neither. You die.

You just met Rush. And you died because nobody told you the one thing that matters: **every Pressure entity announces itself with a unique sound.** Learn the sounds and you stop dying. Ignore them and you die 50 times before Room 50.

This is not a bestiary. You won't find lore paragraphs or stat tables. This is a field manual organized by what you hear. Read the sounds. Memorize the response windows. Practice the 3-minute drill. You will survive.

## The Audio Roster: Sound to Survival

**Clean high-pitched screech, rising, lights flicker once:** Rush. 3.5 seconds. Enter the nearest closet — immediately. Not after looting. Not after one more step. The instant that screech registers, your feet move toward the nearest locker. Exit after the sound passes. The common death: freezing for 2 seconds while your brain processes the sound, then spending 1.5 seconds scanning for a closet. You enter at 3.2 seconds with 0.3 seconds to spare. Sometimes the closet is too far. Rush has killed more beginners than all other entities combined. Learn its sound first.

**Distorted glitchy screech, lower-pitched, wavers, lights flicker repeatedly:** Ambush. 3.5 seconds before first pass. 2-5 seconds between passes. 2-6 total passes. Enter the nearest closet. Now count. After every pass, count to 3. If 3 seconds of silence pass, you are clear. If another screech tears through within those 3 seconds, stay put and count again. The death: exiting between passes. You hear silence for 2 seconds, think it is over, step out, and Ambush claims you on pass 4. Never assume the sequence is over. Count. Every. Time.

**A whisper of "psst" in a dark room:** Screech. 3 seconds. Spin a full 360 degrees smoothly. Screech's glowing white eyes are visible in your peripheral vision. The moment your crosshair crosses its eyes, it vanishes and you take zero damage. The death: panicked, jerky spinning. Your mouse moves in stuttering arcs. You miss the eyes by 10 degrees. You die. Smooth, steady rotation. That is the only technique.

**Heavy breathing and heavy footsteps:** Figure. Detection is instant if you move during its listen phase. Walk only. Crouch-walk is better. When the footsteps stop and breathing becomes audible, freeze completely. Figure is blind and tracks exclusively by sound. Unbind your sprint key if you keep accidentally pressing it. The death: one twitch of the W key during listen phase. Figure locks onto you. Run is not an option.

**Low humming, purple screen tint:** Eyes. Detection is instant on room entry. Immediately look at the floor. Navigate by peripheral vision and wall-following. Looking at Eyes drains roughly 8% HP per second. A 3-second glance costs a quarter of your health bar. The death: you walk into the room, see the purple glow, and your instinct says "look at the threat." Eyes punishes that instinct brutally.

**Loud roar, heavy charging footsteps:** Grumble (The Mines). 2 seconds from roar to charge. Freeze. Grumble tracks movement. Wait 8-15 seconds for it to reposition. Walk to the exit when it moves away. Look up at the ceiling when entering every Mines room. The Hotel never taught you to look up. The death: entering a room, not checking the ceiling, triggering Grumble. It charges. You panic-sprint. Movement is exactly what Grumble locks onto.

**Wet gurgling and splashing:** Giggle swarms (The Mines). Continuous chip damage. Sprint through. Do not fight. Giggles are slow and cannot catch a sprinting player. The death: standing your ground, trying to fight, dying to accumulated chip damage over 15 seconds. You cannot out-DPS a swarm. You can only outrun it.

## Failure Analysis: The 3 Ways You Die

Understanding why you die is as important as knowing what to do. These three patterns account for the vast majority of Pressure deaths. Recognize them. Break them.

### Pattern 1: The Recognition Lag Death

**What happens:** An audio cue plays. Your brain spends 1.5-2.5 seconds identifying it. By the time you know what you are hearing, the response window is already half gone. You scramble, find a closet at 3.2 seconds, and die with your hand on the locker door.

**Why it happens:** Your brain is running a lookup table. "Is that Rush? Or Ambush? Or a distant explosion?" Every moment spent identifying is a moment not spent reacting.

**The fix:** Train audio recognition until it is automatic. Use the 3-minute drill described below. You need to hear the screech and move before your conscious brain names the entity. Reflex, not analysis.

### Pattern 2: The Premature Safety Assumption

**What happens:** You survive the first pass of an entity. Silence. You assume the encounter is over. You step out of the closet. The entity returns and kills you.

**Why it happens:** Your brain treats "silence" as "safe." In Pressure, silence is often the gap between attacks, not the end of them. Ambush exploits this mercilessly. So does any multi-wave encounter.

**The fix:** Always count. After any entity encounter, count to 5 before moving. After Ambush specifically, count to 3 after every pass. If 3 seconds bring no new screech, you are clear. If a screech arrives, reset the count. Counting is boring. Dying is worse.

### Pattern 3: The Instinct Inversion

**What happens:** Your survival instincts from other games kill you in Pressure. You see a threat and look at it (Eyes kills you). You hear danger and sprint (Figure and Grumble lock onto you). You want to fight back (Giggles overwhelm you with chip damage).

**Why it happens:** Most games reward engagement. See enemy, face enemy, fight enemy. Pressure punishes engagement with most entities. The correct response is often the opposite of what your gaming instincts demand.

**The fix:** Relearn your reflexes. Eyes = look down. Figure = stop moving. Grumble = freeze. Giggles = run past. These are the opposite of what feels right. Drill them until they replace your old instincts.

## Decision Framework: The 4-Second Protocol

When you hear any audio cue, you have 4 seconds or less. Paralysis kills. Use this framework:

1. **Categorize the sound (0.5s):** Chase sound (screech, roar) or ambient sound (humming, breathing, gurgling)? Chase sounds mean find cover. Ambient sounds mean change behavior.
2. **Execute the counter (0.5s-1.5s):** You already know the one action for each entity. Do it. Do not think about whether it is the right action. Trust your training.
3. **Verify survival (1s-2s):** Confirm the entity passed, the eyes vanished, the footsteps moved away. Do not leave cover until verified.
4. **Resume with caution (indefinite):** Move forward. But know that Pressure loves stacking encounters. Surviving Rush does not mean Ambush is not 10 seconds behind it.

This framework works because it removes decision-making from the critical window. You categorize, you execute, you verify. The categories are pre-learned. The counters are pre-learned. You are not deciding. You are executing.

## 5 Counter-Intuitive Truths About Pressure Entities

**1. Sprinting is the wrong response to most threats.** Rush, Ambush, Figure, Grumble, and Eyes all punish movement in different ways. Sprinting often triggers detection or wastes your response window on a direction that does not have a closet. The instinct to run is the single deadliest instinct in Pressure. Walk. Crouch. Freeze. Anything but sprint.

**2. Looking at the entity is almost always fatal.** Your eyes want to confirm the threat. Eyes punishes looking directly. Figure punishes being seen (it is blind, but making noise while looking at it still gets you killed). Even Screech — the one entity you must look at — requires you to sweep your crosshair across it, not stare. Peripheral vision is your best friend.

**3. Doing nothing is often the safest action.** Figure approaches. You freeze. Grumble roars. You freeze. You hear an ambiguous sound. You freeze and listen. In a game that feels like it demands constant action, the winning move is frequently complete stillness. New players die because they feel compelled to do something. Veterans survive because they know when to do nothing.

**4. Closets are not safe zones — they are timing tools.** A closet protects you for exactly as long as the entity's pass takes. Exit early and you die. Exit late and you are fine, but you wasted time. The closet is a rhythm instrument. You enter on the beat, you exit on the count. Treating it as a panic room encourages early exit — the most common Ambush death.

**5. Audio is more important than visuals in Pressure.** You can beat the game playing with your monitor off if you know every sound cue. You cannot beat the game with your audio off no matter how good your screen is. Every entity telegraphs through sound before it telegraphs through sight. Invest in decent headphones. Turn off music. Crank the SFX. Your ears are your primary survival organ.

## The Learning Order

Not all entities are equally important. Learn in this order:

**Rush first.** Kills more beginners than all other entities combined. Master the 3.5-second response. Learn to distinguish its clean screech from every other sound in the game.

**Ambush second.** The multi-pass mechanic is the number one killer of intermediate players. You survived Rush. You think you understand closets. Ambush proves you wrong.

**Screech third.** Easy counter once you know it. Requires headphones. Smooth rotation, not jerky panic. Three minutes of practice and you will never die to Screech again.

**Figure fourth.** Only appears at Doors 50 and 100. But those are the doors where dying costs the most progress. Learn to walk, crouch, and freeze on command.

**Eyes fifth.** The simplest fix in the game. Look at the floor. That is it. But you have to override the instinct to look at a threat, and that takes conscious practice.

Master these five and you survive 95 percent of encounters. The remaining ten entities are situational — learned through exposure, not through drills. Ten minutes of audio study, listening to Rush and Ambush side by side, replaces 3 hours of dying in the dark.

## The 3-Minute Drill

This is the fastest way to build entity recognition. It is brutal. It works.

Enter a run. Sprint through rooms. Do not loot. Do not hide. When an entity kills you, restart. Repeat for 30 minutes.

In 30 minutes of drill, you will face 15-20 Rush encounters. In 2 hours of careful play, you might face 5. The drill compresses the learning curve. You die more, you learn faster. Each death teaches you the sound just a little better. By the end of one session, you will hear Rush and move before you consciously register the screech.

The drill is not fun. It is effective. Do it once. Then play normally. You will notice the difference immediately.
