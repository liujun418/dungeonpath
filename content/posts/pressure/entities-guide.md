Pressure has fifteen entities. Every single one announces itself with a unique audio cue before it attacks. The game gives you warnings. You die because you do not recognize them yet. This guide is organized by what you hear, not by entity name. When a sound plays and you do not know what it means, flip to the matching description. The table maps sound to entity to seconds to respond to action to common death. Burn this into your reflexes.

A clean high-pitched screech getting louder means Rush. You have 3.5 seconds. Enter the nearest closet immediately — not in 2 seconds, not after you finish looting that container. The instant you hear the screech, move. Exit after the screech stops and the lights return to normal. The common death is freezing for 2 seconds while your brain processes the sound, then spending the remaining 1.5 seconds looking for a closet, entering at 3.2 seconds with a 0.3-second margin. Rush has killed more beginners than all other entities combined. Learn its sound first.

A distorted glitchy lower-pitched screech that wavers means Ambush. You have 3.5 seconds before the first pass, then 2-5 seconds between subsequent passes. Enter the nearest closet. Count to 3 after every pass. If 3 seconds pass with silence, exit. If another screech is heard within 3 seconds, stay and count again. Ambush passes 2-6 times. The common death: exiting between passes because you assumed "it is probably done." Never assume. Count.

A whisper of "psst" in a dark room means Screech. You have 3 seconds. Spin in a full 360-degree circle smoothly — Screech's glowing white eyes are visible in peripheral vision. The moment your crosshair crosses its eyes, it disappears and you take zero damage. The common death: spinning too slowly, looking around in jerky panicked movements, failing to find the eyes within 3 seconds.

Heavy breathing and heavy footsteps mean Figure. The detection window is instant if you move during Figure's listen phase. Walk only. Crouch-walk is better. When the footsteps stop and breathing becomes audible, freeze completely. Figure is blind and tracks only by sound. The common death: accidentally pressing sprint. Unbind your sprint key before Figure encounters if you keep accidentally pressing it.

A low humming sound accompanied by a purple screen tint means Eyes. The detection window is instant upon room entry. Immediately look at the floor. Navigate by peripheral vision and wall-following. Looking at Eyes drains roughly 8% HP per second of eye contact. The common death: looking around to "see what is happening," draining a quarter of your health in 3 seconds before you realize.

A loud roar followed by heavy charging footsteps means Grumble, exclusive to The Mines. You have 2 seconds from the roar to the charge. Freeze. Grumble tracks movement, not sound. Wait 8-15 seconds for it to move to another ceiling position. Walk — do not run — to the exit when it repositions. The common death: entering a room, scanning horizontally for threats like the Hotel taught you, never looking up at the ceiling, and getting spotted before you knew Grumble existed.

Wet gurgling and splashing sounds mean Giggle swarms, exclusive to The Mines. Damage is continuous chip damage. Sprint through the room. Do not stop. Do not fight. Giggles are slow and cannot catch a sprinting player. The common death: standing your ground trying to fight individual Giggles, dying to accumulated chip damage over 15 seconds that you did not realize was lethal.

The entity recognition learning curve is predictable. Players who do not study sounds die to Rush on roughly 80 percent of their first 20 runs. Players who spend 10 minutes listening to Rush and Ambush audio side by side cut their Rush death rate to under 20 percent within 5 runs. Ten minutes of audio study replaces 3 hours of dying. The 3-minute drill accelerates this further by giving you 15-20 Rush encounters in 30 minutes rather than 5 encounters in 2 hours.
