## "Be Careful" Isn't Advice

Most Pressure survival guides say things like "listen for entity sounds" and "manage your resources." These aren't wrong. They're also useless. You already know you should listen for Rush. You already know you shouldn't waste Medkits. What you need are protocols — specific, repeatable sequences of actions for specific situations.

Here are the 10 protocols that turn "I know I should be careful" into "I know exactly what to do when this happens."

One thing changed the floor-20-to-40 stretch recently: on August 16, 2026, Pressure shipped an update with 6 new modifiers, 2 new badges, and 1 new entity. Modifiers don't just make the game harder — they change which of your old habits still work. Rule 9 below is about re-testing those habits, because a routine that survived a week ago can now get you killed.

---

## Rule 1: The Room Entry Scan (Every Single Room)

You enter a new room. Rush is quiet right now, but it won't stay that way. Most players walk in, look at the loot, and only think about hiding spots when the screech starts. That's the mistake.

Here's the protocol: enter the room, stop for half a second, find the nearest locker or closet, note its direction. THEN explore.

Why this order matters: when Rush screeches eight seconds later, you don't spend 1.5 seconds scanning the room. Your feet already know where to go. You're inside the locker at 2 seconds instead of 3.2 seconds. That gap is the entire difference between a clean clear and a death screen.

Practice it until it's automatic. On your next run, narrate it out loud for the first five rooms: "locker left, closet back right." By floor 30 you'll do it without thinking, and that's the point.

## Rule 2: Rush vs Ambush — The Multi-Pass Count

The sound is the tell, and you get it before you see anything. Rush gives you one clean, rising screech and passes once. Ambush's screech is distorted and glitchy, and it doesn't leave — it loops back.

Here's the protocol:

- Rush screech → enter the locker, step out immediately after it passes.
- Ambush screech (distorted, glitchy) → enter the locker, count to 3 after each pass. No new screech within 3 seconds? Safe. New screech? Count again.
- Never exit between Ambush passes. The one time you do, Ambush is already on its way back.

This matters because the two entities punish opposite mistakes. Rush punishes staying hidden too long (you waste time, which costs you nothing but confidence). Ambush punishes leaving too early (it's still coming). The count-to-3 rule is the only thing that works for both.

| Sound | Entity | What you do | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean rising screech, one pass | Rush | Hide, exit right after the pass | It only comes once; lingering is safe but wastes nothing except time |
| Distorted, glitchy, loops back | Ambush | Hide, count to 3 after each pass | It returns multiple times; exiting between passes is instant death |
| No new sound for 3 seconds | Either, now clear | Step out and move | 3 seconds is the safe window; any longer and you've burned your timing |

## Rule 3: The Screech Spin

You're in a dark room and hear "psst." Screech is in there with you, and it attacks after a short window unless you look directly at its glowing white eyes.

Don't look around slowly. Don't sweep the room in sections. Spin a full 360 degrees smoothly, in one motion. Screech's eyes catch your peripheral vision almost immediately — usually within a second — because bright white on black is impossible to miss when you're turning fast.

The second you lock eyes, it disappears. The attack timer is generous if you spin instead of searching. Slow scanning is what gets people killed here, because Screech's window closes while you're still checking corner one.

This rule pairs with the [full entity timing breakdown](/posts/pressure/entity-timing-windows/) if you want exact second counts for every threat.

## Rule 4: The Medkit Rule — Heal Early, Never Hoard

Here's the counterintuitive one: don't save Medkits. Ever.

You'd think the smart play is to hold them for a real emergency. But in Pressure, the "real emergency" usually kills you before you can open the bag. If you're below 60 HP and you're in a safe room, heal immediately.

Here's why the math works out this way: every run that ends below floor 40 has one thing in common — the player died with Medkits still in their inventory. The player who heals at 60 HP survives the next Rush graze at 25 HP. The player who "saves" their Medkits dies at 45 HP with three of them untouched.

A Medkit in your bag at the death screen does zero healing. A Medkit used at 60 HP keeps you above the one-shot threshold for two more rooms. That's the entire trade.

## Rule 5: The Figure Walk-Only Rule

Figure rooms — the Library, the Electrical Room — are where accidental inputs end runs. Figure is blind, but it hears everything. Sprint is its favorite sound.

If you keep accidentally pressing sprint during a listen phase, unbind it before you enter. Walk only. Crouch-walk is better. One accidental sprint during a Figure listen phase is a guaranteed detection, and detection in a Figure room is almost always a wipe.

The protocol is simple: before you touch a Figure room, tell yourself "walk." Not "be careful," not "stay quiet." "Walk." It's a concrete instruction your fingers can obey.

For the locker mechanics behind all of these rules, see the [locker hiding guide](/posts/pressure/locker-hiding-mechanics-guide/).

## Rule 6: Pandemonium's Locker Minigame

Pandemonium is the exception to every "hide and wait" rule. When it catches you in a locker, you don't wait it out — you play a minigame where you hold your cursor inside a moving circle. Slip, and it pulls you out.

The mistake players make is panic. They grip too hard, jerk the cursor, and overshoot the circle. The protocol is the opposite: loose hand, small adjustments, keep the cursor inside the ring, don't chase it.

Pandemonium is rare, so most players die to it the first time they see it because they've never practiced the motion. The fix is to remember it exists on deep floors and keep your hand relaxed the whole run. You can't practice the minigame on demand, but you can decide now that you won't white-knuckle it when it shows up.

## Rule 7: Light Discipline in Dark Rooms

Dark rooms are where two things live: Squiddles and Screech. They punish opposite behaviors, and that's what makes them hard.

Squiddles hate light. Flash them and they aggro. Screech needs you to see its eyes, which means you need light. So the same tool — your flashlight — is a weapon against one and a trigger against the other.

The protocol: in a dark room, sweep with quick, short flashes, not a held beam. A quick pulse gives you just enough to spot Screech's eyes without giving Squiddles time to react. Hold the beam and Squiddles lock on.

This is the second counterintuitive rule, and it trips people up because it looks backwards: your instinct says "more light is safer." In Pressure's dark rooms, a constant beam is a dinner bell.

Here's the version you'll actually face: you're down to your last battery, and the next room is dark. Do you burn it now, or save it for a room you haven't seen yet? Burn it now, in short pulses. A dark room you're standing in can kill you in five seconds; a hypothetical future room can't. Dead players have full batteries.

## Rule 8: The Kroner Economy — A Hard Choice

Here's the two-way decision you'll hit every single run: you've got one batch of Kroner, and you're standing in front of Sebastian's shop. Do you buy the expensive utility item, or two cheap basics?

The wrong answer is "save it for the big upgrade later." The right answer is: buy a Medkit and a light source first, every time. Those two items convert directly into floor progress — they keep you alive one more room, one more floor.

Here's the framework. Ask one question: "does this buy keep me alive in the next three rooms, or does it only pay off after floor 50?" Medkit and light = alive in the next three rooms. Expensive niche item = dead at floor 35 with a full wallet. Spend on the next three rooms, not the hypothetical deep run.

## Rule 9: The August 16 Update — Re-Test Your Old Routine

On August 16, 2026, Pressure added 6 new modifiers, 2 new badges, and 1 new entity. The specific names are still being documented community-side, so I won't guess them here. What I can tell you is what the update means for your survival rules.

New modifiers in Pressure almost always hit one of three levers: locker timing, visibility or light, and spawn rate. Any one of those quietly breaks a habit from the old meta.

- A locker-timing modifier means your Rule 2 count-to-3 might now be too slow or too fast. Re-test it.
- A visibility modifier means your Rule 7 flash discipline changes — what used to be "too much light" might now be "not enough," or the reverse.
- A spawn-rate modifier means your Rule 1 room scan gets harder, because threats arrive back-to-back.

The takeaway isn't "learn 6 new rules." It's "don't trust your old reflexes." Run each new modifier once on a low floor, note what changed, and only then take it deep. For the full breakdown of what was added, see our [modifiers update guide](/posts/pressure/modifiers-update-guide/).

## Rule 10: Pre-Plan the Deep Floors

Floor 20 is where casual runs die. Floor 20 is also where the game stops being generous — rooms get longer, threats chain, and one mistake snowballs. The players who push to 100 don't out-react anyone. They out-plan.

Before you queue a deep run, decide three things in advance: your route, your item plan, and your death condition. Route — which rooms you'll clear and which you'll skip. Item plan — what you buy at each shop stop. Death condition — the specific situation where you abort and restart rather than push.

The reason this works: on floor 60 you don't have spare attention to make decisions. You're surviving. If you already made the decisions at floor 1, you just execute them. Preparation looks like luck to the unprepared, and it's not luck. It's protocol.

## The Floor 20 Wall: A Full Death Review

Let's walk through the most common death at the floor-20-to-40 wall, because understanding it is worth more than memorizing any single rule.

The mistake: you enter a long room, spot a juicy item at the far end, and sprint to it. Rush screeches. You're mid-sprint, far from any locker, and you have to spend 1.5 seconds scanning for a hiding spot. You find one, but you're late by half a second. Dead at floor 24.

What actually went wrong isn't the sprint. It's that you broke Rule 1 three rooms earlier. You stopped doing the room-entry scan because it felt automatic — except it wasn't automatic, it was habit, and habits decay under pressure.

The recovery: don't blame the Rush. Rebuild the scan as a conscious step. For your next three runs, force the out-loud narration: "locker left" every single room, no exceptions. After three runs it's automatic again. Then — and only then — let yourself sprint for loot again, because now your feet already know the escape route before you need it.

Second common death at the wall: you hear the glitchy screech, dive into a locker, and step out the instant the sound stops — because it sounded like it passed. But it was Ambush, and it's already coming back. Dead at floor 31. The cause wasn't the audio. It's that you treated Ambush like Rush because you were tired, and the two sound almost identical under stress. The recovery: any screech that isn't obviously clean gets the count-to-3. Make the count non-negotiable, not a guess. A wasted three-second wait costs you nothing; one early exit costs the whole run.

The meta-lesson: deaths at the wall are almost never about reaction speed. They're about a rule you quietly stopped doing. Find the dropped rule, and the wall moves.

## The Bottom Line

Everything in this guide is one principle: Pressure rewards preparation, not reaction. The players who clear consistently aren't faster. They made tomorrow's decisions yesterday. They know which room has the nearest locker before Rush screams. They know the heal threshold before they take damage. They know what each of the 6 new modifiers did to their routine before they run it deep.

Preparation looks like luck to the unprepared. It's not luck. It's protocol.
