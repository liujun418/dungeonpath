## You Open MSM and Air Island Has a New Neighbor

You close the game before bed like you always do. Next morning, the App Store badge says there's an update. Version 5.6.0. You tap through the patch notes without reading them properly — everyone does — and load into your islands.

Then you notice it. Air Island isn't quite the same. There's a monster standing there you've never placed, and it's not a normal monster. It's a Legendary. It's *Alcordion*, and it's voiced by "Weird Al" Yankovic.

If you're a long-time player, that name hits hard. Weird Al is a five-time Grammy winner and one of the most requested collaborations in My Singing Monsters history. Fans have been asking for this for years. Big Blue Bubble finally did it, and they didn't just slap his face on a reskin — they built an entire gameplay system around him.

That last part matters more than the novelty. Most crossover monsters are collectibles. You breed them, you place them, you move on. Alcordion is different, and if you treat it like a normal Legendary, you'll walk right past the actual reward buried underneath it.

## What Is Alcordion, Exactly?

Alcordion is a Legendary Monster that lives on Air Island. It's based on "Weird Al" Yankovic, and the name is a portmanteau of "Al" and "accordion" — which fits, because the accordion is basically Weird Al's second instrument after his voice.

The headline detail is a title you'll see on the monster: **Master of Games**. That's not flavor text. Alcordion is the monster that introduces MSM's new **Minigame system**. Before 5.6.0, the game had a Colossingum for battles and various events, but not a dedicated minigame hub tied to a single monster. Alcordion changes that.

Here's the quick reference, then we'll get into what each piece actually means for you.

| Alcordion Detail | What It Means |
|------------------|---------------|
| **Type** | Legendary Monster |
| **Based on** | "Weird Al" Yankovic, Grammy-winning musician |
| **Island** | Air Island |
| **Title** | Master of Games |
| **New system it unlocks** | Minigame system |
| **First minigame** | DIPSTER DIGS |
| **Minigame reward** | Dipster monsters (never seen before 5.6.0) |
| **Forms** | Base "Bigger and Weirder" look + ClassicAl costume |

The important word in that table is "unlocks." Alcordion isn't just a monster you collect. It's the key to a new way to earn new monsters.

## The Two Sides of Alcordion: Bigger & Weirder vs ClassicAl

Like a lot of MSM's special monsters, Alcordion ships with more than one look.

The **base form** is built around Weird Al's current "Bigger and Weirder" persona. That's the version of Al you'd recognize from his recent tours and branding — the modern look. It's the default, and you get it the moment Alcordion joins your island.

The second option is the **ClassicAl costume**. This one is based on his breakout album *In 3-D*, the 1984 record that gave the world "Eat It." It's a throwback to the Al a lot of long-time fans grew up with — glasses, curls, the whole era.

Here's where you need to stop and make a call.

**Do you buy the ClassicAl costume or not?**

The honest answer is that it's a cosmetic decision. The costume changes how Alcordion looks. It does not change the song, it does not change the minigame, and it does not change your Dipster rewards. If you're the kind of player who wants the *In 3-D* look on your island because it means something to you, grab it. If you're optimizing for new monsters and gameplay, the costume is a money sink you can skip.

The trap is treating the costume like content. It's a skin. The content is the minigame, and we haven't gotten to that yet.

## DIPSTER DIGS: The Minigame That Sits on Top of a Legendary

Here's the part most players will misread on day one.

Alcordion looks like a souvenir. A big-name celebrity crossover that exists to get you to log in, take a screenshot, and tell your friends. You place it on Air Island, admire the accordion, and think that's the whole update.

That's the counterintuitive bit: **the monster is not the reward. The minigame is.**

Alcordion's Master of Games title means it opens the Minigame system, and the first minigame is **DIPSTER DIGS**. The name is a pun on "hipster" and "dig," and it's a digging game. The payoff is the part worth your attention: DIPSTER DIGS rewards **Dipster monsters** — creatures that had never appeared in My Singing Monsters before this update.

Read that again. New monsters. Not a reskin, not a rare variant, not a costume. Dipsters are genuinely new additions to the roster, and the only way to get them is through Alcordion's minigame.

So the monster you were about to place and forget is actually a doorway to a whole new pool of collectibles. That's why treating Alcordion as a decoration is the single most expensive mistake you can make in this update. We'll break down that mistake — and three others — in a moment.

### How DIPSTER DIGS Actually Plays Out

You place Alcordion on Air Island. You tap into the Master of Games menu. You select DIPSTER DIGS. Then you dig.

The loop is simple: dig through whatever the minigame puts in front of you, and the payoff for a good run is a Dipster. Since Dipsters are brand new, every Dipster you pull out is a first-of-its-kind for your collection — the kind of thing that, in a normal MSM update, would be gated behind a breeding combo or a seasonal event.

The specifics of exactly how many digs you get or how scoring works are inside the game itself, and the update is only a few days old. The thing to internalize right now is *where* the reward lives. If you spend all your attention on Alcordion the monster and never open DIPSTER DIGS, you leave the new Dipsters on the table.

## How to Actually Get Your Dipsters

You've placed Alcordion. The minigame is unlocked. Now what?

The answer is boring but real: play the minigame, and keep playing it until the Dipster rewards are exhausted.

There's no breeding combo for Dipsters. No seasonal wait. No diamond purchase (as far as the 5.6.0 notes say). The Dipsters come from DIPSTER DIGS, and the minigame comes from Alcordion. That's the whole chain.

If you've been through MSM's other collection grinds — the legendary breeding in our [Legendary Island guide](/posts/my-singing-monsters/legendary-island-guide/), the shard engine in our [Ethereal Island guide](/posts/my-singing-monsters/ethereal-island-guide/) — you know the game loves to hide new monsters behind long timers. A minigame reward is a welcome change of pace, but it only helps you if you actually run it.

One thing to keep in mind while you're at it: your Air Island still needs to generate coins and food for the rest of your progression. Don't let the new shiny thing pull you away from your normal farm. If your coin and food lines are thin, check the [Farming Guide](/posts/my-singing-monsters/farming-guide/) before you sink hours into the minigame. The minigame is a new income stream for *monsters*, not a replacement for your economy.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

Every big MSM update produces the same handful of player errors. Here are the four I'd bet on for 5.6.0, with the reason each one happens and how to fix it.

### Mistake 1: Treating Alcordion as a Decoration

**The scene:** You log in, see the Weird Al monster, get the dopamine hit, place Alcordion on Air Island, and go back to whatever you were breeding. A week later you still haven't opened the Master of Games menu.

**Why it happens:** Crossover monsters usually *are* decorations. Your brain has been trained by years of celebrity skins and limited-time collectibles that the monster is the content.

**The fix:** Alcordion is a gateway, not a trophy. Open the Master of Games menu on day one and start DIPSTER DIGS. The new Dipsters are behind that menu, and the longer you wait, the longer you go without them.

### Mistake 2: Missing the DIPSTER DIGS Rewards

**The scene:** You play the minigame once, dig up one Dipster, see the novelty, and stop. You think "okay, that's the minigame, done."

**Why it happens:** A single Dipster feels like a complete payoff, so you treat the minigame as one-and-done.

**The fix:** Go back. DIPSTER DIGS is a reward pool, not a single pull. The Dipsters are the whole reason the minigame exists, and you want as many of them as the system will hand out. Finish the loop before you call it done.

### Mistake 3: Assuming You Can Farm It Forever

**The scene:** You realize Dipsters are valuable and decide to just sit there and grind DIPSTER DIGS nonstop until you have a full island of them.

**Why it happens:** "New monsters" sounds like an infinite farm, so players assume the minigame is a bottomless faucet.

**The fix:** Slow down. Reward pools in MSM are gated — limited entries, limited reward counts, or both — and a brand-new monster type is exactly the thing the team wants to keep scarce. The safe read is that Dipsters are finite and timed. Don't plan your whole week around an all-you-can-dig fantasy. Get your rewards, then shift back to your normal island work.

### Mistake 4: Letting the Code Expire

**The scene:** You see the free-code post, screenshot it, and think "I'll redeem it later." Later never comes.

**Why it happens:** MSM codes are not evergreen. They expire fast — usually within 24 to 48 hours of going live.

**The fix:** Redeem the moment you see it. One code tied to this window is **WLCM2DACLUBBOX**, which grants 10 Clubbox Tickets. You redeem it through the official My Singing Monsters website using your BBB (Big Blue Bubble) account ID — not in-game. Go to the site, log in, punch in the code, claim. If you read this and the code is dead, don't panic; there will be more, but the lesson stands: codes in MSM are use-it-or-lose-it.

## The Big Decision: Level Alcordion or Farm DIPSTER DIGS First?

Here's a real fork you'll hit on day one, and it's worth thirty seconds of thought instead of reflex.

**Option A:** Spend your food leveling Alcordion, because it's a Legendary and Legendaries should be high level.

**Option B:** Leave Alcordion alone and pour your time into DIPSTER DIGS to start collecting Dipsters.

The instinct is Option A. It's a Legendary. You level Legendaries. That's the rule.

But think about what each path actually buys you. Leveling Alcordion gets you a stronger Legendary on Air Island — more coin or song contribution, same as any other monster. Farming DIPSTER DIGS gets you *new monsters you can't get any other way*.

The counterintuitive read: **the minigame is worth more than the monster.** Alcordion at level 15 is still just one monster. Every Dipster you dig up is a permanent, exclusive addition to your collection, and there's a limited window on the new content before it gets rare or rotates.

So the practical order is: unlock the minigame, exhaust the Dipster rewards you can reach, then circle back and level Alcordion whenever your food farm has recovered. If you want the ClassicAl costume, that's a cosmetic call you can make anytime — it doesn't affect this decision either way.

The one exception is if Air Island is genuinely your best coin producer and you're low on food anyway. In that case, keep your normal farm running (see the [Farming Guide](/posts/my-singing-monsters/farming-guide/)) and fit DIPSTER DIGS in around it. The point is the *priority*, not a strict schedule.
