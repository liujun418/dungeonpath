## Enchant First, Upgrade Later

The game gives you two progression systems at once — enchanting (Gems) and upgrading (Dark Matter). Most beginners split resources between both. This is wrong.

Enchants are multiplicative. An un-upgraded pet with Criticals X does more damage than a fully-upgraded pet with no enchants. The upgrade machine is a percentage multiplier on existing power. If your base power is low because you have no enchants, upgrading multiplies a small number. Enchant first to build a strong base. Upgrade later to multiply that base.

## When Upgrading Is Actually Worth It

Upgrade your pet when:
1. It already has Criticals X (or at least VII)
2. You have 50+ Dark Matter saved (don't upgrade one at a time — the first few levels add minimal stats)
3. This is a pet you'll use for the next 50+ hours of gameplay

Don't upgrade "temporary" pets — pets you'll replace within a week. Dark Matter is too scarce to spend on transitional pets. Every Dark Matter spent on a pet you replace next week is Dark Matter you'll never get back.

## The Bottom Line

Everything in this guide comes down to one principle: the game rewards preparation, not reaction. The players who clear consistently aren't faster. They've already made tomorrow's decisions today. They know which room has the nearest closet before Rush screams. They know the rebirth costs before the button appears. They know where to place the tower before the wave starts. Preparation looks like luck to the unprepared. It's not luck. It's protocol.
