## The Same Enchant: One Pet Doubled, One Pet Flopped

Two players, same week, same enchant. You saved for twelve days, burned 130,000 Gems at the Area 16 enchant machine, and finally landed Criticals X. You scroll your team and snap it onto the pet you are most proud of — the one that cost you a fortune in the trading plaza. Its base Damage reads 8.2M. Back in the Void World your DPS crawls from 8.2M to 12.3M. A 4.1M gain after twelve days of saving. It feels like a slap.

Your friend lands the same enchant three days later. They don't put it on their trophy pet. They drop it on a plain Level 99 Knight sitting in their bank — a pet you would never spend Gems on. That Knight's base Damage is 12.9M. Their DPS jumps from 12.9M to 19.4M. Seven million more damage per second, from the same enchant, at the same cost.

You didn't get unlucky and they didn't get lucky. Criticals X is a multiplier over the pet's base Damage stat. The pet is the damage; the enchant just decides how much of it you get to keep. Put a 50% multiplier on 8M and you gain 4M. Put it on 13M and you gain 6.5M. Same Gems, same roll, different base stat, different world.

That's the entire subject of this guide: how PS99 enchantments actually work, which enchant belongs on which pet type, how pet stats set the ceiling on everything, the stacking rules that save or waste millions, and the phase-by-phase strategy that puts enchant Gems exactly where they pay off.

## How Enchantments Actually Work in PS99

The enchant machine at Area 16 takes Gems and returns a random enchant of a random tier. Tiers run I through X; X-tier enchants like Criticals X carry the biggest multipliers and the worst roll rates. Every pet has two enchant slots — a main slot for a combat or income enchant and a secondary slot for a utility enchant — and every enchant is a percentage multiplier that applies to the pet's stats.

Three mechanics decide everything:

**Enchants are percentages, not flat numbers.** Criticals X roughly doubles a pet's effective damage output on crits. Coins V adds 25% to coins collected. Diamonds V adds 25% to gems. Speed V and Magnet III multiply movement and pickup range. None of them add a fixed number of damage points. A percentage multiplies whatever is underneath it, which means the underneath matters more than the percentage itself.

**The underneath is the pet's base stats.** Every pet in PS99 has separate Damage, Coins, and Gems stats. Two pets of identical rarity can differ wildly: one Huge might be a 9M-damage brawler, another Huge a 2M-damage collector with enormous coin value. Rarity tells you the pet is special. The stat page tells you what it is actually good at — and it's the stat page you should read before rolling, not the rarity color. When in doubt, check the pet's Damage number in the pet menu before spending anything.

**Enchant tier multiplies the multiplier, not the pet.** Criticals IX is a smaller multiplier than Criticals X, and both are multiplied against the same base damage. A player who puts Criticals X on a 4M-damage pet gets 8M worth of output. A player who puts Criticals IX on a 12M-damage pet gets closer to 22M. The cheaper enchant on the stronger pet beats the expensive enchant on the weaker pet every single time.

One more thing to internalize: enchants are permanent on the pet. There is no transfer system, no extractor, no way to pull Criticals X off a pet and move it. The moment you roll, those Gems are welded to that pet forever.

## The Three Enchantment Killers (and How Each One Costs You)

### Killer 1: Enchanting the Wrong Pet

The story is the same every week in trading chats: "Criticals X on my Huge, and my DPS barely moved." The enchant wasn't the problem. The pet was. A player enchants their most expensive pet because it feels like the right move — big rarity, big investment, big expectations. But if that pet's base Damage is mediocre, the enchant multiplies mediocrity.

The classic version: you enchant a transitional pet — the Level 70 you're only using until you hatch something better next week. The enchant costs 100K-150K Gems. Next week the pet gets benched, and every Gem in that enchant is dead weight forever.

The fix is a rule, not a feeling: only enchant pets that will still be in your team 100 hours from now, and among those, always enchant the one with the highest base Damage first. If a pet could plausibly be replaced in a month, it gets no Gems. An unspent enchant roll is always available tomorrow. An enchant stuck on a retired pet is gone forever.

### Killer 2: Treating Every Pet Like the Same Pet

Players run the same two enchants on all five team slots: Criticals X and Strength X, no exceptions. Then they wonder why their coin farming crawls while their friend's bank account explodes. Enchants are role-specific. A Strength X on a pet whose job is collecting coins is a wasted slot; Coins V on your clan-battle damage dealer is equally dead.

The symptom shows up as "I have the meta loadout and worse results than my friend." That's because the loadout is only meta when the enchants match the pet's role. Check each of your five slots and ask: what is this pet for? Fighting, coins, gems, or utility? Then enchant for the job.

### Killer 3: Stacking Without Reading the Rules

Three sub-mistakes under one roof:

- Five copies of Coins V across five pets. Stacking is additive and the returns collapse after the third copy — the 4th adds roughly 14% relative improvement for the full cost of a Master-tier roll.
- Two Criticals X on the same pet. A pet has one main slot and one secondary slot, and duplicates don't stack. The machine will happily take your 5,000 Gems for a duplicate that does nothing.
- Enchants on benched pets. Any pet not in your active team is carrying dead enchants. The "well-equipped" player with 30 enchanted pets loses to the player with 5 correctly enchanted deployed pets.

The recovery strategy for all three is the same: inventory audit. Open your team. Enchant only the five deployed. Never stack past the caps below. And if you have rolled a duplicate this month — stop rolling that enchant on that pet and move on.

## Best Enchantments by Pet Type

Before you roll anything, decide what each deployed pet actually does. Five roles cover the whole game:

| Pet Role | Main Enchant | Secondary Enchant | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| DPS sweeper (highest base Damage) | Criticals X | Strength X or Speed V | Crits roughly double effective damage; Strength adds raw power for clan-boss fights; Speed keeps break speed high |
| Coin farmer | Coins V | Criticals X | Coins V adds 25% coins; the crit keeps the pet's damage relevant in higher worlds |
| Gem farmer | Diamonds V | Criticals X | Same logic as coins, but for Gems — your second income stream |
| Utility runner | Magnet III | Speed V | Bigger pickup radius and movement are what actually accelerate time-gated events |
| Luck hunter | Luck V | Criticals X | Luck shifts rare-drop tables; the crit means the pet still contributes when drops fail |

The order of priority: enchant your DPS sweeper first, then your coin farmer, then your gem farmer, then utility, then luck. Most players run luck first because rare drops feel important — but rare drops are rare precisely because you don't need them every minute. A doubling DPS multiplier pays for itself in everything you do, all the time. Luck pays for itself once a week if you're lucky.

One more assignment note: the DPS role goes to the pet with the highest base Damage, not the pet with the highest rarity or the best trade value. Your trade bait and your DPS pet should never be the same pet — see the trading values guide before you enchant something you might sell.

## Stat Optimization: The Order of Operations

Stat optimization in PS99 is three decisions in a fixed order: pick the pet, enchant the pet, upgrade the pet. The order exists because each step multiplies the step before it.

A quick math walkthrough. Pet base Damage 10M. Criticals X roughly doubles effective output — 20M. A Dark Matter upgrade adding 25% damage turns that into 25M. Now flip the order: upgrade first (12.5M), then enchant (25M). Same final number. So why does order matter? Because the two resources have completely different scarcity. Gems are abundant — 50K to 150K a day from Void World grinding. Dark Matter is scarce — 5 to 10 a week from events, rebirths, and rare drops. The order that spends the abundant resource first (enchant) and saves the scarce one for after (upgrade) is the same product at a fraction of the scheduling risk: you are never holding Dark Matter for a pet that might be replaced.

That leads to the upgrade rule: after enchanting, the best Dark Matter target is the pet with the best enchant. Upgrades multiply whatever the enchant already gave you. Upgrading an unenchanted pet multiplies nothing. This is also why the enchant-first strategy from the upgrades guide isn't just about gems — it sets up every future Dark Matter spend to land on a pet that already doubles its output.

### The 150K Gem Decision

You have 150K Gems and two candidates. Your main DPS sits at 12.9M base damage with Criticals VIII already on it — pushing to Criticals X gains you maybe 10% more of its output, roughly 1.3M DPS for 150K Gems. Your coin farmer sits at 9.1M base damage with no enchant at all — Criticals X on it doubles its contribution, roughly 9M DPS plus a healthier coin stream, for the same 150K Gems.

The framework: enchant the unenchanted high-base pet before upgrading an already-enchanted one. Re-roll only when both are true: the target pet is in your deployed five, and the gain is at least 20% of that pet's current contribution. Anything less and the Gems are better held. Unspent Gems are power you haven't misallocated yet.

## Enchantment Stacking Rules: What Stacks, What Caps, What Wastes Gems

| What You Stack | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Same enchant on same pet | Does not stack; duplicate is wasted | One main slot plus one secondary slot per pet |
| Coins V or Diamonds V across pets | Stacks additively, cap at 3 useful copies | 4th copy is only ~14% relative improvement |
| Criticals across pets | Stacks per-pet; every deployed pet wants it | Each pet rolls crits independently, so crit chance never diminishes |
| Luck on multiple pets | One dedicated luck pet is enough | Luck drop bonuses don't compound across pets |
| Enchants on benched pets | Zero effect | Only deployed pets' enchants apply |

The takeaway under the table: distribution beats duplication. Five pets with five different correctly-matched enchants massively out-perform five pets with the same enchant. The only enchant that breaks this rule is Criticals — because its effect is per-pet and doesn't get cheaper the more you own, every deployed pet earns a copy.

## The Counter-Intuitive Playbook

### 1. Your best enchant belongs on your most boring pet

The pet you'd never show off — plain model, mid rarity, zero trade value — is often the one with the best base Damage. The enchant machine doesn't know rarity. It multiplies the Damage number. Check the stat page, not the design.

### 2. More pets with the same enchant is less power, not more

The natural instinct is "Coins V helped on pet one, so it helps on pets two through five." Additive stacking means the 4th and 5th copies are nearly worthless. The counter-intuitive truth: the best way to stack is to spread — one role per pet, caps respected.

### 3. Upgrading before enchanting is like painting before priming

The final number is the same either way, which is exactly why players do it wrong: they see "multiplier is multiplier" and spend Dark Matter early. But the scarce resource is Dark Matter, and upgrades land on an unenchanted pet's flat damage. Enchant first with abundant Gems, upgrade after with the rare stuff — the product is identical, the risk is not.

### 4. The enchant you haven't rolled yet is free. The one on a benched pet cost you everything.

Players keep enchanting "just in case" and end up with a museum of enchanted pets they never deploy. Every one of those is 100K+ Gems frozen. The most valuable enchant in your account is the one on a pet you actually fight with. Audit your team before you roll, not after.

## The Complete Enchantment Strategy, Phase by Phase

**Phase 1 — First week.** Roll the best enchant you can afford onto your highest base Damage deployed pet, even if the tier is low (Criticals VII beats nothing). Give the rest of your team cheap tier I-III enchants matched to their roles — Speed, Magnet, small Coins. Nothing you enchant in Phase 1 is a pet you might trade. Cost discipline from the economy guide applies from day one: budget 150K Gems per Master-tier pet, stop after 50 failed rolls.

**Phase 2 — Weeks two to four.** Build the full five-slot loadout: DPS sweeper with Criticals X, coin farmer with Coins V, gem farmer with Diamonds V, one utility runner with Magnet III plus Speed V, and the luck pet only if you're farming an event with rare drops. This is the loadout framework from the team compositions guide — the enchant plan and the team plan are the same plan. If a new event pet out-shines your sweeper, don't re-enchant anything until the new pet has earned 50 hours of team time.

**Phase 3 — Steady state.** Monthly audit: confirm all five deployed pets are enchanted, no duplicates, no benched pets holding Master enchants, no pet you plan to sell carrying a roll. Re-roll only per the 150K decision framework above. Spend Dark Matter only on pets whose enchants already double their output. Every time a new update drops, resist the urge to roll the new shiny pet — the machine takes the same Gems and gives the same odds regardless of how new the pet is.

The whole strategy in one sentence: the enchant is a multiplier, the pet is the base, and players who pick the base before they pay for the multiplier beat players who don't, every single week.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My DPS barely moved after I rolled Criticals X. Is the enchant broken?**

No — it landed on the wrong pet. Open the pet's stat page: if its base Damage is low relative to your team, that's the whole story. Criticals X multiplies the base damage the pet already had. The fix: (1) list your five deployed pets by base Damage, (2) hold the next Master-tier roll for the highest-base pet you deploy, (3) keep the wrongly-enchanted pet only if it earns its slot. The enchant is stuck on that pet — the fix is the next roll, not this one.

**Q: Can I move an enchantment from one pet to another?**

No. Enchants are permanently bound to the pet they were rolled on, and trading the pet transfers the enchant with it. Never enchant a pet you might trade or sell — you don't get the enchant back, and the buyer is paying for the pet, not your roll. One question before any 100K+ spend: is this pet in my team for the next month? If "maybe," spend the Gems elsewhere.

**Q: How many pets should carry Coins V?**

Three, maximum. Copies stack additively: 3 pets at Coins V give +75% coins. The 4th copy adds +25% to a base already at +75% — about a 14% relative improvement for the full cost of a Master-tier roll. Use that slot for Criticals X or Diamonds V instead.

**Q: Should I put Luck V on my strongest damage pet?**

No — it wastes both jobs. Luck does nothing for combat, and luck bonuses don't compound, so one dedicated utility pet with Luck V covers it. Keep damage enchants on damage pets. The pet carrying luck should be the one you'd lose the least if a better drop replaced it.

**Q: Should I enchant first or upgrade with Dark Matter first?**

Enchant first, then upgrade the pet you enchanted. Gems are abundant (50K-150K per day), Dark Matter is scarce (5-10 per week), and both are multipliers — so spend the abundant resource first, and save Dark Matter for the pet whose enchant already doubles its output. Upgrades multiply enchants; never spend Dark Matter on a pet you might replace.

**Q: Why doesn't a second copy of the same enchant on one pet do anything?**

A pet has one main enchant slot and one secondary slot, and duplicates on the same pet don't stack — two Criticals X behave exactly like one. This is how players burn 130K+ Gems on rolls that do nothing. Before every roll, check the pet's current enchants; if you already hold the target, stop and roll a different pet or tier.
