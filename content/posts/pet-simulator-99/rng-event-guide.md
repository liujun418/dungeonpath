You've been rolling for hours. Luck potion ticking, rainbow team out, the auto-roll bar crawling upward — and your inventory is fourteen Commons, one Uncommon, and a Golden Rare you'll vendor for 40 gems. Meanwhile the chat feed keeps flashing "[Player] hatched a Huge!" three times in the last hour, and none of them are you. You're starting to think Pet Simulator 99's RNG just hates you.

It doesn't. You're feeding the wrong machine.

The way Pet Simulator 99 hands out Huges and Titanics changed completely this month. Back in May, the Void RNG Event was a self-contained dice-and-weather lottery: stack a Lightning Storm with a Mega Lucky Dice II and pray into a 1-in-3-billion void. That event ended May 23, and a lot of players never updated their approach. As of Update 89 "Fiesta Forever" (August 15, 2026), the highest-efficiency RNG farm in the game isn't an egg, a dice roll, or a potion stack. It's **Cursed Mode** — a maze boss modifier you unlock at Tier 90,000 that multiplies Huge and Titanic odds by **5x** and hands you **five boss drops** per kill.

This guide is about the RNG math underneath all of that: why the 5x only matters if you stack it right, where players throw the multiplier away, and the routing that turns the same hour of farming into Huge drops instead of a pile of Commons. For the full breakdown of the new maze and its pets, read the [Fiesta Forever Update 89 Guide](/posts/pet-simulator-99/fiesta-forever-guide/) — this page is the odds-and-stacking layer on top of it.

## Why Your Egg Hatching Stopped Paying Off

The core idea of RNG in Pet Simulator 99 is simple: every egg, boss, and maze chest rolls against a base odds table, and your job is to push that table as far as you can before the roll happens. The part most players never learn is that luck sources come in two flavors — additive and multiplicative — and they hit in a specific order.

Potions, luck enchants, and the Lucky gamepass add flat percentages on top of your base. Rainbow pets, lucky blocks, and event multipliers like Cursed Mode then multiply your entire pool. That order is why a player with one potion, one rainbow pet, and an active 5x event will out-farm someone chugging five potions with no multiplier. I go deep on the math in the [luck boosting guide](/posts/pet-simulator-99/luck-boosting-guide/), but the takeaway for this page is: **a multiplier is only as good as the pool it's multiplying.**

That's exactly what changed with Fiesta Forever. The Void RNG Event gave you small, fiddly multipliers — dice gave a flat luck percent, weather gave a 3x for two minutes, and you had to line all three up by hand. Fiesta Forever replaced that with one big, clean multiplier on the maze: Cursed Mode's 5x Huge and Titanic chance. One toggle, five times the odds, no weather timing. The tradeoff is that the 5x lives behind a Tier 90,000 unlock and only fires on cursed bosses, which is why so many players are still standing at the egg vendor while a smaller group farms the maze for five times the value.

## Cursed Mode Is the New Fastest Farm

Here's the scene. You've cleared the four zones of a maze run and you're standing in front of the boss door. On a standard run you'd walk in, kill it, take your one drop, and leave. On a Cursed Mode run — which you toggled at the entrance because you hit Tier 90,000 — the same boss comes out harder, and when it dies you get three things instead of one:

| Cursed Boss Reward | Value | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 3x XP | Every kill | Powers the Fiesta Race track faster |
| 5x Huge & Titanic chance | Every kill | Multiplies your whole luck pool, not a flat add |
| 5 boss drops | Every kill | Five loot rolls instead of one per boss |

Five drops is the sleeper stat here. Everyone fixates on the "5x Huge chance" headline, but the five boss drops are what make Cursed Mode a consistent farm. A standard boss gives you one roll at the loot table. A cursed boss gives you five rolls, each of which also benefits from the 5x odds. You're not just more likely to get something good — you're rolling five times as often to get it. That compounds into real Huge drops from the new Fiesta Forever pool: the Huge Desert Coyote, Huge Mariachi Coati, Huge Marigold Manatee, and Huge Marigold Donkey.

To unlock it, you grind standard maze clears until your tier hits 90,000. There's no shortcut and nothing to buy that skips it, so don't ask in chat how to pay for the unlock — the answer is depth. The deeper you push, the faster your tier climbs, so every standard run you do before the unlock is paying down a debt toward the 5x.

### The Counterintuitive Part: Chase the Huges, Not the Titanic

This is the mental trap that ruins Cursed Mode for most players. They unlock it, see "5x Titanic chance," and immediately decide the goal is a Titanic. It's not.

Titanic base odds are so far out — in the billions-to-one range — that multiplying them by five still leaves you in lottery territory. Five times almost-nothing is still almost-nothing. The Huges are a different story. Their base odds are orders of magnitude better, so a 5x multiplier pushes them into a range where consistent farming actually produces drops. One Huge Marigold Donkey is worth real gems on the trading plaza, and Cursed Mode can produce several over a week of focused runs. A Titanic under Cursed Mode is still a once-in-a-thousand-hours fluke.

So the counterintuitive move is to treat Cursed Mode as a **Huge farm with a lottery ticket attached**, not a Titanic farm. Aim your time at the Huges, and let the Titanic be the jackpot you occasionally trip over.

## The Failure Replay: Stacking the 5x on Nothing

Let me show you the exact way players throw Cursed Mode away, because I've watched it happen in chat every day since the update dropped.

You spend a day grinding your maze tier up to 90,000. You finally unlock Cursed Mode, toggle it on, and run it completely bare — no luck potion, no enchants, no rainbow pets, no Boosts Machine. Your logic is: "The game is giving me 5x odds. That IS the boost." Three cursed bosses later you have a stack of Common drops and a growing sense that the 5x is a lie.

It's not a lie. You multiplied nothing. The 5x is a multiplier on your total luck pool, and if the pool is empty, 5 times zero is still zero. The game hands you the multiplier but assumes you'll bring the base for it to multiply.

The recovery is a stacking order, not a bigger potion. Before you ever toggle Cursed Mode on, do this in sequence: pop your highest luck potion (one is enough — the second barely helps), equip your luck enchants, and field your rainbow pets. Those add to your base pool. Then Cursed Mode's 5x multiplies that larger number. Then, if you have lucky blocks, they multiply on top again. Same Cursed Mode run, same 5x, completely different outcome because the additive layers went in first. This is the same rule from the luck boosting guide applied to the maze: the order matters more than the total.

## The Second Mistake: Burning Your Six-Hour Boosts on the Wrong Window

The Boosts Machine is Fiesta Forever's buff dispenser, and it caps at **six hours**. That cap is the whole game, and most players ignore it.

Here's the mistake. You log in, top the Boosts Machine to max the second you arrive, and then do what everyone does — check the trading plaza, run a standard maze, chat with your clan, hatch a few eggs, grab a drink. Your six hours of boosts are ticking down the entire time, and by the time you finally queue a Cursed Mode run, you've got forty minutes of buff left and a pile of buff-hours already wasted on bank-standing.

Why it hurts: the Boosts Machine's value only exists while it overlaps a Cursed Mode run or a 10x XP race window. A buff on top of nothing is nothing. Six hours spent hatching Commons is six hours you can't get back, and the cap means you can't just idle it out and re-top — you burned the day's window.

The recovery is sequencing. Decide on your Cursed Mode block first, then top the Boosts Machine to max right before you enter the maze, not when you log in. Keep your six hours buffered so they all land on cursed bosses. If you're going to run a single focused session, run it hot — boosts on, potions on, Cursed Mode on, back-to-back clears until the buff drops. Then do your dailies after it expires, when your buff is gone and it doesn't matter.

## The Decision Framework: Hard-Grind Cursed Mode or Wait for the Next Event

Every event, players ask the same two-sided question: do I commit everything to Cursed Mode this week, or bank my resources and wait for the next update? Here's the framework that answers it, because the wrong call costs you the event.

**If you're already past Maze Tier 90,000 and holding a boost stockpile — hard-grind now.** The 5x Huge chance on the current new Huges is a known-good rate, and those pets (the Desert Coyote and the three Marigold/Mariachi Huges) are event-locked. When Fiesta Forever ends, they stop dropping, and their plaza value climbs. This is the classic "farm the thing that goes away" window, and there is no next event that guarantees you a 5x Huge multiplier on demand.

**If you're under Tier 90,000 — decide if you can actually reach the gate before the event ends.** The unlock is a real grind. If you're at tier 20,000 with two days left, chasing Cursed Mode is a losing bet — you'll spend the whole event climbing to a gate you never reach, and miss the Fiesta Race 10x XP window that would have leveled you for free. In that case, farm standard mazes and the race track instead, and treat the Cursed Mode unlock as next event's project.

**If you're close to a Huge pity threshold — push now.** Pity mechanics and the 5x multiplier interact, and finishing the pity during a live 5x window is the cheapest Huge you'll get all month.

The "wait for the next event" instinct is almost always wrong during a live 5x window. The multiplier is temporary and event-locked — you can't bank it. Waiting means you keep your resources and lose the one thing that makes them worth spending.

## Fiesta Race Round 2: The 10x XP Window You're Ignoring

Fiesta Race Round 2 is the second track in the update, and the headline prize is a **Gargantuan Sombrero Cat at level 400,000**. Most players read that, glance at their level, and give up before they start. That's the wrong read.

The part that matters is the catch-up mechanic: anyone under **level 150,000** gets **10x XP** on the track. Ten times. And that 10x stacks with Cursed Mode's 3x XP, which means a low-level player farming cursed bosses during the catch-up window is pulling **30x XP** on every kill.

Here's the counterintuitive move: don't sprint for 400,000. Slow down and farm XP under the 150,000 threshold. The 10x catch-up is the real value for the vast majority of players, because it turns the race from an unreachable goal into a passive reward stream. Racing to 400,000 from a low level is how you burn a month of playtime for a prize you'll never see. Farming cursed bosses at 30x XP under 150,000 is how you climb ten levels for the cost of one and collect the track rewards the whole way up. The Gargantuan Sombrero Cat is for the players who were already past the threshold when the update launched — everyone else should milk the multiplier.

## Eternal Maze: Your Three Free Rolls a Day

The Eternal Maze is the new endless mode, and it gives you **three free entries per day**. After those three, going again costs you. The maze runs on a 5:00 timer across four zones plus a boss, and your **deepest run** is your leaderboard rank.

The RNG-relevant detail that most players miss: depth does not change your drop rates. A deeper record only moves your name up the leaderboard. Your actual pet drops come from the bosses you kill, and the only thing that changes their value is whether you run them on Cursed Mode.

So your three free runs have an obvious allocation: spend them on Cursed Mode, once you can reach it. A free cursed boss kill is a free roll at 5x Huge odds and five drops. Chasing depth with your free runs — pushing deeper on standard mode just to see your name climb — is spending your best free resources on a number that doesn't grow your account. The only exception is if you're already top 100 and need a deep push to defend your spot; everyone else should treat depth as a side effect, not a goal.

## The Boosts Machine, Tier III Boosters, and Fiesta Boss Keys

The last pieces of the RNG stack are the ones players bolt on at random. Here's how they actually chain together.

The **Boosts Machine** gives you up to six hours of buffs, and we covered the timing mistake above. The **Tier III maze booster** is the upgraded amplifier you slot into the maze for a bigger multiplier on your runs. The **Fiesta Boss Key** opens a secret room with a **5x boss** — a boss with five times the normal drop value.

The peak window is when all three overlap with Cursed Mode. A cursed boss (5x Huge/Titanic chance, 5 drops) inside a Fiesta Boss Key secret room (5x boss value) with a Tier III booster active and your six-hour buffs running is the single highest-RNG moment available in Pet Simulator 99 right now. Nothing else comes close — not the old Void RNG weather stack, not egg hatching, not potion chugging.

The mistake is firing these one at a time as you get them. Save your Fiesta Boss Keys. Don't pop a key on a standard boss when a cursed boss is one run away, because the key multiplies whatever boss it lands on — and 5x on a cursed boss is worth five times a 5x on a standard one. Same rule as everything else on this page: multipliers multiply each other, so stack them into one hot window instead of spreading them thin across a lazy afternoon.

## A Daily RNG Routine That Actually Holds Together

Put it all together and a good day looks like this. Log in, run your three free Eternal Maze entries on Cursed Mode (or standard, if you're still climbing to Tier 90,000). Before the first run, top the Boosts Machine, pop your one luck potion, and get your rainbow team out so the 5x has a real pool to multiply. If you have a Fiesta Boss Key, spend it on a cursed boss inside the secret room, not on a standard clear. Grind cursed bosses while the buff is live, then do your dailies after it expires. If you're under 150,000 on the race track, let the 10x catch-up do its work instead of sprinting for a rank you won't hit.

Do that for a week and the difference is night and day versus the player standing at the egg vendor. The RNG didn't change — the farm did. Cursed Mode is the highest-efficiency RNG source the game has ever shipped, and the only thing separating you from it is a Tier 90,000 unlock and the discipline to stack your multipliers before you roll.
