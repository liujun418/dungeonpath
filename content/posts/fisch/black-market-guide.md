## You Saved for Three Weeks, and the Sword Was Gone by Noon

Here's a scene that's been playing out every Sunday since the Black Market started rotating real stock.

You decided, about three weeks ago, that you wanted the Sword of Light. You don't fully know what it does. What you know is the price tag — C$117,500,000 — and that a few players in your server already have it. So you stopped buying bait upgrades. You skipped a rod you were "about to" buy. You funneled every catch into the bank, and by Saturday night you're sitting on the full amount, or close enough that one more session finishes it.

Sunday morning you log in early, head down to the Shady Bazaar, and the Sword of Light is already sold out. The stall shows an empty slot where it was. Someone on the other side of the world bought the last one while you were asleep, because their Sunday started hours before yours did.

That's the Black Market in one scene: a shop where the best stuff is limited, timed, and gone fast, and where showing up "on Sunday" isn't good enough — you have to show up at the right hour of Sunday. This guide is about which hour that is, what's actually in the stall this week, and — more importantly — whether the stuff in there is even worth your C$ in the first place. Most of it isn't, and that's the part nobody tells you.

## What the Black Market Actually Is (and Where It Hides)

The Black Market isn't a separate building you sail to. It's a shop tucked inside the Shady Bazaar, a hidden market area that was added to Fisch in the May 23, 2026 update, beneath Moosewood. If you've never found it, that's by design — it's the "shady" part of the name. You don't walk into it from the pier the way you walk into a normal shop.

What matters for your wallet is the distinction between the two layers:

- **The Shady Bazaar** is the area itself, and it runs partly on its own currency, Shady Scrip (S$). You earn Scrip and spend it on unlocking and progressing through the bazaar.
- **The Black Market** is the stall inside that area that rotates limited items, and those items are priced in regular C$ — the same C$ you earn from selling fish.

That split is the first thing people get wrong. They grind Scrip, open the bazaar, see the Sword of Light's C$117.5M tag, and think they've been farming the wrong currency. You haven't. The bazaar unlock is Scrip; the stall's Sunday items are C$. Two separate wallets, two separate grinds.

The second thing people get wrong is thinking the Black Market is always open. It isn't. Its stock is a weekend-and-rotation affair, and the reset clock is the entire reason this guide exists.

## The Sunday Restock, Explained in Two Waves

The Black Market doesn't restock on an in-game day/night cycle. It restocks on real-world Sundays. That's the single most important fact here, because it means the window is tied to your local clock, not to how many game days you've slept through.

The way it actually plays out is two waves, and both matter:

1. **First wave** — opens roughly an hour after the Sunday update goes live. This is the wave the devs drop for their own prime time, and it's the one that sells out the headline items first.
2. **Second wave** — opens roughly half a day later, so players in other time zones get a crack at the same stock instead of always losing to whoever wakes up first.

The exact hours aren't published anywhere in-game, so treat the "one hour after update" and "half a day later" framing as a pattern, not a printed schedule. That's an unconfirmed mechanic — the game never hands you a countdown, and the timing has shifted between rotations. The reliable version is simpler: check Sunday morning, and check again Sunday night. One of those two passes usually catches a wave.

Why this matters is the failure baked into the very first scene. If you only check once, and you check at the wrong moment, you'll assume the item "was never restocked" or "sold out in seconds." What actually happened is you looked between the two waves, or after the second one ended, and you missed it through no fault of the stock itself.

## Failure: You Watched the Wrong Timezone

The most common Black Market loss isn't "not having enough C$." It's "having enough and still missing the drop." Here's the error, cause, and recovery laid out flat, because this one repeats every single week.

**The error:** You saved up, you logged on "Sunday," and the item you wanted was gone.

**The cause:** You treated "Sunday" as a single 24-hour slot, when the restock actually lands in two separate waves with a half-day gap between them. If your Sunday morning is someone else's Sunday night, you checked while the item was already sold out from the first wave, or before the second wave ever opened. You didn't do anything wrong with your money — you did the timing wrong, and the market doesn't care which of the two it was.

**The recovery:** Set two alarms, not one. One for your local Sunday morning, one for your local Sunday evening. Check the stall on both. If you can only make one, make the earlier one — the first wave is where the headline items like the Sword of Light actually go. And if you're reading this on a weekday, don't bother camping the stall at all; the stock sits stale between Sundays, and staring at an empty slot doesn't bring the reset any faster.

There's a second, sneakier version of the same failure, and it's worth naming because it doesn't look like a timing error at all. You see the Sword of Light, you can't afford it this week, and you tell yourself "it'll be back next Sunday." Maybe. Rotations don't promise repeat stock. Items that sell out can absolutely skip the next week or disappear entirely. If you're treating the Black Market like a store with a fixed catalog, you're going to get burned by a rotation that quietly changes the shelves while you weren't looking.

## What's Actually Worth Buying: The Decision Framework

Stop asking "what's in the Black Market?" and start asking "what should I do with my current C$ balance?" Those are different questions, and the second one is the only one that matters. Here's the if-then table.

| If your situation is... | Then do this |
|---|---|
| Under C$5M, still building rods and bait | Skip the Black Market entirely. Your C$ goes to rods and income first. Every coin spent on a Chair or a boat is a coin not going toward a Titanium Rod. |
| C$5M–13M, mid-game, gear mostly sorted | The only item even worth a glance is the Black boat at C$5M, and only if faster travel actually speeds up your farming routes. If it's just a cosmetic skin, skip it. |
| C$13M+, comfortable but not rich | Be suspicious of the Money Bag. Its C$13,333,333 tag is big, but what it actually does is unconfirmed. Don't drop a fortune on an item with no verified effect. |
| C$100M+, endgame, income fully set up | Now, and only now, is the Sword of Light a reasonable buy — and only as a collectible, not as a money tool. |

The through-line: buy income first, collectibles last. That's the same rule our [money farming guide](/posts/fisch/money-farming-guide/) pushes, and the Black Market is where players most often break it, because the big price tags make the items look like investments when they're almost all status symbols.

Now the question everyone actually asks: **should I save for the Sword of Light?** Here's the honest math. C$117,500,000 is not a number you accumulate by accident. It's weeks of disciplined farming, and while you're saving it, you're passing up every other upgrade that number could buy. A Titanium Rod or a Cusk Purger pays you back in faster C$ per hour for the rest of the game. The Sword of Light, as far as anyone can confirm, does none of that — it's a rare, expensive flex that looks incredible in your inventory and doesn't speed up your next catch. So the answer is a clean if-then: if your money rods are done and you want the trophy, save for it. If they aren't, you're not "saving for the Sword of Light," you're just delaying the rods that would've made the Sword of Light easier to afford later.

## The Counterintuitive Truth About the Black Market

This is counterintuitive, so read it twice.

The Black Market looks like it should be where Fisch sells its best money-making items. It's hidden, it's gated, its items cost more than almost anything else in the game, and it only opens on a schedule. Every one of those signals screams "high-end gear." But the items that actually make you money in Fisch — the rods, the bait economy, the farming upgrades — are not sold here. The stuff in the Black Market is overwhelmingly collectibles and cosmetics. The expensive items are the *least* useful for income, not the most.

The reason this trips people up is that we're trained to read price as power. A C$117.5M item *feels* like it should be the strongest thing in the game, so players skip a Titanium Rod to save for it, then realize the Sword of Light doesn't farm any faster than the rod they passed up. The Black Market's genius is that it sells rarity, not utility, and rarity has no impact on your C$ per hour.

So the counterintuitive move isn't "save up and buy the biggest item." It's the opposite: treat the Black Market as a place you visit *after* you're rich, to spend money that no longer matters on things that look cool. The genuinely useful purchases in Fisch all live in the normal shops, at lower prices, with actual stat sheets. If you wouldn't buy a C$10,000 Bunch of Balloons with your current balance, the only difference between it and the Sword of Light is how long you'd have to save.
