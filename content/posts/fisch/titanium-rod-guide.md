You're standing on the seafloor of The Deep, watching another player hook something enormous. Their rod bends. The progress bar crawls forward, then a chunk of it jumps on its own — auto-slash firing. They reel in a fish the size of a small truck, and the sell price pops at nine times its base value. You check their loadout. Titanium Rod.

The Titanium Rod is one of four craftable rods added in Fisch's The Deep update (v1.96.0, dropped August 8, 2026). It is not the most expensive rod in the game — that's the Cusk Purger at 750,000 C$. It is not free like the Luminous Rod, either. It sits right in the middle: 200,000 C$, a Level 250 requirement, and two separate quest chains feeding it its two named parts.

What you get back is a rod with 100% Lure, 180% Luck, infinite max weight, and a 20% Titanium mutation passive that multiplies fish value by nine. For the money you spend, it is the single best general-purpose fishing rod in The Deep update.

This guide covers the whole path in the order you will actually hit it, including the mistakes that burn hours. For the update itself — how to reach The Deep and what else it added — start with our [The Deep update guide](/posts/fisch/the-deep-update-guide/).

## What the Titanium Rod Actually Does

Before you commit 200k C$ and two quest chains, know exactly what you're buying.

### Full Stats

| Stat | Value | What It Actually Means |
|------|-------|------------------------|
| Lure | 100% | Max bite speed. You are not waiting between casts. |
| Luck | 180% | High-tier rare fish rolls, near endgame level. |
| Control | 0.3 | A wide, forgiving catch bar. The minigame is easy. |
| Resilience | 75% | Fish rarely escape. Boss-class fish still fight. |
| Max Weight | Infinite | No weight cap. Nothing is too big to land. |
| Durability | 200 | Long before it needs repair. |
| Line Distance | 120m | Reaches far into The Deep's trench zones. |

Those numbers are strong, but the stats alone don't tell you why this rod prints money. The three passives do.

### Titanium Mutation: 20% Chance, 9x Value

This is the money engine. Every catch has a 20% chance to roll the **Titanium mutation**, which multiplies the fish's sell value by nine. A 12,000 C$ fish becomes 108,000 C$. A 40,000 C$ trench fish becomes 360,000 C$.

That nine-times multiplier is the same one the Cusk Purger's Monstrous mutation uses — except you get it at a 20% proc rate on a rod that costs 550,000 C$ less. For farming cash, this passive is the entire point of the rod.

### Auto-Slash: 35% Chance Every 0.5 Seconds

Auto-slash is easy to misread as a crutch for players who can't do the minigame. It is not. It is a throughput stat.

Every half-second, there's a 35% chance the rod auto-triggers a slash for you — adding 5% Progress and applying a 0.15-second stun to the fish. That means your progress bar fills in bursts even while you're holding steady, and the brief stun gives you a beat to reposition after a bad flick.

**The scene:** You hook a heavy Lower Deep fish that would normally fight the bar for a full minute. With auto-slash firing every half-second, a chunk of progress lands before you even react. The stun buys you a breath. You land it, it rolls Titanium, and the sell screen shows 9x. That loop is what this rod is built to run.

### +30% Progress Speed

The catch bar fills 30% faster. Stack that with 100% Lure and you're finishing casts noticeably quicker than rods in the same tier. Faster casts mean more rolls, and more rolls mean more Titanium mutation procs.

## Level 250 and the Ancient Archives: The Gate You Can't Skip

Here's the first mistake people make, and it's the most expensive one.

**The scene:** You see a clip of the Titanium Rod, decide you want it today, and swim straight for the Ancient Archives in Lower Deep. You have 200k C$ sitting in the bank. You arrive at the crafting station... and the prompt won't appear. You're Level 187.

The Titanium Rod has a hard Level 250 requirement that sits above every material grind. There is no bypass, no "craft it anyway" trick, and no handing materials to a friend. The Ancient Archives simply will not show you the Titanium Rod recipe until you're 250. The game checks it server-side.

This is the single most common wasted trip of the update. People burn an hour getting to the Archives, stand at the station, and get nothing.

**Why the order matters:** You technically *can* start Sledge's quest chain and Forge Hand Marcus's chain before Level 250. But you'll be grinding The Deep's zones with an under-leveled rod, which slows everything down. The efficient move is to hit 250 first, using a free rod to get there, then commit to Titanium materials.

**Recovery:** If you're below 250, don't farm Titanium parts first. Do Dr. Vane's free Luminous Rod quest in Outer Deep, use it to grind XP and cash to 250, then pivot. Getting the order wrong — materials before level — is how people end up with a bank full of Titanium parts and no way to use them.

## Sledge's 5-Quest Chain: Getting the Titanium Shaft

Here's the counterintuitive part of this whole guide: Level 250 is not the hard gate. Sledge's quest chain is.

Most players assume the Titanium Shaft is a drop or a vendor purchase. It is neither. It comes exclusively from Sledge's five-quest chain inside The Deep, and those quests are not fast. This is the real time sink of the rod, and it catches people off guard because it looks like "just some NPC quests."

**The scene:** You hit Level 250, you have the 200k C$, you read that you need a "Titanium Shaft," and you assume it's a 50k vendor item or a rare drop from a trench fish. You spend an hour fishing Lower Deep, waiting for it to drop. It never drops. The shaft is quest-gated, not RNG.

**Why the chain is the wall:** Sledge is the same NPC who runs the shop in The Deep — you'll be selling fish to Sledge's shop to fund the rod anyway. His quest chain is five steps, and the Titanium Shaft is the final reward. That's actually good news in one way: you can't get "unlucky" and never see the shaft. But there's no shortcut, either. Five quests is five quests.

**The efficient order:** Start Sledge's chain the moment you enter The Deep, before you think about the 200k. The quests overlap with the money grind — you're fishing The Deep for C$ regardless, so the two happen at the same time. Treating them as two separate tasks doubles your total time for no reason.

**Recovery for the wrong order:** If you've been fishing Lower Deep for an hour "farming the shaft," stop. Find Sledge, open his dialogue, and start quest one. The shaft is the reward for finishing, not a thing that drops in the water.

## Forge Hand Marcus, the Titanium Reel, and Refined Scrap

The Titanium Reel comes from Forge Hand Marcus's quest chain. The name tells you what he's about — he's the forge NPC in The Deep, and his chain rewards the reel.

**The scene:** You've got the shaft. You've got the cash. You're missing the reel and the scrap. You check your inventory — no reel, and you've never heard of "Refined Scrap." Now you're reading patch notes in the middle of a trench at 2am.

The reel is Marcus's quest reward — same deal as the shaft. Quest-gated, no drop. That part is familiar by now. The Refined Scrap is a different problem entirely, and it's the material everyone overlooks.

**Refined Scrap from Crushed Diving Helmets:** You need 5 Refined Scrap. The source is Crushed Diving Helmets — diving helmets that get crushed down into scrap and then refined. The shaft and reel have big named NPC quests attached, so they stay on your radar. "5 Refined Scrap" reads like a tiny ingredient, so it slides right off. It's not tiny. It's a separate farm.

**The classic fail:** You finish both quest chains, feel the hard part is over, and then discover you're 3 Refined Scrap short. You've never farmed a single helmet. Now you're grinding a material you didn't know existed while the rod's other three ingredients sit in your bank doing nothing.

The lesson is simple: bank the scrap *before* you think you're done. The recipe requires all four things — 200,000 C$, the shaft, the reel, and 5 Refined Scrap — at once. You can't craft until every piece is sitting there. Missing one scrap means you own zero of a rod.

## Titanium Rod vs Luminous Rod vs Cusk Purger: What to Build First

Here's the decision that actually trips people up. You're in The Deep with limited time and cash. Three rods are on the table. Which one do you commit to first?

| Rod | Cost | Luck | Standout Passive | Best For |
|-----|------|------|------------------|----------|
| Luminous Rod | Free (quest) | 95% | Luminous mutation (9x) | First pick. Funds everything else. |
| Titanium Rod | 200k C$ + mats | 180% | Titanium mutation (20%, 9x), auto-slash | Money farming, general use. |
| Cusk Purger | 750k C$ + mats | 225% | +7 Disturbance, Monstrous mutation | Hunt fish endgame only. |

**The order is not a contest. It's a ladder.** Build the Luminous Rod first — it's free, and its 9x Luminous mutation is the fastest way to generate the cash for everything else. Then build the Titanium Rod, because it's the best money-farming tool before the Cusk Purger exists. Then, and only then, decide whether you actually need the Cusk Purger.

The Cusk Purger is a specialist. Its whole job is hunt fish — that +7 Disturbance spawns aggressive, high-value fish on nearly every cast, and its Monstrous mutation rolls the same 9x multiplier. But it costs 750k and it's overkill for general fishing. If you're not running the Monstrous Cusk hunt endgame, you don't need it. I go deeper on that in the [Cusk Purger rod guide](/posts/fisch/cusk-purger-rod-guide/).

The Titanium Rod, meanwhile, has **zero Disturbance** and cannot spawn hunt fish. If hunt farming is your entire goal, skip straight to saving for the Cusk Purger. If you just want to fish, get rich, and fund whatever comes next, the Titanium Rod is the stop you should make.

## Is the Titanium Rod Worth 200,000 C$?

Short answer: for money farming, yes. For casual fishing, probably not.

Here's the counterintuitive read that most guides miss. The Titanium Rod's headline stat is **Max Weight: Infinite**, and players fixate on it because "infinite" is flashy. But for most players, infinite weight is nearly useless. A Level 250 player with 75% Resilience and 0.3 Control is already landing almost everything the game throws at them. The infinite cap only matters for a handful of boss-class catches that you'll rarely, if ever, hook. You're paying for a stat you'll almost never test.

The real value is the **20% Titanium mutation at 9x**. That's the part that pays for the rod. With 100% Lure and +30% Progress Speed, you're completing casts fast, and one in five of those catches is worth nine times its base value. Run that math over an hour and the 200k C$ crafts itself back quickly.

There's a second counterintuitive point. The 200k price tag looks like the gate, but it's the fastest line item on the list. The Luminous Rod's 9x mutation generates cash quickly, and if you farm C$ alongside Sledge's and Marcus's quests, the money shows up before the quests finish. The quest chains are the slow part. Save your stress for those, not for the cash.

**When to skip it:** If you fish casually — log in, catch a few, log off — keep the free Luminous Rod and save the 200k. The Titanium Rod is an investment that pays off through volume, and volume only happens if you're putting in hours. For the full picture on turning those hours into C$, see our [Fisch money farming guide](/posts/fisch/money-farming-guide/).

**When to build it:** If your next goal is the Cusk Purger, the Titanium Rod is the bridge. It's the best farming tool in the game until the Cusk Purger exists, and it clears the 750k for the Purger faster than anything else short of endgame. Build the free Luminous Rod, build the Titanium Rod, then farm the Purger with it.

The Titanium Rod is not the flashiest rod in The Deep update. It's just the most useful one for the price. Infinite weight is the cover story; the 20% Titanium mutation is the actual reason to spend.
