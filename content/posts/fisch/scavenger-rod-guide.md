You dock at the oil rig off Rosslit Bay, climb the metal stairs to the top floor, and turn right into the Laboratory. Dr. Crookspine is hunched over a bench, and instead of asking for something rare or expensive, he wants junk. Two Silver Rusty Bolts, five Scrap Metal, one Lunar String. Bring him the trash and he hands over the Scavenger Rod.

It sounds too easy, and for once it is — with one asterisk. Two of the three items are not items, they are **items with a specific mutation attached**, and every player who gets stuck on this quest gets stuck on exactly that detail. They fish bolts for an hour and wonder why the counter never moves.

This guide covers the whole line: where Crookspine stands, the exact zone, rod, bait and mutation method for each item, how the finished rod performs, and the preparation order that takes the quest from "maybe tonight" to under an hour.

If you are still on starter or early rods, this is the most accessible power jump in the Astral update — no boss, no rare-event fish, no cross-day waiting.

## Why This Quest Eats Evenings (And Why It Doesn't Have To)

Three traps sit between players and this rod, and every guide I have seen walks into at least two of them.

**They list items instead of conditions.** "Catch two Rusty Bolts" is half a sentence. The full sentence is "catch two Rusty Bolts *carrying the Silver mutation*." A plain bolt doesn't move the counter and doesn't tell you why. Same for the Lunar String — the string is common, the *Lunar* string is the quest.

**They recommend luck bait for junk items.** Rusty Bolts and Scrap Metal are deliberately at the bottom of the rarity pool. Stacking luck bait pulls rarer fish into your casts and physically crowds the junk out. The correct bait is the one that sounds wrong: Garbage Bait.

**They treat mutation forcing as optional.** Fishing raw and hoping Silver eventually rolls is a percentage of a percentage — a rare base condition inside a random mutation roll. Mutation Totems and Mutated Sharky bait exist to delete the second roll. Players who skip them do the same quest five times longer.

The rest of this guide is built around avoiding those three traps in order.

## How We Evaluate a Rod Quest

The framework I use for every Fisch rod, applied to this one so you can see exactly where the time goes:

**1. Event dependency.** None. Unlike the [Meteoric Rod](/posts/fisch/meteoric-rod-guide/), nothing here waits on an Eclipse, Starfall or meteor shower. This is the single biggest reason the Scavenger Rod is the beginner-friendly Astral pickup.

**2. Prerequisite depth.** Moderate: Titanium Rod, Garbage Bait, and at least one mutation-forcing tool (Mutated Sharky or Mutation Totems). Moonlight Rod is a luxury shortcut, not a requirement.

**3. RNG exposure.** Two layered rolls on the bolts (base item plus mutation), one on the string, zero on Scrap Metal. Forcing mutations converts all of it into deterministic effort.

**4. Time gating versus effort gating.** Pure effort gating. Nothing grows in an aquarium overnight and nothing waits on a shop rotation. What you put in is what you get.

**5. Payoff.** 100% Luck plus a 3x Rare-Finds passive is a genuine mid-game economy engine, not a sidegrade.

**6. Failure recovery.** Every cast is cheap, nothing consumed except bait, and a wrong-zone session costs minutes rather than an event window. The quest is forgiving by design.

## Where to Find Dr. Crookspine

Sail to the oil rig off the coast of **Rosslit Bay**, dock, and climb to the top of the main building. Go through the entrance and turn right — that room is the Laboratory, and Crookspine works in there.

The quest marker points at the rig, so navigation is not the hard part. Two notes that actually matter: you can **hand each part in separately** as you finish it, which means you never risk selling a quest item after a long session — bank each set the moment it is complete. And if the lab is empty, you are on an old server; rejoin one running the Astral Observatory update.

## The Three-Part Quest, Step by Step

The items are not rare. The requirements are specific. Fish them in the order below, which puts the deterministic chore first and the double-roll grind last.

### Part 1 (do first): 5 Scrap Metal

Fish **The Depths during daytime**. Any rod, **Garbage Bait**, no mutation requirement. Scrap Metal is bottom-rarity and bites constantly — call it two to five minutes of actual fishing.

Why do this first? Two reasons. It clears the one step with zero thinking while you are still near the rig, and it warms up the rhythm of junk fishing — deliberately catching the worst things in the pool — that the bolt step needs.

One detail people miss: **daytime**. The Depths fish table shifts at night, and night casts spend more of your time on things you don't need. If you arrive in the dark, do the Lunar String first and come back at dawn.

### Part 2: 1 Lunar String

Fish the **Desolate Pocket** inside Desolate Deep. No bait preference, no weather preference, no time-of-day rule. The only requirement is the **Lunar mutation**.

This is where the Moonlight Rod does its best trick in the whole game: it applies Lunar to **every catch**, which turns this part into one cast. Catch the first string, confirm the Lunar tag, hand it in.

Without the Moonlight Rod, equip **Mutated Sharky** or force the mutation with a **Mutation Totem** at the appraiser, then fish until a String rolls out. Realistically five to ten minutes. Don't fish this raw — String has siblings in that pool and untagged catches don't count.

### Part 3 (do last): 2 Silver Rusty Bolts

The long pole. Fish the **Mine Shaft** with the **Titanium Rod** and **Garbage Bait** until two bolts roll out carrying the **Silver mutation**.

Plain Rusty Bolts are common. Silver Rusty Bolts are two rolls stacked — the bolt itself, then Silver on top — so raw fishing is a slow, appraise-and-pray loop. Force Silver before you start: **Mutated Sharky** bait or a **Mutation Totem**, refresh as the effect expires, and every bolt that bites is the right bolt. Budget ten to twenty minutes and roughly two totem cycles.

If you own the **Toxin Burst** rod, its multi-catch property meaningfully shortens this step — more bites per cast means more bolt rolls per minute. It is optional; it is also the single biggest time-saver in the quest.

When both bolts are tagged and banked, return to Crookspine, hand in the final set, and the rod is yours.

## Scavenger Rod Stats and What the Passive Actually Does

| Stat | Value | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lure Speed | 75% | Casts resolve fast; more rolls per fishing hour |
| Luck | 100% | Mid-upper rarity pool on a free rod |
| Resilience | 20% | The weak spot — big fish fight harder |
| Max Kg | 75,000 | Handles mid-game catches, not endgame leviathans |
| Scavenged mutation | 40% chance, 3x | Nearly half your catches can triple in value |
| Rare Finds passive | 3x roll chance | Rare variants appear three times as often |

The two passives stack into one economic idea: **this rod manufactures surprise payouts**. A normal rod sells fish at face value. The Scavenger rolls the Rare-Finds table three times as often and then tags nearly half of what it catches with a 3x sell multiplier. Fish it in a zone whose rares you actually want and the average value per hour beats rods with prettier stat sheets.

The honest weakness is Resilience at 20% and the 75,000 Kg ceiling. This is not your boss fish rod, and it is not the rod you take to the deepest bestiary entries. It is a **money engine for the middle of the game**, and judging it as anything else misses the point. Once you graduate to endgame targets, it stays in your bag as the rod you equip when the goal is cash rather than a specific fish.

## Where to Fish It Once You Own It

A money rod only earns if you point it at the right water, and the Scavenger's passives tell you exactly what that water looks like.

**Pick a zone you can fish without thinking.** The 3x Rare-Finds roll pays out over volume, and volume comes from casts per hour rather than value per cast. A zone where you lose fish, reposition constantly, or get bullied by the Kg cap produces fewer rolls than a calmer zone you can hold for a full session. Comfort beats rarity here.

**Match the zone to the 75,000 Kg ceiling.** This is the constraint players ignore on the celebratory first trip. If the table's big species snap the line, every lost fish is a Rare-Finds roll that never happened. Mid-weight tables — the kind of zones you fished on the way up — are the sweet spot, and most players already know two or three by heart.

**Stay for the Scavenged tags.** The 40% 3x mutation is a session-average effect, not a per-cast promise. Twenty minutes is too small a sample to judge it; an evening is not. Players who test the rod for five catches and call it weak are measuring noise. Keep the [rod and bait combination guide](/posts/fisch/rod-bait-combination-guide/) open while you choose the bait for the chosen table, because the rod multiplies a good setup — it doesn't rescue a bad one.

**Don't use it as your only rod.** The professional way to carry it is as the second slot: Scavenger for the hour when the goal is C$, switching to a resilience rod when a specific bestiary target or a max-weight fish is on the agenda. Rods in Fisch are tools for jobs, and this one's job is payroll.

## How Mutation Forcing Works (The Mechanic the Quest Assumes)

Since the entire quest hinges on forcing mutations, here is the underlying logic so you are not following steps you don't understand.

Every catch normally rolls its mutation the way a die rolls — no tag is common, Silver and Lunar are faces on the die, and raw fishing means accepting whatever comes up. The quest asks for specific faces *and* specific base items simultaneously, which compounds two dice. A Mutation Totem appraised before fishing or the Mutated Sharky bait does the same thing: it loads the die so the next catches carry the chosen mutation, collapsing two rolls into one. The Moonlight Rod goes further and simply removes the roll — every catch is Lunar, which is why the String part collapses to a single cast with it equipped.

The practical rule that follows from that: refresh the forcing effect before it expires rather than after, because one unforced cast mid-farm can hand you an untagged bolt that looks identical and goes straight into the sell pile. Once you think of the quest as dice removal rather than fishing luck, every step below becomes obvious.

## 5 Counter-Intuitive Truths

### 1. Garbage Bait Is the Correct Bait for a Legendary-Grade Quest

Every instinct says better bait, better results. Here the objective *is* the trash. Luck bait raises the floor of what bites and quietly removes Rusty Bolts and Scrap Metal from your pool. Equip Garbage Bait, catch junk on purpose, finish in minutes.

### 2. The "Cheapest" Rod Quest Is Expensive in Attention

It costs almost no C$ in materials and no event windows. What it costs is *reading*: missing the word "Silver" or "Lunar" turns a one-hour quest into a multi-evening one. The difficulty is informational, not mechanical.

### 3. Turning Items In Early Beats Carrying Everything

Most players collect all eight items and sail back once. That creates eight chances to misclick-sell a tagged bolt. Hand in each part the moment it is complete — the quest explicitly allows it — and your junk becomes irreversibly banked.

### 4. The Passive Is Better in Boring Zones Than in Glamorous Ones

A 3x Rare-Finds roll pays the most in zones with long, stable fish tables where you can fish for an hour uninterrupted. Taking the rod to a flashy event zone competes with event drops and dilutes the roll. The Scavenger earns its keep in the spots nobody else is showing off.

### 5. The Titanium Rod Is Both Your Quest Tool and Your Warning Sign

You use the Titanium Rod to earn the Scavenger Rod because resilience matters while farming junk. If after the upgrade you find yourself swapping back to Titanium constantly, you are fishing content the Scavenger wasn't built for — big, hard-fighting fish. The swap isn't a failure of the new rod; it's a signal you've outgrown mid-game money fishing.

## Failure Retrospective: Six Ways the Quest Stalls

**Failure 1: Fishing bolts at the rig.** The NPC is on the rig, so players assume the fish are nearby. They catch commons off the platform for half an hour. Bolts live in the Mine Shaft zone. The quest gives an NPC and a map; they are not the same place.

**Failure 2: Appraising away the tagged item.** A Silver Bolt comes up, muscle memory appraises or sells it, and the quest counter never moved. This is why forced mutations plus immediate hand-ins are the safe pairing: the item is correct on the first catch and banked before the appraiser temptation.

**Failure 3: Stacking luck bait.** Rarer fish feel like progress. They aren't progress when progress is literal garbage. Switch to Garbage Bait, watch the pool get worse, and watch the counter move faster.

**Failure 4: Hunting the Lunar String in weather.** There is no weather condition. Time spent waiting for the "right sky" is invented difficulty. If the mutation isn't forced, the sky was never the problem — the rod and bait were.

**Failure 5: Fishing The Depths at night.** Night changes the table, the catch rate of Scrap drops, and the "easy five-minute step" stretches to twenty. Check the clock before you dive; if it's dark, reorder the quest rather than fighting it.

**Failure 6: Skipping the rod you were told to bring.** Players who like their current rod insist on using it in the Mine Shaft and then complain about bolt rates. The Titanium Rod recommendation is not brand loyalty — the junk pool there is tuned around a neutral lure profile, and high-luck rods quietly tilt the table away from bolts. Quest steps specify equipment for the same reason recipes specify ingredients.

The common thread across all six: nothing here is a skill check. Every failure is an information or preparation error made before the first cast — which is good news, because those are the only failures you can fix at the dock instead of in the water.

## Methodology: The Under-an-Hour Route

This is the order I would run it on a fresh account, and the order I recommend:

**Prep, on land.** Confirm the Titanium Rod. Buy a full stack of Garbage Bait. Put Mutated Sharky on the hook or carry two Mutation Totems. Equip the Moonlight Rod if owned; note the Toxin Burst as the bolt accelerator. Bag space checked.

**Minute 0–5, The Depths (day).** Scrap Metal, any rod, Garbage Bait. Five pieces, immediate sail-back-and-hand-in if the rig is close; otherwise carry them.

**Minute 5–15, Desolate Pocket.** Moonlight Rod: one cast, one Lunar String. Without it: force Lunar, fish up to ten minutes.

**Minute 15–40, Mine Shaft.** Titanium Rod (or Toxin Burst), Garbage Bait, Silver forced. Two tagged bolts, handing them in together with the rest.

**After the quest.** Don't take the new rod to a boss fish to "test" it. Take it to a stable mid-game zone, fish an hour, and count the surprise payouts — that's the test the Scavenger Rod actually passes. See the [rod and bait combination guide](/posts/fisch/rod-bait-combination-guide/) for the zones that pair best with Rare-Finds builds.

The whole design lesson is one line: **force the mutations, fish the junk on purpose, bank each part immediately**. Everything else is boat travel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I get the Scavenger Rod in Fisch?**

Sail to the oil rig off Rosslit Bay, enter the top-floor Laboratory, and talk to Dr. Crookspine. Complete his three-part quest: 2 Silver Rusty Bolts from the Mine Shaft, 5 Scrap Metal from The Depths in daytime, and 1 Lunar String from Desolate Pocket. The bolts need the Silver mutation and the string needs Lunar. No boss fight, no event window.

**Where is Dr. Crookspine?**

Oil rig off Rosslit Bay, top floor of the main building, turn right into the Laboratory. Parts can be handed in separately, so bank each set as you finish it. Empty lab means an outdated server — rejoin.

**How do I get a Silver Rusty Bolt?**

Mine Shaft, Titanium Rod, Garbage Bait, Silver forced through Mutated Sharky or a Mutation Totem. Toxin Burst speeds the farm with extra catches. Plan two totem cycles for two bolts.

**Where do I catch Lunar String?**

Desolate Pocket in Desolate Deep, any bait and any weather. The requirement is the Lunar mutation. The Moonlight Rod makes every catch Lunar — one cast and done — otherwise force it the same way as Silver.

**Is the Scavenger Rod better than the Titanium Rod?**

As a permanent mid-game money rod, yes: 75% Lure Speed, 100% Luck, a 3x Rare-Finds roll and a 40% chance at a 3x Scavenged sell. Keep Titanium for high-resilience fishing; the Scavenger is for cash per hour, not big-fish control.

**How long does the quest take?**

Prepared, under an hour — Scrap Metal two to five minutes, Lunar String one cast with the Moonlight Rod, bolts ten to twenty minutes. Raw fishing with no mutation forcing is the only thing that stretches it to a full evening.

**What do I need before starting?**

Mutated Sharky bait or two Mutation Totems, the Titanium Rod, plenty of Garbage Bait, and optionally the Moonlight Rod and Toxin Burst. That's the entire loadout.
