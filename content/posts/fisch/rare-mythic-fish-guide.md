## How Rarity Works in Fisch

Fisch uses a layered rarity system. Each fish has a base catch rate modified by your rod, bait, enchantments, and environmental conditions.

- **Common:** ~50-60% | 5-50 C$
- **Uncommon:** ~20-25% | 50-150 C$
- **Rare:** ~8-12% | 150-500 C$
- **Legendary:** ~2-4% | 500-2,000 C$
- **Mythic:** ~0.3-1% | 2,000-15,000+ C$

A 0.3% base rate becomes ~2-3% with a fully optimized Mythic hunting loadout — 10x better odds.

## Luck Stacking: The Full Formula

Total catch quality is determined by:

```
Effective Luck = (Rod Luck + Enchant Luck) × (1 + Bait Luck) × (1 + Server/Potion Boost)
```

Example with max setup:
```
(45% Destiny + 15% Divine) × (1 + 25% Night Shrimp) × (1 + 20% Luck Potion)
= 60% × 1.25 × 1.20
= 90% effective luck modifier
```

This turns a 0.5% base Mythic rate into a ~1% rate — double your odds. Every piece of the setup matters.

## Failure Analysis: Why You Keep Missing Mythics

The mistakes table above tells you *what* goes wrong. Here is *why* it keeps happening.

**Sunk Cost Fallacy at the Wrong Dock.** You traveled to the Haunted Shipwreck, set up your Trident Rod, and realized it is sunny afternoon. Most players keep casting anyway because "I am already here." In Fisch, persistence is not a virtue — it is a trap. A fish with a zero-percent spawn rate does not care how long you have been waiting. Every cast in wrong conditions is C$ and bait flushed.

**Misreading Luck as Direct Chance.** A 45% Luck rod does not mean a 45% chance at a Mythic. It means a 45% modifier applied to a 0.3% base rate. Players see "45%" and mentally round up to "almost half." The actual math is brutal: even with maxed gear, you are looking at roughly a 1% effective rate. That is one Mythic per hundred ideal casts, not per ten.

**Confirmation Bias from Near-Misses.** Fisch shows the rarity tier of a fish as it fights. When you see "Legendary" pop up and lose it at the boat, your brain records that as "I was close." You were not close. The game rolled a Legendary, not a Mythic. Those are separate outcomes in the loot table. Chasing "near misses" keeps players fishing in suboptimal spots for hours.

**The "Any Condition" Trap.** Mythics like the Megalodon list "Any" for time and weather. Players read that as "easy to find." It actually means the fish shares a spawn pool with every other "Any" fish across all conditions, which dilutes its appearance rate. Targeting a fish with strict weather requirements — Phantom Ray during foggy nights — gives you a *smaller* but *more focused* spawn pool.

## Mythic Hunting Decision Framework

Use this flowchart before you leave port.

**Step 1: Check the Clock and Weather Station.**  
If your target fish requires night plus fog, and it is currently sunny afternoon, do not go to the location. Go to Step 4.

**Step 2: Assemble the Loadout for the Fish, Not for Your Inventory.**  
Match bait to the fish's preference first, then layer luck on top. If a fish only bites Maggots, using Night Shrimp gives you zero casts at that fish regardless of its Luck bonus.

**Step 3: Fish for 15–20 Minutes, Then Audit.**  
Set a timer. If you have not seen at least one fish from your target's rarity tier in twenty minutes, something is wrong — wrong spot, wrong depth, or wrong server state. Do not "just keep going." Debug.

**Step 4: Pivot or Server-Hop.**  
If conditions are wrong, server-hop immediately. If conditions are right but you are not getting bites after the audit, switch targets to a fish that matches the *current* weather and time. Fisch rewards flexibility more than stubbornness.

**Step 5: Log Your Sessions.**  
Track which rods, bait, and conditions produced results. Patterns emerge fast. Most players who complain about bad luck are actually fishing the same wrong setup repeatedly because they never logged what worked.

## Counter-Intuitive Mythic Insights

**1. A Higher-Luck Rod Can Reduce Your Catch Rate.**  
If you switch from a Trident Rod to a Destiny Rod for the Volcanic Serpent, but forget the Fireproof enchantment, you will lose 10 HP per cast and be forced to retreat before the spawn timer resolves. The "best" rod is the one that lets you stay at the location longest, not the one with the highest number.

**2. Server-Hopping Too Fast Breaks Spawn Tables.**  
Players who hop servers every five seconds to find blizzards sometimes land on servers where the weather state has not fully synced to the client. You see snow, but the server still registers clear. Wait ten seconds after loading in before casting — let the state settle.

**3. "Any" Conditions Are a Statistical Penalty.**  
Fish that spawn during "Any" weather or time compete with the *entire* global spawn pool. The Phantom Ray, restricted to foggy nights, has fewer total competitors for its spawn slot. In practice, a restricted fish can be *faster* to target than an "Any" fish because you eliminate most of the competition by waiting for the right window.

**4. Faster Lure Speed Can Hurt Mythic Farming.**  
Worms give +10% Lure Speed, which sounds efficient. But Mythic catches are not about volume — they are about rolling the right number in a fixed pool. A faster lure means you cycle through more *wrong* fish faster, burning bait and rod durability without increasing your Mythic roll probability. Slow and correct beats fast and random.
