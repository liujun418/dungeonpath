You are standing on the Moosewood dock with 7,000 C$ in your pocket. The question that has been asked a million times in the Fisch Discord sits in front of you: Carbon Rod or Steady Rod?

Two years ago the answer was clean. Buy the Carbon Rod for 2,000 C$, use it to grind the 7,000 C$ for the Steady Rod, then start saving for a real endgame rod. That was the whole progression. Nobody argued.

Then The Deep update dropped on August 8, 2026, and it quietly broke that advice in half. It added a free rod — the Luminous Rod — that does most of what the Steady Rod does, costs nothing, and comes with a 9x sell multiplier on a third of your catches. Suddenly the "Carbon first, then Steady" path is not obvious anymore. In some cases it is wrong.

This guide rewrites the old Carbon vs Steady answer for the post-Deep game. Here is what to buy, what to skip, and why the mid-game decision is no longer about which rod to spend 7,000 C$ on.

## The Quick Verdict, Updated for The Deep

**Buy the Carbon Rod. Skip the Steady Rod.** Go straight from Carbon to the free Luminous Rod in The Deep, then aim for the Titanium Rod.

The old path was:

```
Flimsy Rod → Carbon Rod ($2K) → Steady Rod ($7K) → Endgame Rod ($100K+)
```

The new path, now that The Deep is out:

```
Flimsy Rod → Carbon Rod ($2K) → Luminous Rod (free) → Titanium Rod ($200K)
```

The Steady Rod fell off the list. Not because it got nerfed — the update did not touch existing rods. It fell off because the Luminous Rod does the same job for free and sells fish for nine times the price. That single fact is the whole story, but you need the numbers to see why.

## Carbon Rod vs Steady Rod: The Stats That Matter

Before the new rods confuse things, get the baseline straight. Here is the head-to-head.

| Stat | Carbon Rod | Steady Rod | What It Means |
|------|-----------|------------|----------------|
| Price | 2,000 C$ | 7,000 C$ | Carbon is 3.5x cheaper |
| Location | Moosewood (Marc's Shop) | Roslit Bay (Alfie) | Carbon is on your starting island |
| Lure Speed | +15% | -60% | Carbon bites fast, Steady is slow |
| Luck | 25% | 35% | Steady rolls rare fish slightly more |
| Control | 0.05 | 0.05 | Tie — neither has a wide catch bar |
| Resilience | 10% | 30% | Steady absorbs fish thrashes better |
| Max Weight | 600 kg | 250,000 kg | Steady lands anything, Carbon breaks on big fish |
| Special | None | Larger shake button UI | Steady eases the minigame on mobile |

Read that table once and the real story is the **lure speed**. The Carbon Rod bites fast. The Steady Rod bites 60% slower than normal. Every hour you spend farming with a Steady Rod is an hour with fewer casts, fewer catches, and fewer C$.

### Carbon Rod: The Speed Pick

**The scene:** You are Level 6, still on the Flimsy Rod, watching a level 40 player reel in a shark while you pull up your third salmon. You check Marc's Shop and see the Carbon Rod for 2,000 C$. That is the entire decision, and it is not a decision at all.

The Carbon Rod is the first meaningful upgrade in Fisch, and it is still the best 2,000 C$ you will ever spend. It doubles your luck off the Flimsy Rod and gives you positive lure speed, which means more bites per minute. The 600 kg weight cap is the catch — it breaks on anything heavy. That is fine for the early game, because you are not catching sharks at Level 6. You are catching whatever is fast and sellable at the Moosewood docks.

**What the Carbon Rod is for:** the first 15-20 levels, grinding the cash for your next move, and any session where speed beats size. It costs so little that skipping it to save money makes no sense. You earn the 2,000 C$ back in your first ten minutes of fishing.

### Steady Rod: The Stability Pick

**The scene:** You are at Roslit Bay, Level 22, and a whale shark just snapped your Carbon Rod's line for the third time. The 600 kg cap is the problem. You buy the Steady Rod from Alfie, and suddenly the same fish lands with room to spare.

That is the honest case for the Steady Rod. Its 250,000 kg max weight means no normal fish breaks your line. Its 30% resilience means fish thrash less, and the bigger shake button makes the minigame more forgiving — especially on a phone. For a long stretch of the game's history, that was worth 7,000 C$.

The trade-off is the -60% lure speed. Fish take noticeably longer to bite. Over a multi-hour farm, that slower bite rate compounds into fewer catches, and fewer catches means slower C$ per hour. The Steady Rod was always a rod you tolerated for its safety, not one you loved for its speed.

## What The Deep Update Changed

On August 8, 2026, The Deep (v1.96.0) added a full underwater trench biome, three new zones, and four new rods. Two of those rods — the Luminous Rod and the Scalding Hook — are free quest rewards. That is the part that reshuffles the mid-game.

Here is the relevant trio, with the numbers that matter for this decision.

| Rod | Source | Lure | Luck | Control | Resilience | Max Weight | Key Passive | Cost |
|------|--------|------|------|---------|------------|------------|-------------|------|
| Luminous Rod | Dr. Vane quest (Outer Deep) | 70% | 95% | 0.1 | 25% | 60,000 kg | 10% Bioluminescent (4x), 30% Luminous (9x), +10% Progress Speed | Free |
| Scalding Hook | Fester quest (Lower Deep) | 82% | 180% | 0.12 | 30% | 400,000 kg | Scalded mutation, hooks ghost-type fish | Free |
| Titanium Rod | Ancient Archives craft | 100% | 180% | 0.3 | 75% | Infinite | 20% Titanium (9x), auto-slash, +30% Progress Speed | 200,000 C$ + mats |

### Luminous Rod: The Free Steady Rod Killer

Here is the counterintuitive part of this whole guide, and the reason the Steady Rod is dead for most players.

The Luminous Rod is free. You get it from Dr. Vane in Outer Deep after a four-part questline — collect Bone Shards, collect Bio-Fluid, catch specific mutated fish, bring back proof. No C$, no level gate. And the stats are not "free rod" stats: 95% luck beats the Steady Rod's 35%, 70% lure blows its -60% out of the water, and 60,000 kg max weight handles every mid-game fish you will actually hook.

On top of that, it rolls the **Luminous mutation on 30% of catches, selling the fish for 9x**. A third of everything you pull up is worth nine times base price. The Steady Rod has no sell multiplier at all. It just catches fish at face value.

**The scene:** You walk into Outer Deep with a Steady Rod you paid 7,000 C$ for, feeling prepared. The player next to you finished Dr. Vane's questline an hour ago and is reeling in Luminous-mutated fish for 9x while you are still waiting on a -60% bite timer. They are earning money faster than you, with a rod that cost zero C$ and has better luck. That is the moment the old advice dies.

### Titanium Rod: The Real Target

If the Luminous Rod is the new mid-game, the Titanium Rod is the new endgame goal. It requires Level 250 and 200,000 C$ plus materials, so it is a long way off — but it is the rod you are now saving toward instead of a Steady Rod.

The Titanium Rod's headline is infinite max weight and a 20% Titanium mutation that also sells for 9x, on a rod with 180% luck and 100% lure. The Luminous Rod is your bridge to it: you use the Luminous Rod's 9x mutation to grind the cash for the Titanium Rod. For the full crafting path, see the [Titanium Rod guide](/posts/fisch/titanium-rod-guide/).

### Scalding Hook: Niche, Not a Priority

The Scalding Hook from Fester in Lower Deep has 180% luck, which is tempting. But it is a specialist rod for ghost-type fish and ramping progress on stubborn deep-sea catches. It is not a mid-game farming tool, and it does not change the Carbon vs Steady question. Treat it as an optional pickup later, not part of your progression line.

## The Mid-Game Decision: What to Actually Do With Your 7,000 C$

This is the decision framework. You have saved 7,000 C$ with your Carbon Rod. Before The Deep, the answer was automatic: buy the Steady Rod. Now it splits three ways.

### Path A: Buy the Steady Rod (only if you cannot reach The Deep)

If you are locked out of The Deep for some reason — you have not grabbed the Deep Survey Device, or you are playing an older version of the game — the Steady Rod still has a role. Its 250,000 kg cap lets you land heavy fish your Carbon Rod breaks. Buy it, use it to reach whatever your next goal is, then replace it.

### Path B: Skip the Steady Rod, go for the Luminous Rod (recommended)

This is the new default. Use your Carbon Rod to fish your way to The Deep, claim the free Deep Survey Device at the NPC on the seabed, then run Dr. Vane's questline in Outer Deep. The Luminous Rod is free, has better luck and lure than the Steady Rod, and pays a 9x mutation on a third of catches. You keep your 7,000 C$ and put it toward the Titanium Rod fund instead.

### Path C: The budget split (if you want a safety net)

Some players like a dedicated big-fish rod in reserve. The honest read: at mid-game, the Luminous Rod's 60,000 kg cap covers almost everything, so the Steady Rod's 250,000 kg is mostly overkill. You are paying 7,000 C$ for a cap you will rarely test. If you insist on the safety net, buy the Steady Rod — but know you are spending money to feel safe, not to earn faster.

**The tie-breaker question:** Are you going to reach The Deep this week? If yes, skip the Steady Rod. If no, and a heavy fish is actively blocking your progress, buy it. That single question replaces the old automatic purchase.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

### Buying the Steady Rod, Then Discovering the Luminous Rod Is Free

**The mistake:** You follow an old guide, grind 7,000 C$, buy the Steady Rod at Roslit Bay, then walk into The Deep and watch a free rod with better stats get handed to another player.

**The recovery:** The 7,000 C$ is not a total loss — the Steady Rod still lands heavy fish and you can keep it as a specialist. But do not double down. Finish Dr. Vane's questline anyway and switch to the Luminous Rod for your daily farming. Treat the Steady Rod as a lesson, not a reason to skip the better free rod.

### Using the Steady Rod to Farm Fast Common Fish

**The mistake:** You keep the Steady Rod equipped for everything, including quick cash sessions on cheap common fish. The -60% lure speed means you are casting into silence while a Carbon Rod player next to you is pulling a fish every few seconds.

**The recovery:** Match the rod to the job. Use a fast rod — Carbon or Luminous — for volume farming where you want many quick catches. Save the Steady Rod for the moments you need to land something heavy that your fast rod cannot. One rod for speed, one rod for size. Never use the slow rod to grind volume.

### Skipping the Carbon Rod to Save Money

**The mistake:** You decide 2,000 C$ is 2,000 C$ and try to grind all the way to The Deep on the Flimsy Rod.

**The recovery:** This one is pure math. The Flimsy Rod's low luck and slow bites mean it takes you three to four times longer to earn any given amount. The Carbon Rod pays for itself inside fifteen minutes and then compounds. There is no scenario where skipping the cheapest upgrade in the game makes you richer. If you already did this, stop, buy the Carbon Rod immediately, and do not look back.

## The Counterintuitive Read: Cheaper Is Better Here

The old way of thinking about Fisch rods was linear: more C$ spent equals a better rod equals more progress. The Deep update broke that model, and it is worth spelling out because it is easy to miss.

The Steady Rod costs 7,000 C$ and looks like an upgrade over the Carbon Rod. The Luminous Rod costs nothing and looks like a "free quest item" you should not take seriously. In practice, the free rod is the better mid-game rod by almost every metric that matters for earning: higher luck, higher lure, a 9x sell mutation, and enough max weight to cover mid-game. The Steady Rod's two real advantages — 250,000 kg cap and 30% resilience — only kick in on fish that, at mid-game, you are not catching often enough to justify the price.

This is the same logic that plays out again at the top of the new rod ladder. The Titanium Rod at 200,000 C$ looks expensive, but its real value is the 20% Titanium mutation, not the flashy "infinite max weight" stat. The Deep's rods reward you for reading what they actually do, not what they cost. For a deeper dive on that whole new ladder, see the [The Deep update guide](/posts/fisch/the-deep-update-guide/).

## The Updated Progression Path

Here is the post-Deep mid-game route, all in one place.

| Milestone | Rod | Cost | Verdict |
|-----------|-----|------|---------|
| Start | Flimsy Rod | Free | Replace immediately |
| 2,000 C$ | Carbon Rod | 2,000 C$ | Buy, always |
| Reach The Deep | Luminous Rod | Free (quest) | Grab, replaces Steady Rod |
| (Optional) | Steady Rod | 7,000 C$ | Skip unless locked out of The Deep |
| Level 250 | Titanium Rod | 200,000 C$ + mats | New endgame target |

The Carbon Rod is still the first buy. The Steady Rod is now an optional side path, not the default. The Luminous Rod is the free stepping stone that gets you from mid-game to the Titanium Rod, and it is the single biggest reason the Carbon vs Steady debate stopped being a debate.
