## You Fire, and the Line Goes Tight at the Same Time

You're standing on the seafloor in The Deep, harpoon gun raised, a Monstrous-class fish drifting past a pillar maybe thirty studs out. You settle the reticle, squeeze the trigger, and the bolt flies. That should be it — one harpoon, one hit, one chunk of damage, and a fish you still have to cast for with a normal rod. Instead, the moment the bolt connects, the catch bar is already on your screen. The reel is engaged. The fish is on your line before you even finish the trigger pull.

No second cast. No weapon swap. You harpooned it and hooked it in the same motion.

That's the Halibut Harpoon, and it is not a reskinned rod. It is the first weapon in Fisch that is a rod and a harpoon gun at the same time, and it landed in a small follow-up patch on August 15, 2026 — about a week after The Deep (v1.96.0) went live on August 8. Same week, same trench, a pile of smaller additions: a free Developer Gift, three new Plaguereaver quests, a Stabilizer Chip, and a weekend luck buff.

This guide walks the whole thing in the order you'll actually hit it. If you haven't reached The Deep yet, start with our [The Deep update guide](/posts/fisch/the-deep-update-guide/) — it covers the entrance, the Survey Device, and the three zones. If you're already in and wondering whether this beats your current rod, we cover that too.

## What the Halibut Harpoon Actually Does

The Halibut Harpoon is a quest reward from Dr. Monty, one of the NPCs tucked into The Deep. You don't buy it, and you don't craft it at the Ancient Archives like the [Titanium Rod](/posts/fisch/titanium-rod-guide/) or the [Cusk Purger](/posts/fisch/cusk-purger-rod-guide/). You earn it by finishing Dr. Monty's questline, which runs through three puzzle answers and then four Pufferfish tasks.

Here's the stat sheet:

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Lure Speed | 132% |
| Luck | 288% |
| Max Kg | Infinite |

Lure Speed controls how fast fish bite, Luck controls your odds of rolling rare and mutated catches, and Max Kg is your line's weight limit. The first two numbers are good. The third is the one that matters.

A normal rod has a finite Max Kg. Put a fish on the line that weighs more than that number, and the line snaps. Some of the biggest fish in The Deep — the Monstrous-class ones, the kind that show up during hunts — are simply too heavy for most rods to land no matter how well you play the minigame. The Halibut Harpoon has no limit. The line doesn't snap on weight, full stop. That alone changes which fish you can even attempt.

On top of the stats, the weapon runs a passive minigame. Because it's a hybrid, the harpoon fires while you reel, so a single cast is pulling double duty: harpoon damage and a normal rod catch, together. That's not a flavor line. It means the weapon can trigger both rod mechanics and harpoon mechanics off one pull, which we'll get into below.

## Dr. Monty's Puzzle: Three Answers You'll Type Wrong

Finding Dr. Monty is the easy part — he's in The Deep, and if you've done any of the update's other NPC lines, you'll pass him. The hard part is his dialogue. He asks three questions in a row, and the game checks your answers against exact strings. Get one wrong and the quest line stalls until you retype it.

Here are the three answers, in order:

| Question | Exact Answer |
|---|---|
| First | Hubbermistchen |
| Second | 13,388.82 C$ |
| Third | Orange…? |

That third one is the trap. The answer is not "Orange." It's not "orange." It's **"Orange…?"** — capital O, an ellipsis of three dots, and a question mark. The first one is a trap too: "Hubbermistchen" has a double B and a "ch" in the middle, and people drop a letter or add an extra one.

Here's the failure you'll hit if you rush this. You type "Orange?" and the prompt rejects it, or you type "Hubbermischen" and it rejects that too. Then you assume the quest is bugged, you leave, you come back, and you burn twenty minutes re-running dialogue. It's not bugged. It's just strict about spelling and punctuation.

How to recover: copy the answers above, paste them, and don't add spaces. If a prompt still rejects you, close the dialogue and reopen it — occasionally the input box keeps a stray trailing space from a previous attempt. Type slow, match the string exactly, and the line moves forward.

One more note: the second answer, "13,388.82 C$", needs the comma and the decimal exactly as written, including the "C$" at the end. Skip the comma and it won't take.

## The Four Pufferfish Tasks After the Puzzle

Solving the three puzzles is not the end of Dr. Monty's line. It's the gate. Once the puzzles are done, he hands you a batch of four Pufferfish tasks. These are fetch-and-catch jobs — you go out, you land Pufferfish (and a few things near them), and you bring them back. Each task ticks a counter, and the Halibut Harpoon drops into your inventory only when the fourth one closes.

The tasks themselves aren't hard if you know where Pufferfish spawn. They cluster in specific pockets of The Deep, and the timing matters more than the rod you bring. Bring anything with decent Lure Speed so you're not sitting on empty water. The Halibut Harpoon's own 132% Lure Speed is the reward you're chasing, not the tool you'll use to chase it.

This is where the limited code ties in: `COLLECTMYPUFFERFISH`. It's a wink at exactly these tasks, and it pays out the Giant Jet Ski. You'll want it anyway, but if you're about to grind Pufferfish, redeem it first so you're not doubling back across the trench on foot.

## 288% Luck Is Not Why You Want This

Here's the thing most guides get wrong, and it's worth slowing down for. People see "288% Luck" and file the Halibut Harpoon under "high luck rod." That's the wrong drawer.

288% Luck is good. It is not rare. Plenty of rods in The Deep push high Luck numbers, and the [Cusk Purger](/posts/fisch/cusk-purger-rod-guide/) out-moneys it on the hunt side with its +7 Disturbance. If all you want is more rare catches per hour, you already have options.

The two things that actually make the Halibut Harpoon worth chasing are the other two lines on that stat sheet.

First, infinite Max Kg. This is not a "nice to have." It's the difference between attempting a Monstrous-class catch and having the line snap the instant it bites. Every other rod caps out at some weight, and the heaviest fish in The Deep live above that cap. The Halibut Harpoon is the only weapon that removes the weight check entirely. For those specific fish, no amount of Luck or Lure Speed on another rod helps you — they break your line before those stats matter.

Second, the hybrid trigger. Because the weapon is a rod and a harpoon gun at once, one cast can fire both sets of mechanics. You get the harpoon's damage and its interaction on top of the rod's normal catch and mutation rolls. That means one pull can do two jobs, and in a game where time is the resource, doing two jobs in one cast is the real upgrade.

So the honest read: 288% Luck is the flashy number, but infinite Max Kg plus the double-trigger is the actual value. Chasing this rod for Luck alone is a waste of the grind.

## Harpoon Now, or Titanium / Cusk Purger First?

This is the decision most players face, so let's put it in a frame instead of a hot take.

Ask yourself two questions. First: are you below Level 250 with no Titanium Rod? Second: do you already own the Cusk Purger?

If you're below Level 250 and don't have a solid money rod yet, do not chase the Halibut Harpoon first. Dr. Vane's free Luminous Rod in Outer Deep is your first stop, then the [Titanium Rod](/posts/fisch/titanium-rod-guide/) at Level 250. The Harpoon is a utility weapon, not a money engine, and grinding its questline without a good farming rod just slows you down. You'll want income before you want a specialty harpoon.

If you already have the Cusk Purger, the Halibut Harpoon is a sidegrade, not a strict upgrade. The Cusk Purger still makes more C$ per hour on hunts thanks to Disturbance. What the Harpoon buys you is the infinite weight limit and the hybrid catch — utility you'll feel on the heaviest Monstrous fish, not in your bank balance.

The clean rule: chase the Halibut Harpoon when one of two things is true — either you're actively hunting the biggest, heaviest catches and snapping lines is your bottleneck, or you want the hybrid playstyle for its own sake. If neither is true, finish your Titanium or Cusk Purger line first and treat the Harpoon as the next project.

## The Developer Gift: A Free Sword That's Also a Rod

The same August 15 patch dropped a Developer Gift, and it's the easiest thing on this page. Any player can claim it free, no quest, no level gate. You grab it from the gift menu while the window is open.

Inside: the **Darkheart sword**, the **Silly Stone Guy**, and new fish species.

The failure here is assuming the Darkheart is just a cosmetic sword. It isn't. It works as a rod too, which means it's a free catch tool dressed up as a melee weapon. Players who glance at the gift, see "sword," and skip it are leaving a free rod on the table. If you're early-game or just want a spare loadout, claim it before the window closes.

The second failure is timing. Developer Gifts like this don't sit in your mailbox forever. The claim window opened August 15, and when it's gone, it's gone. If you're reading this and the gift menu still shows it, claim it now — you can sort out whether you'll use the Darkheart later.

## Plaguereaver Mastery, Stabilizer Chip, and the Weekend Buffs

The patch shipped a few smaller things alongside the Harpoon and the gift, and they're easy to miss because none of them announce themselves.

**Plaguereaver Mastery** adds three new quests — Master of the Garden, The Plague, and A Great Disturbance. Finish them for the Plague Doctor title, the Wings of Wrath, and the Plaguecleaver skin. If you're a completionist, this is the new checklist.

**Stabilizer Chip** is a toggle module for your Survey Device. It smooths out underwater movement so you don't drift when you stop reeling. If you already finished Nereus's questline, the chip is granted automatically — check your Survey Device's toggle list, it's probably already there waiting to be switched on.

**Double Luck Weekend** is running, which stacks with the Harpoon's 288% Luck and with any server buff. If you're going to grind rare Pufferfish for the quest or chase mutated catches, do it while the weekend buff is live.

**Black Market restock** is set for this Sunday, with limited rods. These sell out fast and rotate, so if you've been waiting on a specific rod, this is your window — and it's a good reminder to bank your C$ now.

Last, the **Sunshell Merchant** got a new skin, Lemonade Serenade. Cosmetic, but if you like the merchant line, it's a cheap pickup.

## Grab These Before Sunday

Three things have a clock on them, and it's worth being blunt about the order.

First, redeem `COLLECTMYPUFFERFISH` for the Giant Jet Ski. It's case-insensitive, so `CollectMyPufferfish` works the same. Codes like this one expire, and they don't come back, so do it before anything else.

Second, claim the Developer Gift. It's free, it's live now, and the Darkheart doubles as a rod. There is no reason to skip a free rod.

Third, set an alarm for the Black Market restock on Sunday. Limited rods rotate out fast, and if you've been saving for one, this is the drop to watch.

The Halibut Harpoon itself isn't going anywhere — Dr. Monty's questline is permanent. That means you can take your time on the puzzles and the Pufferfish tasks. Just don't sit on the time-limited stuff. Grab the code, claim the gift, and then settle into the grind with a full weekend of double luck behind you.
