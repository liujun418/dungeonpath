You spawn at Northern Expedition, run past the camp selling the Avalanche Rod and Pickaxe, and jump up three tiers of stone platforms. At the top stands a wiry guy with a telescope — Meteor Maniac Matthew, the quest marker above his head reading **Anomaly Investigation: SPACE CANDY**. The name sounds silly. The items behind it are not.

This questline ends with the Meteoric Rod, and here's the honest summary before we start: the hard part is not any single item. It is that the quest asks for things from **three different event systems at the same time**, and most players only find that out after the first event window has already closed.

This guide walks the whole line in order: where Matthew stands, every item source for both quests, what to prepare before the events, and how the meteor-dodge passive plays once the rod is actually yours.

## Why Most Meteoric Rod Guides Waste Your Evening

Every quick guide I found makes the same four mistakes. Knowing them up front keeps you out of the traps.

**They list items without listing the events that gate them.** Telling you "you need five Solar Blaze Stalactite" is useless without the rest of the sentence: *during an Eclipse, with a specific rod, in a specific cave*. The quest's real difficulty is event scheduling, and item lists hide that completely.

**They treat the Starfall Totem as a reliable source.** A totem starts a shower, but the meteor lands where it lands, the pink beam fades, and if you are standing in the wrong place the relic is gone. Guides that say "use a totem, get the relic" skip the part that actually fails.

**They don't separate the two quests' prerequisites.** Quest 1 wants Overgrowth Canal fish. Quest 2 wants Crimson Cave fish and a hidden cavern in a third canal. Players blend the two and fish the wrong water during a rare window.

**They never tell you what to prepare.** The players who finish this in one evening are the ones holding the Wicked Fang Rod, a Pickaxe, Garbage Bait and totems *before* talking to Matthew. Everyone else spends the weekend in prerequisites. That prep list is the most valuable part of this guide.

## How We Evaluate a Rod Questline

Before the steps, here is the framework I use for every Fisch rod quest, because it tells you exactly where a questline will hurt:

**1. Event dependency.** How many items only exist during timed events? Here: Eclipse, Starfall and meteor showers. That is three, which is high.

**2. Prerequisite depth.** What rods, baits and tools must already exist? Wicked Fang, Cerulean Fang, Pickaxe, Magnet Bait, Garbage Bait, Starlight Worm. Deep.

**3. RNG exposure.** Which items are rolls rather than chores? Quartz Glass and the gemstones. The rest is deterministic effort.

**4. Time gating versus effort gating.** Is the wait real-time (aquarium, Daily Shop) or grind-time? Both exist here, and that mix decides whether the quest takes an evening or a week.

**5. Payoff versus the alternatives.** Does the rod do anything the [best rods tier list](/posts/fisch/best-rods-tier-list/) regulars cannot? Yes — the only skill-based passive in that tier.

**6. Failure recovery.** If an event window is wasted, how bad is it? Eclipse windows are the expensive ones to lose; totems are renewable C$. Score the windows accordingly.

Now, the actual quest.

## Finding Meteor Maniac Matthew

Head to **Northern Expedition**. The camp you want is the one selling the Avalanche Rod and the Pickaxe. From there, look up — Matthew is on a three-tier platform stack behind the camp, not on the ground.

Jump the tiers one by one. The platforms are meant to be climbed, so don't look for a staircase or a teleporter. At the top, Matthew (sometimes called Astral Matthew) stands near the telescope. Talk to him and the first quest pops: **SPACE CANDY**.

One note: he only exists on servers running the Astral Observatory update. If the platform is empty, you're on an old server. Rejoin a current one.

## Quest 1 — Anomaly Investigation: SPACE CANDY

Three objectives. This is the warm-up, but the Cosmic Relic trips more players than it should.

**1. Cosmic Relic (x1).** Buy or find a **Starfall Totem**, use it to trigger a meteor shower, and watch the sky. When a meteor lands, a **pink beam** shoots from the impact point. Sprint to the beam and interact. Do the prep first: stand on high open ground, clear inventory space, and keep moving the moment the crater glows. If you watch the shower like fireworks, you will lose track of which beam was yours.

**2. Moonstone (x1).** Three routes, easiest first: your personal aquarium can produce it passively; Meteor Totem drops can give it; the Daily Shop sells it in stock. Check the aquarium and shop before you burn a single totem.

**3. Solar Blaze OR Nova Frozen Walnut (x1).** Fish the **Overgrowth Canal** with the **Cerulean Fang Rod** and **Starlight Worm** bait. The event decides the result: an **Eclipse** rolls Solar Blaze (the Fan of the Eclipse rod gives the best odds; the Wicked Fang Rod carries a 30% chance), a **Starfall** rolls the Nova mutation. Either one satisfies the quest, so fish whichever window arrives instead of waiting for a specific one.

One trap worth spelling out: don't burn an Eclipse chasing Solar Blaze when a Starfall is forecast later that evening. Both fish complete the objective, and the Eclipse hours are the ones you also need for quest two's Stalactites. Read the event order like a schedule — most lobbies cycle the two events on a visible timer — and spend each window where its fish is hardest to replace.

Turn in the three items. A cutscene plays and meteors rain across Northern Expedition. Quest two starts immediately — if you haven't prepped, that cutscene is your warning shot.

If Matthew won't accept the hand-in, the usual cause is a stacked objective: players hand in the Cosmic Relic and Moonstone but the third fish is the *wrong Solar Blaze*. Solar Blaze Stalactite is a different fish from plain Solar Blaze and does not count here. Check the item name in your bag against the quest text before you assume the NPC is broken.

## Quest 2 — Anomaly Investigation: METEORS GALORE

Four objectives. This is where the grind happens, and two objectives are locked to events. Work them in this order.

**1. Break 10 Fallen Meteors.** **Equip the Pickaxe first** — bare hands do nothing, and this is the quest's dumbest failure. The crash sites glow with a blue-star light and sit where the cutscene meteors landed. Start at the top of the island and work downward so you don't walk past a cleared site and double-count.

**2. Quartz Glass (x1).** Exotic fish, hidden location. In the **Cryogenic Canal**, go under the bridge and enter the dark portal at the bottom — that's the Stone Cavern. Use **Magnet Bait** and fish in **Foggy weather**. Clear weather gives you nothing, so don't camp the cavern on a bright day and call it unlucky.

**3. Solar Blaze Stalactite (x5).** **Wicked Fang Rod + Garbage Bait, active Eclipse, Crimson Cave.** Tidefall also works, but Crimson Cave is the standard grind. Five catches in one window is tight, so have the bait stacked and the rod in hand before the sky changes. The [rod and bait combination guide](/posts/fisch/rod-bait-combination-guide/) covers stocking Garbage Bait cheaply.

**4. Gemstones — Ruby, Amethyst, Opal, Lapis Lazuli, Moonstone (one each).** All five drop from **Meteor Totem** events on Ancient Isle, and the aquarium generates them passively. Treat Moonstone as the bottleneck. Note that the Moonstone from quest 1 does not double-count — you need another.

Two practical notes from watching this quest get finished. Other players on the same server are breaking the same Fallen Meteors, so a busy lobby can leave you circling for the last two sites — a quieter server is faster than a second lap. And Quartz Glass does not have to be caught after the other objectives; any time fog is up, detour and clear it. This quest is best worked as a checklist you carry for a few days, not a speedrun.

Hand it all over. Rod acquired.

## The Meteor-Dodge Passive — A Mechanical Breakdown

The stats are printed above. The passive is the part no table explains, and it is the entire reason to grind this rod.

Every **4 to 6.5 seconds**, a meteor drops onto your fishing minigame — up to five per catch, each covering roughly **13% of the bar width**. Dodge out of its column: **+10% progress**. Stay in its way: **−15% progress**.

Do the math for a full five-meteor catch. Five clean dodges are **+50%**, which shortens the catch hard. Five hits are **−75%**, which can turn a normal fish into a line-breaking slog. The asymmetry is deliberate: the rod rewards calm more than speed.

Three mechanics decide how well you actually do:

**Read the shadow, not the meteor.** The ground shadow moves before the rock does. Track the shadow's column and move on it.

**Settle back to centre after each dodge.** Players who dodge and stay parked on an edge get clipped by the next drop because they had nowhere left to move. Centre reset every time.

**Don't dodge your own catch rhythm.** The minigame still asks you to hold the bar. Players who chase every meteor lose the fish underneath. The meteor is worth ten points; the fish is the whole bar. Fish first, dodge in the gaps.

Here is what a good catch feels like in practice. First meteor at four seconds: shadow starts left of centre, you slide right, +10, slide back. Second at eleven seconds: you're already near the bar edge, so you hold position and let it miss you by the gap — a dodge doesn't require a full screen-width move, just leaving the column. Third at sixteen: +10. Fourth and fifth follow the same reset rhythm, and the bar closes maybe twenty percent faster than a normal catch. A bad catch is recognisable too: the first rock clips you, you panic-slide for the second and overrun the bar's safe zone, the third clips you while you're recovering, and what should have been a quick fish becomes a two-minute rescue job. The passive doesn't reward reaction speed. It rewards not panicking.

## Who Should Skip the Meteoric Rod

It is a great rod for the player it's built for. It is also genuinely the wrong pick for three kinds of fisher, and nobody's guide ever says this out loud.

**The half-attention fisher.** If you cast while watching something on the second monitor, the −15% hits will land without you noticing and you will earn less than with a rod that ignores you. This rod needs eyes on the bar.

**The cramped-screen mobile fisher.** The dodge asks you to read a moving shadow and slide within a 13% column. On a small screen in a bumpy environment that is a real handicap — players who master it exist, but the learning curve costs more fish than on desktop.

**The single-target endgame grinder.** If your whole goal right now is one specific bestiary entry and you need consistency over flair, a familiar rod whose rhythm you already know beats a new passive you're still learning. Grind the Meteoric Rod later when the novelty isn't competing with an objective. Come back to it from the [Fisch hub](/games/fisch/) once your other goals are banked.

If none of those descriptions fits you, the quest is worth the week of checklist work.

## 5 Counter-Intuitive Truths

### 1. The Aquarium Beats Totem Gambling

Your first instinct for the gemstones: burn Meteor Totems on Ancient Isle until all five drop. Moonstone says no. The zero-RNG route — aquarium plus Daily Shop, checked daily — fills four of the five for free while you do other things. Totems are the top-up for the last gap, not the engine.

### 2. Dodging Matters More Than the Cragged Roll

The 7x mutation is what gets screenshotted. It rolls on 20% of catches and you cannot influence it. The dodge passive happens on **every** catch and swings the catch by up to 50%. Over a fishing session, the skill you control earns more than the roll you don't.

### 3. Prepare During the Wrong Weather

Most players start preparing when the Eclipse banner appears — buying bait, finding the cave, equipping the rod. The window is already burning. The efficient players stock Garbage Bait and camp the cave entrance *before* the event, then spend the whole window fishing. Eclipse prep happens in clear skies.

### 4. "Rare" Here Mostly Means Patient

The quest feels RNG-heavy. Look closer: ten meteors are guaranteed work, five Stalactites are a fixed event window, the Cosmic Relic is one totem with a visible beam. Only Quartz Glass and Moonstone genuinely roll. Most of what stalls players is missed conditions, not bad luck.

### 5. A 175%-Luck Rod Can Lose You Money

If you eat meteors consistently, the −75% progress swings lengthen catches and snap big fish off — the 550,000 Kg cap doesn't help a line you're not managing. A player who can't read the drops earns less per hour with this rod than with a boring stat stick. Learn the passive in shallow water before you fish value with it.

## Failure Retrospective: Five Ways the Quest Breaks

**Failure 1: Clicking meteors bare-handed.** "Break 10 Fallen Meteors," so they run out clicking. No Pickaxe equipped, nothing breaks, and they think the quest is bugged. Equip first, then walk.

**Failure 2: Fishing Overgrowth Canal during the Eclipse.** They need five Stalactites, read "Solar Blaze," and camp the quest-1 canal. Stalactite is the Crimson Cave fish. The Eclipse window closes empty. Pull the objective list up and match fish to water *before* the event.

**Failure 3: Totems fired from the low ground.** They trigger the shower on the beach, the meteor lands behind a ridge, no beam is visible, totem wasted. Fire from high open ground with the sky in view.

**Failure 4: Camping the Stone Cavern in clear weather.** Quartz Glass needs Fog; the portal is easy to find once and players assume the fish is the grind. It isn't — the weather is. Leave and come back when it's foggy.

**Failure 5: Starting cold and blaming the RNG.** No Wicked Fang, no bait stock, no totems, Matthew already talked to. Every event window that week goes to prerequisites instead of objectives. The quest didn't get unlucky; it got started without a bag. The tell that you're in this failure is queueing at a bait shop while the Eclipse timer sits on screen — that is the exact moment the evening stops being an objective run and starts being an errand run.

The retrospective lesson across all five: not one of these deaths is caused by a tough fish. Every one is a preparation or information error, which means every one is avoidable before you ever climb to Matthew's platform.

## Methodology: The Minimum-Session Route

This is the order that finishes the line in the least real time:

**Session zero — prep, any day.** Confirm the Wicked Fang and Cerulean Fang rods. Buy Garbage Bait and Magnet Bait — Garbage Bait in stacks of twenty-plus, since five catches in one window can eat a dozen casts with misses. Keep at least one Starfall Totem and a Pickaxe. Check the aquarium and Daily Shop and bank any gemstones already there. One last check: confirm you have bag space. Nothing is worse than landing the Cosmic Relic beam and being too full to take it.

**Day one — first event window.** Whichever event opens, fish it: Eclipse in Crimson Cave for Stalactites (quest 2) or Solar Blaze (quest 1); Starfall in Overgrowth for the Nova Walnut. Never let an event pass empty.

**Day one, after the event — chores.** Talk to Matthew, do SPACE CANDY's totem reload from high ground, break the ten meteors with the Pickaxe equipped, and collect whatever gems the aquarium has produced.

**Fog window.** When fog is forecast, take the portal under the Cryogenic Canal bridge and fish Quartz Glass with Magnet Bait.

**Final session — fill and turn in.** Top up the missing gemstones with totems, hand both quests in, then take the rod to shallow water and learn the drop timing before fishing anything valuable.

The whole trick is event hygiene: prepare before, fish the right water during, and never burn a window on a prerequisite.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I get the Meteoric Rod in Fisch?**

Finish both Anomaly Investigation quests from Meteor Maniac Matthew at Northern Expedition. SPACE CANDY asks for a Cosmic Relic, a Moonstone, and either Solar Blaze or a Nova Frozen Walnut. METEORS GALORE needs 10 broken Fallen Meteors, one Quartz Glass, 5 Solar Blaze Stalactite, and one each of Ruby, Amethyst, Opal, Lapis Lazuli, and Moonstone. No shop price, no code.

**Where is Meteor Maniac Matthew?**

Northern Expedition, top tier of the three-tier platform behind the camp selling the Avalanche Rod and Pickaxe. Jump the platforms. He also goes by Astral Matthew and only exists on servers running the Astral Observatory update. Empty platform means old server — rejoin.

**How do I get a Cosmic Relic?**

Use a Starfall Totem from high open ground, watch for the pink beam at the impact point, and sprint to it. Each totem is one shower. If the meteor lands out of sight, that totem is spent — fire the next from somewhere you can see the sky.

**How do I catch Solar Blaze Stalactite?**

Wicked Fang Rod, Garbage Bait, active Eclipse, Crimson Cave (Tidefall also works). You need five. Stack the bait before the event so the whole window goes to fishing. Outside an Eclipse the mutation won't roll at all.

**Is the Meteoric Rod worth it?**

For late-game players who want an active minigame, yes: 75% Lure Speed, 175% Luck, 550,000 Kg, 20% Cragged at 7x, and the dodge skill check. If you want passive fishing, pick something off the [best rods tier list](/posts/fisch/best-rods-tier-list/) that doesn't punish inattention.

**What should I have before starting?**

Wicked Fang Rod, Pickaxe, Garbage Bait stock, at least one Starfall Totem, and ideally the Cerulean Fang Rod. Missing these is what turns an evening quest into a week of chasing prerequisites through event windows.

**How long does the quest take?**

Prepared: one good evening, assuming an event window cooperates. Cold: two to four days of real time, because Eclipse and Starfall don't run on demand and Moonstone can stall on aquarium and shop timing.
