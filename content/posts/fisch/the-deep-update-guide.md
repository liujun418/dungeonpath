## What The Deep Update Actually Adds

The Deep (v1.96.0) dropped on August 8, 2026 at 16:30 UTC. It is not a balance patch or a QoL tweak — it is a full biome expansion set inside a massive underwater trench. Three new zones, four new rods, one hunt boss, sixty-plus fish, and a piece of gear that changes how you move underwater.

This is not the Desolate Deep. That area launched back in July and lives in a different part of the map. The Deep is a separate trench biome with its own NPCs, its own progression, and its own endgame target.

Before you do anything else, grab the code `TheDeepIsVeryDeep`. It expires August 9, so if you are reading this on August 12, that ship has sailed — but `CARBON` and `SCARLET` still work at the time of writing.

Let's walk through the whole thing in the order you will actually encounter it.

---

## Your First Descent: Getting the Deep Survey Device

**The scene:** You swim out from Moosewood toward the open ocean, following coordinates you saw on Discord. The water darkens. You hit -194, 130, -4669 and spot an NPC standing on the seabed. That NPC hands you the Deep Survey Device. No cost, no quest prerequisite. Just claim it.

The Deep Survey Device does two things that matter. First, it lets you walk on the ocean floor instead of swimming — your movement becomes ground-based. Second, and more important, it lets you cast your fishing rod while submerged. Without this device equipped, you cannot fish inside The Deep at all. The game treats underwater casts differently from surface casts, and the device is the gate.

Equip it from your inventory immediately. Do not store it or bank it. If you swim into The Deep's trench without it, you will bob around unable to cast, wondering why everything is broken. This is the single most common mistake people made in the first 24 hours of the update.

**Recovery if you lose it:** The NPC at the same coordinates will reissue it. It is not a one-time item.

## The Deep Zone Breakdown

The trench has three layers. You move through them in linear order — Outer Deep first, then Gloomy Crevice, then Lower Deep. Each one gates the next.

### Outer Deep

This is the entry zone. It looks like a half-lit submerged city — broken pillars, scattered debris, a few functional beacons. The ambient light is low but not pitch black. You can navigate by the beacon lights.

Dr. Vane waits here, near the 1P-3 beacon. This is where you pick up your first new rod questline. The fish in Outer Deep are mid-tier — nothing that tests an endgame rod, but enough to generate some cash while you work through Dr. Vane's tasks.

Fish here until you have at least 50,000 C$ in the bank. You will need it.

### Gloomy Crevice

Deeper and darker. The crevice is a narrow canyon with vertical rock walls on both sides. Visibility drops significantly. You will rely on the Survey Device's floor-walking to avoid getting turned around.

This zone matters for one reason: the collapsed pillars. You need to survey them as part of the MK II upgrade for your Deep Survey Device — and that upgrade is mandatory before you can face the Monstrous Cusk. We will cover that sequence in the Cusk section below.

The fish here start hitting harder. If you are still using a mid-tier rod, you will feel the gap.

### Lower Deep

The bottom layer. Near-total darkness, high-value fish, and the Fester of the Deep NPC who rewards the Scalding Hook. This is also where the Monstrous Cusk hunt triggers once you have met all the prerequisites.

The Lower Deep is not a place you visit once. You will be back here repeatedly — for the Scalding Hook quest, for Cusk hunt attempts, and for material farming.

## The Four New Rods: What They Do and When They Matter

The update added four rods. Two come from quests, two from crafting at the Ancient Archives. Here is the full picture.

| Rod | Source | Lure | Luck | Control | Resilience | Max Weight | Key Passive | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Luminous Rod | Dr. Vane quest (Outer Deep) | 70% | 95% | 0.1 | 25% | 60,000kg | 10% Bioluminescent (4x), 30% Luminous (9x), +10% Progress Speed | Free (quest) |
| Scalding Hook | Fester quest (Lower Deep) | 82% | 180% | 0.12 | 30% | 400,000kg | Scalded mutation (ramping progress speed), hooks ghost-type fish | Free (quest) |
| Titanium Rod | Ancient Archives craft | 100% | 180% | 0.3 | 75% | Infinite | 20% Titanium mutation, auto-slash (35% chance, 5% progress + 0.15s stun every 0.5s), +30% Progress Speed | 200,000 C$ + materials |
| Cusk Purger | Ancient Archives craft | 88% | 225% | -0.05 | 25% | Infinite | Vacuum (pulls deep-sea monsters to line), Monstrous mutation (9x), +40% Progress Speed, **+7 Disturbance** | 750,000 C$ + materials |

### Luminous Rod — Your First Pickup

**The scene:** You find Dr. Vane in Outer Deep, standing near the 1P-3 beacon. She gives you a four-part task line: collect Bone Shards, collect Bio-Fluid, catch specific mutated fish, and return with proof. Each part unlocks the next. At the end, she hands you the Luminous Rod.

This rod is not an endgame weapon. Lure 70% is fine, Luck 95% is fine, max weight 60,000kg will get overwhelmed by anything serious. But its mutation passives are what pay off early — Bioluminescent at 10% gives you a 4x value multiplier, and Luminous at 30% gives you a staggering 9x. Combined with the flat +10% Progress Speed, you will clear fish faster and sell for more while you save up for Titanium Rod materials.

**Do not skip this rod.** If you try to go straight for Titanium Rod by grinding Outer Deep with whatever rod you brought in, you will take three times as long. The Luminous Rod pays for itself in sell value within your first hour of using it.

### Scalding Hook — Niche but Useful

The Fester of the Deep NPC in Lower Deep rewards this after a questline. Its main draw is the Scalded mutation — your progress speed ramps up the longer you fight a fish, which makes it strong against high-resilience deep-sea catches. It also hooks ghost-type fish that other rods cannot target.

That said, at Lure 82% and Control 0.12, it is not your daily driver. Treat it as a specialist tool: pull it out when you need ghost-type fish for a collection or when a particular Lower Deep fish has resilience numbers that other rods struggle against.

### Titanium Rod — The Workhorse

This is the rod most players will spend the most time using. Max weight Infinite means no fish shrugs it off. Lure 100%, Luck 180%, and Control 0.3 are all solid — nothing flashy, nothing weak. The 20% Titanium mutation (9x value) keeps your income healthy.

The standout passive is auto-slash: every 0.5 seconds, there is a 35% chance to deal 5% progress damage and stun the fish for 0.15 seconds. This adds up fast. Against a stubborn catch that normally takes 30 seconds, auto-slash can shave off 6-8 seconds without you doing anything extra.

**Crafting cost:** 200,000 C$ plus a Titanium Shaft, Titanium Reel, and five Refined Scrap. The Shaft and Reel come from Sledge's and Forge Hand Marcus's questlines. Refined Scrap drops from breaking down junk items or buying from certain NPCs. This is the bottleneck — five Refined Scrap is not a quick farm.

### Cusk Purger — The Hunt Specialist

On paper, the Titanium Rod looks like the best rod in the update. Infinite max weight, auto-slash, great all-around stats. Why would you spend 750,000 C$ and a long list of rare materials on the Cusk Purger?

Because of one stat: **+7 Disturbance.** That is the highest Disturbance value on any rod in Fisch. Disturbance increases the spawn rate of hunt-class fish and boss encounters. If you are farming the Monstrous Cusk, you want this rod. Without it, your Cusk encounter rate is painfully low — you might spend an hour fishing the Lower Deep with a Titanium Rod and see the Cusk once. With the Cusk Purger, it shows up reliably.

The Vacuum passive also pulls deep-sea monsters toward your line, which matters when you are trying to bait a specific hunt target. The Monstrous mutation at 9x value is a nice bonus, but it is not why you build this rod.

**The full crafting cost:** 750,000 C$ + 3 Monstrous Cusk Tooth + 1-3 Line of the Deep + 3 Refined Scrap + 3 Hardened Chitin + 3 Abyssal Bio-Fluid + 1-3 Radiant Prism Scale + 3 Ancient Bone. That is a multi-hour material grind. Do not start it until you have already completed Chief Engineer Nereus's main questline, upgraded your Deep Survey Device to MK II, and surveyed the collapsed pillars in Gloomy Crevice. You need all three prerequisites before the materials even become available.

## The Rod Route: What Order to Build In

**Decision framework — your fastest path through the four rods:**

| Step | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Get Luminous Rod (Dr. Vane questline) | Free, unlocks 9x value mutations, funds everything else |
| 2 | Grind Outer Deep + Gloomy Crevice with Luminous Rod | Build 200,000 C$ + gather Refined Scrap |
| 3 | Complete Sledge's + Forge Hand Marcus's questlines | Unlock Titanium Shaft and Titanium Reel |
| 4 | Craft Titanium Rod at Ancient Archives | Your all-purpose rod for the rest of the update |
| 5 | Complete Chief Engineer Nereus main questline | Unlocks Cusk Purger crafting prerequisites |
| 6 | Upgrade Deep Survey Device to MK II, survey Gloomy Crevice pillars | Mandatory for Cusk access |
| 7 | Craft Cusk Purger | Hunt specialist for Monstrous Cusk |
| 8 | (Optional) Get Scalding Hook from Fester in Lower Deep | Only if you need ghost-type fish |

**Why this order and not a different one?** The Luminous Rod's mutation income is the fuel that makes steps 2-4 bearable. If you skip it and grind 200,000 C$ with a pre-update rod, you are leaving money on the table with every cast. The Titanium Rod then becomes your universal tool — it handles everything from Outer Deep trash fish to Lower Deep heavy catches. The Cusk Purger is last because it is a specialist item and its cost is absurd without the income from the Titanium Rod first.

The Scalding Hook is deliberately at the bottom. Unless ghost-type fish are blocking a specific goal, you can skip it entirely and lose nothing.

## The Monstrous Cusk Hunt

The Monstrous Cusk is the update's boss hunt. It spawns in the Lower Deep and hits hard — this is not a fish you reel in with a Luminous Rod.

### Prerequisites (Do Not Skip Any)

1. Complete Chief Engineer Nereus's main questline. This is a multi-step chain that runs through all three Deep zones.
2. Upgrade your Deep Survey Device to MK II. The standard device cannot detect Cusk spawns.
3. Survey the collapsed pillars in Gloomy Crevice. There are several of them. The game marks them on your map once the MK II upgrade is active.
4. Have a rod that can handle it. Titanium Rod works. Cusk Purger is better.

### The Fight Itself

When the Cusk spawns, the screen dims further and a distinct audio cue plays — a low rumble. The fish has a massive health bar and high resilience. Your rod's Control and Resilience stats matter here, not just max weight.

**Common failure pattern:** Players show up with a high-Lure rod but low Resilience and get snapped off because they cannot absorb the Cusk's thrash cycles. The Titanium Rod's 75% Resilience is comfortable here. The Cusk Purger's 25% Resilience requires more active management — you need to react to the thrash windows.

The Cusk drops Monstrous Cusk Teeth, which are crafting materials for the Cusk Purger itself. Yes, you need to beat the Cusk with a non-Cusk rod to get the materials to craft the Cusk rod that makes farming the Cusk easier. This is intentional. The intended loop is: beat the Cusk once or twice with your Titanium Rod, collect enough teeth, then craft the Cusk Purger for faster subsequent kills.

## New Mutations: What Procs and What to Farm

The update introduces several new mutations. Here is what you need to know for farming purposes:

- **Bioluminescent (4x value):** Exclusive to the Luminous Rod, 10% proc rate. Good early income, outclassed later.
- **Luminous (9x value):** Also Luminous Rod, 30% proc rate. This is the best early-game value mutation in the update. If you are grinding cash in Outer Deep or Gloomy Crevice, the Luminous Rod pays out fast.
- **Titanium (9x value):** Titanium Rod exclusive, 20% proc rate. Slightly lower rate than Luminous but on a rod with higher Luck, so the effective income is similar or better.
- **Refracted:** Triggered when you hit a fish with the Sightless Oracle harpoon gun — 50% proc rate. Applies on harpoon hits, not rod casts.
- **Rusty:** Scrap-Cannon harpoon gun, 30% proc rate. Same deal — harpoon-triggered, not rod-based.
- **Monstrous (9x value):** Cusk Purger exclusive. High value but unreliable as a farming strategy because the Cusk Purger's real job is hunt spawns, not cash grinding.

**Farming priority:** If you are still building up cash, the Luminous Rod's 30% Luminous mutation is your best friend. Once you have the Titanium Rod, switch to it — the higher Luck stat means more total mutated fish even if the individual proc rate is lower.

## Harpoon Guns and Other New Gear

Harpoon guns are a new equipment category. They can be baited, which means you can stack bait effects with harpoon effects. The Sightless Oracle applies Refracted on 50% of hits, and the Scrap-Cannon applies Rusty on 30%.

One important interaction: harpoon guns do not replace your fishing rod. You equip them in a separate slot. You can harpoon a fish to apply a mutation, then reel it in with your rod and potentially stack a second rod-based mutation on top. This is not always practical in combat, but against high-value stationary targets, it adds up.

The update also adds sixty-plus new fish species spread across the three Deep zones. A full species list is beyond the scope of this guide, but the important distinction is that not all new fish are catchable in every zone. Outer Deep has the shallowest pool. Lower Deep has the rarest spawns. If you are hunting a specific species for a quest or collection, [check our mutations and bait guide](/posts/fisch/mutations-bait-guide/) for spawn-condition details.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

### Mistake 1: Swimming into The Deep without the Survey Device

You dive into the trench, try to cast, and nothing happens. You cannot fish. You cannot interact with NPCs properly. You swim back up, confused.

**Fix:** Go to coordinates -194, 130, -4669, talk to the NPC, claim the device, equip it. Done in 60 seconds. This mistake costs you time, not resources.

### Mistake 2: Grinding Titanium Rod Materials Before Finishing the Luminous Rod Questline

You decide to skip Dr. Vane's quest because 200,000 C$ for the Titanium Rod feels achievable with your current rod. You spend hours farming Outer Deep, earning maybe 30-40% of what you would earn if you had the Luminous Rod's mutations running.

**Fix:** The Luminous Rod questline takes maybe 45-60 minutes if you focus on it. The income difference makes that time back within your first two hours of post-quest fishing. The math is not close. Get the Luminous Rod first.

### Mistake 3: Forgetting to Upgrade the Deep Survey Device Before Hunting the Cusk

You finish Nereus's questline, swim to Lower Deep, and fish for two hours without a single Cusk spawn. You assume the spawn rate is just low.

**Fix:** The MK II upgrade and the collapsed pillar survey are hard gates — the Cusk literally cannot spawn until both are done. Check your Survey Device status. If it says MK I, go back to the upgrade NPC. If it says MK II but you have not surveyed the pillars, open your map and look for the marked locations in Gloomy Crevice.

### Mistake 4: Overestimating the Cusk Purger's General Usefulness

You spend 750,000 C$ and hours of material farming to craft the Cusk Purger, then equip it as your main rod for all content. Its -0.05 Control makes small fish annoying to reel in, and its +7 Disturbance is wasted on non-hunt targets.

**Fix:** The Cusk Purger is a hunt rod. Use the Titanium Rod for everything else. Swap to the Cusk Purger only when you are actively farming the Monstrous Cusk or other hunt-class spawns in Lower Deep. For general advice on matching your rod to the situation, [see our rods tier list](/posts/fisch/best-rods-tier-list/).

## What Did Not Change

The update adds content. It does not rebalance existing rods. Your pre-1.96 rods have the same stats they had before. If you have a rod you already like, it still works everywhere it worked before. The only reason to switch is if one of the new rods does something your current rod cannot — infinite max weight on the Titanium Rod, hunt spawns on the Cusk Purger, or the Luminous Rod's mutation suite for cash farming.

[Our enchantment timing guide](/posts/fisch/enchantment-timing-guide/) still applies to all four new rods. Do not enchant them blind — plan your enchants around the rod's role, not its stats on paper.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I get the Deep Survey Device and actually enter The Deep?

Swim to the NPC at coordinates -194, 130, -4669 and claim the Deep Survey Device for free. Equip it to walk on the seafloor and cast your rod underwater. Without it, you cannot access any of The Deep's three zones — Outer Deep, Gloomy Crevice, or Lower Deep.

### Which of the four new rods should I build first?

Start with the Luminous Rod — it is a free quest reward from Dr. Vane in Outer Deep (near the 1P-3 beacon) after completing a four-part task line. Its Bioluminescent (4x value) and Luminous (9x value) mutations will fund your Titanium Rod materials. Skip the Scalding Hook unless you specifically need to catch ghost-type fish.

### How do I defeat the Monstrous Cusk hunt boss?

You need the Deep Survey Device upgraded to MK II, must have surveyed the collapsed pillars in Gloomy Crevice, and must complete Chief Engineer Nereus's main questline. The Cusk Purger rod — crafted at the Ancient Archives for 750,000 C$ plus rare materials — is the only rod with +7 Disturbance, which dramatically increases hunt fish spawns. Without it, the Cusk encounter rate is punishingly low.
