You just bought the Steady Rod for 25,000 C$. You have 15,000 C$ left. The enchantment altar is right there. You spend 8,000 C$ rolling enchants. You land on Lucky — +10% Luck. Your Steady Rod now has 25% effective Luck instead of 15%. You feel accomplished. Then you check the shop. The Mythical Rod costs 110,000 C$. Your enchant added roughly 150 C$ per hour to your fishing. It will take 53 hours for that enchant to pay for itself. The enchant was not an investment. It was a delay.

This guide covers the optimal enchantment timing for every rod tier in Fisch — updated for The Deep Update (v1.96) with its new mutations, new rods, and new economy. You will learn when to enchant, when to reroll, and when saving your relics for the next rod is the smarter play.

## The Real Economics of Enchantments

Enchantments are percentage multipliers on your rod's base stats. A +15% Luck enchant on a rod with 0% base Luck adds 15% effective Luck. The same enchant on a rod with 45% base Luck pushes you to 60%. The enchant relic is identical. The rod is different. The value scales with the rod it sits on.

But there is a second multiplier most players ignore: **mutations**. In The Deep Update, certain rods come with built-in mutation chances that multiply fish sale value by 4×, 9×, or more. A Luck enchant on a rod that frequently produces 9× value Luminous fish hits your C$ per hour far harder than the same enchant on a rod that never mutates. Enchantment value is no longer just about base Luck — it is about **Luck × mutation probability × mutation multiplier**.

Here is the break-even math, updated for current rod prices and typical hourly catch rates:

| Rod | Base Luck | Enchant Cost (Avg) | Extra C$/Hour | Break-Even (Hours) | Worth Enchanting? |
|-----|-----------|-------------------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Flimsy Rod | 0% | 5,000 C$ | ~40 C$/hr | 125+ hrs | No |
| Carbon Rod | 10% | 5,000 C$ | ~70 C$/hr | 71 hrs | No |
| Steady Rod | 15% | 6,000 C$ | ~150 C$/hr | 40 hrs | One roll, accept result |
| Mythical Rod | 25% | 8,000 C$ | ~200 C$/hr | 40 hrs | Yes, budget 30k-50k |
| Luminous Rod | 95% | 10,000 C$ | ~400 C$/hr | 25 hrs | Yes, strong case |
| Titanium Rod | 180% | 12,000 C$ | ~600 C$/hr | 20 hrs | Yes, invest heavily |
| Destiny Rod | 45% | 10,000 C$ | ~250 C$/hr | 40 hrs | Max investment |
| Cusk Purger | 225% | 15,000 C$ | ~700 C$/hr | 21 hrs | Yes, but need Controlled |

## Rod-by-Rod Enchantment Timing

### Flimsy Rod and Carbon Rod: Never Enchant

You start the game with the Flimsy Rod. You fish for 20 minutes. You hit Level 10 and unlock the Carbon Rod at Moosewood for 2,000 C$. You replace the Flimsy within 2 hours. The Carbon Rod carries you to Level 40 and the Steady Rod at 25,000 C$. Total time on these two rods combined: roughly 10 to 12 hours.

Enchanting either rod is lighting relics on fire. A +10% Luck enchant on Flimsy (0% base) adds almost nothing. On Carbon (10% base) you gain maybe 1 extra rare fish per hour. Neither pays for itself before you outgrow the rod. Save every relic. Save every C$. Your only job at this stage is reaching the Steady Rod.

### Steady Rod: One Roll, Accept the Result

You are Level 40. You just bought the Steady Rod at Moosewood. This rod carries you from Level 40 to roughly Level 100 — call it 30 to 40 hours of fishing. At 15% base Luck, an enchant finally matters.

Here is the decision: budget **one roll session**. Spend 5,000 to 8,000 C$ on enchant relics. Roll until you hit any positive Luck enchant — Lucky (+10%), Divine (+20% Luck, -0.05 Control), or even Breezed (+15% Lure Speed). Take the first one you get. Do not reroll. Do not chase Divine. Every C$ you burn chasing a perfect roll on the Steady Rod is C$ you are not putting toward the Mythical Rod at 110,000 C$.

If you roll Unbreakable or Controlled on your first try, keep them. Unbreakable saves you bait loss and Controlled improves your hook accuracy. Those are not wasted enchants — they just pay out differently than straight Luck.

### Mythical Rod: Budget 30,000 to 50,000 C$

You hit Level 100. You buy the Mythical Rod. You now have 25% base Luck and you will use this rod for 80-plus hours while you grind toward Destiny Rod or one of The Deep's new rods.

Budget 30,000 to 50,000 C$ for enchants on this rod. Roll until you hit Divine or Lucky — stop at whichever comes first. Divine is the dream at +20% Luck with a minor Control penalty, but Lucky at +10% Luck pure is perfectly serviceable. The difference between Divine and Lucky on the Mythical Rod over 80 hours is maybe 8,000 to 10,000 C$ — not worth the relic burn of an endless reroll session.

A common mistake: players get Lucky on roll three, say "I can do better," keep rolling, burn through 50,000 C$, and land on Mutated or Sea King. Stop at Lucky. That extra 10% Luck from Divine is not worth the risk of ending up with something worse.

### Destiny Rod and Beyond: Max Investment

Destiny Rod at 45% base Luck is a 200-hour rod. If you get here before acquiring a Deep Update rod, invest everything. Roll Divine. Roll until you hit it. At this Luck value, every percentage point matters. A +20% Divine enchant on 45% base gives you 65% effective Luck — the difference between 45% and 65% over 200 hours is tens of thousands of C$.

## The Deep Update — How New Mutations Change Enchantment Timing

The Deep Update (v1.96) introduced six new mutations. Two of them are game-changers for enchantment math because they multiply fish sale value by 4× and 9× respectively. Here is what you need to know.

### Bioluminescent and Luminous (Luminous Rod Mutations)

The Luminous Rod has two built-in mutation chances: **Bioluminescent** at 10% probability (4× fish sale value) and **Luminous** at 30% probability (9× fish sale value). Together, 40% of your catches on this rod get a value multiplier — and Luminous at 9× is the single highest-value mutation in the game.

Why this changes enchantment timing: every point of Luck you enchant onto a Luminous Rod gets multiplied by those mutation payouts. Catch a rare fish worth 2,000 C$, trigger Luminous (9×), and that fish sells for 18,000 C$. Now add +15% Luck from an enchant — you catch more rare fish, more of them trigger Luminous, your C$ per hour explodes.

The Luminous Rod sits at 95% base Luck. That is already higher than Destiny Rod's 45%. An enchanted Luminous Rod at 95% base Luck with 40% mutation rate generates more C$ per hour than an enchanted Destiny Rod at 60% Luck with no built-in mutations. The break-even on enchanting a Luminous Rod is only 25 hours — one of the fastest payoffs in the game.

### Titanium Mutation (Titanium Rod)

The Titanium Rod has a 20% chance to produce the **Titanium** mutation. While the exact value multiplier for Titanium is still being tested by the community, early data suggests it is comparable to or exceeds the Hexed mutation (1.5×). Combined with the Titanium Rod's 180% base Luck, you are looking at a rod that generates high-value mutated fish on one in every five catches.

Enchant this rod. Budget at least 50,000 C$ for rolls. At 180% base Luck, a Divine enchant pushes you past 200% effective Luck, which means rare and mythic fish become your baseline catch rate. Twenty percent of those already-juiced catches get the Titanium multiplier on top.

### Refracted and Rusty (Harpoon Mutations)

Two new mutations come from harpoon mechanics rather than rod fishing: **Refracted** (50% chance when the Sightless Oracle harpoon hits) and **Rusty** (30% chance from the Scrap-Cannon harpoon). These do not directly affect rod enchantment decisions — you cannot enchant a harpoon — but they change the overall economy. If you are farming harpoon-exclusive fish with Refracted or Rusty multipliers, your income is less dependent on your rod's enchantment. This frees up relics for your next rod upgrade rather than rerolls.

**Entrenched** is a third new mutation whose exact trigger conditions and multiplier are still unconfirmed as of v1.96. Preliminary testing suggests it may be tied to deep-layer fishing zones. Treat it as a bonus multiplier when it hits — do not plan your enchantment strategy around it until the numbers are confirmed.

## New Rods from The Deep — Enchant or Skip?

### Luminous Rod (95% Luck, Built-in Bioluminescent and Luminous)

You get the Luminous Rod somewhere around Level 150-200, depending on your grinding route. Its 95% Luck base stat is absurd for its acquisition stage. The 40% combined mutation rate makes it even better.

**Should you enchant it?** Yes — but with a caveat. You will eventually replace the Luminous Rod with either Titanium Rod (180% Luck) or Cusk Purger (225% Luck). So enchant it once, budget roughly 10,000 to 15,000 C$, and take the first of Divine, Lucky, or Breezed. Do not chase a specific enchant. Treat it the same way you treated the Steady Rod — one session, first good roll, keep moving.

**Why this is counterintuitive:** The Luminous Rod is not an endgame rod, yet it may be the most enchantment-efficient rod in the game. Its 95% base Luck plus mutation multiplier means a single Lucky enchant pays for itself in under 25 hours. Most players skip enchanting "mid-game" rods. The Luminous Rod is the exception. Enchanting it earns back its cost so fast that skipping it actually slows your progression to Titanium or Cusk Purger.

### Titanium Rod (180% Luck, 20% Titanium Mutation)

This is the rod where you go all-in. At 180% base Luck with a 20% built-in mutation chance, enchanting is not optional — it is the difference between "good income" and "I can buy anything in the game."

Budget heavily: 50,000 C$ minimum for enchant relics. Roll until you hit Divine. If you land on Lucky first, consider whether to keep rolling — at 180% base Luck, the jump from 190% (Lucky) to 200% (Divine) is proportionally smaller than on lower-Luck rods. You can stop at Lucky here and still dominate. But if you have the relics and the patience, Divine pushes you into territory where every cast has a meaningful chance at a mythic fish, and one in five of those gets the Titanium multiplier.

### Cusk Purger (225% Luck, +7 Disturbance, Control -0.05)

Cusk Purger is The Deep Update's ultimate rod — 225% base Luck. But it comes with a catch: **+7 Disturbance**. High Disturbance reduces the spawn rate of rare and mythic fish. You can have 250% Luck and still catch garbage if Disturbance is scaring away the good spawns.

This creates the most interesting enchantment dilemma in the game right now. Do you enchant for more Luck (Divine, +20%) or do you enchant for Control (Controlled, -0.05 Disturbance) to offset the rod's built-in penalty?

**The answer:** roll for Controlled first, then consider whether to reroll for Divine later when you have surplus relics. A Cusk Purger with Controlled at 225% Luck and neutral Disturbance catches better fish than a Cusk Purger with Divine at 245% Luck but +7 Disturbance. Disturbance is a silent killer — you cannot see the fish it scares away, so players blame "bad RNG" when the rod itself is the problem.

If you land on Divine first, keep it and test your catch rates for 5 to 10 hours. If you notice your rare fish rate is lower than expected (fewer mythics per hour than your Titanium Rod), save up and reroll for Controlled. The data will tell you what your gut cannot.

## Three Common Enchantment Failures (and How to Recover)

### Failure 1: Enchanting Before Checking Mutation Synergy

You unlock the Mythical Rod at Level 100. You immediately spend 40,000 C$ rolling enchants. You land on Divine. You are happy — until you realize The Deep Update's Luminous Rod at Level 150 gives you 95% base Luck and 40% mutation rate. Your Divine-enchanted Mythical Rod at 45% Luck is suddenly a worse earner than an unenchanted Luminous Rod at 95% Luck. You spent 40,000 C$ on a rod you will replace in 30 hours.

**Recovery:** There is no refund on enchants. Once you hit Luminous Rod territory, sell your old fish stockpile to fund the new rod's first enchant session. The Mythical Rod's Divine enchant was not wasted — it earned you income during those 30 hours. Just do not make the same mistake again. Check the upgrade path before committing relics.

### Failure 2: Low-Level Reroll Addiction

You are Level 50 with a Steady Rod. You roll Lucky on your third try. You say "Divine is only 5% more Luck, let me try." You burn 6 more relics. You land on Sea King. You roll again. Mutated. Again. Storm. Now you have 2,000 C$ left and a Mutated Steady Rod with no Luck bonus. Your income drops. Your Mythical Rod grind stretches from 20 hours to 40.

**Recovery:** Fish without an enchant for a few hours to rebuild your C$ reserve. Buy one relic. Roll once. Accept the result regardless of what it is — even a non-Luck enchant at this stage is better than burning more C$ chasing perfection. The Steady Rod phase is not about optimization. It is about reaching the Mythical Rod as fast as possible.

### Failure 3: Ignoring Disturbance on High-End Rods

You get Cusk Purger. You see 225% Luck. You immediately roll Divine and hit it on your fifth relic. You are thrilled — 245% effective Luck, the highest number in Fisch. Then you fish for 10 hours and cannot figure out why your Titanium Rod was catching more mythics per hour. The answer is Disturbance. That +7 modifier is scaring away the exact fish your Luck stat is trying to attract.

**Recovery:** This is the most painful fix because you already have Divine. You have two options. Option A: save 5 to 10 relics and reroll for Controlled, accepting that you will lose Divine. Option B: keep Divine and accept that your catch composition will lean toward common and uncommon fish, but those fish will sell for more due to the Luck multiplier. Option A is better for rare fish collectors. Option B is acceptable if you are fishing for volume. Test both for an hour each and compare your C$ per hour before committing.

## The Reroll Stop-Loss Rule

Every enchantment session needs a hard stop. Without one, you will chase perfection and end up with nothing. Here is the rule:

**Set a relic budget before your first roll. When the budget is gone, stop — regardless of what enchant is on the rod.**

For the Steady Rod, your budget is 3 relics. For Mythical, 6 to 8 relics. For Luminous Rod, 4 to 5. For Titanium and Cusk Purger, 8 to 10. If you hit a good enchant early, stop immediately. The budget is a ceiling, not a target.

The math behind this: each reroll has an independent chance of hitting any given enchant. Rolling 10 times at 5% odds per roll gives you roughly a 40% cumulative chance of hitting a specific enchant. That means even with 10 rolls, there is a 60% chance you do not get Divine. Chasing that last 60% is how you go broke.

## The Two-Handed Decision Framework

When you are standing at the enchantment altar holding a new rod, ask two questions:

1. **How many hours will I use this rod?** If the answer is under 20, walk away. Enchantments do not pay for themselves in under 20 hours on any rod below Mythical tier. The Luminous Rod is the one borderline case — its mutation multipliers can make a 15-hour enchantment pay off, but only if you are actively fishing in mutation-friendly zones.

2. **Does this rod have built-in mutations that multiply enchantment value?** If yes, enchant sooner and more aggressively. A +10% Luck enchant on a rod with 9× mutation potential is worth more than +20% Luck on a rod with no mutations. The Luminous Rod at 95% Luck with Lucky (+10%) outperforms an unenchanted Titanium Rod at 180% Luck in C$ per hour in mutation-heavy fishing zones.

If the answer to question 1 is "over 20 hours" and the answer to question 2 is "yes," you enchant. If question 1 is "under 20 hours," save your relics. If question 2 is "no" but question 1 is "yes," enchant conservatively — one session, first good roll, no rerolls.

## The Luminous Rod Loophole

Here is the most counterintuitive advice in this guide: **you should enchant the Luminous Rod even though you will replace it with Titanium Rod or Cusk Purger.**

The conventional wisdom says never enchant a transitional rod. The Luminous Rod breaks that rule because its 95% base Luck and 40% combined mutation rate create a payoff curve that no other mid-game rod has. A single Lucky enchant on the Luminous Rod pays for itself in roughly 25 hours. You will use the Luminous Rod for at least 40 to 60 hours while grinding toward Titanium. That is 15 to 35 hours of pure profit from the enchant — C$ that directly accelerates your progression to the next rod.

Compare this to enchanting the Mythical Rod at 25% base Luck with zero built-in mutations. The Mythical Rod's enchant takes 40 hours to break even. You use it for roughly 80 hours. Net profit: 40 hours. The Luminous Rod's net profit: 15 to 35 hours, plus the mutation multiplier makes those hours dramatically more lucrative.

The Luminous Rod is the exception that proves the rule. Do not apply this logic to the Carbon Rod or Steady Rod. Their base stats are too low. The mutation synergy is what makes the Luminous Rod work.

## Conclusion

Enchantment timing in Fisch comes down to one principle: **multiply value where the base is already high.** Enchanting a low-tier rod wastes relics on a small multiplier. Enchanting a high-tier rod with built-in mutation potential multiplies an already-large number.

The Deep Update raised the stakes. Luminous Rod at 95% Luck with 40% mutation rate is the most enchantment-efficient rod the game has ever seen. Titanium Rod at 180% Luck with 20% Titanium mutation is where you go all-in on Divine. Cusk Purger at 225% Luck demands Controlled before you even think about Luck enchants.

Save your relics early. Spend them wisely in the mid-game. Go all-in at the top. And whenever you are unsure: fish for an hour, check your C$ per hour, and let the numbers decide.
