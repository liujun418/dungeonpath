You are standing on the seafloor for the first time, watching your character walk instead of swim. Ten minutes ago you grabbed the Deep Survey Device from the NPC at -194, 130, -4669, and now the ocean floor holds you like the surface used to. You open the Outer Deep bestiary for the first time and see the number: **21 entries**. Two of them are crates. The rest are fish, and almost none of them will bite for a rod you just cast blind.

That moment is where most players stall. They see 21 slots and think "grind harder." But the Outer Deep bestiary is not a volume problem. It is a condition problem. Every entry here is gated by some combination of bait, weather, time, and season, and the game does not hand you that combination. You have to work it out — or read it here.

This guide walks the whole thing: what Outer Deep actually is, how the 21-entry bestiary works, the hunt order that saves you hours, and how to farm the one drop (Radiant Prism Scale) that ties the whole update together.

## Outer Deep, Explained — Where You Actually Are

Before you hunt, you need to know what kind of zone you are standing in, because the bestiary behaves differently here than anywhere else in Fisch.

The Deep is not one area. It is a stack of layers inside a trench, and they do not play by the same rules.

**Deep City** is the safe hub. No aggressive creatures, no damage, just NPCs, shops, and a place to bank and sort. This is where you breathe. You will run back here constantly to sell, repair, and drop off materials.

**Outer Deep** is the first dangerous layer. The task prompt calls it a no-pressure environment, but "no pressure" means something specific here: the ambient sea creatures are aggressive and they hurt. You are not just fishing against a timer here — you are fishing while something nips at your health. That changes how you hunt, because you cannot park yourself in one spot and cast for twenty minutes without managing the damage.

**Abyssal Trenches** sit below and around Outer Deep, and they are the same deal but worse — deeper, darker, and full of the same aggressive spawns at higher density. The bestiary entries that feel impossible usually live here.

**Gloomy Crevice** is the endgame layer, and it needs the MK II Deep Survey Device upgrade to properly access. It is outside the scope of the 21 Outer Deep entries, but the materials you farm here feed it.

**The scene that matters:** You swim out of Deep City's light and into Outer Deep for the first time. A sea creature lunges. Your health ticks down. You panic-cast, catch a Common, and realize the glowfish-type entry you wanted never had a chance — wrong bait, wrong time, and you were too busy surviving to notice either. That is the Outer Deep bestiary in a nutshell. You are not just matching conditions. You are matching conditions while the zone actively works against you.

If you have not finished the basics of getting into The Deep, read [our full The Deep update guide](/posts/fisch/the-deep-update-guide/) first — it covers the Deep Survey Device, the three-layer structure, and the rod route.

## How the 21-Entry Bestiary Actually Works

Twenty-one entries. Two of them are magnet-pulled crates — boxes you do not catch with a rod, but wrench open with a harpoon gun's pull. The other nineteen are fish spread across Outer Deep and the Abyssal Trenches.

The mistake everyone makes is treating the bestiary like a checklist of "rare vs common." That is the wrong axis. The right axis is **condition specificity**.

Every fish in this bestiary has a set of conditions that put it in your catch pool. A "common" entry with a brutal condition combo can take longer than a rare-looking entry with no gates. This is the counterintuitive part, and it is the single most important idea in this guide:

**Rarity on the label is not what makes an entry hard. Condition specificity is.**

A deep-sea glowfish-type entry that the community calls "common" might only spawn in one season, at night, during a specific weather, on one bait. A bigger, scarier abyssal predator-type entry might spawn in any weather, any time, on any bait, and just be a lower roll chance. The glowfish is the one that will eat your afternoon.

So you do not hunt "the rarest first." You hunt **by which conditions are active right now**. That is the decision framework for the whole bestiary:

1. **Check what conditions are live** — season on your HUD, time of day, current weather, and what bait you are holding.
2. **Knock out every entry whose conditions are currently true.** Weather and time flip constantly; season lasts weeks. When a weather window opens, you take every entry that shares it.
3. **Save the single-condition entries for gaps.** If a fish only needs "night," you catch it any night while you wait for the rare weather on something else.
4. **Treat the two crate entries as a separate job.** They are harpoon work, not rod work. You do them in one dedicated pass, not interspersed with fish.

Here is the condition system in one view:

| Condition | What it controls | How to check it |
|---|---|---|
| Bait | Some entries flat-out won't bite without the matching bait; wrong bait removes the fish from your pool entirely | Your equipped bait slot before every cast |
| Weather | Rain, fog, clear, and the Deep's own effects gate entire groups of entries | The weather readout; some weather is zone-local |
| Time | Day/night (and finer windows for a few entries) flip the pool | The in-game clock, top of your HUD |
| Season | The longest-running gate; a season-gated fish is gone for weeks once the season turns | The season indicator on your HUD |

Do not memorize a fish name and its location. Memorize the **condition set**, because that is what actually determines whether the fish exists in your pool at all. A fish that is "not there" is not rare — it is excluded.

## The Efficient Hunt Route

The order you collect in matters more than your rod luck. Here is the route that respects how conditions actually flip.

### Step 1: Light the Sector Beacons First

Outer Deep has sector beacon towers you can repair. Repairing a beacon does two things worth doing before any serious hunting: it lights up that part of the map, and it pacifies the aggressive creatures near it.

Do not underestimate the pacify part. Remember the zone damages you. If you are fishing in a beaconed sector, you are not bleeding health between casts, which means you can actually hold a spot long enough to wait out a weather window. Repair beacons in the sectors where you still need entries before you start the time-gated hunts. It is unglamorous, but it is the difference between fishing and babysitting your health bar.

### Step 2: Group Entries by Condition, Not by Rarity

Make three mental piles:

- **Always-available entries.** No bait gate, no weather gate, any time. Clear these in your first session just to shrink the list.
- **Single-gate entries.** Need only night, or only a season, or only a bait. These are your filler — you catch them whenever the one gate is open, while waiting on the third pile.
- **Multi-gate entries.** Need two or more conditions at once. These are the real grind. A fish that wants night + fog + a specific bait only exists in your pool during that overlap.

**The scene:** It is foggy in Outer Deep at night. A multi-gate entry you have been chasing finally has its window open. You cast with the right bait and land it in three tries. The player next to you has been here for two hours reeling commons, because they never realized fog was the missing piece — they thought the fish was just "rare." That is the entire bestiary in one exchange.

### Step 3: Use the Stat Chip System as You Go

As you hunt, you collect crafting materials. The Stat Chip System lets you turn those materials into **permanent** stat upgrades — harpoon accuracy, harpoon range, line strength.

There is a real decision here: do you sink materials into stat chips now, or save them for rod and harpoon crafting later? The answer is **accuracy first, range second, line strength third**, and do it early. Harpoon accuracy pays off immediately because the two crate entries and the Formosa Seabass farm all run through your harpoon. A few accuracy chips turn a frustrating harpoon session into a quick one. Do not hoard everything for the Cusk Purger — the Radiant Prism Scale farm in particular is so much faster with upgraded harpoon accuracy that the early investment pays for itself.

## Farming Radiant Prism Scale — The Purple Formosa Seabass

This is the drop that connects the whole update, and it is also where the bestiary players get stuck.

Radiant Prism Scale is a rare drop from the **purple Formosa Seabass** in Outer Deep. It feeds three things: the **Cusk Purger Rod**, the **Luminous Rod quest line**, and the **Sightless Oracle harpoon**. So if you are doing anything serious in The Deep, you need these scales.

The catch — literally — is that **you do not get it with a rod.**

You need a **harpoon gun**. The purple Formosa Seabass variant is a harpoon target, not a rod target. Players who grind Outer Deep for hours with a rod and see zero scales are not unlucky. They are using the wrong tool, and the game does not tell them.

**How to get the harpoon gun you need:** the **Scrap-Cannon** comes from Sledge's first two tasks — **Restoring 1P-4** and **Scrap Extraction**. Complete both and the Scrap-Cannon is yours. It is not a deep grind; it is early, and it comes with +30% starting progress and **3x Rare Finds**, which is exactly what you want when the drop you are farming is a rare one.

**The scene:** You finish Scrap Extraction, grab the Scrap-Cannon, and swim back to the spot you have been rod-fishing for two days. First harpoon pull on a purple Formosa Seabass, and a Radiant Prism Scale drops. The rod was never going to get it. The harpoon got it in minutes. The difference was never patience — it was the tool.

**Farm order tip:** upgrade harpoon accuracy through the Stat Chip System before you settle in for a long Formosa Seabass session. Missing a pull wastes the rare spawn. Accuracy chips are cheap insurance on the rarest drop in the zone.

When you finally have enough scales, they go into the Cusk Purger craft — the full material path and what else you need is in [our Cusk Purger rod guide](/posts/fisch/cusk-purger-rod-guide/).

## Harpoon Guns and Stat Chips — Why They Matter for Collecting

Most players think of the harpoon gun as a side weapon and stat chips as an afterthought. For the Outer Deep bestiary, both are core.

The two **magnet-pulled crates** in the bestiary are only openable by harpoon pull. You cannot complete 21/21 without a harpoon gun, full stop. That alone makes the Scrap-Cannon a requirement, not a luxury.

Beyond the crates, the harpoon gun is how you apply the mutation-style rolls that some entries want, and it is the only way to touch the Formosa Seabass farm described above. If you are serious about the bestiary, you run rod and harpoon side by side — not one or the other.

The **Stat Chip System** is where your leftover crafting materials turn into edge. Harpoon accuracy is the priority, then range, then line strength. These are permanent, so they compound across every future hunt. The trap is treating every material as "save it for the Cusk Purger." Some materials are better spent on a chip that makes your whole collection faster than on a single rod you will craft once.

**Decision framework — spend now or save?** Ask one question: does this material also feed something you need this week? If yes, save it. If it only feeds a distant craft, chip it. The bestiary rewards the player who upgrades their tools early, not the one who hoards for a craft they are still ten hours from unlocking.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

### Mistake 1: Rod-fishing the Formosa Seabass for scales

You spend hours in Outer Deep with a rod, watch zero Radiant Prism Scales drop, and assume the drop rate is brutal.

**Why it happens:** The purple Formosa Seabass is a harpoon target. The game gives you no hint that the scale is gated behind the harpoon gun, and every other drop in the game has come from your rod so far.

**Recovery:** Do Sledge's first two tasks — Restoring 1P-4 and Scrap Extraction — for the Scrap-Cannon. Equip it, target the purple variant, and pull. The drop rate stops being the problem the moment you switch tools.

### Mistake 2: Re-casting the same wrong setup for an hour

You know the fish is "in" Outer Deep. You stand in the right sector and cast the same bait for an hour, catching commons.

**Why it happens:** You are checking location but not the other three gates. Bait, weather, or time is off, so the fish is excluded from your pool. More casts at the same wrong setup change nothing.

**Recovery:** Stop casting. Read the four conditions — bait, weather, time, season. Change the one that is wrong. If the entry is multi-gate, wait for the overlap rather than forcing it. Check the season carefully; a season-gated fish is not "rare," it is simply not in season, and no amount of casting fixes that until the season turns.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring the beacon towers and dying to aggressive creatures

You skip the sector beacons, park yourself in a dark corner, and spend half your time re-potioning or running back after a death.

**Why it happens:** Beacons feel like optional map decoration. They are not. A repaired beacon pacifies nearby aggressive creatures and lights the sector.

**Recovery:** Before a long hunt in a sector, repair its beacon. The pacify effect is the difference between holding a spot through a weather window and getting driven off mid-window. This one small step saves more time than any bait optimization.

### Mistake 4: Hunting "rarest first" and missing live windows

You chase the scary-sounding entries first, ignoring the fact that a fog window just opened for three easier entries. The fog passes. Now you wait hours for it to come back.

**Why it happens:** You are ranking by perceived rarity instead of condition availability. The label says "rare," so you fixate on it.

**Recovery:** Reverse the order. When a weather or time window is live, take every entry that shares it, no matter how common they look. The multi-gate commons are the real time sinks. The rare-but-ungated entries will still be there when the window closes.

## FAQ

### How do I even enter Outer Deep and start filling the bestiary?

Swim to the NPC at coordinates -194, 130, -4669 and claim the free Deep Survey Device. Equip it to walk on the seafloor and cast underwater. Without it equipped you cannot fish in any of The Deep's zones, so the Outer Deep bestiary is locked until this one step is done. If you lose the device, the same NPC reissues it.

### Why can't I catch the rare entries even though I'm standing in the right spot?

Because most Outer Deep entries are gated by bait, weather, time, and season — not just location. A fish can be "at" a spot but out of your catch pool if even one condition is wrong. Before every hunt, check all four: your bait, the current weather, the in-game clock, and the season on your HUD. Fix the mismatch instead of re-casting the same setup for another hour.

### How do I farm Radiant Prism Scales for the Cusk Purger and Luminous Rod quest?

Target the purple Formosa Seabass in Outer Deep, but do not use a rod — bring a harpoon gun. You need the Scrap-Cannon from Sledge's first two tasks (Restoring 1P-4 and Scrap Extraction), which gives +30% starting progress and 3x Rare Finds. Harpoon the purple Formosa Seabass variant to roll for the Radiant Prism Scale drop. Rod-only players miss it entirely because the drop is tied to the harpoon.
