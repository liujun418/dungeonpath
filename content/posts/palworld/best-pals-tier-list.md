## The Wipe: Four Evenings, One Fight, Zero Coverage

You spent four evenings grinding. You caught every Pal on the Reddit "DEFINITIVE S-TIER LIST" thread. Frostallion. Jetragon. Shadowbeak. All level 50. All hyper-trained. Gold passives on all three. You looked at your roster and felt like the game was already over.

Then you walked into the Tower of the PIDF to fight Marcus and Faleris, and your whole team died in about ninety seconds.

Here's how it went down. You led with Frostallion. Faleris is Fire-type and Frostallion is Ice — on paper, you have the type edge. But Marcus's own Pal is the one putting in the work, and it hits Neutral and Dark. Frostallion folded fast. You swapped to Shadowbeak, your second-best. Dark-type. Dark on Dark gives you no advantage at all. You sent Jetragon last. Dragon-type into a Fire-type boss — Faleris tore it apart in roughly twelve seconds.

The cause wasn't weak Pals. It was zero type coverage. You built a shelf of "best in game" trophies that couldn't answer the one specific fight in front of you.

The recovery is simple: stop asking "what's the best Pal?" and start asking "what am I fighting, and what level am I?" That question is the entire list below.

Instead of rebuilding your old team, you rebuild around coverage. You bring one Pal per type the fight actually uses — Ice for the Dragon, Neutral for the Dark, and a lead that doesn't get one-shot by Fire. You slot a bred Anubis for Earth coverage against the types your legendaries leave open. The wipe cost you thirty minutes. Reading the types before you fly costs you two.

**The rule nobody prints: raw combat power means nothing if your team doesn't cover the types you're actually facing.**

## What Players Get Wrong About Tier Lists

Every tier list you've read has the same flaw. It treats "S-tier" like a sticker that applies everywhere. It doesn't. Here's where people trip.

**Treating tier lists as absolute truth.** A Pal that's S-tier in base work can be D-tier in combat. A mount that's unbeatable over open water is useless in a mountain cave. Ranking Pals without context is like ranking "the best tool" without asking whether you're driving a nail or cutting a board.

**Ignoring type matchups.** You wouldn't lead a Dark-type boss fight with a Neutral Pal, but that's exactly what happens when you follow a numbered list. Type advantage in Palworld is huge. A correctly-matched B-tier Pal out-damages an S-tier Pal with the wrong typing, every single time. Run it on your next boss: a mid-level Blazamut throwing Fire into a Grass target out-damages a max-level Shadowbeak hitting the same target for neutral. The tier list said Shadowbeak is better. The type chart disagreed.

**Smushing combat, base, and mount into one ranking.** Frostallion is S-tier in all three, which makes it the outlier, not the rule. Most Pals are great at one job and bad at the other two. When a list averages everything into one number, you catch a combat monster when you actually needed a base workhorse.

**Chasing endgame Pals while your base rots.** We've all done it. You see Jetragon at the top and think "that's my goal." Meanwhile your benches are staffed by a Cattiva and a depressed Lamball, and a single Mega Sphere takes six real-world minutes. A level-19 Anubis saves you more time than Jetragon ever will.

## The Decision Framework: Which Pal for Your Stage

Stop asking "which Pal is best?" Ask "what's my problem right now?" Here's the if-then table.

| If you're... | Then get... | Because... |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1-15, first run | Foxsparks + Tanzee | First Kindling and Grass coverage, both in the starting area |
| Level 15, sick of walking | Nitewing | First flying mount; the saddle unlocks at 15 |
| Level 19, crafting is a crawl | Anubis (breed Penking + Bushi) | Handiwork 4, roughly 4x craft speed |
| Level 25-30, farms dry and ore is slow | Jormuntide + Digtoise | Watering 4 and Mining 3 |
| Level 35-45, stuck on a boss | Shadowbeak + Blazamut | Type coverage for Sanctuary and Scorching Mines content |
| Level 45-50, want speed and a carry | Frostallion + Jetragon | Legendary combat and the fastest flyer in the game |

That table is the answer to "what should I get next?" — and the answer changes at every stage. The same Pal that's useless at level 10 is mandatory at level 45.

## The Counterintuitive Read: Your Base Pals Out-Earn the Legendaries

Here's the tip most guides skip. For the first 40 levels, the Pals that improve your account the most aren't legendaries. They're work Pals.

A level-19 Anubis has Handiwork 4. A Cattiva has Handiwork 1. A craft that takes Cattiva 60 seconds takes Anubis 15 seconds — 4x faster. Over a night of crafting spheres, ammo, and armor, that's literal hours saved. A Jetragon you'll catch at level 50 saves you travel time eventually, but you have to survive to level 50 first.

The counterintuitive part: players chase the S-tier combat Pal and ignore the boring work Pal. But the boring work Pal is the one that gets you to the S-tier combat Pal faster. The best Pal for a level-19 account is not a legendary you can't catch yet. It's a bred Anubis.

## The Partner Skill Trap: Why the Stat Block Lies

The stat block is the wrong place to read a Pal. Half of what makes a Pal good is its Partner Skill, and that line is easy to skip.

Take Anubis. On paper it's a 950-attack fighter, below the legendaries. But its Partner Skill, Guardian of the Desert, adds Earth-type damage and a dodge while it's active — and Earth coverage is exactly what the Ice and Dragon legendaries don't give you. That's why Anubis stays relevant for 30 levels while a higher-statted Pal falls off.

Frostallion is the same story. Its raw numbers are good, not special. What makes it the best single Pal is the Partner Skill combo: ride it, and its ice trail damages ground enemies while you fly. You're not just mounting a fast flyer. You're mounting a weapon. A Frostallion without that skill slotted is just a mid Ice Pal.

The rule: before you invest in any Pal, read the Partner Skill line. Two Pals can sit on near-identical stats and do totally different jobs because of that one line.

## Combat Pals: The Tier List That Moves With Your Level

"What's the best combat Pal?" is the wrong question, because the answer changes every few levels.

**Frostallion (1100).** The most versatile combat Pal in the game. S-tier in combat, mount, and base work at once. It demolishes Dragon-type bosses but folds against Fire. You can't catch it before the late 40s, so it's a goal, not a plan.

**Jetragon (1300).** Highest raw stats. Fastest flyer in the game, and it turns boss fights into speed runs. But it's uncatchable before level 50, so it's a post-game pick.

**Shadowbeak (1250).** The mid-to-late-game boss killer. Wrecks Neutral types, gets shredded by Ice. Reachable at Sanctuary No.2.

**Anubis (950).** The exception to every rule. Breedable at level 19, stays relevant for 30+ levels. Outclassed only by legendaries in pure endgame boss fights.

Here's the real S-tier at each stage:

- **1-15**: Foxsparks (Fire) or Tanzee (Grass), caught in the starting area
- **15-25**: Nitewing + Direhowl — Nitewing at 15, Direhowl in the grasslands
- **25-35**: Anubis (breed) + Faleris — Penking + Bushi makes Anubis; Faleris at Sanctuary No.3
- **35-45**: Shadowbeak + Blazamut — Shadowbeak at Sanctuary No.2, Blazamut at the Scorching Mines
- **45-50**: Frostallion + Jetragon + Paladius — the endgame legendary hunt

The insight nobody talks about: some low-tier Pals are irreplaceable for one specific job. Beegarde is garbage in a fight but it's the only reliable Honey source for breeding cake. Cattiva won't survive a boss fight but it's your best early Transport Pal. Don't judge every Pal by its combat tier. ## Mount Pals: Speed Isn't the Only Number

Everyone ranks mounts by top speed. Speed only matters if you actually reach your destination without falling off.

**First flyer: Nitewing (1800).** Available at level 15. Everything faster needs a level 34+ saddle.

**Ground exploration: Direhowl (1400).** Fastest ground mount you can catch before level 25.

**Mountain climbing: Eikthyrdeer (1300).** The double jump clears cliffs. Faster than flying for vertical terrain.

**Water traversal: Jormuntide.** The only Pal that moves fast in water. Surfent is the budget option.

**Long-distance flight: Jetragon (3300).** Twice the next-best flyer. Worth the level 50 grind.

**Combat flight: Frostallion (2800).** The ice trail damages ground enemies. More useful than Jetragon for combat, even if it's slower in a straight line.

Now the stamina reality check:

- **Nitewing (300):** about 45 seconds, one biome
- **Faleris (350):** about 55 seconds, two biomes
- **Frostallion (420):** about 70 seconds, three biomes
- **Jetragon (350):** about 50 seconds, two biomes — but much faster while it lasts

The surprising fact: Frostallion has the longest flight duration, not Jetragon. Jetragon's rocket boost drains stamina fast. On long exploration runs where you can't land often, Frostallion is the better mount even though it's slower in a straight line. For the full Frostallion-versus-Jetragon breakdown, see [Jetragon vs Shadowbeak](/posts/palworld/jetragon-vs-shadowbeak/).

## Work Pals Worth a Team Slot

Handiwork is the single most impactful work stat in the game. Every sphere, weapon, armor piece, and structure you craft is gated by it.

**Anubis (Handiwork 4).** Breed Penking + Bushi at level 19. 4x craft speed.

**Orserk (Handiwork 3).** Wildlife Sanctuary No.3, level 35+.

**Katress (Handiwork 2).** Nighttime forests, level 20+.

**Cattiva (Handiwork 1).** Starting area.

A level-19 Anubis crafts 4x faster than a Cattiva. The 60-second sphere becomes 15 seconds. Over hundreds of crafts, that's hours back.

The other jobs you can't skip, with the top Pal for each:

- **Kindling** — Jormuntide Ignis (4), then Blazamut (3), Faleris (3). No Kindling means no ingots, and no ingots means no spheres or ammo.
- **Watering** — Jormuntide (4), then Penking (2). No Watering means empty farms and starving Pals.
- **Mining** — Astegon (4), then Anubis (3), Digtoise (3). Ore and coal are the mid-game backbone.
- **Medicine** — Lyleen (3), then Katress (2). Sick Pals don't work.
- **Generating** — Orserk (4), then Grizzbolt (3). Powers the assembly lines.
- **Transport** — Wumpo (4), then Helzephyr (3), Mossanda (3). Moves items to storage so stations don't clog.

The priority order by level: Cattiva + Foxsparks early, then Penking + Tanzee at 15-19, breed Anubis at 19, Digtoise + Beegarde at 25-30, Jormuntide + Lyleen at 30-35, Jormuntide Ignis + Astegon at 40+, Frostallion + Wumpo at 45+.

## The Hybrids: Build Your Account Around These

Some Pals do more than one job. These are the ones worth real investment.

**Anubis** — Handiwork and Mining. The highest overall impact in the game. Breed it at 19 and use it for 30+ levels.

**Frostallion** — Combat, mount, and Cooling. The best single Pal, but locked behind level 48+.

**Jormuntide Ignis** — Kindling and solid combat. Transforms your smelting and fights well.

**Lyleen** — Planting and Medicine. Keeps your base fed and healthy.

**Orserk** — Generating and combat. Powers everything and fights. The best mid-game all-rounder.

## The "Don't Waste Your Time" List

Some Pals look good on paper and aren't worth the effort.

**Warsect.** Tanky but low damage. Fights drag on forever. Take Anubis instead — faster kills, and it crafts.

**Relaxaurus.** High HP, terrible attack. "Dragon type" sounds great, performs poorly. Jormuntide Ignis is the actual dragon damage.

**Bushi.** Outclassed by every other Fire type. Blazamut or Faleris both do it better.

**Sweepa.** Marketed as "Ice for Dragon bosses," but dies in three hits. Cryolinx actually survives.

**Chillet.** Cute and easy to catch early, but its stats fall off a cliff past level 20. Direhowl is the ground mount that stays relevant.
