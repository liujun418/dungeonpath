You are level 70. You just closed the patch notes for the August 12, 2026 Palworld 1.0.3 update and one line jumped off the screen: Jetragon's tech dropped from level 79 to level 70. A full nine levels earlier. You open your Technology menu and there it is — Jetragon's saddle, sitting in the list where it had no business being last week.

Now you are staring at two names. Jetragon, the rocket-fast Dragon that has defined Palworld's endgame since Feybreak. And Shadowbeak, the Dark-type specialist everyone swears is the better damage dealer in the right fight. You have enough Ancient Technology points for one big unlock, maybe two if you push it. You need to pick.

This guide breaks down the two, updated for 1.0.3, so you spend those points on the Dragon that actually fixes your next ten hours of play.

---

## The 1.0.3 Patch Just Changed This Fight

Before August 12, 2026, the Jetragon-versus-Shadowbeak debate was simple. Jetragon was the late-game crown jewel locked behind a level 79 tech requirement. You only thought about it after you had already finished everything else. Shadowbeak was the earlier, cheaper legendary that carried you through the mid-game while Jetragon sat out of reach.

The 1.0.3 update flipped that. Jetragon's tech unlock dropped from level 79 to level 70, and its riding gear got cheaper to craft. That is not a small tweak. It moves Jetragon out of "late-game trophy" territory and into "mid-game accelerator" territory.

**The practical result:** at level 70, a player who last week could not even unlock Jetragon's tech can now craft the saddle and ride the fastest flyer in the game. Shadowbeak got no such treatment — its unlock level and gear costs are unchanged.

That asymmetry is the whole story of this guide. One legendary got pulled forward nine levels. The other stayed put. The comparison is no longer "late-game generalist vs mid-game specialist." It is "the specialist you could always get vs the generalist that just arrived early."

Read the full [Palworld 1.0.3 Update Guide](/posts/palworld/update-103-guide/) for everything else in the patch — this article is only about what the change means for your Dragon decision.

## Quick Verdict: Who Wins Where

| Question | Winner | Why |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Faster map traversal | Jetragon | Fastest flying mount in the game, period |
| General combat DPS | Jetragon | Big Dragon hits with a strong signature move |

**Bottom line:** Jetragon is the stronger standalone Pal and now arrives earlier. Shadowbeak is the stronger team player for specific fights. In 1.0.3, Jetragon is the better *default*, but Shadowbeak is still the better *answer to a specific problem*.

## Jetragon: The Rocket Dragon You Can Now Grab at 70

Picture it. You hit level 70, craft Jetragon's saddle, and mount up. You launch off a cliff and clear a mountain range in the time your old flying Pal needed just to climb. That is the Jetragon experience, and it is the main reason to unlock it first.

### Combat Performance

Jetragon is the generalist. Its signature Dragon-type damage hits hard across most content, and it shrugs off fights that your mid-game Pals would stall on. You do not bring Jetragon to counter one specific enemy. You bring it to clear most of them and to not have to think too hard while doing it.

The exact damage numbers will vary with your level, passives, and condensation — check the Paldeck in-game rather than trusting a stale Reddit number. The point that matters is the *shape* of it: Jetragon is a reliable, high-damage Dragon that wins by being broadly strong, not by exploiting one matchup.

### Mount Speed

This is Jetragon's real selling point, and 1.0.3 made it available nine levels sooner. Jetragon is the fastest flying mount in the game. There is no caveat. On a server with friends, the Jetragon rider is the one already at the boss while everyone else is still over open water.

For exploration, base-to-base supply runs, and raid-farm commutes, no other Pal saves you more raw hours. If your current bottleneck is "I spend too long getting places," Jetragon is not a luxury — it is a fix.

### The Catch Is the Real Gate

Here is the part the level-70 headline hides: unlocking the tech is not the same as catching the Pal. Jetragon's saddle unlocks at 70 now, but you still have to actually *get* a Jetragon. It spawns in the Mount Obsidian volcano region, it needs heat-resistant gear just to stand near it, and it is a genuinely hard catch even with Ultra or Legendary Spheres.

So 1.0.3 did not make Jetragon free. It moved the wall. Last week the wall was "grind to 79, then catch it." Now it is "hit 70, then catch it." The catch is still there. Budget for multiple attempts and bring more spheres than you think you need.

## Shadowbeak: The Specialist That Didn't Move

Now the other side. You are level 55, farming Holy-type bosses, and your generalist Dragons keep trading blows instead of winning clean. Someone in chat says "just get Shadowbeak." You do. And suddenly the fight you were losing is over in half the time. That is Shadowbeak's lane.

### Combat Performance

Shadowbeak is Dark-type, and it is the pick when you are facing Holy-type or otherwise specialized content where raw Dragon damage is not the right tool. In those fights Shadowbeak out-performs Jetragon, sometimes dramatically, because it is built for the matchup instead of for general coverage.

Against everything else, Shadowbeak is strong but not Jetragon-level. It is an S-tier specialist, not an S+-tier generalist. That distinction matters more after 1.0.3, because Jetragon now arrives early enough that the two overlap for longer.

### Team Utility

Shadowbeak's real advantage is its partner skill, Modified DNA. While Shadowbeak fights alongside you, it buffs Dark-type damage and boosts your own attack. The exact percentage is something to check in-game, but the effect is concrete: you hit harder while Shadowbeak is out.

This makes Shadowbeak the pick for players who fight *alongside* their Pal rather than just commanding from the back. If you actively participate in combat — firing your own weapon, not just watching — that player-attack buff is real value Jetragon does not give you.

### Easier Acquisition

Shadowbeak spawns at the No. 3 Wildlife Sanctuary and is broadly easier to reach and catch than Jetragon's volcano spawn. Its unlock level is lower, its gear is cheaper, and you can plausibly have one online long before level 70.

That used to be Shadowbeak's trump card: "you can get me first." 1.0.3 did not take that away, but it did shrink the gap. Jetragon still has the harder catch, but it no longer has the much higher unlock level.

## The Two-Dragon Decision Framework

So you are level 70, both names are on your list, and you have limited Ancient Technology points. Here is the decision, laid out as the two-way choice it actually is.

**Scenario A — your map time is killing you.** You are doing base-to-base runs, farming a boss across the map, and half your session is travel. Unlock Jetragon first. The flying speed alone pays back the investment inside a few sessions, and the combat power is a bonus.

**Scenario B — a specific boss is walling you.** You have a Holy-type fight you cannot clear with your current team. Unlock Shadowbeak first. It is the direct counter to the exact problem, and its player-attack buff helps you contribute instead of watching your Pal carry.

**Scenario C — you can afford both, but not in one sitting.** This is the trap most players hit. Do not split your points. Unlock one fully — saddle crafted and Pal caught — before starting the other. A half-unlocked Jetragon and a half-unlocked Shadowbeak is the worst of both worlds: you spend the points but get no flyer and no counter.

**The counterintuitive bit:** Jetragon *looks* like the "late-game" Dragon you save for last. After 1.0.3, it is arguably the better *mid-game* purchase, because nine levels of extra flying speed compounds over hundreds of hours of travel. The "endgame reward" became a "mid-game accelerator" overnight. Do not treat it like a trophy you polish at level 79. Treat it like a speed boost you grab at 70.

If you are lost on how to sequence your tech points across the whole game, the [Palworld Technology & Progression Guide](/posts/palworld/technology-and-progression-guide/) has the unlock roadmap.

## Where Players Blow It

Every guide should tell you how the decision goes wrong. Here are the three mistakes I see most, with the recovery for each.

**Mistake 1: spending your Ancient Technology points on Shadowbeak's gear right before the patch, then watching Jetragon drop to 70.** A week before 1.0.3, Shadowbeak looked like the only reachable legendary. You dumped points into it. Now Jetragon is at your level and you are broke.

*Recovery:* farm more Ancient Civilization Parts (they come from bosses and high-tier chests) and treat Jetragon as your next unlock, not a replacement for the Shadowbeak you already built. You did not waste the points — Shadowbeak still does its job. You just changed what the *next* purchase should be.

**Mistake 2: assuming Jetragon is automatically stronger in every fight.** It is not. Send a Jetragon into a Holy-type matchup that Shadowbeak was built for and you will watch your "best Pal" trade hits while the specialist would have ended it. Type still matters more than raw tier.

*Recovery:* check the Paldeck for the enemy's type before you commit. Keep both Pals in your box. "Jetragon is the best" does not mean "Jetragon is the best *here*."

**Mistake 3: reading "unlocked at 70" as "I get it at 70."** The tech is at 70 now. The catch still sits in a volcano behind heat-resistant gear and a low catch rate. Players who spend their only spheres on a bad Jetragon attempt at 70 and come away empty-handed have made the mistake of confusing the unlock with the acquisition.

*Recovery:* go in prepared. Heat-resistant armor, a stack of high-tier spheres, and the willingness to reset and retry. The unlock is cheaper now — the catch was never cheap.

## The Stat Fruit Rework Nobody Is Talking About

There is a second change in 1.0.3 that quietly changes this whole decision, and it is easy to miss while you are staring at the level-70 headline.

If you used to power up your Jetragon or Shadowbeak by stacking Stat Fruits, your route just changed. Moonlord raids no longer drop Stat Fruits the way they used to — the system was reworked, and the new path runs through Bounty Tokens instead. So if your plan was "catch the Dragon, then feed it Stat Fruits from Moonlord raids to push its stats into the stratosphere," that plan no longer works the same way.

What this means for the Jetragon-versus-Shadowbeak call: you can no longer assume a steady Moonlord Stat Fruit pipeline will patch up a Pal's weak spots. A Jetragon with a mediocre spread of stats needs a different fix now, and so does a Shadowbeak you were going to juice with Fruits. Check the in-game Bounty system to see the new token economy before you commit either Dragon to a long-term stat plan. The [Palworld Skill Fruit Complete Guide](/posts/palworld/skill-fruit-complete-guide/) covers the Fruit side of it in more depth.

This rework tilts the decision slightly. Raw base stats and passives matter a bit more now, because the "just farm Stat Fruits to fix it later" safety net got thinner. That is one more reason the default leans Jetragon — its baseline is strong enough to not need a rescue.

## Breeding and Passives: Don't Chase a Myth

Both Jetragon and Shadowbeak naturally carry the Legend passive, one of the strongest passives in the game. That is the one thing worth remembering when someone tells you to "breed them for the best Pal."

Here is the honest version: do not chase a specific percentage someone quoted from a two-year-old Reddit post. The breeding system in Palworld has specific rules for legendary Pals, and the exact parent pair that produces the offspring you want is something to confirm in-game or in a current breeding calculator, not from memory. What holds up is this: the Legend passive is valuable, both Dragons have it, and building toward it through the [Palworld Breeding Perfect Passives Guide](/posts/palworld/breeding-perfect-passives-guide/) is a strong long-term goal.

For the Jetragon-versus-Shadowbeak decision, passives are a tiebreaker, not a reason to pick one over the other. They both carry Legend. They both benefit from the same passive-stacking logic. Grab whichever one solves your immediate bottleneck first, then worry about breeding toward a perfect spread later.

## The Bottom Line

In 1.0.3, Jetragon got pulled forward nine levels and became the better default pick for most players. It is the fastest flyer in the game, a strong generalist in combat, and it now arrives early enough to pay for itself in travel time long before endgame.

Shadowbeak is still the right answer when the question is specific: Holy-type bosses, Dark-type coverage, and a Pal that makes *you* stronger while it fights. It was not nerfed by the patch, and it is still the more forgiving catch.

The order that serves most players: unlock Jetragon at 70 for speed and raw power, keep Shadowbeak on the shortlist for the specialized fights, and never split your Ancient Technology points halfway. Get both eventually — they fill different roles and both carry Legend. But if you have to pick one *first* after the 1.0.3 update, pick Jetragon, then spend your very next session farming the spheres to actually catch it.
