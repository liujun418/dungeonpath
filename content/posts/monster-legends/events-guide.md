## The Maze Coin Graveyard You Walked Into Last Week

You open the Maze event on day one. The first node costs 3 coins. Free reward. You tap it. 5,000 food. Fine. The next node is 5 coins. 10,000 gold. Sure. You keep going, following the path that looks like it leads to the big monster egg icon in the center. Twenty nodes later, you have burned 120 coins on a dead-end branch that gives you a level 2 rune and a "congratulations" screen. The real path to the monster was three nodes to the left. You just did not have the map.

Now you are 120 coins short of the Legendary prize with 2 days left. You earn 400 coins per day. That is 800 coins in 2 days. The path costs 950 coins from where you are standing. You are 150 coins short. The gem shop offers a 50-coin pack for 25 gems. You buy one. Then another. Then the 100-coin pack for 50 gems. By the time you reach the monster, you have spent 75 gems on a prize that was free if you had just waited 4 hours for the community map to drop.

This is the Maze coin trap. Almost every player falls into it at least once. The fix is not grinding harder. It is knowing when to stop pressing buttons.

## The Real Event Economy: Why Casual Play Burns Resources

Monster Legends runs four core event types on a rotating schedule: Maze, Race, Puzzle, and Team Dungeons. Breeding events are a fifth type, covered in detail in our [breeding guide](/posts/monster-legends/breeding-guide/). Each event type has a different resource economy, and mixing up which resource matters for which is the fastest way to stall your account.

Here is the failure pattern. A player logs in, sees a Maze active, and starts collecting every node they can reach. They burn through their maze coins, pick up some food and gold, and feel like they accomplished something. Then the Race event starts two days later, overlapping with the Maze. They have no food saved, no eggs stockpiled, and no gold banked — because they spent it all on Maze nodes that gave them 5,000 food each. They finish the Race in 47th place. The exclusive monster goes to someone else. They repeat this cycle every month.

The fix: **Maze events consume coins. Race events consume stockpiled resources. Puzzle events consume tokens. Mixing them up means you show up to every event empty-handed.**

When a Maze is active, your only job is earning and spending maze coins. Your food, gold, eggs, and dungeon keys sit untouched. When a Race is active, your maze coins sit at cap while you dump everything else. This single habit change — keeping resource types separate per event — is worth more than any gem you could spend.

## Maze Events: The Only Event Where Waiting Is the Optimal Play

Maze events are the most common event type and the most reliable source of free Legendary and Mythic monsters. They run 7-10 days and use maze coins earned from the Adventure Map, dungeons, and daily missions.

### The Map Problem

Every Maze has a fixed layout. The community maps it within hours. The path to the main monster prize is always knowable before you spend your first coin. The nodes that look like they lead toward the monster on day one are bait — Social Point intentionally places dead-end branches with low-value rewards near the entrance to drain coins from impatient players.

The optimal Maze strategy in three words: **wait for map.** Do not spend a single coin until you see the full path posted on the Monster Legends subreddit or Wiki. This takes 2-4 hours after launch. The coins you "lose" by waiting are nothing compared to the 120-200 coins you save by not walking into dead ends.

### Node Priority: What to Skip

Once you have the map, you still should not collect every node on the prize path. Some are negative value — the coin cost exceeds what the reward is worth.

**Always collect:** Monster eggs, cells, gems. A monster egg node costs 15-30 coins. A Legendary in the shop costs 500+ gems. The math is not close.

**Usually collect:** Food above 10,000, gold above 100,000, runes level 5+. A 15-coin node that gives 15,000 food is fine — you would spend more than 15 coins worth of effort farming that food from habitats.

**Skip:** Food below 5,000, gold below 50,000, runes level 1-3, any decoration or cosmetic. These nodes cost 5-10 coins and give you something you could generate in 10 minutes of passive habitat collection.

**The trap node:** Mystery boxes near the end of prize paths. These cost 25-40 coins and give a random reward from a pool that includes both Legendary cells and 2,000 food. The expected value is negative. Skip them unless you have excess coins and the main prize is already secured.

### Coin Math That Actually Matters

You earn approximately 400-500 maze coins per day from all sources. The coin cap is 600. Any coins earned above 600 are deleted.

The decision that separates efficient players from everyone else: if a Maze has 3 days left and you are 1,200 coins away from the main prize, you cannot reach it. You will earn at most 1,500 coins, and you are already at cap. Quit the Maze. Stop earning coins. Let them sit at 600 and carry over to the next Maze. The 600-coin head start on the next event is worth more than 20 nodes of consolation food and gold.

The counterintuitive move: **quitting a Maze event early is often the correct play.** If you cannot reach the main monster, every coin you spend on side rewards is a coin you do not have for the next Maze where you can reach the main monster. The food and gold from side nodes do not compound. The Legendary from next month's Maze does.

## Race Events: The 6-Hour Window

Race events are PvP-like progress races against approximately 100 other players. You earn points by completing tasks: breeding, collecting gold, feeding monsters, hatching eggs, winning PvP battles, and clearing dungeon nodes. Reach point milestones for rewards. Finish top 3 for the exclusive monster.

### The Arms Race Trap

The biggest mistake in Race events is pushing for top 3 in the first 48 hours. You surge to 2nd place on day one by burning your saved eggs, food, and gold. The player in 1st sees you gaining and spends 50 gems on energy refills to stay ahead. You respond with 50 gems. They respond with 100. By day two, you are both 1,500 points ahead of 4th place and you have each spent 200+ gems. You are now committed. The arms race continues until one of you runs out of gems or the event ends.

The player who wins this exchange spent 400-500 gems on a monster they could have won for 50 gems if they had just waited.

The correct Race strategy: **stay in the 10-20 range for the first 2.5 days.** Complete your daily tasks naturally. Do not burn stockpiled resources. Do not spend gems. The players in the top 3 on day two are almost never the players in the top 3 when the event ends. They burn out, run out of resources, or get outspent by someone who saved everything for the final push.

At the 6-hour mark, you make your move. Dump every saved egg. Feed every monster you have been starving. Collect every habitat. Burn your dungeon keys. You will jump from 15th to 3rd in under an hour. The players who were in 1st and 2nd are now out of resources and watching you pass them with nothing left to spend.

### Preparation Checklist (Do This Before the Race Starts)

The Race event calendar is roughly predictable — they appear around week 3 of each monthly cycle. When you see a Maze or Team Dungeon wrapping up, start preparing:

- Stockpile 3-4 eggs in your Hatchery. Do not hatch them.
- Stop feeding monsters for 2-3 days. Let food accumulate.
- Leave habitat gold uncollected.
- Cap your dungeon keys at 3/3.
- Drop your PvP rank by losing 5-10 matches. Easier opponents mean faster wins.

These five steps cost zero gems and are the difference between finishing top 3 and finishing 47th.

### Gem Spending Rule for Races

Only spend gems on a Race if all three conditions are true: you are within 500 points of 3rd place, there are less than 30 minutes remaining, and the exclusive monster fills a gap in your roster you cannot fill any other way. If any condition is false, save your gems. The exclusive monster will return. The 200 gems you save will buy you a guaranteed Legendary from the shop during a sale.

## Puzzle Events: The Token Economy You Are Bleeding

Puzzle events are the most F2P-friendly event type. Zero preparation. Zero gems. You earn puzzle tokens from the Adventure Map and dungeons, then spend them to flip tiles on a grid. Matching pairs give rewards. The monster prize is always on a known tile.

### The Over-Flip Problem

The mistake: trying to flip every tile. You cannot. Token income is capped at roughly 40-60 tokens per day. A 5x5 grid has 25 tiles. Each flip costs 3-6 tokens. Clearing the full board costs 150+ tokens. You earn maybe 300 tokens across the entire 5-7 day event. You can flip roughly half the board.

The correct approach: **flip the monster path first, then stop.** Community maps show which tiles contain the monster and its cells. Flip those tiles. Ignore the rest. Tokens do not carry over between Puzzle events, so spending your last 6 tokens on a tile that gives you 3,000 food is better than letting them expire — but only after the monster path is secured.

Take a screenshot of the board before you start flipping. This prevents the "I just spent 4 tokens to flip a tile I already revealed 2 days ago" mistake that drains 10-15% of your token budget.

## Team Dungeons: The Contribution Math Nobody Explains

Team Dungeons are cooperative events where your team clears nodes and defeats bosses across multiple floors. Rewards are distributed based on contribution.

The dungeon completion reward is fine but not why you play Team Dungeons. The real value is the **contribution ranking bonus.** The top 3 contributors on each boss floor get extra monster cells, runes, and sometimes gems. On a 5-floor dungeon, that is 5 separate bonus payouts.

Here is what most players miss: contribution is measured by **node count, not damage dealt.** Clearing 10 donation nodes (cost: some gold and food) gives the same contribution credit as soloing 10 battle nodes (cost: your best team's stamina and potentially a revive). Donation nodes are the efficiency play. They cost resources you regenerate passively and give the same contribution score as difficult combat nodes.

You do not need to be online for 5 days straight. Log in once per day. Clear 3-5 donation nodes. That is 15-25 contribution points across the event. On most casual teams, that puts you in the top 10 contributors. On active teams, it puts you in the top 5.

If your team has 5+ members who have not logged in during the event, leave. The dungeon boss has a 24-hour timer per floor. If your team cannot clear the boss before the timer expires, the dungeon stalls and you lose the completion reward. Join a new team through the in-game team search — filter for "active" and "event participation required." The 24-hour cooldown on joining a new team is worth the switch.

## The Monthly Event Calendar: What to Prioritize

Monster Legends follows a roughly predictable monthly rotation. Events typically overlap by 1-2 days. When two events are active simultaneously, the priority order is: **Race (if you are competing for top 3) > Maze (if you can reach the main prize) > Breeding Event > Team Dungeon > Maze (if you cannot reach the main prize).**

The key insight: if you are not pushing for top 3 in a Race, ignore it entirely and focus on the Maze. The milestone rewards in a Race are not worth the resources you spend to earn them if you are not getting the exclusive monster. Put those resources into the Maze or save them for the next Race.

## The Gem Decision Framework

Every event type tempts you to spend gems. Here is a single framework that applies to all of them:

**Before spending gems on any event, ask: "Does this purchase get me a monster I cannot get any other way?"** If the answer is no, do not spend. Food, gold, runes, and cells are all farmable through normal gameplay. Gems are the only currency that buys exclusives. Spending gems on farmable resources is trading a permanent asset for something you can generate in 30 minutes of habitat collection.

The only three gem spends that pass this test:

1. **Maze coin pack (25-50 gems) when you are 50-100 coins short of the main prize on the final day.** A Legendary or Mythic monster for 25-50 gems is a 90%+ discount versus the shop price.
2. **Race energy refill (50-100 gems) when you are 500 points from 3rd place with 30 minutes left.** The exclusive monster is worth 500+ gems. Spending 100 gems to secure it is a 5x return.
3. **Breeding event speed-up (50 gems total) when you need one more breed before the event timer expires.** The event-exclusive breedable is time-gated. Speed-ups during the event window are the only way to access it.

Everything else — speed-ups during normal breeding, energy refills on day 2 of a Maze, gemming donation nodes in Team Dungeons — is a net loss. A 10-gem speed-up here and a 25-gem refill there adds up to 300+ gems per month. That is a free Mythic from the shop during a sale, gone.

## Two Counterintuitive Rules That Changed My Account

**Rule 1: The best Maze strategy is to skip the first 2 days entirely.** The community map takes 2-4 hours to post. By day 2, every path is mapped, every dead end is labeled, and the exact coin cost to reach the main prize is calculated. The coins you "lose" by not playing on day one (roughly 400-500) are less than the coins you waste on wrong-path nodes (typically 120-200). The math favors waiting. Nobody does it because opening a new event and not tapping anything feels wrong. Do it anyway.

**Rule 2: A Race event you do not push for top 3 in is a resource-negative event.** The milestone rewards — 10,000 food, 50,000 gold, a few cells — are worse than what you would earn by spending the same resources on normal gameplay. The only reason to participate in a Race is the exclusive monster. If you are not going for it, treat the Race as if it does not exist. Stockpile your resources for the next one. This is the opposite of what the game UI tells you to do. The game UI is designed to make you spend.
