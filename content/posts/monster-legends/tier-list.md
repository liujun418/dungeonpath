> *Last updated: August 15, 2026. This tier list reflects the Kitakami Era meta after the Wind element dropped on August 13. Rankings cover Arena, Team Wars, and Adventure Map, and factor in the new Season 1 monsters and the Pseudo Ancestor.*

You pulled the shiny new monster. The Kitakami Era banner dropped, you burned your saved gems on the Season 1 hatch, and out came Extremely Bad Kitty, the new Wind monster everyone is talking about. You slotted it into your Arena team, hit queue, and lost three in a row to teams you beat yesterday.

The tier list you were following said your monsters were S-tier. So what went wrong?

Nothing about your roster changed. The counter chart did. You added a Wind attacker to a team with no Dark coverage, no Earth pressure, and no plan for how Wind actually slots into the meta. This guide fixes that. Here is how the tier list works now that Wind is in the game, where every Kitakami monster actually lands, and how to spend your food so you do not end up with a shiny benchwarmer.

## The Scene: Your "S-Tier" Team Just Folded to a Wind Monster

Here is the exact loss I keep seeing since the Kitakami Era launched.

You run the classic S-tier core: Ugluk the Earth/Fire tank up front, Griffania on speed, Zombic on possession, Kaguya healing. This team carried you to Diamond last season. Then the enemy drops a Wind attacker — Extremely Bad Kitty — onto their lead slot, and your Ugluk melts in two turns.

You panic. You throw your best food at a random new monster you saw in a YouTube short. You rank it to 40, equip whatever runes are lying around, and queue again. You lose worse.

**Why it went wrong.** Two things. First, Ugluk and Rockantium — the two tanks the old tier list told you to trust — are Earth-typed. Wind counters Earth. You brought a tank that the new element was explicitly designed to kill. Second, you responded to a typing loss by grabbing *any* new monster instead of the *right* new monster. The fix was never "get the new thing." It was "get a Dark answer."

**How to recover.** Wind takes bonus damage from Dark. You already own the answer: Zombic, Barbatos, or even the budget Miserus all slap Wind monsters out of the fight before they reach your tank. Rerun your speed ordering so your Dark denier moves first and locks the Wind attacker down on turn one. Then slot a Wind counter of your own — or just put your Earth tank on the bench against Wind-heavy teams — and your win rate snaps back. You do not need a full rebuild. You needed one counter and a speed reroute.

## Wind Rewrites the Counter Chart You Memorized

For a year, the Monster Legends counter wheel was stable enough that most players stopped thinking about it. Fire beats Nature, Nature beats Magic, Magic beats Thunder, Thunder beats Water, Water beats Fire, and Light and Dark trade blows. Earth sat comfortably at the edge, mostly used for bulky tanks that nothing specific punished.

The Kitakami Era broke that comfort. Wind enters the chart like this:

- **Wind beats Earth.** Every Earth tank you relied on — Ugluk, Rockantium, Rockilla — now has a hard answer.
- **Dark beats Wind.** Wind monsters die fast to the Dark deniers and attackers that already saturate the top of the meta.

That second line is the part most players miss. Wind looks like a free win against Earth-heavy teams, so everyone rushes to add a Wind attacker. But the same teams that run Earth tanks are already loaded with Dark. Your Wind monster gets countered by the exact monsters you were trying to fight. Wind is not a "press this to win" button. It is a tool that only pays off when you control the board around it.

Here is what actually changed for team building:

1. **Earth tanks dropped a half-tier.** Ugluk and Rockantium are still good, but you cannot mindlessly leave them up front anymore. Against a Wind lead, they are liabilities.
2. **Dark got even more valuable.** Every Dark monster that was already meta — Zombic, Barbatos, Miserus, Lord of the Atlantis — now doubles as your anti-Wind insurance. Dark was already the best offensive typing; now it is also a defensive counter.
3. **You need one Wind answer, not a Wind team.** A single Dark denier moving first solves the Wind problem. You do not need to rebuild around Wind to survive it.

The practical rule: before you spend anything on the new era, ask whether your current team already has a Dark monster that can move before the enemy's Wind attacker. If the answer is yes, you are fine. If it is no, fix speed and coverage before you chase the shiny new monster.

## The Kitakami Era Monsters, Ranked

The Season 1 hatch introduced four new monsters plus the new Pseudo Ancestor type. Here is where each one actually lands, based on what we have seen in the first two weeks of the era.

| Monster | Element | Role | Tier | Where It Fits |
|---------|---------|------|------|---------------|
| **Extremely Bad Kitty** | Wind | Attacker | A | Anti-Earth pressure; punishes Ugluk and Rockantium leads |
| **Owlroot** | Nature | Controller | B | Nature control slot; fine but you likely have a better denier |
| **Dogburn** | Fire | Attacker | B | Fire damage; outclassed by Barbatos and Nebotus |
| **Geckodepth** | Water | Attacker | B | Water attacker; Warmaster Thalassa owns this niche |
| **Pseudo Ancestor** | Varies | Flex | A- | New typing wildcard; strong but depends on which element you pull |

Extremely Bad Kitty is the one that matters. Its Wind typing gives it a job nobody else in the roster fills: deleting Earth tanks on turn one. In Arena, that is a genuine meta shift. In Adventure Map, it clears the Earth-heavy nodes that used to slow down Yedra comps. If you pulled one, build it. If you did not, do not spend gems trying — the other three Season 1 monsters are standard typing, and your roster almost certainly already covers Fire, Nature, and Water damage.

The Pseudo Ancestor is the wildcard. Its whole value depends on which element you roll, so we cannot rank it higher until the community data settles. Treat it as a flexible filler for war nodes, not a monster to anchor a team around yet.

## How to Read a Tier List Like a Roster Builder (Decision Framework)

Here is the problem with tier lists, including this one: they rank monsters in isolation, but you never fight with a monster in isolation. You fight with four monsters, and the gaps between them decide the match.

Use this four-question framework before you spend a single food, gem, or rune on any monster. If the answer to the first three is not a clear "yes," leave it in storage.

1. **Does it plug a counter hole I actually have?** Look at your four current monsters. If a Wind attacker kills your Earth tank every match, the answer is a Dark denier or a Wind counter — not whatever is trending. Counter holes beat peak power.
2. **Is its element relevant to this era?** Dark and Wind are the two typings that matter right now. Dark counters Wind and pairs with everything. Wind punishes Earth. A monster in any other typing has to be genuinely exceptional to justify food over a Dark or Wind option.
3. **Can I actually finish it?** A monster you can rank to 5 and equip properly beats an S-tier you can only get to rank 2. Accessibility is a stat. If you cannot rank it, it is not S-tier *for you*.
4. **What role is it filling — and is that role already covered?** You need a denier, a tank or speed controller, a healer, and a damage dealer. If you already have a great denier, a second one does not make your team better. It makes your team redundant.

Run those four questions and the tier list becomes a shortlist, not a shopping list. Most "bad tier list" experiences are just players skipping question three, building a monster they could not finish, and blaming the list.

## Failure #2: You Fed a Rotating "Season" Monster Like It Was a Keeper

This is the quieter mistake, and it costs more long-term than the Wind loss above.

A month ago a player I know spent two weeks of food, all his rank-up cells, and a full set of Mythic runes on a past-season monster that every tier list from three months ago called S-tier. It is not S-tier anymore. The new era power-crept it, the meta rotated away from its typing, and now it sits at rank 5 doing nothing while his actual team starves for food.

**Why it went wrong.** He treated the tier list as permanent, so he invested in a monster based on where it *was*, not where it *is*. Seasonal monsters rotate. The thing that dominated last era is often the thing the next era is designed to dethrone. He also fell for sunk cost: once the food was in, he kept ranking it to "finish the job" instead of cutting his losses.

**How to recover.** The recovery is simple and painful: stop. Redirect all future food to a monster that passes the four questions above. Do not sell or feed the old monster out of spite — a rank 5 off-season monster is still free filler for a Team War node or a dungeon restriction. Slot it there, let it be mediocre, and put your real resources into a current counter. The food you already spent is gone. The only decision left is whether you waste more.

The takeaway: never invest a resource you care about into a monster that is currently rotating out of the meta. If a monster's era just ended, it is a war-node filler, not a project.

## The S, A, B, C, D Tiers in the Kitakami Meta

Here is what each tier means, then the roster that currently matters.

| Tier | What It Means | Invest If |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| **S** | Best in role, no cheap substitute | You can finish it and it plugs a hole |
| **A** | Excellent, flexible across modes | You need its typing or role |
| **B** | Solid, outclassed by a specific upgrade | You are F2P and it is cheap to finish |
| **C** | Niche, single-mode only | A war node or dungeon requires its element |
| **D** | Power-crept | Never — breeding parent only |

**S-Tier (meta-defining).** Barbatos (Fire/Dark attacker-denier) still carries teams with AoE damage and Possession in one kit, and it now doubles as your anti-Wind tool. Zombic (Dark/Magic denier) is the possession king and your cleanest Wind answer. Warmaster Thalassa (Water/Thunder) still one-shots anything that is not a tank. Kaguya (Nature/Light) remains the only full revive that keeps a losing match alive. Griffania (Thunder/Nature) owns the speed race and applies AoE stun. Moonhaze (Dark/Light controller) shuts down physical teams. Ugluk (Earth/Fire tank) stays in S only with a caveat now: he is the best pure tank in the game, but you cannot start him into a Wind lead.

**A-Tier (excellent).** Nebotus remains the best F2P Legendary and now matters more because his Fire/Dark typing gives you a budget Dark answer to Wind. Miserus is the budget Zombic. Lord of the Atlantis (Dark/Water) freezes and possesses, and his Dark typing is Wind insurance. Cryocrawler, Druid, Pandalfio, Ra'Zhul, Drop Elemental, Vadamelter, Yedra, Nishant's Pet, and Rockantium all hold their spots — Rockantium a half-step lower now that Wind exists. Extremely Bad Kitty lands here as the era's standout new attacker.

**B-Tier (good).** Flawless, Hydratila, Razfeesh, Firekong, Mersnake, Genie, Pandaken, and Tarzape. These are fine while you work toward an A or S replacement. Owlroot, Dogburn, and Geckodepth land here too: correct typing, but your roster already covers their jobs.

**C-Tier (niche).** Rockilla, Thunder Eagle, Treezard, Light Spirit, Goldfield. Keep these only for element-restricted war nodes and dungeons. Do not rank them past what the restriction demands.

**D-Tier (avoid).** Every Common, plus the starter Fire/Treezard/Rockilla/Thunder Eagle line. They exist to be breeding parents. Any food or rune you put in here is gone forever.

## The Counter-Intuitive Play: A B-Tier Counter Beats an S-Tier Meta Pick

Here is the piece of advice that feels wrong until you watch it win a match: **the monster everyone runs is the monster everyone has already built a counter for.**

Barbatos and Zombic are S-tier, which means every competitive team you face has a plan for them. They have seen the possession, they have built the speed, they have slotted the cleanse. Your S-tier monster is walking into a lobby full of people who teched specifically against it.

A B-tier Wind or Dark counter — the monster nobody bothers to counter — can walk through a team that over-invested in beating the meta. Extremely Bad Kitty, or even a cheap Dark attacker, catches teams whose entire game plan was "stop Barbatos." The value of a monster is not its tier. It is the gap between what it does and what the other player prepared for.

So when you are stuck, do not ask "which S-tier am I missing." Ask "which off-meta typing is nobody teching against this week." In a meta that revolves around Dark and Earth, a fast Wind attacker with no hard counter in the lobby is often the highest-impact pick in your box — even if it is a full tier below the meta monsters on paper.

## Runes by Role

Runes are the difference between a monster that works and a monster that dies on turn one. Here is the short version; the full breakdown is in the runes guide.

| Role | Rune Set | Priority |
|------|----------|----------|
| Attacker | 3x Strength | ATK% > Speed > HP% |
| Speed controller | 3x Speed | Speed > HP% > ATK% |
| Denier | 3x Speed | Speed > Accuracy > HP% |
| Tank | 3x Life | HP% > DEF% > Speed |
| Healer/support | 3x Speed | Speed > HP% > DEF% |

One note specific to the Kitakami meta: your Dark denier's Speed stat just got more important. If your Zombic or Miserus cannot move before the enemy's Wind attacker, your Earth tank is already dead. Speed-check your denier before you worry about anything else.

## What Changes When the Meta Rotates Next

Every era follows the same cycle. A new element or monster type drops, the top of the meta churns for two to three weeks, a few counter picks settle into place, and the tier list stabilizes until the next drop. Right now we are in the churn: Wind is new, the Pseudo Ancestor is unproven, and players are still over- and under-valuing the Season 1 monsters.

The only tier list that survives a rotation is the framework, not the rankings. Hold the four questions in your head, keep a Dark denier fast, and you will ride the Kitakami meta without getting farmed by it. The rankings above will move. The method will not.
