You have 60 credits. The ship terminal offers Flashlights at 15 each, a Shovel at 30, a Walkie-Talkie at 12, a Pro-Flashlight at 45, a Stun Grenade at 30, and a Zap Gun at 50. You can afford any combination within your budget. What you pick determines whether your crew survives Day 1.

Most new crews buy one Pro-Flashlight at 45 credits because "better brightness equals better vision." One crew member can see slightly further in the dark. The other three stumble around with no light at all. The Shovel doesn't get purchased because "we'll just avoid the monsters." Then the crew enters the facility. A Thumper spawns in Room 4. Nobody has a Shovel. The Thumper charges — it's fast in straight lines, and the crew member who tries to run gets caught immediately. They die. Their scrap drops. The remaining crew has no way to fight the Thumper. They retreat to the ship with 40 scrap. Quota is 130. Day 1 is already a failure.

Same 60 credits, different choices: two Flashlights and one Shovel. Two crew members can see. Everyone can fight. The Thumper spawns in Room 4. The player with the Shovel stands their ground, backpedals while swinging, and kills it in three hits. The crew collects the Thumper's room scrap plus everything from the surrounding rooms. They return to the ship with 120 scrap. Quota met with surplus. The Shovel cost 30 credits. The dead crew member in the first scenario lost 70 credits of scrap. The Shovel paid for itself more than twice over in a single encounter.

Equipment in Lethal Company isn't about individual item quality. It's about coverage — making sure your crew has a counter for every common threat. Flashlights counter darkness. The Shovel counters Thumpers and Bunker Spiders. The Walkie-Talkie enables the ship operator to guide the team around danger using the radar terminal. These three items — two Flashlights, one Shovel, and eventually one Walkie-Talkie for the ship operator — are the foundation. Everything else in the shop is either a luxury for later quotas or a trap for beginners.

The Pro-Flashlight costs three times what a standard Flashlight costs for roughly 30% more brightness. Beginners buy it thinking the brightness upgrade will save them. It won't. You'll die to a Bracken you didn't see or a Coil-Head you couldn't counter regardless of how bright your flashlight is. And when you die, you drop the Pro-Flashlight and lose the 45-credit investment. Buy standard Flashlights until your crew can reliably survive 5+ quotas. Upgrade to Pro-Flashlights only when deaths are rare enough that the investment sticks.

The Stun Grenade and Zap Gun are even worse traps for beginners. The Stun Grenade stuns entities for 5 seconds — but only if you hit them with it, and only if you can capitalize on the 5-second window. Beginners don't know entity timing well enough to use that window. The Zap Gun requires two coordinated players — one to zap, one to loot or attack. Beginner crews don't have that coordination. Both items sit unused in dead crew members' inventories, 80 credits down the drain.

## Failure Analysis

Three repeatable failure patterns that drain credits and wipe crews:

**The Brightness Trap.** One player buys a Pro-Flashlight. Three players have no light. The Pro-Flashlight holder moves faster, separates from the group, and dies alone in a dead-end room. The crew loses 45 credits of gear plus whatever scrap that player was carrying. This pattern repeats because the buyer thinks "I'm helping the team" when they're actually fragmenting it.

**The Weapon Without a Wielder.** A crew buys a Zap Gun and assigns it to their least experienced player because "they need the protection." That player panics, misses the zap timing, and dies with the 50-credit item. Experienced crews assign Zap Guns to their best players — but beginner crews shouldn't buy them at all.

**The Radio Chorus.** Every crew member buys a Walkie-Talkie. That's 48 credits for four radios when only the ship operator needs one. The operator can see the radar and call out threats. The ground team should be in visual range of each other, not split up across the map chatting on radios. Four radios is 48 credits that could have been three Flashlights and a Shovel — the actual survival kit.

## Decision Framework

Use this priority order for every pre-landing purchase:

1. **Can every player see in the dark?** If no, buy standard Flashlights until the answer is yes.
2. **Can the crew kill a Thumper or Bunker Spider?** If no, buy a Shovel.
3. **Is someone staying on the ship to operate radar?** If yes, buy them one Walkie-Talkie.
4. **Have we survived 5+ quotas with zero gear-loss deaths?** If yes, consider Pro-Flashlights.
5. **Does the crew have coordinated discipline?** If yes, consider Stun Grenades. If very yes, consider a Zap Gun.

If the answer to step 1 or 2 is no, do not proceed to step 3. If the answer to step 4 is no, do not proceed to step 5. This framework prevents 90% of beginner credit waste.

## Counter-Intuitive

- **Dying with a Pro-Flashlight costs more than dying with a Shovel.** The Shovel stays valuable because it prevents deaths. The Pro-Flashlight only helps after you've already survived — it doesn't stop the things that kill you.
- **Buying fewer items increases your total scrap yield.** A crew with two Flashlights and one Shovel collects more scrap than a crew with four random items because they can clear rooms instead of fleeing them. Coverage beats variety.
- **The Walkie-Talkie is the highest-ROI item in the shop at 12 credits.** A single radio in the hands of a ship operator prevents more deaths than a Zap Gun prevents at one-fourth the cost. Most beginners ignore it because "it's just talking" and buy weapons instead.
- **The best time to buy nothing is your first quota.** If you land on Assurance with zero purchased gear, you can still find Flashlights and Shovels inside the facility. Found gear costs zero credits. The risk is higher, but the math works if your crew is cautious.

## Equipment Purchase Order Per Quota

Here is the equipment buy order that maximizes survival per credit spent at each stage of a run. This framework assumes a four-person crew with one designated ship operator.

**Quota 1 (130 credits):** Two Flashlights and one Shovel. Total cost: 90 credits. The remaining 40 credits go to Walkie-Talkies if you have a dedicated ship operator, or a second Shovel if you do not. Do not buy Pro-Flashlights, Stun Grenades, or Zap Guns at this stage — the credits are too tight and the extra utility does not justify the cost before you have basic survival tools.

**Quota 2 (250 credits):** Upgrade one flashlight to a Pro-Flashlight for the scout. Add a second Shovel. Buy one Walkie-Talkie for the ship operator if you skipped it previously. Total cost: 127 credits. The remaining budget is for replacing any gear lost on failed runs.

**Quota 3 (500 credits):** Full Pro-Flashlights for the interior team of two. At least two Shovels for the interior team. One Walkie-Talkie per player who does not already have one. Add a single Stun Grenade for coil-head emergencies. Total cost: 220-280 credits. Do not buy a Zap Gun yet — the crew is still developing the coordination to use it effectively without friendly fire.

**Quota 4+ (800+ credits):** This is when premium equipment becomes viable. A Zap Gun for the dedicated interior fighter, a Jetpack for the outdoor scout on paid moons, and Radar Boosters for monitoring critical corridors. At this stage the crew should be landing on paid moons where the equipment investment pays for itself in higher scrap density per room.

## Equipment Rules That Prevent Team Wipes

These rules sound obvious but are the first things abandoned under pressure:

- **Never enter the facility without a light source.** A player without a Flashlight in a dark room cannot see Screech, cannot see traps, and cannot navigate to hiding spots. If someone drops their light source, they leave the facility immediately. No exceptions.
- **The Stun Grenade is a team tool, not a personal weapon.** The player who carries it stays near the rear of the formation and deploys it only when the group is retreating. A Stun Grenade thrown by the front scout while everyone else is still spread out stuns one monster and alerts three others.
- **Do not bring premium equipment on moons where the crew is learning.** A Zap Gun on Experimentation is a liability. New players need to learn monster patterns, not rely on gear that will not be available when credits are tight on a paid moon run.
