Every Lethal Company crew has that player who volunteers for ship duty. The rest of the crew rolls their eyes — they think the ship operator is the person who's too scared to enter the facility. Someone who will scroll their phone while the "real players" collect scrap.

This is completely backward. The ship operator is the most important role on the crew. The radar terminal shows a top-down map of the facility interior. Every crew member appears as a blue dot. Every enemy — Brackens, Thumpers, Coil-Heads, Jesters, everything — appears as a red dot. Every piece of scrap appears as a yellow dot. The ship operator sees the entire map in real time. The players inside the facility can see roughly 15 studs ahead of them in the dark. The operator sees everything, everywhere, simultaneously. A good operator saves more lives than any Shovel, Flashlight, or Stun Grenade ever will.

The most important callout you will ever make: warning players about enemies they cannot see yet. A player walks down a corridor toward a door. On your radar, a red dot sits in the room on the other side. You say: "Stop. Bracken in the next room. Go around through the left corridor." The player changes direction. They never see the Bracken. They never know it was there. You just saved a life without anyone realizing you did it. If you make this callout 3-4 times per facility entry — and you will, because the radar shows threats constantly — you are preventing deaths that would otherwise wipe your crew.

The second most important callout: scrap direction. Yellow dots appear on radar as soon as scrap spawns. You can see which rooms have the most valuable clusters before anyone enters them. "Room 7 has 3 yellow dots — that is likely high-value scrap. Room 12 has 1 — probably a single low-value item. Go to Room 7, skip Room 12." Your crew earns 30-40% more scrap per facility entry because you are directing them to valuable rooms and telling them to skip dead ones. Over a 3-day quota cycle, that is hundreds of extra credits.

The teleporter is your emergency button. When a player's HP drops below 30 — or when they scream "TELEPORT ME" into the walkie-talkie — you hit that button instantly. The teleporter pulls them back to the ship with all their scrap intact. A teleported player keeps everything. A dead player drops everything on the floor in a room that now has a Bracken or Coil-Head guarding the body. Teleport early. Teleport often. The worst outcome is pulling someone out who was fine — that costs 30 seconds of walking. The alternative — not teleporting and having them die — costs all their scrap and potentially the entire crew if someone dies trying to retrieve the corpse.

The terminal has commands the tutorial never teaches you. `view monitor` cycles through each player's first-person perspective — you see exactly what they see. Useful when a player says "there's something here but I can't identify it." Switch to their view, identify the entity, tell them the counter. `ping [player name]` sends an audio cue only that player can hear — one ping means "check surroundings," two rapid pings means "THREAT — MOVE NOW." `switch [player name]` remotely toggles their flashlight — saves players in total darkness whose battery died. The terminal stores your command history — press up arrow to cycle through previous commands instead of retyping `moons` or `store` every time.

## One Operator, Two Crews: The Difference Radar Makes

Two identical crews run five quotas each. Crew A has no dedicated operator — all four players enter the facility together. Crew B has one player on radar full-time. Both crews are equally skilled, equally equipped.

After five quotas: Crew A has lost 11 crew members. They failed two quotas entirely due to full-team wipes. They averaged 180 scrap per successful facility entry. They abandoned 40 percent of entries early because someone died and the team panicked. Crew B has lost 2 crew members. They failed zero quotas. They averaged 310 scrap per entry — 72 percent more than Crew A. They abandoned zero entries. The only variable was the ship operator.

The radar shows threats before anyone sees them. The operator prevents deaths before they happen. The scrap difference comes from the operator directing the team to high-value rooms and telling them to skip dead ones. Eleven deaths versus two. The ship operator did not carry a Shovel, a Flashlight, or a Stun Grenade. They saved more lives from a chair than any item in the game.

## Three Ship Operator Mistakes That Wipe Crews

**Mistake one: leaving the terminal to help carry scrap.** A crew member dies. The walk from entrance to ship is 15 seconds. The operator thinks "I will run out and help carry." They leave the terminal. During those 30 seconds off radar, a Bracken stalks a crew member two rooms away. Nobody sees it. Nobody warns them. The crew member dies. The operator returns to the terminal and sees a red dot on top of a blue dot that is no longer moving. Never leave the terminal while crew members are inside the facility.

**Mistake two: teleporting too late.** A player says "I might need a teleport soon." The operator waits. The player says "OK teleport me now." The operator hits the button. The teleporter takes 1.5 seconds to activate. The Bracken kills in 1.0 seconds. The player dies mid-teleport. The corpse arrives at the ship with zero scrap. Teleport on the first warning, not the confirmation. A teleported player keeps their scrap. A dead player drops everything.

**Mistake three: not counting crew members.** The operator sees three blue dots in the facility. They assume everything is fine. What they missed: the crew entered with four players. One died five minutes ago and the operator did not notice because they were focused on scrap directions. Count blue dots every 30 seconds. Know exactly how many crew members are alive at all times.
