## You Bring Old Ghost to the New Patch and Get Farmed

You rolled Ghost months ago because the Revive passive looked like a free second life. You play it the old way: dump your points into Fruit, pop V, and stand in the middle of the fight expecting the clones to carry you. Last week that worked, sort of. This week you queue into a 5M bounty server near the Café, see a Dark Blade user, and lose the same fight twice.

Not two fights. The same one. Your revive procs, you come back with 50% HP and a flash of invincibility, and they just combo you again the second the frames end. Your clones float there doing nothing. You never once made them swing.

That's the Summer PvP Update (Balance Patch 001, August 6–7, 2026). It didn't really buff Ghost. It rewired what the fruit is for. The devs barely touched its damage numbers. What they changed is the V move: your summoned ghosts now mirror your sword skills. That one change moved Ghost from a meme revive pick to a real sword-build engine — and if you're still playing it like the old Revive Fruit, you're throwing.

Here's the honest framing, and it's the whole thesis of this guide: Ghost is now a sword fruit that happens to have a revive. Not a revive fruit that happens to carry a sword.

This guide covers the post-rework build, the V-mirror combo, and why Ghost is S-tier specifically for sword mains. If you want the full patch picture first, read our [Summer PvP Update guide](/posts/blox-fruits/summer-pvp-update-guide/). If you're still shaky on neutral and confirms, start with the [PvP combat mastery guide](/posts/blox-fruits/pvp-combat-mastery-guide/) before you worry about a fruit.

## What Actually Changed (And What Didn't)

Ghost is the reworked version of the old Revive Fruit. It's a Rare Natural type, which is cheap for what it does now — 940,000 Beli or 1,275 Robux from the Blox Fruit Dealer. Before the patch you'd grab it for the revive and basically ignore the rest of the kit. Now the kit matters.

The buffs, in plain terms:

- **Z and F cast times cut in half.** Z (Shivering Possession) went from a telegraphed "please dodge this" dash to something you can actually land. F (Wandering Soul) transforms you into a flying ghost with a blue trail — no damage, pure mobility and escape.
- **X range up 15%.** Spectral Release spins a ghost whirlwind around you. The outer ring traps and pushes; the inner circle can break Instinct. That Instinct break is huge, and it's the part of the kit nobody used before.
- **C knockback down 30%.** Cries of the Underworld fires a cone of ghosts that stuns and knocks back. Less knockback means your target stays in combo range instead of sailing across the arena where you can't follow up. You can hold C to extend it, and the first hit does the most damage.

And the one that matters:

- **V (Ghost Busters) now mirrors your sword skills.** This is the entire rework. When you press V, you summon two ghost clones for about 20 seconds. Before the patch they copied your movement and M1s. Now they copy your sword abilities too. Swing your blade, and a phantom version of that swing comes out of the clone at the same time.

Here's the part most people get wrong, and it's why the fruit felt weak when people first tested it: **the mirror damage scales with your Sword stat, not your Fruit stat.** Early testers built Fruit, popped V, saw the clones tickle people, and wrote the rework off as a dud. Then someone maxed Sword, and the same clones started chunking.

It is **not** triple damage, though. The clones don't stack the same skill three times. A clone M1 hits for roughly 50% of your normal M1 in PvP, and the mirrored sword swings add extra range, body-blocking, and screen clutter more than a straight multiplier. The value is coverage and pressure, not a 3x number that doesn't exist.

## The Failure Everyone Hits: Building It Like a Fruit Fruit

Let's talk about the mistake, because it's the first thing every Ghost player does wrong after the patch, and it loses fights before they start.

**The error:** You keep your old stat spread — high Fruit, a splash of Sword for a utility swing or two — and expect the reworked V to make that build good. You press V, the clones spawn, and you still don't do meaningful damage because there's no Sword stat behind the mirror.

**The cause:** Old Revive Fruit habits. For months, Ghost was a revive bot. You built Fruit because the clones didn't scale off anything useful, and the only reason you carried the fruit was the passive. The rework didn't announce itself as "respec now," so you kept your points where they were.

**The recovery:** Move your primary damage stat into Sword. Max Sword first, then split the rest between Defense and Melee for survivability. Fruit becomes your lowest priority — you only need enough to use the moves, and Ghost's moves don't need high Fruit to be useful as setup tools. The Z stun, the X Instinct break, the C knockdown: none of them care about your Fruit number. They're setup. The kill comes from your sword, doubled by the mirror.

The fastest way to see the difference is to test it: reset your stats, run max Sword with a high-damage blade, pop V, and M1 a training dummy. Watch the clone swings land. Then put the points back into Fruit and do it again. The gap is obvious in ten seconds, and it's the single cheapest lesson you'll get in this game.

If you need a stat refund, the KITT_RESET code was live as of the update — the [codes guide](/posts/blox-fruits/codes-guide/) tracks what's current.

## The V-Mirror Combo, Explained

Here's how the mirror actually changes a fight, not just a training dummy.

Pop V before you engage — never mid-combo, you'll waste the summon frames. Now you have two clones trailing you for about 20 seconds. When you swing, they swing. When you fire a sword skill, they fire a phantom version of it. A Dark Blade X that used to hit once now covers a wider arc with a ghost copy overlapping it. A Pole V2 X that used to leave a gap on whiff now has a second swing covering that gap.

The combo philosophy flips. Before the rework, Ghost was a stun-and-run fruit. Now it's a "set up with the fruit, kill with the sword" fruit. Your fruit moves create the opening. Your sword, mirrored, does the actual damage. So the pattern is:

1. Land a fruit move to lock them down — Z freeze, or X Instinct break, or C knockdown.
2. Immediately go to your sword while they're stuck.
3. Let the clones mirror every swing and skill.

A clean route that people are running right now, Ghost plus Sharkman Karate plus Cursed Dual Katana:

- V (pop clones) > X (break Instinct with the inner ring) > C (knockdown) > Sharkman C (temp HP buff, and it extends the stun) > Cursed Dual Katana X > Ghost Z (re-freeze) > Sharkman X > Sharkman Z.

The order matters less than the principle: every sword input happens inside a fruit stun, and every sword input lands twice. If you drop the combo, don't chase — you just wasted your V window, and the clones will expire. Reset and wait for V to come back.

Two more pairings worth testing, pulled from what's circulating in the community:

- **Ghost + Pole V2 + Sharkman Karate.** This is the recommended loadout after the rework. Pole V2's X end-lag got halved in the same patch, so the mirrored follow-ups chain faster than almost anything else. Sharkman's C gives you a temporary HP buffer so you can commit to the trade.
- **Electric Claw + Ghost + Yama.** A more aggressive line: Electric Claw C into Z to open, Ghost X into C, then Yama Z as the finisher. Higher damage ceiling, less forgiving timing.

Don't treat these as gospel. Treat them as skeletons. The mirror works with any sword that has generous, multi-hit skills — that's why Triple Dark Blade keeps coming up. ## Decision Framework: Ghost vs the S-Tier Field

Ghost isn't for everyone, and it's not a strict upgrade over the other S-tier picks. Here's the if-then frame so you can decide in two minutes instead of guessing.

| If you... | Pick this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Main sword builds and want your swings to land twice | Ghost | V mirrors sword skills and scales off Sword; no other fruit doubles your blade like this |
| Want an Instinct break plus a reliable one-rotation assassin | Awakened Dark | Dark X breaks Instinct cleanly, and the kit got range and cast-speed buffs in the same patch |
| Want the safest top-tier pick with no rework gamble | Dragon (East/West) | Untouched by the nerfs; best raw damage, mobility, and tankiness in the game |
| Win fights by outlasting people in extended trades | Werewolf | 3% lifesteal passive in transformed form means you heal while they don't |
| Want a revive as a crutch while you learn PvP | Ghost, but only if you build sword | The revive is a real safety net, but it doesn't help if your sword has no stat behind it |

And the second half of the frame, because this is the question every Ghost player actually faces — sword build or fruit build:

| If you... | Go | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Want Ghost to be anything more than a revive button | Sword build | The entire rework scales off Sword; a Fruit build leaves the V mirror dead weight |
| Already have a maxed Fruit build and don't want to respec | Skip Ghost | You'd be buying a fruit you can't use properly without reallocating stats |
| Love long, multi-hit sword combos with wide coverage | Sword build | Mirror adds a second swing to every skill, which patches whiffs and widens arcs |
| Prefer burst damage and simple confirms | Skip Ghost, take Dark or Dragon | Ghost's damage is spread across mirrored hits, not a single big nuke |

The clean rule: if you're not willing to put your primary stat into Sword, Ghost is the wrong fruit for you. It's not a bad fruit — it's just a sword fruit now, and half of its value lives in a stat you might not be investing.

## Two Loadouts That Work Right Now

**Build 1 — The Mirror Brawler (Ghost + Pole V2 + Sharkman Karate).** This is the community pick after the rework, and it's the one I'd start with.

- Fruit: Ghost (Rare Natural, 940k Beli / 1,275 Robux)
- Sword: Pole V2 — its X end-lag got cut in half this patch, so mirrored follow-ups chain fast
- Fighting style: Sharkman Karate — C gives a temporary HP buff on activation, which you pop right before you dive in
- Stat split: Sword maxed, Defense and Melee secondary, Fruit minimum
- Game plan: Pop V before engaging, land X to break Instinct, commit with the sword while the clones mirror everything, use Sharkman C as your engage buffer. You win the trade because your damage lands twice and you have a revive as insurance.

**Build 2 — The Instinct-Break Assassin (Awakened Dark + Dark Blade + Death Step).** If you test Ghost and decide the mirror play isn't for you but you still want that post-patch sword-adjacent pressure, this is the fallback. Awakened Dark's X breaks Instinct, Death Step's V cooldown got halved so it's up almost every engagement, and Dark Blade is the highest-damage blade for confirming kills. It's a different philosophy — burst instead of mirrored coverage — but it shares Ghost's sword-first stat spread, so respecing between the two is nearly free.

The reason Build 1 works and Build 2 exists: they both lean on the patch's two big sword-side buffs — Pole V2's faster X and Dark Blade's raw output. If you're torn, ask yourself one question. Do you want your damage to land *twice* (Ghost), or land *once but hard* (Dark)? ## The Counter-Intuitive Parts of Playing Ghost

Three things about Ghost feel wrong at first, and they're all true.

**This is counterintuitive: Ghost is a sword fruit, and the fruit abilities are the support.** Every instinct you have from other fruits says the fruit is the damage source and the sword is a sidearm. With Ghost post-rework, it's the reverse. Your fruit moves exist to freeze, break Instinct, and knock down. Your sword is the actual weapon, and the clones are just your sword landing a second time. Play it like a sword main who carries a weird utility fruit, not a fruit main who happens to own a blade.

**This is counterintuitive: don't use the revive as a panic button.** The resurrection passive gives you 50% HP and 2 seconds of invincibility when you die. Most players treat it as a "get out of jail free" card and burn it the first time they're losing. That's backwards. Against good players, the revive is a second round — you get a clean reset with half a bar and two seconds to reposition. Use those two seconds to get space and set up your V-mirror engage, not to mash a panic escape into the same wall you died on. Players who panic after reviving die twice to the same combo. Players who treat it as a fresh neutral reset turn it into a win condition.

**This is counterintuitive: pressing V before you see the enemy is correct.** You'd think you'd save V for when you're close enough to swing. But the clones need a moment to form, and the summon has frames where you're not attacking. Pop V as you're closing distance, not when you're already in their face. By the time you're in range, the clones are up and swinging with you from the first input. If you wait until you're point-blank, you've already wasted part of the 20-second window on the summon animation.

## Who Ghost Beats — and Who Folds You

Ghost's matchups split cleanly along one line: can they survive your mirrored sword pressure, or do they need to run?

**Ghost wins against:** melee brawlers and slow tanks that have to walk into your range. Buddha players who stand there and M1-trade get doubled to death, because your mirrored swings out-trade their single swings. Anything that relies on one big telegraphed engage — Magma, Leopard — gets interrupted by your Z freeze and punished with the mirror. And fruit builds that break Instinct once and then have nothing: your revive means their one-rotation kill attempt fails, and now they're on cooldown against a full-sword mirrored onslaught.

**Ghost folds to:** hit-and-run players who never let you finish a swing. Light and Portal users who land a quick combo and vanish will whittle you down while your clones swing at air. The mirror is only dangerous when you're in range, and a player who refuses to stay in range deletes your entire game plan. Zone controllers with strong range, like a good Dough or Venom, can also keep you at the edge of your arc where the mirror's extra coverage matters least.

**How to beat a Ghost player, if you're on the other side:** keep your distance after their first engage, don't panic when they revive (it's round two, not round three), and punish every whiffed sword skill — a whiffed swing means the clone whiffs too, and that's your opening. The moment you see V come out, count the 20 seconds. Once the clones expire, Ghost is just a mid fruit with a revive and no mirror. That's when you push. For the Instinct side of these matchups, the [Observation Haki guide](/posts/blox-fruits/observation-haki-complete-guide/) goes into dodge timing against exactly these setups.

## Is Ghost Actually S-Tier?

Depends on who's asking.

For a sword main who's willing to respec, yes. The rework gave Ghost something no other fruit has: a way to double your sword damage and widen every arc, on a kit that already had a free second life. That combination — doubled sword pressure plus a revive — is why the fruit jumped from meme to meta pick inside a week. It's not the strongest fruit in the game. Dragon still hits harder, Dark still breaks Instinct more reliably, Werewolf still out-sustains. But for a specific player — the sword main who wants a fruit that supports the blade instead of replacing it — Ghost is arguably the best pick that exists right now.

For everyone else, it's a trap. Build it on Fruit and it's the same old revive bot it always was. The tier only opens up if you're actually investing in the stat the rework rewards.

So the real question isn't "is Ghost S-tier." It's "are you a sword player." If the answer is yes, respec, grab a Pole V2 or a Dark Blade, and go pop V. If the answer is no, this fruit will frustrate you — and the [fruits tier list](/posts/blox-fruits/fruits-tier-list/) will point you at something that fits better.
