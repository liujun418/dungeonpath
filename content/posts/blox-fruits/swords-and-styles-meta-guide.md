## You Load Your Old Build and Get Farmed in Three Trades

You log into a Third Sea server near the Café, same loadout you've run for months: Yama in the sword slot, Sanguine Art for melee, a fruit you know inside out. You spot a 5M bounty player, close the gap, and fire Yama X to pull them in and break their Instinct. Standard opener. You've done this a thousand times.

Except this time, their Instinct doesn't break. They dodge the pull, flash-step behind you, and land a Death Step combo that deletes half your health bar before you can turn around. You try to reset with Sanguine C, but the range is shorter than you remember — you whiff, they punish the whiff, and you're dead. Your Yama never landed. Your Sanguine never connected. Your entire game plan collapsed on the first two inputs.

That's Balance Patch 001, which dropped August 6, 2026. It didn't tweak a few numbers. It reshuffled which swords and fighting styles are worth equipping, and it did it in ways that aren't obvious from the patch notes. If you're still running the same loadout you ran in July, you're losing trades you used to win — and the reason isn't that you got worse.

This guide is not a catalog of every sword and fighting style. Those already exist: the [swords and weapons guide](/posts/blox-fruits/swords-weapons-guide/) lists every blade, and the [fighting styles guide](/posts/blox-fruits/fighting-styles-guide/) walks through every style. This guide is the post-patch meta piece — which sword to actually equip right now, which fighting style pairs with what, and why the correct answer depends on how you play, not on a tier list.

## The Patch in One Paragraph

Balance Patch 001 hit swords, fighting styles, guns, and core combat systems all at once. Swords: Dark Blade buffed, Pole V2 X end-lag halved, Twin Hooks got predictable landings, Kabucha and Venom Bow got stun upgrades, Yama lost its X Instinct break, Dragonheart's Instinct drain was halved, Skull Guitar's hitbox shrunk 15%, and True Triple Katana traded slower end-lag for faster travel speed. Fighting styles: Death Step got massive range and cooldown buffs, Sharkman Karate was reworked (C now grants temporary HP, X no longer launches), Sanguine Art was nerfed (C range and hitbox both down). System-level: all Gun M1 attacks got Aim Assist, and Instinct dodge regen dropped from 40 seconds to 30 seconds. That last one affects everything — every combo, every matchup, every dodge-bait window you've memorized.

If you want the full patch breakdown across all categories, start with the [Summer PvP Update guide](/posts/blox-fruits/summer-pvp-update-guide/). This article zooms in on the sword-and-style layer specifically.

## Swords: The New Hierarchy

The patch didn't just buff some swords and nerf others. It changed what swords are *for*. Before the patch, the sword meta was about two things: raw damage (True Triple Katana, Dark Blade) and combo initiation (Yama). After the patch, it's about three things: damage, end-lag, and whether your sword can chain into a fighting style without the enemy escaping.

**Dark Blade** got a straight damage buff. It was already a top-three blade; now it hits harder. If you own it, keep it equipped. The buff doesn't change how you play it — it just makes the same combos kill faster.

**Pole V2** is the sleeper winner. Its X end-lag dropped from 0.3 seconds to 0.15 seconds. That sounds like a tiny number. It isn't. End-lag is the recovery animation after an ability fires — the window where you can't act and the enemy can. Cut it in half, and you can chain Pole V2 X directly into a fighting style Z or a fruit move without a gap. Before the patch, a good player could flash out of your Pole V2 X recovery. Now they can't. That one change turns Pole V2 from a niche pick into a combo engine, and it's the reason the [Ghost Fruit PvP guide](/posts/blox-fruits/ghost-fruit-pvp-guide/) recommends Pole V2 as the post-rework pairing — the mirrored sword swings from Ghost V chain even faster with the halved end-lag.

**Yama** took the hardest nerf. Its X no longer breaks Instinct. The pull still works, the range is the same, and the damage is untouched. But the Instinct break was the entire reason Yama was the universal combo initiator. Without it, a player who sees the pull coming can dodge it, and your combo dies before it starts. Yama is not dead — you can still use it as a follow-up tool after landing a stun from your fruit or fighting style. It's just not the opener anymore. If you were a Yama main, you have two options: switch to a sword with a stun or Instinct break (Pole V2, Dark Blade), or rework your combo so the opener comes from a different slot.

**True Triple Katana** got an adjustment, not a straight buff or nerf. X end-lag increased by 0.5 seconds, but travel speed increased by 25%. The net effect: TTK is better at chasing and worse at chaining. If you used TTK X as a mid-combo link, you'll feel the extra recovery frames. If you used it as a gap-closer or a finisher, the faster projectile makes it more reliable. TTK is still the best grinding sword in the game — nothing touches its raw M1 damage — but its PvP combo role got narrower.

**Dragonheart** got its Instinct drain halved. Before the patch, Dragonheart was a niche anti-Instinct specialist. Now it's just a decent sword with a mediocre gimmick. If you were running it specifically to drain Instinct, switch to a sword that kills faster instead.

**Skull Guitar** lost 15% of its hitbox. The damage is the same, but the smaller hitbox means you'll whiff more often against players who know how to sidestep. It's still usable. It's just less forgiving.

**Kabucha** and **Venom Bow** got stun upgrades that matter for sword users who carry a gun in the offhand. Kabucha's Z now stuns instead of knocking up, which means the target stays in place instead of sailing out of sword range. Venom Bow's X stuns even when the target dodges with Instinct. Both are gun changes, but they affect sword combos: a gun stun is now a reliable way to set up a sword kill, especially if your sword doesn't have its own stun.

**Twin Hooks** got predictable landings — the knockback trajectory is now consistent instead of random. That sounds minor, but it means you can plan a follow-up instead of guessing where the enemy will land. For a sword that already had decent damage, predictable positioning is a real buff.

## Fighting Styles: What to Pair With What

The fighting style changes mirror the sword changes: movement toward aggression and away from passive play.

**Death Step** is the biggest winner among fighting styles. Z range and hitbox both increased by 50%. C end-lag dropped to 0.1 seconds — functionally instant recovery. V cooldown was halved, so you can use it almost every engagement. The result: Death Step went from a B-tier style you grinded for God Human prerequisites to an A-tier aggressive option that pairs naturally with sword builds. If you want to close distance, land a fighting style combo, and confirm with your sword, Death Step is the pick. Pair it with Dark Blade for raw damage, or with Pole V2 for a fast-chaining double-combo.

**Sharkman Karate** got reworked, not just buffed. Its C now grants temporary HP based on damage absorbed. The hold can't be cancelled. X no longer launches opponents upward. The net effect: Sharkman shifted from an offensive launcher to a sustain tool. Before the patch, you picked Sharkman for the X launch combo. Now you pick it for the C HP buffer — you pop C before engaging, tank the enemy's opener, and win the trade because you had more effective HP. This pairs well with sword builds that need to survive the engage phase. If you're running a squishy sword build with low Defense, Sharkman's C is your insurance policy.

**Sanguine Art** got nerfed. C range dropped 15%, C hitbox dropped 10%. The spacing you memorized is wrong, and you'll whiff more often. Sanguine's lifesteal is still strong, but the nerf makes it harder to land the moves that trigger it. Most Sanguine players should switch to Death Step — it feels like old Sanguine but with faster cooldowns and bigger hitboxes. If you're committed to lifesteal, run Werewolf race instead (3% lifesteal passive in transformed form) and pair it with Death Step or Sharkman.

**Electric Claw** wasn't touched by the patch, but the meta shift around it makes it more relevant. Electric Claw is still the fastest-attacking style in the game, and its stun is still one of the best combo starters. With Yama's Instinct break gone, Electric Claw's stun is now one of the few reliable ways to open a combo against a target with Instinct up. Pair it with a sword that has strong follow-up damage (Dark Blade, Cursed Dual Katana) and treat the claw as your opener, not your finisher.

The system-level changes matter here too. Instinct dodge regen dropped from 40s to 30s, which means every player has their dodge available more often. Fighting styles that rely on landing a single big opener (old Sanguine, old Sharkman X launch) are punished harder because the opener gets dodged more frequently. Fighting styles with fast, multi-hit chains (Death Step, Electric Claw) are rewarded because they pressure the opponent's dodge timing more effectively.

## The Counterintuitive Truth: Pole V2's 0.15s Matters More Than Dark Blade's Name Value

Dark Blade is the flashy winner. It got a buff, it's a mythic, it costs Robux, and every tier list puts it at the top. If you have it, use it. But the sword that actually changed the most — and the one that will win you more fights you used to lose — is Pole V2.

Here's why: end-lag is the real combo killer. A sword ability that hits for 5,000 damage but leaves you stuck in recovery for 0.3 seconds is worse than a sword ability that hits for 3,000 damage and recovers in 0.15 seconds. The 0.3-second window is long enough for a good player to flash out, dodge your follow-up, and punish you. The 0.15-second window is not — by the time their brain registers that your X fired, you're already into your next input.

Pole V2's X end-lag was cut in half. That means a Pole V2 X into Death Step Z is now a true chain — the enemy can't act between the two moves. A Dark Blade X into Death Step Z still has a gap. Dark Blade hits harder, but Pole V2 hits more reliably, and in PvP, reliability beats raw damage every time.

This is the same principle that makes Electric Claw good: speed creates windows that damage can't. If you're trying to decide between swords, don't look at the damage number first. Look at the end-lag. The sword with the shortest recovery is the one that lets you chain the most moves, and the player who chains the most moves wins the trade.

## Failure Retrospective: The Instinct-Dodge-Regen Build That Broke Overnight

Here's a real scenario that happened to a lot of players in the first week after the patch.

**The error:** You built your entire playstyle around the 40-second Instinct dodge cooldown. You memorized the timing: bait their dodge, count to 40 in your head, and full-combo them while they're vulnerable. Against aggressive players, you'd dodge their opener, wait for them to commit, and then punish because you knew exactly when your dodge was coming back. You had a rhythm, and it worked.

**The cause:** The patch dropped Instinct dodge regen from 40 seconds to 30 seconds. You didn't read the patch notes closely, or you read them but didn't think a 10-second change would matter. You loaded into a server, ran the same rhythm, and got punished.

**The failure cascade:** You dodge the enemy's opener. You count to 40 in your head. At second 31, you commit to a full combo — and their dodge is already back. They flash out of your second move, counter-combo you while you're in recovery, and you die. You try again. Same thing. You assume the enemy is just better than you, or that the server is lagging, or that the patch broke something. None of those are true. Your internal clock is wrong, and every fight you take on the old timing is a fight you lose.

**The recovery:** Stop counting to 40. The new number is 30. If you're the aggressor, shorten your combos to three moves max — you don't have the 40-second window anymore, and a four-move combo will get dodged before the finisher. If you're the defender, you're stronger now: you get your dodge back faster, which means you can bait more aggressively and punish more often. The defensive playstyle got a buff, and if you're not taking advantage of it, you're leaving free wins on the table.

The deeper lesson: if your entire build depends on a single cooldown number, a patch can break it overnight. Builds that rely on spacing, hit confirm, and fast chaining are more patch-resistant than builds that rely on memorized timers. ## The Ghost Factor: Why Sword Builds Got a Second Wind

One fighting style change that isn't in the fighting style section: Ghost Fruit's reworked V now mirrors sword skills. That's a fruit change, not a fighting style change, but it affects the sword-and-style meta directly because it creates a new build archetype: the sword main who uses a fruit purely for utility.

The loadout is Ghost + Pole V2 (or Dark Blade) + Death Step (or Sharkman). Pop V before engaging, let the clones mirror every sword swing, and use the fighting style to cover the gap between sword cooldowns. The clones last about 20 seconds, clone M1s hit for roughly 50% of your normal M1 damage, and the mirrored sword skills add coverage and pressure more than raw triple damage.

This build works because the patch buffed both the sword (Pole V2's faster end-lag) and the fighting style (Death Step's bigger hitboxes and faster cooldowns) that pair with it. It's a patch-native build — every piece of it got better in the same update. If you're building from scratch after the patch, this is the most coherent option. ## What's Unconfirmed

This article is meta-analysis, not a changelog. The confirmed numbers come from the patch notes and community testing. The interpretations — which sword is "best," which pairing is "optimal" — are inferences based on how the numbers interact. Here's what's inference and what's confirmed.

**Confirmed:** Dark Blade buffed, Pole V2 X end-lag 0.3s to 0.15s, Yama X no longer breaks Instinct, Dragonheart Instinct drain halved, Skull Guitar hitbox -15%, TTK X end-lag +0.5s and travel speed +25%, Twin Hooks predictable landings, Kabucha Z stuns instead of knockup, Venom Bow X stuns through Instinct dodge. Death Step Z range/hitbox +50%, C end-lag 0.1s, V cooldown halved. Sharkman Karate C grants temporary HP, hold can't be cancelled, X no longer launches. Sanguine C range -15%, hitbox -10%. Gun M1 Aim Assist, Instinct dodge regen 40s to 30s. Ghost V mirrors sword skills, clones ~20s, clone M1 ~50%.

**Inferred:** The claim that Pole V2 X into Death Step Z is a true chain is based on the end-lag numbers but hasn't been frame-counted. The claim that Yama is still viable as a follow-up tool is based on the pull being unchanged but depends on the specific combo. The claim that Death Step feels like old Sanguine is a community sentiment, not a data point. The matchup implications of the 30s Instinct regen are inferred from the math but depend on player skill, ping, and specific fruit interactions.

If you're building a competitive loadout, test the combos yourself in the Arena before committing Fragments and Beli. The numbers are right. The feel is personal.
