> *Last updated: August 27, 2026. This guide covers Fallen mode in Tower Defense Simulator: the economy-first build order, wave-by-wave strategy, mini-boss and Fallen King counters, and the loadout that survives all 40 waves. Exact enemy HP values shift with balance patches, so treat hard numbers as approximate.*

## The Wave 20 Wipe

You clear Molten easily and queue Fallen solo, feeling ready. You rush your first Accelerator by wave 8 and skip your second Farm to afford it. It carries the early waves. Then, around wave 20, a Void Reaver spawns with a wall of Fallen Guardians trailing it. Your Accelerator is only level 2, your gold is gone, and the Reaver strolls past your base with half its health bar left.

That wipe is the Fallen learning curve in one scene. Fallen is not Molten with bigger numbers. It is a test of economy discipline first and damage second. Every failed run comes down to the same thing: spending on DPS before the Farm rush is done.

Fallen is the hardest standard mode in Tower Defense Simulator. It unlocks after Molten, runs 40 waves, and ends with the Fallen King, a boss with a huge health pool that summons minions mid-fight. Enemies hit harder, have more health, and your income is tighter than any earlier mode.

## What Fallen Mode Actually Changes

Fallen feels like the same game until wave 10, then it stops pretending.

The big changes:

- **Income is tighter.** You start with the same base cash, but upgrades cost more and the margin for mistakes shrinks.
- **Enemy health scales hard.** Walkers that died to one early tower shot now eat through a whole magazine.
- **Mini-bosses arrive mid-run, not just at the end.** The Void Reaver lands around wave 20, Fallen Titan and Fallen Guardian spawns follow, then the Fallen King on wave 40.
- **Speed matters.** Fallen Fast enemies are genuinely fast, and without slows they outrun your single-target towers.

Compare that to [Hardcore mode](/games/tower-defense-simulator/), which starves your income even harder and gates you by level. Fallen is the natural step between Molten and Hardcore: it teaches the Farm-first discipline that Hardcore demands. The single biggest shift is economy. In Normal you can limp through with sloppy gold management. In Fallen, a bad waves 5-15 economy means you are dead at wave 30 no matter how good your towers are.

## Unlock Path and What to Bring

Fallen unlocks once you clear Molten mode. There is no level requirement to queue it, but you do need the right towers. Going in with a Molten loadout is a fast ticket to the wipe screen.

You get five or six loadout slots depending on gamepasses. In a team, roles split: one player runs economy with Farms, one runs heavy DPS with the Accelerator or Minigunner, one runs support with the Commander and DJ, and one handles crowd control. Solo, you pack all of it into one loadout.

A solid solo Fallen loadout looks like this:

- **Farm** — non-negotiable. Your entire early game is Farm upgrades.
- **Accelerator** — your best boss killer. It ramps damage the longer it fires, so it melts the Fallen King.
- **Minigunner or Ranger** — your mid-game DPS and early boss damage before the Accelerator ramps up.
- **Commander** — the damage buff. Save its ability for boss spikes.
- **DJ** — range buff plus a discount on upgrades. Place it to cover your damage towers.
- **A slow or stun tower** — Electroshocker, Sledger, or Cryomancer for the fast and swarm waves.

For a full ranking of what earns a slot, see the [TDS Towers Tier List](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/towers-tier-list/). For how to assemble a loadout across modes, read the [TDS Loadout Guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/loadout-guide/).

If you don't own the Accelerator yet, you can substitute stacked Minigunners or Rangers, but the boss kill gets noticeably slower. The Accelerator's ramping single-target damage is the whole point — it is the tower that actually ends the Fallen King.

## Economy: The Farm Rush Plan

Fallen is won or lost in the first fifteen waves, and those waves are all about Farms.

The plan is simple. Place a Farm on wave 1 or 2. Drop a cheap damage tower — a Scout or a level-1 Minigunner — to hold the first few waves. Then pour almost every coin into Farm upgrades until you have three or four Farms at level 3-4 by around wave 12-15.

Milestones to hit:

- **Waves 1-2:** first Farm down, one cheap damage tower.
- **Wave 5:** second Farm down, first Farm to level 2.
- **Waves 8-10:** third Farm down, first Farm to level 3.
- **Waves 12-15:** Farms at level 3+, now start the real DPS.
- **Wave 20+:** Farms maxed, shift income into Commander and Accelerator upgrades.

If you are behind this schedule, stop buying damage towers. Every coin goes into Farms until you catch up. The run that wipes at wave 30 is always the one that was late on Farms at wave 10.

## Wave-Phase Strategy

Fallen breaks into three phases, and the decisions change at each one.

### Early (waves 1-10): farm, don't fight

Your only job is to survive cheaply while Farms grow. One Scout or low-level Minigunner at the curve holds the early walkers. Don't build more than you need — leaked gold now is a missing Farm later. Place Farms near your damage zone so you can defend them and still reach the path. For where to build that kill zone, see [Tower Placement Theory](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/tower-placement-theory/).

### Mid (waves 11-30): damage comes online

This is where you convert gold into towers. Get your Minigunner or Ranger leveled, then drop your first Accelerator around wave 15-20. The Void Reaver arrives around wave 20, and it is the first real DPS check. Don't face it with level-1 towers.

Around the late 20s, the Fallen Titan and Fallen Guardian mini-bosses show up. They are tanky, not fast, so single-target damage works — but they come with support waves that need your slow or stun tower.

### Boss phase (waves 30-40): save everything for the King

From wave 30 on, stop spending on anything except the final setup. Max your Accelerators, put Commander and DJ abilities on cooldown discipline, and bank gold for an emergency. The Fallen King spawns on wave 40 with a huge health pool and summons minions throughout the fight.

## The Mini-Bosses, Wave by Wave

Fallen's mini-bosses are the parts most players forget to prepare for. They are spaced out so you face one before you have finished the last.

The **Void Reaver** shows up around wave 20. It is tanky, slow, and usually spawns with a support wave. Treat it as a DPS check: if your single-target towers can't bring it down before it reaches the base, your economy was late. Focus it with everything and pop one buff if it is close.

The **Fallen Titan** and **Fallen Guardian** appear in the late 20s. They are big health pools with small minion escorts. Split your fire: heavy towers on the mini-boss, crowd control on the escorts. Don't burn your Commander ability here unless one of them is about to leak — the King needs it more.

The **Fallen King** is the run-ender. Its health pool is huge and scales a bit with player count, so expect to need several hundred thousand damage before it drops. It summons Fallen Swordsmen first, then Fallen Titans and Fallen Guardians as the fight goes on. The summons are what actually kill most teams: everyone stares at the King's health bar while a pack walks around the side.

## Fallen King Strategy

Here is the sequence.

Before wave 40, position your damage towers where they can hit the boss path from the moment the King spawns. Max your Accelerators. Make sure the Commander and DJ cover them.

When the King spawns:

1. Pop the Commander ability immediately so your ramping damage starts high.
2. Hold the DJ ability for when the summons appear — the range helps your towers pick off adds while still hitting the King.
3. Crowd control ignores the King entirely. Your slow or stun tower focuses the summon wave.
4. Don't panic-place new towers mid-fight. Placements now come online too slowly to matter. Pre-place everything before wave 40.

The summons are the priority until they are controlled, then all fire returns to the King. Repeat that rhythm each summon wave and the King dies before it reaches your base.

## What Went Wrong: The Wave 20 Wipe

This is the exact run from the opening.

**The mistake:** I rushed a third Accelerator at wave 15 before my Farms were leveled. The plan was "more DPS beats the mini-boss." The early waves melted, so it felt right.

**Why it failed:** my Farms were still level 2, so I had no income to upgrade the Accelerators past level 2. When the Void Reaver arrived around wave 20, I was holding three weak Accelerators and no gold. The Reaver tanked everything and walked through.

**The recovery:** I restarted, dropped to two Farms and pushed them to level 3 before touching a second Accelerator. The Void Reaver died this time with room to spare, and the extra income carried the whole run. The lesson stuck: **a level 3 Farm beats a level 2 Accelerator in the mid-game every time.** More towers with no income is just a slower wipe.

## If-Then Decision Table

Use this when you are not sure what to build next.

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Your Farms are below level 3 before wave 12 | Upgrade Farms first; skip the damage tower you wanted |
| The Void Reaver is 2 waves out and your DPS is level 1 | Level your best single-target tower now, hold the buff |
| A swarm wave is leaking the far lane | Drop or level a slow or stun tower, not another DPS tower |
| The Fallen King is 1 wave out | Stop all spending, pre-place damage on the boss path, save abilities |
| You have 3+ maxed Farms and spare gold in the 20s | Convert to damage: max Accelerators and Commander first |

## The Counterintuitive Tip

Skipping the damage tower to fix your economy is the single most important Fallen skill. Every new player's instinct is "I'm losing damage, buy more damage." In Fallen that's backwards. The tower you skip at wave 10 becomes three towers at wave 20 if the gold goes into Farms instead. A maxed Farm beats a second DPS tower in the mid-game: the Farm prints gold while the extra DPS tower sits idle. When in doubt, build the Farm.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I unlock Fallen mode in TDS?

Beat Molten mode. Once Molten is cleared, Fallen appears on the mode select. There is no separate level gate, but the towers you bring matter more than your level — coming in with a Molten loadout gets you wiped before the first mini-boss.

### What's the best loadout for Fallen mode?

Farm, Accelerator, a mid-game DPS tower like Minigunner or Ranger, Commander, DJ, and a slow or stun tower such as Electroshocker, Sledger, or Cryomancer. That six covers economy, boss damage, buffs, and crowd control. If you are short a slot, drop the crowd control first and lean on team support. For the slot-by-slot reasoning, see the [TDS Loadout Guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/loadout-guide/).

### Can Fallen mode be soloed?

Yes. Solo Fallen is a real challenge but very doable with a maxed-out loadout and clean Farm discipline. A team is more forgiving because roles split the economy pressure, but solo gives you full control of the build order, which is often more consistent than a random lobby. Our [Co-op Guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/coop-guide/) covers how to split roles if you go multiplayer.

### What's the most important tower for the Fallen King?

The Accelerator. Its damage ramps up the longer it fires, which is exactly what a giant boss health pool demands. A few maxed Accelerators under a Commander buff are what actually kill the King before it reaches your base. Commander ability timing matters almost as much as the tower itself.

### Is Fallen worth farming over other modes for coins?

For raw coin-per-hour, not always. Fallen pays well but takes longer and risks more than Molten. If you are after coins, the Golden mode and a dedicated Farm setup win out. Check the [Golden Mode Guide](/games/tower-defense-simulator/) and the [Coin Farming Guide](/games/tower-defense-simulator/) for the comparison. If you are after the endgame challenge, Fallen is the hardest standard mode and the natural step before Hardcore.
