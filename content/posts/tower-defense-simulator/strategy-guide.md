## The Same Loadout, Different Results

You and another player run the exact same towers: Militant, Farm, Commander, DJ, Minigunner. They clear Fallen Mode. You leak at wave 28. Same towers, same upgrade order, same map. Nothing about the setup is different.

What's different is a handful of coin-spending choices you didn't know you were making. TDS throws five or six decision points at you per game — moments where you have coins in hand and two options that both sound reasonable. Farm upgrade or a second DPS tower? Max one tower or place a new one? Sell the Militant or keep it limping along?

New players don't see these as decisions. They just buy whatever they can afford and hope. Hope is not a strategy. Here's the thing: the game already told you the answer. It's in the coins, the wave number, and the damage your towers actually deal. You just have to read it.

This guide breaks down the five decisions that decide most TDS games, why one side of each is wrong, and how to recover when you pick badly.

## The 5 Decision Points

### Decision 1: Wave 5 — Farm or Defense?

You have 700 coins. Farm costs 400. Militant costs 500.

**The mistake:** You buy the Militant because "I need defense." Wave 5 enemies have 10 HP. Your starter tower one-shots them, and it keeps doing that through wave 8. Your new Militant does nothing useful for three whole waves, while the other player drops a Farm and starts printing coins.

**Why it happens:** You're judging "what kills things" instead of "what makes coins." Early waves don't need more damage. They need your economy to come online before the real waves hit.

**How to recover:** If you already bought the Militant, don't sell it — that eats a 30% tax on 500 coins. Just place a Farm next, before you spend anything on upgrades. You've lost one wave of income, not the game. The Militant will do real work later.

**Why the opposite is wrong:** A level-2 Farm by wave 10 has generated 1,750 coins. It paid for itself and left you about 1,050 coins ahead of the Militant-first player. Money compounds in TDS. Damage doesn't, until you can afford it.

### Decision 2: Wave 12 — Second Farm or Commander?

Two level-2 Farms. 1,200 coins in hand. Commander costs 800 and gives +25% fire rate to nearby towers. A second Farm runs about 700 total and makes more income.

**The mistake:** You buy Commander at wave 12 because "+25% fire rate" sounds like power. But you have one level-2 Militant on the field. Plus 25% of a weak tower is still weak. Your Commander has nothing meaningful to buff, and now you're broke.

**Why it happens:** You bought a multiplier before you had a base. A buff's value scales with the damage already standing there. No damage, no value.

**How to recover:** Place the second Farm for 700, then buy Commander on wave 14 or 15 once the Farm income has topped you back up. You're two waves later, but now the Commander buffs a real DPS tower and you still have income behind it.

**Why the opposite is wrong:** The second Farm finances the Commander. Commander first leaves you flat-broke at the exact moment your DPS tower needs its next level. A buff with nothing to buff is a decoration, and you paid 800 for it.

### Decision 3: Wave 20 — DJ or Max Militant?

Boss incoming. Your Militant is level 2. You can max it to level 3 for 1,800 coins, or place DJ for 1,200. DJ gives +15% range and a 10% discount on upgrades.

**The mistake:** You dump everything into maxing the Militant. The boss comes, your single tower hits hard, but it can't cover enough track, and enemies leak on the far side while you stare at your one strong tower.

**Why it happens:** You treat damage and range as two separate problems. They're the same problem. DJ's +15% range is effectively 15% more track covered by every tower, and the 10% discount makes every future upgrade cheaper.

**How to recover:** Buy DJ first, then upgrade the Militant. DJ's discount applies to the upgrade itself, saving roughly 180 coins. If you already maxed the Militant, drop DJ immediately after — before the next upgrade cycle — so the discount hits everything from here on.

**Why the opposite is wrong:** DJ plus a level-2 Militant outperforms a level-3 Militant alone. Range and discount are DPS in TDS, just hidden behind different labels. A bigger single tower can't cover two lanes at once.

### Decision 4: Wave 27 — Sell or Keep?

Your level-2 Militant now deals 8% damage per shot to wave 27 enemies. It's effectively useless. Selling returns about 1,260 coins. Minigunner costs 1,500.

**The mistake:** You keep the Militant out of sentiment — "I already paid for it." It chips 8% per shot while wave 27 enemies stroll past. It's dead weight eating a placement slot and your attention.

**Why it happens:** Sunk-cost thinking. You're judging the tower by what it cost, not by what it's doing right now. What it cost is gone. What it's doing is nothing.

**How to recover:** Sell it. That 1,260 coins is 84% of a Minigunner. It's not a loss — it's a down payment. You don't recover a sunk cost by keeping a bad tower on the field; you recover it by converting the tower into a better one.

**Why the opposite is wrong:** Any tower dealing under 15% damage per shot is negative value. It occupies a slot a real tower could use, and it makes you feel safer than you are. The 30% sell penalty is the cheapest way out. Keeping a bad tower because selling "loses money" is how you die with coins locked inside a useless tower.

### Decision 5: Wave 32 — Max One or Place Multiple?

4,500 coins. Minigunner at level 2. Option A: max the Minigunner to level 4 for 3,200. Option B: buy a second DPS tower for 2,500 or more.

**The mistake:** You buy the second tower because "more towers equals more damage." Now you have two half-upgraded towers, neither fully buffed, both leaking. You split your coins and ended up with two mediocre towers instead of one great one.

**Why it happens:** You think tower count scales linearly. In TDS, Commander's fire rate and DJ's range are multiplicative — they multiply one strong tower's output. Two half towers each get a fraction of that same buff.

**How to recover:** Max the Minigunner to level 4. One maxed tower under full Commander + DJ buffs out-damages two un-upgraded towers. If you already bought the second tower, commit: upgrade one of the two and treat the other as lane coverage, not a carry.

**Why the opposite is wrong:** Buff stacking is multiplicative, tower count is additive. One level-4 tower with +25% fire rate and +15% range beats two level-2 towers that each only absorb part of the support. You're not buying damage with the second tower — you're buying a tax on your own buffs.

## The Counterintuitive Move: Sell Your Farms on the Final Wave

This is counterintuitive. You spent the entire game building Farms. On the final wave, sell all of them.

Here's the logic. A Farm generates coins over time. On the final wave, there is no more time — the wave ends and the game is over. A Farm sitting at full health during the last wave is doing nothing. Selling every Farm returns thousands of coins you can dump into one last upgrade that actually fires.

Most players hoard their Farms to the end out of pure instinct. That instinct loses games. Coins in a Farm on the last wave are coins that aren't shooting. The final wave is the one place in TDS where economy is worthless and only damage matters, so liquidate everything and put it on the towers.

While we're at it: buying nothing is also a decision. If a decision point arrives and neither option clearly helps you clear the next wave, holding your coins for one wave is a valid call. Spending because you can afford it — not because it's right — is a leak, and leaks are how runs die slowly.

## The Decision Framework

When you're mid-run and can't remember the guide, use this. It's the whole article compressed into an if-then table.

| Situation | Right call |
|---|---|
| Wave 5, 700 coins, no Farm placed | Farm, then level 2 — skip the Militant |
| Wave 12, two Farms, 1,200 coins | Second Farm first, Commander on wave 14-15 |
| Wave 20, boss incoming, Militant at level 2 | DJ first, then upgrade the Militant |
| Any tower dealing under 15% per shot | Sell it and put the coins toward the next carry |
| Wave 32, one carry at level 2, 4,500 coins | Max the one carry instead of buying another |
| Final wave, Farms still placed | Sell them all, dump it into one last upgrade |

## How to Actually Practice These Decisions

Reading this once won't change your runs. You'll still panic at wave 20 and buy whatever's affordable. So drill it deliberately.

Play one run where you narrate every purchase out loud: "Wave 5, 700 coins, no Farm — Farm first." The narration forces the decision into your awareness instead of letting it stay automatic. Do that for three runs and the framework stops being a table you read and becomes a reflex you run.

Second drill: after every wipe, write down the exact wave and what you spent your last 1,000 coins on. Most players can't remember, which is the problem — the losing decision was invisible to them. Once you can name the decision you got wrong, you're halfway to not making it again. ## Quick Scenario Answers

**"I keep dying around wave 25-28. What's my actual problem?"**

It's almost never your placement. It's that you never switched from early economy to mid-game damage. Either you built too many Farms and have no DPS, or you kept upgrading a Militant you should have sold and replaced. Check the bank: at wave 25 you should have Commander and DJ placed and at least 3,000 coins already committed toward your Minigunner. If you're at wave 27 with a level-2 Militant doing 8% per shot and no Minigunner, you didn't make the sell decision in time.

**"Is it ever right to place a tower I can't afford to upgrade yet?"**

Yes. A level-1 tower that covers a lane you'd otherwise leak is worth more than coins in the bank that let the wave through. Placing a cheap tower to hold a lane while you save is a holding play, not a waste. The trap is the reverse — upgrading a tower that already covers its lane while another lane sits empty. Coverage beats levels when enemies are getting through.

**"What changes if I'm playing duo instead of solo?"**

A lot. In duo, one player can greed economy while the other holds defense, which changes every decision above. Don't both farm, and don't both go pure DPS. Split the roles, and let the farm player rush Farms while the defense player covers early waves cheap. This is its own topic — see the [coop guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/coop-guide/) and the [solo strategies guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/solo-strategies-guide/) for the full split on that.

## The Bottom Line

Everything in this guide comes down to one principle: the game rewards preparation, not reaction. The players who clear consistently aren't faster. They've already made tomorrow's decisions today. They know which tower to place before the wave starts, which one to sell the moment it stops earning its slot, and where every coin goes before it arrives.

That's the whole skill. Not aim, not reflexes, not some secret loadout. It's reading the wave number, checking the bank, and making the call before the wave forces one on you. Preparation looks like luck to the unprepared. It's not luck. It's protocol.
