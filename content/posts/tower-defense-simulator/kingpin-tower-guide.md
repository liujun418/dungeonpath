You log into Tower Defense Simulator a day after the v2.3.0 update drops on July 27, 2026. You open the shop and there it is: Kingpin, a new Evolved Tower, sitting next to the Boomerang Tower and a refreshed Pirate Crate. The price tag stops you cold. Fifteen thousand coins. Five thousand five hundred gems. Oh, and a Level 20 Crook Boss first. You hover over the buy button and think: is this the new meta, or a gem sink wearing a fedora?

That's the exact question this guide answers. I'm not going to hand you a stat dump and call it a day. I'm going to walk you through what Kingpin actually does, when it's worth the grind, and the three specific ways players throw away their investment within an hour of buying it.

## What Kingpin Actually Is — and Why It's Not Just a Buffed Crook Boss

Here's the thing you need to internalize before spending a single gem: Kingpin is the evolved form of Crook Boss. It doesn't replace Crook Boss in your inventory. It's a separate tower you buy after you've already leveled your Crook Boss to 20. You hold Crook Boss in matches, it earns XP automatically, and once it hits 20 you get the privilege of paying 15,000 coins and 5,500 gems to unlock Kingpin.

The base tower gives you a hint of what you're paying for. It costs $3,600 to place, caps at 4 placements per player, and opens with 24 damage, a 1.1 second fire rate, and 17.5 range. On paper that's nothing special. Crook Boss already does a lot of that.

The difference shows up in three places, and each one matters more than the raw numbers:

**Lead penetration.** Base Crook Boss and Golden Crook Boss can't punch through Lead enemies. Kingpin can — top path at Level 4, bottom path at Level 6. If you've ever watched a Crook Boss dump an entire magazine into a Lead zombie and do nothing, you know exactly why this matters.

**The Bounty ability.** This is the real reason Kingpin exists. From Level 4 on the top path, you get a 150-second-cooldown ability that slaps a hit order on an enemy. Kingpin stops shooting for two seconds. When that marked enemy dies, you get a bonus percentage of its cash reward, capped at a ceiling. At Level 4 it's 50% up to $10,000. At Level 6 it's 100% up to $60,000. That's not a DPS tool. That's an economy engine.

**The summons.** Kingpin runs unit queues the way Crook Boss runs its goons, but with better options. We'll get into each one below, because choosing the wrong unit is the single most common way players sabotage themselves.

There's one more catch most guides skip: a freshly bought Kingpin can only upgrade to Level 4. Levels 5 and 6 on both paths are locked behind Kingpin's own evolution level, which you grind separately. So buying Kingpin isn't the finish line. It's the start of a second XP grind.

## How to Unlock Kingpin Without Wasting Your Resources

The unlock chain is strict, and I want you to see the full cost before you commit.

First, level Crook Boss to 20. If you already use Crook Boss in your rotation, this happens passively and you barely notice. If you don't, you're either grinding it or shelling out 599 Robux to skip the XP. Then you need 15,000 coins and 5,500 gems on hand.

The gems are the bottleneck. Five and a half thousand gems is a serious pile, and it's the same currency you'd spend on other gem-gated towers. So before you buy, ask the question the shop conveniently doesn't ask: what else could 5,500 gems buy you?

**Decision framework — Kingpin or Accelerator?**

If you don't own Accelerator yet, stop. Accelerator is still the S+ boss killer in the current [TDS tower tier list](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/towers-tier-list/), and it costs gems too. Spending 5,500 gems on Kingpin while you're still running without a real boss-damage tower is a trap. Kingpin is a versatile mid-game tower with an economy gimmick. It doesn't replace Accelerator, Engineer, or any of the hardcore gem towers.

So the rule is simple: if Accelerator is unlocked, and you have coins and gems sitting idle, Kingpin is a reasonable next gem purchase. If you don't have Accelerator yet, Kingpin waits.

The second check is your actual coin economy. Kingpin's Bounty ability only pays off if you're running modes where big enemies drop big cash. In Normal mode you'll barely feel it. In Fallen and Hardcore, one marked boss can refund a huge chunk of your setup. If you spend most of your time in Normal lobbies, the 15,000 coins buy you a tower whose best feature you'll never really use.

## The Two Paths, Explained Like You're Building a Run

Kingpin splits at Level 4 into a top path (income) and a bottom path (DPS and summons). You can't have both on a single Kingpin. You commit. Here's the branch map with the costs and, more importantly, the *why* behind each step. These numbers are the July 2026 v2.3.0 snapshot — treat them as close, not gospel, and check the wiki after any balance patch.

| Level | Path | Cost | What changes | Why it matters |
|-------|------|------|--------------|----------------|
| 1 | Neutral | $1,400 | Faster fire rate, more range | Cheapest early damage bump; do this while your eco ramps |
| 2 | Neutral | $4,000 | Second unit queue | Two summons instead of one means you finally feel the tower |
| 3 | Neutral | $6,000 | Flying detection | Without this, air enemies fly right past you in mid-game |
| 4 | Top | $7,250 | Bounty ability (50%, $10k cap) | The economy engine turns on; mark big enemies before they die |
| 4 | Bottom | $14,000 | Unlocks Bouncer summon | Your first real damage unit; carries mid-game waves |
| 5 | Top | $16,500 | Bounty 70%, $25k cap | Bigger bonus and ceiling; pays for itself faster on bosses |
| 5 | Bottom | $32,500 | Stronger summons | Bouncers hit harder; needed for Fallen mid-game |
| 6 | Top | $38,500 | Bounty 100%, $60k cap | Mark a Fallen boss, pocket up to $60k when it dies |
| 6 | Bottom | $64,000 | Unlocks Contractor sniper | Long-range Hidden/Lead/Flying coverage; the capstone |

Notice the shape of this table. The top path is cheap to start and gets more expensive in a smooth line. The bottom path is expensive to start and becomes brutal at the end. $64,000 for a single Level 6 bottom Kingpin is a whole late-game budget. Don't plan to max bottom path unless your economy is already rolling.

And here's the part most new Kingpin owners get wrong: the summons. You get four unit options across the game, and they are not interchangeable.

**Lackey** is your default SMG unit. Single target, decent DPS, and critically, it has Hidden detection. It's available from Level 0, which makes it the answer to a problem we'll circle back to.

**Money Runner** doesn't attack. It just runs at enemies and dies, and when it dies you get cash. It's pure economy, and it only makes sense on the top path where you're already committing to income.

**Bouncer** is the Level 4 bottom-path unit: a high-health melee goon with a bat. Hidden and Lead detection, high DPS, and enough HP to body-block. This is Kingpin's strongest combat summon for mid-game.

**Contractor** is the Level 6 bottom-path unit: a long-range sniper with all three detections. Slow fire rate, but it's the only summon that handles air, Lead, and Hidden in one package. Some community write-ups call this unit a hitman; functionally it's your late-game sniper.

## The Bounty Ability Is the Whole Reason You Bought It

Here's the counter-intuitive part, and I want you to sit with it for a second.

Kingpin *looks* like a DPS tower. The Bouncer is flashy, the Contractor is a sniper, and everyone screenshots the bottom path. So most players max the bottom path first and treat Bounty as an afterthought. That's backwards.

The top path's Bounty ability is what makes Kingpin's price tag actually make sense. A maxed Bounty marks one enemy for 100% bonus cash, capped at $60,000. In Fallen mode, the bosses in the back half drop serious money. Mark a high-value boss, let your team shred it, and you pocket up to $60,000 of pure bonus — off one ability press. That refunds entire towers. That's how you fund the $64,000 bottom Kingpin later, or how you bank an Accelerator for the final wave.

The catch is the cooldown: 150 seconds. So you don't get to spam it. You have to read the wave and mark the *right* enemy. Mark a random mob that dies in half a second and you waste the cooldown for pocket change. Mark the boss that's about to walk into your Accelerator's line and you just printed money.

The counter-intuitive takeaway: Kingpin is an economy tower disguised as a gangster tower. The DPS is real but replaceable. The Bounty engine is what you can't get anywhere else for this role.

## Failure Review — The Three Ways Players Throw with Kingpin

Here's the part I see over and over in Fallen lobbies, and it always ends the same way.

**Death one: buying Kingpin and placing four of them on wave one.**

You spend $14,400 placing four base Kingpins before your economy exists. You have no Farm, no early DPS, just four towers that each shoot one slow bullet and one Lackey each. Wave 8 comes with a horde and you leak. Error: you treated Kingpin like an early-game tower. It's not. It's a mid-to-late-game investment that needs a strong economy behind it. Consequence: you have zero cash to fix the leak. Correct play: place *one* Kingpin early for the cheap neutral levels, build your Farm and early DPS first, and only add more Kingpins once your income is online.

**Death two: leaving the unit queue on Money Runner, then a Hidden wave hits.**

You're on the top path, you set Money Runners because you want the economy, and you forget. A Hidden wave rolls in. Your Kingpin's main body has no Hidden detection, and Money Runners don't attack, so the entire tower contributes nothing while Hidden enemies walk to your base. Error: assuming the tower always shoots. Consequence: you leak a wave you should have cleared. Correct play: the moment you see Hidden enemies incoming, swap the queue to Lackey or Contractor. Check your queues every time you hear the Hidden-wave alarm.

**Death three: splitting paths on one Kingpin.**

You grab Bounty at Level 4 top, then decide you also want Bouncers, so you start investing in bottom path on the same tower. Now you have a Level 4 top and a Level 4 bottom on one tower — which the game won't even let you do cleanly — and you've spent way more than a committed build while getting less. Error: not committing to a branch. Consequence: your "maxed" Kingpin underperforms a properly-built one that cost less. Correct play: one Kingpin, one branch. If you want both an income Kingpin and a DPS Kingpin, that's two separate towers, which is exactly what the 4-placement limit is for.

## Decision Framework — Bottom Path First, or Bounty First?

This is the two-dilemma question every new Kingpin owner hits, so let me make it concrete.

You're mid-Fallen, you've got one Kingpin at Level 3, and you're sitting on about $20,000. A boss is three waves out. Do you push top path to Level 4 for Bounty, or bottom path to Level 4 for Bouncers?

**If your team's DPS is already solid** — someone's got Accelerators down and the current waves are holding — go top path. The Bounty will be ready for the boss, and marking that boss pays you the bonus that funds the rest of your game. You're trading a few waves of extra damage for a pile of cash at the exact right moment.

**If your team is struggling to clear the current hordes** — enemies are leaking, or you're the only one with a mid-game tower — go bottom path. Bouncers are the survival play. A dead player banks no Bounty. You can always save the top path for a second Kingpin once you've stabilized.

The common mistake is defaulting to "more damage is always right." It isn't. The correct path is whichever one stops you from dying *first*, and only after that do you optimize income.

## Kingpin + Boomerang — The v2.3.0 Combo That Actually Works

Since both towers dropped in the same update, it's worth asking whether they're meant to work together. The short answer: yes, and the pairing is cleaner than you'd think.

Boomerang is a cheap beginner tower — $550 to place, 300 coins to own — built around pierce and crowd control. It hits multiple enemies per throw and gets Lead detection at Level 3. It's the update's answer to Demoman, and it fills the exact gap Kingpin leaves open: early waves and dense hordes.

Kingpin is weak early. Boomerang is strong early. Kingpin spikes in the mid-to-late game with Bouncers and Bounty. Boomerang falls off late. So the synergy is a handoff: open with a couple of Boomerangs to clear the first several waves and control hordes, then let Kingpin's economy and Bouncers take over once your Farm is humming. Boomerang buys Kingpin the time it needs to come online, and Kingpin's Bounty funds your late-game boss killers. ## Is Kingpin Worth It? A Mode-by-Mode Verdict

Let's be honest about where Kingpin earns its keep, because "worth it" is not a yes-or-no question.

**Normal mode:** Skip it. The waves are too weak and the enemies too cheap for Bounty to matter, and your placement slots are better spent on raw DPS that clears fast. Kingpin's economy engine has nothing to feed on. If you only play Normal, don't spend the gems.

**Golden mode:** Niche. Golden mode punishes early leaks hard, and Kingpin's weak early game means you're leaning on your other towers until it ramps. It can work if you're already comfortable and just want the Bounty income on the back half, but it's not carrying you.

**Fallen mode:** This is Kingpin's home. Big bosses, big cash drops, long games. One well-timed Bounty on a Fallen boss refunds your entire mid-game, and Bouncers give you the mid-game backbone you need before the Accelerators finish. If you farm Fallen, Kingpin is genuinely good here.

**Hardcore mode:** Situational but real. The top path's Bounty shines against Hardcore's late bosses, and the community consensus is that the bonus cash on difficult modes is exactly where Kingpin's economy pays off. The catch is the placement limit of 4 and the weak early game in a mode that punishes every misstep. Bring it if your team already has its Hardcore fundamentals covered; don't make it your anchor.

The honest verdict: Kingpin is a strong, interesting tower that fixes Crook Boss's weaknesses — Lead, economy, and summon quality — but it's a luxury, not a necessity. If your gems are earmarked for core towers you're still missing, those come first. If you've got the roster and you farm Fallen or Hardcore, Kingpin will earn its 5,500 gems back faster than you'd expect. Just don't buy it because the fedora looks cool. Buy it because you've got a wave full of bosses and you want to tax them.
