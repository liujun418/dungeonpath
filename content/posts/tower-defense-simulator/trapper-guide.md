## Two Players, Same Wave 4. One Leaks, One Doesn't.

Two players load into a Normal match on the same map, same difficulty. Player one is saving. He's got a Scout down and he's banking every dollar toward an expensive DPS tower — the kind that costs a few thousand. Wave 4 rolls around with a swarm of quick, weak enemies, and his one Scout can't kill them all. A few slip past. He leaks, and it costs him cash and hearts.

Player two spent four hundred on a Trapper back on wave 2, dropped its spikes on the straight stretch before the bend, and put the rest into a Farm. When wave 4's swarm walks over the spikes, it melts. He never leaks, and his Farm runs the whole time.

Same waves. Same map. The difference is a four-hundred-dollar tower. That's the whole point of the Trapper: it isn't the flashiest tower in the game, but it is the cheapest reliable lane-clearer, and it buys you the room to get your economy online.

This guide covers the spike mechanic, where to place it, how far to upgrade it, and the one mistake that makes people swear the tower is useless.

## What the Trapper Actually Does

The Trapper doesn't shoot. It drops a set of metal spikes onto the path. Enemies that walk over those spikes take damage over time — the spikes stay on the ground for a while, then disappear. When they're gone, you place another set.

Four things follow, and they explain every strength and weakness the tower has.

**It hits everything that walks over it.** No targeting, no accuracy, no range check. If an enemy's feet touch a spike, it takes damage. That's why it shreds crowds — ten enemies crossing at once all get hit at once.

**It's damage over time, not a single hit.** The spikes don't do one big number. They tick. Enemies bleed health as they stand on the spikes, which is why the shape of the lane matters more than the exact spot where you place the tower.

**It's an area you control, not a shot you aim.** You pick where the spikes land. Put them on a long straight stretch and they hit everything that walks down it. Put them on a short bend and they hit nothing.

**It needs enemies to walk over it.** Air units fly straight over the spikes. Enemies that path elsewhere never touch them. This is the tower's one hard weakness, and the thing to plan around from wave one.

## The Upgrade Curve: More Spikes, Not Just Bigger Spikes

The Trapper is priced like an early tower, and its cost curve stays flat enough to keep it useful all game. Treat these numbers as approximate — I'll flag what I can't verify at the end.

| Level | Approx. cost | Spikes per placement | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (place) | ~$400 | ~2 | Basic spikes, low damage |
| 1-2 | a few hundred | 3-4 | More spikes, slightly more damage |
| 3-4 | a few hundred more | 5-6 | Noticeably more spikes and damage |
| 5 (max) | low thousands total | ~8+ | A full carpet of spikes with real punch |

The pattern: the spike count goes up faster than the damage per spike. A maxed Trapper isn't one big spike — it's a whole stretch of path covered in them, so every enemy crossing eats damage for longer.

Cost-wise, the Trapper is one of the cheapest towers to max. Even the top end sits in the low thousands, pocket change next to a Minigunner or an Accelerator. The [tier list](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/towers-tier-list/) won't put it in S-tier for damage, and it shouldn't. That was never the job.

One quirk: spike placements aren't permanent, so you re-buy them all game. That's fine — each placement is pocket change, so you re-lay the carpet every few waves and barely notice the gold leaving.

## Where It Fits: Long Lanes and Swarm Waves

The Trapper is a map-dependent tower, so "is it good" is the wrong question. The right question is "does this map have a long, straight lane?"

**Normal mode:** This is home turf. Early waves are waves of weak enemies, and a Trapper on a straight stretch clears them for a fraction of what a real DPS tower costs. You'll out-tempo everyone in the lobby.

**Intermediate mode:** Still strong. Same swarm logic, and the Trapper keeps your early economy safe while you save for the mid-game.

**Fallen mode:** Situational but real. Fallen's back half sends dense groups down one lane, and a Trapper placed there clears the small stuff so your DPS towers can focus the big stuff. It won't carry, but it patches a real hole. Full walkthrough: the [Fallen mode guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/fallen-mode-guide/).

**Hardcore mode:** Niche at best. Enemies hit hard and slots are precious, so spending one on a tower that doesn't scale late is hard to justify. Skip it.

Maps are the bigger filter. The Trapper wants one thing: a long, straight stretch where every enemy walks the same line. The longer an enemy sits on the spikes, the more damage it takes. On a short or winding map, enemies cross the spikes in a second and the tower does almost nothing. The [best maps guide](/games/tower-defense-simulator/) will point you at the straight-lane maps.

## How to Use It: Placement, Slows, and Anti-Air

Placement is the whole game with this tower, and there's one rule: spikes go on the path, not next to it. The Trapper only works where enemies actually walk. For the deeper theory, the [placement guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/tower-placement-theory/) covers how to read a lane — but the Trapper-specific version is simple.

Put the spikes on the longest continuous stretch of path you can find. Straight beats curved. Early in the lane beats late, because enemies at full health spend longer on the spikes before dying, and you catch the wave while it's still bunched.

Then two pairings matter enough to plan your whole loadout around.

**Pair it with a slow.** A Sledger or Cryomancer holding enemies in place makes the spikes do double duty, because enemies stand on them instead of walking through. The damage-over-time stacks with the slow: the slower they walk, the more ticks they eat. The [synergy guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/tower-synergy-combos/) covers this pairing.

**Pair it with anti-air.** The Trapper can't touch flying enemies, and it never will. You need a Militant, a Sniper, any tower that hits air — or you'll leak every air wave. This is the one slot you can't skip when leaning on the Trapper for early damage.

That's the loadout in two sentences: Trapper for the ground swarm, a slow to hold them on the spikes, and an anti-air tower for the flyers. Fill the rest with Farms. The [loadout guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/loadout-guide/) has the full five-slot picture.

## Failure Review — The Air-Unit Leak

Here's the mistake I see in almost every lobby where someone calls the Trapper useless.

**The error.** You build two Trappers on wave 3 and skip the Militant, thinking the spikes will carry you. Ground waves melt. Then the first air wave arrives and you leak the entire thing, because spikes do zero damage to enemies that don't touch them.

**The cause.** The Trapper is ground-only by design. It never hits flying enemies, and no amount of upgrading changes that. You bet your whole early defense on a tower that literally cannot damage a chunk of the enemy roster.

**The recovery.** Don't sell the Trappers — they're doing their job on the ground. Build anti-air before the air wave arrives. A single Militant covers it. The lesson isn't "don't build Trapper" — it's "the Trapper is half a defense, and you fill the other half."

There's a quieter version of the same mistake on winding maps. You place spikes on a short bend, enemies cross it in half a second, and the tower looks like it does nothing. That's not a bug. That's placement. Move the spikes to the long straight stretch — if the map doesn't have one, the Trapper isn't the right pick, and a shooting tower will serve you better.

## Decision Table: Trapper or Another Cheap Tower?

New players ask which cheap tower to commit to first. Here's the straight if-then table.

| If your situation is... | Then the right pick is... | Because... |
|---|---|---|
| A long straight lane and swarm waves | Trapper | It clears the crowd for a fraction of the cost |
| Air units are your main threat | Militant or another anti-air tower | The Trapper can't hit flyers |
| Short or winding map, no straight stretch | Militant or Scout | Spikes need enemies to sit on them |
| You're saving hard for a big DPS tower | Trapper plus Farms | It holds waves while your economy builds |
| Mid-game, single high-HP enemy | A real DPS tower | Trapper's ticks are built for crowds, not bosses |

The pattern: the Trapper is a swarm answer, not a general answer. When the threat is many weak enemies on one line, it's the best money you can spend early. When the threat is one big enemy or a flyer, it's the wrong tool, and a Militant does the job for barely more.

## The Counterintuitive Part: It's an Economy Tool Disguised as Damage

Everyone files the Trapper under "damage tower" and compares its DPS to a Minigunner. That's the wrong comparison, and why so many players skip the tower.

The Trapper's real job is economy. Here's the concrete version. A Minigunner costs a few thousand — money that could be a Farm, or two Farms, compounding from wave 2 onward. The Trapper costs four hundred and holds the early waves by itself, so you keep buying Farms while everyone else saves for one gun.

Run the two paths side by side. Player one saves for a Minigunner and has nothing but a Scout until wave 8. Player two buys a Trapper on wave 2 and Farms every round after. By wave 10, player two has triple the income and can afford the Minigunner anyway — now backed by a full Farm economy. The Trapper didn't beat the Minigunner at damage. It beat it at timing. That's why the [coin farming guide](/games/tower-defense-simulator/) and the Trapper belong together: the Trapper protects your Farms while they grow.

And the second counterintuitive bit: when you do upgrade the Trapper, buy spike count before spike damage. More spikes means a longer carpet, which means every enemy stands on the spikes longer and eats more ticks. A slightly stronger spike that enemies walk off in half a second does less total damage than a wider carpet they can't escape. Volume beats punch for crowds — and the [enemy wave guide](/posts/tower-defense-simulator/enemy-wave-guide/) is worth reading if you want to time placements to the wave schedule.


## FAQ

### What does the Trapper do in Tower Defense Simulator?

The Trapper drops a set of metal spikes onto the path. Enemies that walk over the spikes take damage over time while the spikes persist. When the spikes disappear, you place a new set. It doesn't shoot or aim — anything that steps on the spikes gets hit.

### Is the Trapper good for early game?

Yes, and it's arguably where it's best. Early waves are waves of weak enemies, and a Trapper on a straight stretch clears them for a fraction of what a real DPS tower costs. That frees your cash for Farms while everyone else is still saving for a big gun.

### Can the Trapper hit flying enemies?

No. Spikes sit on the ground, and air units fly straight over them. You need a separate anti-air tower — a Militant, Sniper, or similar — or you'll leak every air wave. This is the tower's one hard weakness, and upgrades never fix it.

### Which towers pair best with the Trapper?

A slow tower first — Sledger or Cryomancer — because it holds enemies standing on the spikes so they eat more damage ticks. Then an anti-air tower, because the Trapper can't touch flyers. Fill the rest with Farms and let the Trapper protect your economy.

### Where should I place the Trapper's spikes?

On the longest straight stretch of path you can find, where enemies walk a single predictable line. Straight beats curved, and early in the lane beats late. If the map has no long straight stretch, the Trapper is probably the wrong pick and a shooting tower will serve you better.
