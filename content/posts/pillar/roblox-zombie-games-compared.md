It is 1812. Your formation is holding a farmyard wall in the rain. Twenty of you — line infantry, a surgeon, a sapper — and beyond the wall there is a sound like a very large crowd eating something.

Then the wall section on the left goes down, someone fires a musket too early, and the horde comes through in a wave of muddy hands. Your gun takes thirty seconds to reload, so you fix the bayonet. Ten minutes later, if everything goes well, you are rowing away from a burning shore and half the people you started with are dead.

That is **Guts & Blackpowder**, and that scene — not a shopping mall with an SMG — is currently the most-played zombie experience on Roblox. Roughly ten and a half thousand people were in servers like that when I checked.

Here is what happened to the genre while nobody was writing it down. "Roblox zombie games" used to mean one thing: survive waves, buy guns, repeat. It now means six different games with six different contracts between you and the undead. Pick the wrong one and you'll bounce off in ten minutes, even though another game on this list would have owned your weekend.

## Why Most "Best Roblox Zombie Games" Lists Are Stuck in 2020

**They still lead with the famous old games.** Zombie Attack has 1.7 billion visits and gets put at number one on autopilot. It currently runs about 200 concurrent players. Lists that rank by name recognition are describing the genre as it existed six years ago.

**They treat all six games as wave shooters.** Guts & Blackpowder is an objective escape game with melee as a primary weapon. Field Trip Z is a story campaign with scripted beats and endings. A round-based player can hate both and swear the genre is dead. The ranking below is built by *loop*, not by headcount.

**They ignore lobby size.** Project Lazarus caps at six, Zombie Uprising at five, Guts & Blackpowder at twenty. Lobby size changes everything about how a zombie game plays — who gets revived, who gets left, how fast comms need to move — and listicles never mention it.

**They quote vote totals without the dislike rate.** Field Trip Z carries a 22% downvote rate. The other five sit between 4.2% and 6.2%. That gap is information, and it is the kind of thing you want to know before an evening commitment rather than after.

Every game below was confirmed live, searchable and populated on the Roblox index on 12 November 2026.

## How We Evaluate Roblox Zombie Games

Six factors, the same ones I use for every genre ranking on this site:

**1. The core loop under pressure (25%).** What does the game ask you to do *while zombies are in your face* — shoot, stab, build, run to an objective, read a scripted story beat? A loop that stays coherent in chaos is the whole product.

**2. Difficulty honesty (20%).** Does the game telegraph its rules, and are deaths fair? Friendly fire, grab ranges, boat timers — the genre's best games are hard because they're legible, not because they're cheap.

**3. The round economy (15%).** Points, cash, wall buys, intermission shops: does spending reward knowledge? A good zombie game lets a skilled player convert currency into survival on a schedule.

**4. Team dynamics (15%).** Revives, classes, shared objectives and what happens to stragglers. Some of these games are single-player games with strangers in them; others are genuinely co-op instruments.

**5. Content depth and update cadence (15%).** Map count, weapons, specials, seasonal events — and whether the developers are still there in 2026.

**6. Monetisation fairness (10%).** Does Robux buy convenience or the win button?

Deliberately excluded: how scary it looks in the thumbnail. Zombie thumbnails are the most dramatic on the platform and predict almost nothing.

## 1. Guts & Blackpowder — The Genre's New Standard

If you only play one, play this one, and go in knowing what it is: a co-op objective-survival game in the Napoleonic Wars, created by the verified Guts & Blackpowder group and live since early 2023, with over 629 million visits and a downvote rate of just 6.2%.

**The loop.** Each map tells you to hold positions across stages and then *extract*. The finale is a boat (or equivalent exit) on a timer. Survivors aboard sail away. Anyone still on the shore dies and loses the run. That simple rule — punctuality is survival — produces more shouting-with-friends than any jump-scare in the genre.

**Why melee carries runs.** Muskets hit hard but reload on a long, interruptible animation. In a breach, the line fixes bayonets or draws sabres, and spacing matters: a packed formation blocks each other's swings; a loose one gets surrounded. This is the only Roblox zombie game where footwork and formation are first-order skills.

**Classes create real jobs.** The Surgeon keeps people alive through revives and bandages; the Sapper handles construction and explosives; the Officer and Line Infantry manage the firing rhythm; the Chaplain and Seaman each change how a group composes itself. Nobody is playing the same character.

**The specials.** Fast runners break lines; swollen exploder types punish clumps; heavier elite zombies demand focused fire or a coordinated kite. Ignore them to farm common zombies and the run collapses on schedule.

**Your first night, concretely.** Spawn in, ask in chat what the group needs, and take Surgeon. Keep behind the firing line, watch who drops, and drag attention to bandaging — you'll learn every map's safe pockets without the pressure of holding the front. Deaths here have two stages: you go down when grabbed, you're finished if a zombie reaches you or the extraction leaves, so a nearby medic is the difference between a scare and a lost run.

**What difficulty actually means in practice.** Friendly fire is on, so a line that fires through friends' backs finds out fast. Sound carries useful information — the specials and the horde are audible before they're visible. And the game punishes silence: groups that call "runner left," "bomb mid," "boat in ten" win runs that identically skilled silent groups lose. It is the closest thing on Roblox to a co-op game that treats communication as gear.

**Who it's for.** Players who want a game that asks for teamwork and pays it back. **Who bounces off:** anyone wanting immediate gun power, solo silent runs, or low-stakes relaxation. If this one grabs you, it's a month of evenings.

## 2. Field Trip Z — The Story Campaign

Made by Splitting Point Studios, verified, and over 1.28 billion visits since April 2020. The premise: a school field trip runs straight into the outbreak, and the lobby plays the story beat by beat — classroom, bus, evacuation, holdouts, a final choice.

**The loop is not wave survival.** The game moves you through scripted sections with quests and named characters, breaking into combat arenas between story beats. Your "score" is reaching the ending, and there is more than one ending depending on the late-game choices the group makes. That branching is the replay engine: people run it again specifically to see the road not taken.

**It plays best in populated lobbies.** Servers allow up to 43 players, and story sections need bodies pushing objectives together. An off-hours lobby can stall at a holdout and feel broken when it's actually just understaffed.

**About that 22% downvote rate — the genre's outlier.** Some of it is genuine: old rough edges and sections that feel like they wait for kids to catch up. Some of it is how story games get rated by younger players who hit a confusing beat, die, and express themselves with a downvote. Read it as "this game can waste your time if you're lost," which is exactly why you should follow the lobby and the quest markers rather than exploring ahead.

**How to actually finish it.** Stay with the largest group, follow every quest prompt rather than roaming, and at holdouts understand that the game is waiting for a kill count or interactable — if the lobby is stuck, the answer is almost always "clear the remaining infected" rather than a bug. For the branching endings, the choice point is late and obvious; if you're hunting a specific ending on a replay, tell the lobby in advance so the group doesn't commit you down the other branch by default.

**What it does that no other game here does.** It ends. There is a credits-shaped satisfaction to a campaign clear that endless wave games structurally cannot offer, and the story pacing means difficulty arrives in designed doses instead of a tuning curve. That is also why younger players remember it fondly — it gave them a *thing they finished together*.

**Who it's for.** Players who want a campaign with characters and an ending instead of an endless score chase. Pair it with the [horror games comparison](/posts/pillar/roblox-horror-games-compared/) if the scripting is what you enjoy.

## 3. Project Lazarus: ZOMBIES — The Classic, Done Properly

Since June 2016. Let that land: a Roblox game old enough to drive, by verified creator logitech101, still pulling about 500 players and holding the genre's **healthiest vote ratio at 4.2%** dislikes. Nearly 689,000 upvotes.

It says exactly what it is: a love letter to classic wave-based Call of Duty Zombies, lobbies of up to six.

**The round economy is the game.** Points come from hits, kills and repairing barricade boards. Spend on wall weapons, doors to new areas, the mystery-box roll, perk-style machines, and the upgrade-table equivalent of pack-a-punch. Knowing the buy order *is* the skill — a player who knows the map outlasts a player with better aim.

**The rhythm.** Rounds escalate, special rounds break the pattern, the power comes on when someone reaches it, and downed players crawl for a revive. It is legible, fair and built to be replayed on the same maps until they're memorised.

**A workable early order for new players.** Keep the starter sidearm earning until you can afford the first wall weapon, repair boards every early round for the safe points, open doors toward wherever the power sits, buy the survival perk machine first and the speed one second, and only roll the mystery box after the essentials are banked. That order is boring and is exactly why it works — flashy box rolls are the rookie currency sink every round.

**Solo versus team.** Teams earn slower per person but cover the map and provide revives; solo earns faster and moves on your schedule, but one mistake at the wrong round ends the run. Most players learn in teams for map knowledge and then test themselves solo — treat solo as an exam, not the classroom.

**Who it's for.** Anyone who wants the original round-based contract: small team, shared map knowledge, one more round. If you bounce off it, the genre's wave games probably aren't for you — but try it anyway, because nothing else distills the formula this cleanly.

## 4. Those Who Remain — The Best Place to Learn

One of Roblox's original zombie experiences, by the verified Peak Development Studios, live since August 2016, about 430 players and a 5.9% dislike rate. At the time of writing it is running its Hallows 2026 event, so it's not a museum piece.

**The loop.** Endless waves across holdout maps, with an intermission economy: loot, buy weapons from the shop, place defensive build pieces, complete objectives that pop during waves, hold out, repeat. Support drops and specials keep rounds from flattening.

**Why it's the best first game.** Waves are announced, the shop gives you a calm minute to think, and building lets a cautious player shape the battlefield instead of just reacting. Every habit you learn here — spend between waves, hold a lane with an exit, spend on the next power spike — transfers to the harder round games.

**What building actually changes.** Placeable barriers buy seconds at the lanes you choose rather than the ones the map gives you, and seconds compound — a choke delayed for one beat lets the team focus fire before anything reaches melee range. The classic beginner upgrade path is simple: one weapon upgrade, one defensive investment, repeat. Never let a full wallet sit through a wave.

**The seasonal hook.** Events like the current Hallows run add themed maps, drops and objectives, which is a genuinely good reason to time a first visit — lobbies are full, guides are fresh, and the event content usually stays accessible after the season. Old games with active seasonal teams behave like live games.

**Who it's for.** Beginners, obviously, but also players who like defense-building as an activity. Servers run up to 50 players, so there's always a revive nearby. It pairs naturally with the [survival games comparison](/posts/pillar/roblox-survival-games-compared/).

## 5. Zombie Uprising — The Weapon-Grinder

By the verified USSF group, May 2020, 1.25 billion visits, 4.9% dislikes, over a million upvotes. Small lobbies — five players maximum.

The pitch from the game itself: over 150 weapons, customisable, across challenging locations, with battle-pass seasons. The October update added weapons, a pass and rewards, so the grind has a moving finish line.

**The loop.** Waves of zombies on modern maps, cash to unlock and kit out guns, attachments and camos as the long tail, boss-level threats to break up rounds, and prestige-style long-term goals. The weapon count *is* the content depth here; players who love loadout engineering have a lot to tune.

**Why it ranks fifth despite the biggest upvote pool in the genre.** Small lobbies mean you're often matching solo or with one friend, and pure gun-grind loops flatten for players who don't care about attachments. It does what it does completely — it just serves a narrower taste than the top four.

**What the attachment grind buys you.** Recoil control and handling are felt, not just displayed, and a tuned familiar weapon outperforms an unmodded higher-tier one in most lobbies. The pass seasons give the unlock pile a deadline, which matters because the real failure mode of weapon-collector games isn't difficulty — it's the pile feeling shapeless.

**The five-player cap shapes the culture.** You see the same people across a session, pickups feel like co-op rather than crowds, and solo-queueing rarely means true solo. It is the best argument that small lobbies are a feature, even in a genre that defaults to chaos.

**Who it's for.** Loadout tinkerers and anyone who measures progress in unlocks. See the [FPS games comparison](/posts/pillar/roblox-fps-games-compared/) for its closest cousins.

## 6. Zombie Attack — The Legacy Chaos

December 2017, verified Zombie Attack Official group, **1.7 billion visits — the genre's all-time leader** — 1.37 million upvotes, and now around 200 live players.

**The loop.** Team waves, weapons bought as you earn, boss fights every set number of rounds, rare zombie variants that drop better rewards, pets, crates. It is built to be understood in five seconds and played at a noisy lunch table.

**The honest caveats.** It is the genre's heaviest monetiser — gamepass weapons and dual-wield-style conveniences sit clearly above free options — and its systems are shallow next to the games above it. Both claims are compatible: this was the right game at the right time, which is why the visit number is so enormous.

**What boss rounds teach.** The boss health bars are the first thing that asks for sustained focus instead of flailing, and rare variants exist to reward the players paying attention rather than the ones spending most. Group membership still grants a free pet and tag, which remains one of the simplest onboarding hooks in the genre.

**How to enjoy it honestly.** Treat it as the arcade version: short sessions, no meta to study, social noise as part of the product. Trying to make it a deep game — buying passes to extend what is fundamentally a simple loop — is the one way this experience gets expensive.

**Who it's for.** Younger players, quick sessions, nostalgia for the late-2010s wave era. If it clicks and you want depth, move to Those Who Remain or Project Lazarus rather than buying deeper into its passes.

## Failure Retrospective: Five Ways Runs Actually End

**1. Bringing a shooter brain to Guts & Blackpowder.** Back-pedalling and trying to kite at range fails against runners, and wasted musket shots leave you empty in the breach. The fix is melee discipline: fix bayonet, hold spacing, fire in volleys.

**2. Missing the boat.** Someone chases one last zombie or loots one last corner; the extraction timer doesn't negotiate. In objective games, the group that moves on the call keeps its run. Stragglers write their own ending.

**3. Camping a favourite lane blind to flanks.** Round games open new spawns as areas unlock, and players who memorise one hallway get rolled from behind. Glance at the minimap and the team's position every few seconds.

**4. Hoarding points past the power spike.** New players bank currency in Lazarus and Uprising "for later" while fighting under-geared. The economy wants to be spent on schedule — a perk or area bought early pays back for the rest of the run.

**5. Farming trash while a special works.** Exploder types reach the clump, brutes walk through untouched, and the wave breaks on the objective. Call specials out loud and focus them the moment they spawn.

## 5 Counter-Intuitive Truths

### 1. The Biggest Zombie Game Is Mostly Melee

Expectation: zombie games are shooters. Guts & Blackpowder runs on bayonets, sabres and pike-length spacing, because muskets were slow in 1812 and the game respects that. Historical realism is its most modern feature.

### 2. 1.7 Billion Visits and Nearly Dead

Zombie Attack's lifetime total dwarfs everything here while its live count sits near 200. Genre leadership moved; the counters just haven't. Choose by live population, not the leaderboard of 2019.

### 3. The Downvote Outlier Is Still Worth Playing

Field Trip Z's 22% looks like a warning siren, but story games collect frustration votes at dead story beats rather than ratings of the whole product. The campaign is unique in the genre. Read the dislike rate as a map of its weak minutes, not a verdict.

### 4. The Best Lobbies Are the Smallest

Lazarus at six and Uprising at five play *better* than the big-cap servers for round survival: everyone gets revived, comms stay simple, nobody is a stranger by wave ten. More players isn't more co-op; usually less.

### 5. Most Deaths in Guts & Blackpowder Are Tardiness

You don't lose those runs by being out-killed. You lose by being somewhere else when the group moves. The genre's hardest game is won largely by following the crowd on time — closer to a field trip than a duel.

## Methodology: How to Work Through the Genre

Three evenings cover everything with minimum wasted motion.

**Evening one — learn the basics.** Those Who Remain. Finish a solid stretch of waves, spend every intermission, build one defensive line per map. The transferable habits: economy timing, lane discipline, revive awareness.

**Evening two — learn the classic economy.** Project Lazarus. Follow the buy order of whoever knows the map: early weapon, doors toward power, perk-style machines, the upgrade table. Compare it directly to evening one and you'll feel what round depth means.

**Evening three — the main event.** Guts & Blackpowder with a group and a mic. First run as Surgeon to learn map geography under low pressure; second run as Line Infantry; third, chase the boat. After that, Field Trip Z is a different-flavour weekend and Zombie Uprising or Zombie Attack are there if the specific craving shows up.

The only real rule across all six: the genre rewards the player who spends on time and moves with the group. Everything else is flavour.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Roblox zombie game in 2026?**

Guts & Blackpowder, by live population and by how much it asks of a coordinated team — around 10,500 concurrent players, Napoleonic objective survival ending in an extraction. For the classic round format, Project Lazarus is the cleanest; for beginners, Those Who Remain.

**Which is best for a beginner?**

Those Who Remain. Announced waves, an intermission shop, buildable defenses, and crowded servers with revives available. Zombie Attack is simpler but monetises harder and teaches fewer transferable habits.

**Is Guts & Blackpowder too hard?**

It is hard on purpose — friendly fire, slow reloads, grabby zombies, boat timers — but every rule is readable. Start as Surgeon, follow the formation, move when the group moves, and the difficulty turns into the reason to stay.

**Are these games pay-to-win?**

No for the top five: cosmetics, supporter packs and round-earned gear. Zombie Attack sells gamepass weapons and pets that clearly outperform free options, but no game here gates content behind Robux.

**Which has the most players?**

Guts & Blackpowder leads live at ~10,500. Zombie Attack leads all-time with 1.7 billion visits but under 200 concurrent today. Live and lifetime are different contests in this genre.

**Can I play them solo?**

Wave shooters, yes — Those Who Remain, Zombie Attack, Zombie Uprising, and self-imposed-hardmode solo in Lazarus. Guts & Blackpowder and Field Trip Z are built for groups and understaffed lobbies can genuinely stall.

**Which one is like Call of Duty Zombies?**

Project Lazarus, explicitly: hit/repair points, wall buys, mystery box, perk machines, an upgrade table. Zombie Uprising is the modern gun-grind alternative with deep customisation.
