## Deflection 4 to 5: The Wall

Every Blade Ball beginner hits the same wall. You can block deflections 1-4. You feel like you're getting the hang of it. Then deflection 5 comes, the ball seems to teleport from the opponent's blade to your face, and you're eliminated.

You didn't get worse at deflection 5. The game changed. The speed curve crossed a line your visual system can't cross.

Understanding the speed curve is understanding that you're playing three different games in one match: a reaction game (deflections 1-4), a flash game (5-8), and a prediction game (9+).

## The Three Phases

### Phase 1: Reaction Game (Deflections 1-4)

This is the phase where aiming skill matters. You can watch the ball, track its path, position yourself, and time your block based on visual input. This phase rewards good fundamentals — crosshair placement, positioning, reading the opponent's deflect direction.

**What to do:** Play normally. Watch the ball. Block based on seeing it approach.

**The phase transition hint:** At deflection 4, the ball starts to feel "fast" — you notice the gap between seeing it and reacting to it narrowing. This is your signal to shift mental modes.

### Phase 2: Flash Game (Deflections 5-8)

Visual tracking stops working. The ball moves faster than your brain can register, process, and command a block response. You need a faster cue.

The game provides one: the white flash. When the ball enters your parry window, your character emits a bright white ring. This flash triggers your reflexive response (which is 0.1-0.15s, twice as fast as visual tracking). Reacting to the flash instead of the ball is the single skill that separates Gold players from Bronze.

**What to do:** Stop watching the ball. Watch your character. Press block the instant you see the flash.

**Training drill:** Enter a match. For deflections 1-4, block normally. The moment deflection 5 starts, force yourself to look at your character, not the ball. Block when you see the flash. Die anyway a few times while you adjust. After 5-10 matches, flash-blocking becomes automatic.

### Phase 3: Prediction Game (Deflections 9+)

Past deflection 8, even the flash is borderline — the ball arrives almost the same instant the flash appears. You need to swing BEFORE the ball arrives, based on predicting where it's going.

At this level, you're reading the opponent's blade angle, body position, and movement patterns to guess where they'll deflect. You swing at where the ball WILL be, not where it is.

**What to do:** Watch the opponent's blade, not the ball. Their blade angle telegraphs the deflect direction. If their blade is angled left, they're deflecting left. Pre-position your crosshair and swing as they hit.
