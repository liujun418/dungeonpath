## The Story of Your Last Run

You're on Door 12. You pop open a drawer and there it is — a Crucifix. Your heart does a little flip. This is it. The insurance policy. The one item that says "I'm not dying today."

You carry it for 80 doors.

Door 37: you skip the Bandages. You're at 65% HP, but you've got the Crucifix, right? That's the safety net. Door 52: you pass on the Flashlight. Your Lighter's still got a flicker left. Door 71: you walk right past a Skeleton Key. You don't need more loot — you've got the Crucifix.

Every time you open your inventory, that golden cross stares back at you. It's not an item anymore. It's a trophy. A promise. "I'll use it when it really matters."

Door 90. The library. The lights die. Figure's footsteps shake the floorboards. You reach for your Lighter — dead. You scramble for the Crucifix — but your inventory's a mess of unused Lockpicks, three Vitamins you never drank, and a Flashlight you found on Door 28 that's been gathering dust. You scroll past the Crucifix twice before you find it.

Too late. Figure's already there.

You died with the Crucifix still in your bag. Again. And the worst part? You know exactly why.

This guide is for every player who's ever hoarded the perfect item for the wrong moment. Let's fix it.

## Why You Keep Dying With a Full Inventory (Failure Analysis)

Most DOORS deaths aren't from entities. They're from the six inches between your keyboard and your chair. Here's the psychology that's killing you:

### 1. The Hoarder's Fallacy

You see a rare item and your brain screams "KEEP IT." So you carry a Crucifix for 50 doors, passing up Bandages on Door 37, a Flashlight on Door 52, and a Skeleton Key on Door 71. Every slot that Crucifix occupies is a slot that could've held something you actually needed. When the emergency finally comes, you can't find it in your cluttered inventory — or worse, you're already dead because you skipped the Bandages.

The fix: ask yourself "Will I use this in the next 10 doors?" If the answer's no, drop it for something you'll use right now.

### 2. The "More is Better" Trap

Jeff's Shop is a candy store and you're a kid with a credit card. 100g for Vitamins? Why not! 250g for a Lockpick? Sure! 200g for a Flashlight even though you already have a Lighter? Add to cart!

You walk out with 7 items, none of which help when Figure corners you in the library. You don't need more items. You need the right items. A single Bandage at 20% HP is worth more than a Lockpick, Vitamins, and Flashlight combined.

The fix: before buying anything, ask "What's the one item I'd regret not having in the next emergency?" Buy that. Skip everything else.

### 3. Gold Economy Ignorance

You don't track your gold. You just spend it. 200g on a Flashlight at Door 30 feels fine — until Door 52 arrives and you're 50g short of a Crucifix. That 200g Flashlight just cost you 500g in opportunity.

Gold in DOORS is finite. You get roughly 1,500-2,000g per full Hotel run if you loot everything. Blowing 20% of your total run budget on a marginal upgrade is a math error, not a strategy.

The fix: mentally budget your gold. Know exactly what you're saving for at each stage of the run.

### 4. Fear of Wasting

You found a Crucifix in a rare container. It feels special. So you refuse to use it on anything less than Figure. But then you die to Rush on Door 33 because you had no Bandages and couldn't outrun it. The Crucifix didn't save you. It just took up a slot.

A Crucifix used on Door 60 is infinitely more valuable than a Crucifix still in your inventory on Door 100. The "perfect moment" you're saving for? It already passed. You just didn't notice.

The fix: use consumables the moment they prevent a death. Not a minute later.

## The Jeff's Shop Decision Framework: If I Have X Gold at Door Y, What Do I Buy?

Stop making impulse purchases. Use this framework to decide exactly what to buy based on your gold and progress:

### Early Hotel (Doors 1-50): ≤300g

- **First priority (100g): Lighter** — Dark rooms and Screech are immediate threats. Don't even think about the Flashlight yet. The Lighter's battery handles early dark rooms perfectly, and the 100g you save buys a Bandage.
- **Second priority (100g): Bandages** — One Screech bite or a missed jump can drop you to 20% HP. Always have one.
- **Third priority (100g): Vitamins** — Only if you have extra gold. Save them for the Door 50 Seek chase.

### Mid Hotel (Doors 50-100): 300-600g

- **First priority (300g): Skeleton Key** — Guarantees Jeff's Shop access and skull lock openings. A shop spawn can completely change your run.
- **Second priority (500g): Crucifix** — Only if you have essentials covered. Use it on the first lethal encounter, not as a trophy.
- **Third priority (200g): Flashlight** — Upgrade from Lighter only when you have extra gold. The Lighter still works fine.

### The Mines (Doors 150-200): 600+g

- **First priority (250g): Shears** — Bramble vines block progression. Buy immediately if you don't have them.
- **Second priority (300g): Bulklight** — Longest battery for the darkest Mines areas. The difference between Bulklight and Flashlight in the Mines is life and death.
- **Third priority (250g): Alarm Clocks** — Collect from containers or buy if needed for Door 200. You need at least 2-3.
- **Fourth priority (500g): Crucifix** — Mines entities hit harder. Don't leave home without it.

## Counter-Intuitive Advice: Five Rules That Break Conventional Wisdom

### 1. The Lighter Beats the Flashlight Early Game

Every new player gravitates toward the Flashlight. It's brighter. It feels safer. It's 200g.

Here's the math that'll save your life: the Lighter costs 100g. The Flashlight costs 200g. That 100g difference buys you a Bandage. Early game dark rooms are short — the Lighter's battery handles them fine. The Bandage, meanwhile, will patch you up after a Screech attack or a missed jump.

The Flashlight is an upgrade, not a starter item. Buy the Lighter first, spend the difference on Bandages, and upgrade to Flashlight only when you have surplus gold. You'll thank me when you're at 30% HP on Door 25 with a Bandage in hand instead of staring at a bright flashlight and a death screen.

### 2. Using the Crucifix Early Is Smarter Than Saving It

Everyone hoards the Crucifix for Figure. Everyone dies with it in their inventory.

Here's the truth: using a Crucifix on a guaranteed lethal encounter in the mid-game beats dying with it in your bag. If you're at 20% HP, have no Bandages, and Rush is screaming down the hallway — use the Crucifix. It's already paid for itself. A Crucifix that saves you on Door 60 is worth more than a Crucifix that's still in your inventory on Door 100.

The "perfect moment" you're waiting for? It's the moment you're about to die. That's it. That's the bar.

### 3. Carry Fewer Items to Survive Longer

Your inventory holds 8 slots. You should treat 5 as the real limit.

More items means more scrolling, more decision fatigue, and more seconds wasted fumbling during a chase. When Figure is three seconds away, you don't have time to scroll past three Lockpicks and two Vitamins to find your Crucifix. A lean loadout of Lighter + Bandages + Crucifix + Skeleton Key + Vitamins will outperform a cluttered bag of 8 "just in case" items every single time.

Speed of access beats quantity of options. Always.

### 4. Skip the Lockpick Entirely

The Lockpick costs 250g. It opens locked containers. That's it. And those locked containers? They might give you gold. They might give you a Bandage. They might give you nothing useful at all.

Meanwhile, 250g is almost a Skeleton Key (300g), which guarantees a Jeff's Shop spawn and opens Skull Locks. It's two and a half Bandages. It's a Shears plus 50g to spare in the Mines.

The Lockpick is a gamble. And in DOORS, you don't gamble with your survival. Skip it. Buy guaranteed value instead.

### 5. Gold on the Ground Isn't Worth the Detour

You see 15g sparkle in the corner of a dark room. You sprint over, grab it, and — Screech spawns. You're at 20% HP. That 15g just cost you a Bandage (100g) and possibly your run.

Going out of your way to grab loose gold costs time. Time costs light battery. Light battery costs safety. Only grab gold that's directly in your path — the gold you'll find naturally by looting containers on your route is more than enough. Chasing every sparkle is how you end up dead in a dark corner with 47g and zero Bandages.

## Understanding DOORS Items

Items in DOORS are the backbone of your survival strategy. From basic illumination to entity-banishing relics, knowing what each item does, where to find it, and when to use it separates players who beat Door 100 from players who die on Door 33.

Unlike most games where you buy everything from a central shop, DOORS scatters items across containers, hidden rooms, and Jeff's Shops located at specific doors. Resource management is everything — you can't carry it all, and gold is limited.

## Currency Overview

Before we talk items, understand the currencies you use to acquire them:

- **Gold** — In-run currency. Source: containers, ground pickups, entity defeats. Used for: Jeff's Shop items. Does not persist between runs. Spend it or lose it.
- **Knobs** — Permanent currency. Source: codes, achievements, daily login. Used for: Lobby Shop permanent upgrades. These are your long-term investment.
- **Stardust** — Permanent currency. Source: completing The Outdoors, achievements. Used for: unlocking The Outdoors (10 Stardust). Required for endgame content.
- **Revives** — Consumable. Source: codes (777), Lobby Shop, achievements. Used for: auto-revive on death. Use sparingly — they're rare.

## THE HOTEL ITEMS

### Jeff's Shop — The Hotel (Doors 52-59)

Jeff's Shop appears between Doors 52 and 59. Here's every item, ranked by priority:

- **Lighter** — 100g. Basic light source with limited fuel. High priority. Essential for dark rooms and Screech prevention.
- **Bandages** — 100g. Restores moderate HP. High priority. Always carry at least one.
- **Vitamins** — 100g. Temporary speed boost. Medium priority. Situational but clutch for chases.
- **Flashlight** — 200g. Longer-lasting light than Lighter. High priority. Upgrade once you have extra gold.
- **Lockpick** — 250g. Opens locked drawers and containers. Low priority. Skip it — buy guaranteed value instead.
- **Skeleton Key** — 300g. Opens any locked door and Skull Locks. High priority. Guarantees shop access.
- **Crucifix** — 500g. Banishes or protects from most entities. High priority. Use it, don't hoard it.

### Jeff's Shop Tips

- **Buy the Lighter first.** Dark rooms start early. Screech is a real threat without light.
- **Save 500g for Crucifix if you can.** It's the most powerful defensive item in the game.
- **Skeleton Key guarantees a shop.** If your key opens a locked door, Jeff's Shop is guaranteed on the other side.
- **Don't buy Bandages at full HP.** Save the gold. Buy healing when you're actually damaged.
- **Vitamins for Seek chase.** Pop them right before a Seek encounter for a critical speed advantage.

### Items Found in Containers (The Hotel)

Found by searching drawers, cabinets, wardrobes, and other containers throughout The Hotel:

- **Gold** — Common. All containers. Currency for Jeff's Shop. Collect every piece in your path.
- **Lighter** — Common. Some containers. Grab if you don't have one. Skip if you do.
- **Flashlight** — Common. Some containers. Upgrade from Lighter if found. Skip if you already have one.
- **Bandages** — Common. Some containers. Always take. Healing is never wasted.
- **Vitamins** — Common. Some containers. Take only if you have space.
- **Crucifix** — Rare. Rare containers. Immediately equip or drop something else for it.
- **Lockpick** — Uncommon. Rare containers. Take only if you have space. Usually not worth it.
- **Skeleton Key** — Very Rare. Very rare containers. Immediately equip or drop something else for it.
- **Shears** — Not available in Hotel. Mines item only.

## THE MINES ITEMS

### Jeff's Shop — The Mines (Doors 152-162)

Jeff's Shop appears between Doors 152 and 162. The selection differs from The Hotel:

- **Lighter** — 100g. Basic light source. High priority.
- **Bandages** — 100g. Heal HP. High priority.
- **Vitamins** — 100g. Speed boost. Medium priority.
- **Flashlight** — 200g. Better light source. High priority.
- **Shears** — 250g. Cut through Bramble vines. High priority — required for progression.
- **Lockpick** — 250g. Opens locked containers. Low priority — skip it.
- **Bulklight** — 300g. Stronger flashlight with longer battery. High priority — best light source in the game.
- **Straplight** — ~250g. Hands-free head-mounted light. Medium priority — frees hands for items.
- **Crucifix** — 500g. Entity protection. High priority.
- **Multitool** — 500g. Versatile tool with multiple uses. Medium priority.
- **NVCS-3000** — 1,000g. Night vision device. Low priority — expensive and situational.

### Mines-Specific Items (Found in Containers)

- **Alarm Clock** — Uncommon. Containers throughout Mines. Distracts Figure — place away from your position. Collect at least 2-3 for Door 200.
- **Glowstick** — Uncommon. Containers. AoE light source. Great vs Gloombats. Grab for bat-heavy areas.
- **Barrel of Starlight** — Rare. Containers. Powerful entity banishment. Last resort only.
- **Fuses** — Required. Specific rooms. Power generators to open doors. Collect as needed.
- **Shears** — Uncommon. Containers (alternative to shop). Cut Bramble vines. Grab if you don't have them.
- **Crowbar** — Very Rare. Containers (~3% spawn rate). Skips generator rooms entirely. Immediately use or keep safe.
- **Bread** — Common. Containers. Feed to Caws in The Outdoors. Grab if heading to Outdoors.
- **Lotus Petals** — Required. The Outdoors. Collect 8 to craft Lotus Flower. Collect all 8.

### The Crowbar — The Most Valuable Find

The Crowbar has a ~3% spawn rate in containers. It's the single most valuable item you can find in The Mines.

It skips generator rooms entirely. That means you save significant time and avoid Figure encounters in those rooms. The trade-off is you miss the loot inside generator rooms, but the safety benefit is enormous. If you find a Crowbar, your run just got dramatically easier.

### Mines Item Strategy

1. Buy **Shears** the moment you encounter Bramble without them
2. Buy **Bulklight** for superior illumination in dark Mines
3. Buy **Crucifix** if you can afford it
4. Collect **Alarm Clocks** for The Nest (Door 200)
5. Stock up on **Bandages** — The Mines is significantly more dangerous
6. Grab **Glowsticks** for Gloombat-heavy areas
7. Buy **Straplight** if you need hands-free illumination

## ITEM USAGE STRATEGIES

### Light Source Priority

The single most important item category. Without light, you're vulnerable to Screech, Gloombats, and general disorientation.

- **Early Hotel (Doors 1-50)**: Lighter (100g). Cheap and sufficient for early dark rooms.
- **Mid Hotel (Doors 50-100)**: Flashlight (200g). Longer duration for more dangerous areas.
- **The Mines**: Bulklight (300g). Longest battery for the darkest areas.
- **Gloombat-heavy rooms**: Glowstick. AoE light repels entire swarms.
- **Figure encounters**: No light. Turn it off — light reveals your position.
- **The Nest (Door 200)**: No light. Figure hunts by sound. Light is irrelevant.

### When to Use Each Item

- **Bandages**: When HP drops below 50%. Not at full HP, not at 80%. Below 50%.
- **Vitamins**: Right before Seek chases or Figure encounters. Not in safe rooms — that's wasted.
- **Crucifix**: When an entity is about to kill you. Not on Timothy or Eyes. On lethal threats.
- **Lockpick**: When you see locked containers and have space. Otherwise skip.
- **Skeleton Key**: On locked doors when you suspect a shop. Not on every locked door.
- **Shears**: When Bramble blocks your path. Not needed otherwise.
- **Alarm Clock**: During The Nest (Door 200) to distract Figure. Not during regular Figure encounters.
- **Glowstick**: In Gloombat-heavy dark rooms. Not in well-lit areas.
- **Barrel of Starlight**: Absolute last resort against powerful entities. Not early in the run.

## ITEM FAQ

**Q: What is the most important item to buy first?**
A: The Lighter (100g). Dark rooms and Screech are immediate threats from the early doors. 100g is easy to accumulate, and the remaining gold buys a Bandage.

**Q: Should I save gold or spend it immediately?**
A: Spend on essential items immediately (Lighter, Bandages). Save for the Crucifix only if you're accumulating gold and already have essentials covered.

**Q: Can I carry multiple of the same item?**
A: Yes, but it's generally inefficient. One good light source beats two cheap ones. Stack Bandages if you have space, but don't double up on situational items.

**Q: What happens to my items when I die?**
A: All in-run items and gold are lost. Only permanent currencies (Knobs, Stardust) and out-of-run upgrades persist.

**Q: Is the Skeleton Key worth 300g?**
A: Yes, if you can afford it. Using a Skeleton Key on a locked door guarantees Jeff's Shop spawns, which can change your entire run. It also opens Skull Locks.

**Q: How many Alarm Clocks do I need for The Nest?**
A: Carry at least 2-3 Alarm Clocks to effectively distract Figure during the Door 200 Anchor activation sequence.

**Q: Can I use items during entity encounters?**
A: Yes. You can use Vitamins during a Seek chase, throw an Alarm Clock during a Figure encounter, or activate a Crucifix when an entity is about to hit you.

**Q: What is the Barrel of Starlight?**
A: A rare, powerful banishment item found in containers. Use it as an absolute last resort against entities that are about to kill you.

**Q: Do items stack in inventory?**
A: Consumable items like Bandages and Vitamins stack. Unique items like the Crucifix and Skeleton Key take individual inventory slots.

**Q: Is the Lockpick worth buying?**
A: No. At 250g, it's a gamble on container contents. That gold is better spent on guaranteed value items like Bandages, a Skeleton Key, or saved toward a Crucifix.
