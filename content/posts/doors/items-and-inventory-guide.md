## The Story of Your Last Run (Scene-Driven Opening)

You found a Crucifix in a drawer on Door 12. It felt like a sign — the perfect insurance policy for whatever horrors awaited. You held onto it for 80 doors, passing up Bandages on Door 37, a Flashlight on Door 52, and even a Skeleton Key on Door 71. "I'll save it for the perfect moment," you thought. Every time you opened your inventory, that Crucifix stared back at you like a trophy. You felt safer just knowing it was there.

Then Door 90 hit. The library went dark. Figure emerged, its heavy footsteps shaking the floor. You fumbled for your Lighter — dead battery. You sprinted for the exit, tripping over books. As Figure closed in, you finally reached for the Crucifix... but your inventory was so cluttered with unused Lockpicks and Vitamins that you couldn't find it in time.

You died with the Crucifix still in your inventory. Again.

This guide is for every player who's ever hoarded the perfect item for the wrong moment. Let's fix that.

## Why You Keep Dying With a Full Inventory (Failure Analysis)

Most DOORS deaths aren't from entities — they're from bad inventory decisions. Here's the psychology behind your mistakes:

### 1. The Hoarder's Fallacy
You see a rare item and immediately think "I might need this later." So you carry it for 50 doors, sacrificing space for essential items like Bandages or a working light source. When the "later" finally comes, you can't find it in your cluttered inventory.

### 2. The "More is Better" Trap
You buy every cheap item at Jeff's Shop because it feels like a deal. 100g for Vitamins? Why not! 250g for a Lockpick? Sure! But you end up with 7 items in your inventory, none of which are useful when Figure corners you.

### 3. Gold Economy Ignorance
You don't track how much gold you're spending or what you're getting in return. You blow 200g on a Flashlight early game when a 100g Lighter would have been sufficient — and then you can't afford Bandages when you need them.

### 4. Fear of Wasting
You found a Crucifix in a rare container, so you refuse to use it on anything less than Figure. But then you die to Screech because your Lighter ran out. The Crucifix didn't save you — it just took up space.

## The Jeff's Shop Decision Framework: If I Have X Gold at Door Y, What Do I Buy?

Stop making impulse purchases. Use this framework to decide exactly what to buy based on your gold and progress:

### Early Hotel (Doors 1-50): ≤300g
- **First priority (100g): Lighter** — Dark rooms and Screech are immediate threats. Don't even think about the Flashlight yet.
- **Second priority (100g): Bandages** — One mistake can leave you at 20% HP. Always have one.
- **Third priority (100g): Vitamins** — Only if you have extra gold. Use them for the Door 50 Seek chase.

### Mid Hotel (Doors 50-100): 300-600g
- **First priority (300g): Skeleton Key** — Guarantees Jeff's Shop access and skull lock openings.
- **Second priority (500g): Crucifix** — Only if you have essentials covered. Use it early on Figure, not as a trophy.
- **Third priority (200g): Flashlight** — Upgrade from Lighter only when you have extra gold.

### The Mines (Doors 150-200): 600+g
- **First priority (250g): Shears** — Bramble vines block progression. Buy immediately if you don't have them.
- **Second priority (300g): Bulklight** — Longest battery for the darkest Mines areas.
- **Third priority (250g): Alarm Clocks** — Collect from containers or buy if needed for Door 200.
- **Fourth priority (500g): Crucifix** — Mines entities hit harder. Don't leave home without it.

## Counter-Intuitive Advice: Three Rules That Break Conventional Wisdom

### 1. The Lighter Beats the Flashlight Early Game

Here's a tip that will save your life: **Buy the Lighter instead of the Flashlight for your first purchase.**

Why? Let's do the math:
- Lighter costs 100g, Flashlight costs 200g
- The 100g difference buys you a Bandage
- Early game dark rooms are short — Lighter battery lasts long enough
- The Bandage will save you from a Screech attack or minor fall damage

You'll thank me when you have 100g left for Bandages after Door 25.

### 2. Using the Crucifix Early Is Smarter Than Saving It

Everyone hoards the Crucifix for Figure or Seek. But here's the truth: **using it on a guaranteed lethal encounter in the mid-game beats dying with it in your inventory.** If you're at 20% HP, have no Bandages, and encounter a deadly entity, the Crucifix has already paid for itself. A Crucifix used on Door 60 is worth more than a Crucifix still in your bag on Door 100.

### 3. Carry Fewer Items to Survive Longer

Your inventory holds 8 items, but you should treat 5 as the real limit. More items means more scrolling, more decision fatigue, and more seconds spent fumbling during a chase. A lean loadout of Lighter + Bandages + Crucifix + Skeleton Key + Vitamins will outperform a cluttered bag of 8 "just in case" items every single time. Speed of access beats quantity of options when Figure is three seconds away.

## Understanding DOORS Items

Items in DOORS are the backbone of your survival strategy. From basic illumination to entity-banishing relics, knowing what each item does, where to find it, and when to use it separates casual players from seasoned survivors.

Unlike many games where items are purchased from a central shop, DOORS scatters items across containers, hidden rooms, and Jeff's Shops located at specific doors. Resource management is critical — you cannot carry everything, and gold is limited.

This guide covers every known item, its location, cost, and optimal usage strategy. For entity-specific item recommendations, see our [DOORS Entities Bestiary](/posts/doors/entities-bestiary/).

## Currency Overview

Before discussing items, understand the currencies you use to acquire them:

- **Gold** — Price: In-run | Source: Open containers, find on ground, defeat entities | Effect: Jeff's Shop items during the run | Priority: Most important — spend wisely, it doesn't persist between runs
- **Knobs** — Price: Permanent | Source: Codes, achievements, daily login | Effect: Lobby Shop permanent upgrades | Priority: For permanent enhancements
- **Stardust** — Price: Permanent | Source: Complete The Outdoors, achievements | Effect: Unlock The Outdoors (10 Stardust) | Priority: Required for endgame content
- **Revives** — Price: Consumable | Source: Codes (777), Lobby Shop, achievements | Effect: Auto-revive on death | Priority: Use sparingly

## ITEM USAGE STRATEGIES

### Light Source Priority

The single most important item category. Without light, you are vulnerable to Screech, Gloombats, and general disorientation.

- **Early Hotel (Doors 1-50)**: Lighter (100g) — Cheap and sufficient for early dark rooms
- **Mid Hotel (Doors 50-100)**: Flashlight (200g) — Longer duration for more dangerous areas
- **The Mines**: Bulklight (300g) — Longest battery for the darkest areas
- **Gloombat-heavy rooms**: Glowstick — AoE light repels entire swarms
- **Figure encounters**: No light (turn off) — Light can reveal your position to Figure
- **The Nest (Door 200)**: No light — Figure hunts by sound; light is irrelevant

### When to Use Each Item

- **Bandages**: When HP drops below 50% — NOT when you are at full HP or above 80%
- **Vitamins**: Right before Seek chases or Figure encounters — NOT in safe rooms (wasted)
- **Crucifix**: When an entity is about to kill you or in unavoidable situations — NOT on minor entities like Timothy or Eyes
- **Lockpick**: When you see locked containers — NOT when you have limited inventory space and no containers nearby
- **Skeleton Key**: On locked doors when you suspect a shop is behind — NOT on every locked door (save for guaranteed shop)
- **Shears**: When Bramble blocks your path — NOT needed if you have no Bramble encounters
- **Alarm Clock**: During The Nest (Door 200) to distract Figure — NOT during regular Figure encounters (less effective)
- **Glowstick**: In Gloombat-heavy dark rooms — NOT in well-lit areas (unnecessary)
- **Barrel of Starlight**: As an absolute last resort against powerful entities — NOT early in the run (save for emergencies)

## ITEM FAQ

**Q: What is the most important item to buy first?**
A: The **Lighter** (100g) is the best first purchase. Dark rooms and Screech are immediate threats, and 100g is easy to accumulate early in a run.

**Q: Should I save gold or spend it immediately?**
A: Spend on **essential items immediately** (Lighter, Bandages). Save for the Crucifix if you are accumulating gold and already have essentials.

**Q: Can I carry multiple of the same item?**
A: Yes, you can carry multiple copies of most items, but it is generally inefficient. One good light source is better than two cheap ones.

**Q: What happens to my items when I die?**
A: All in-run items and gold are lost when you die. Only permanent currencies (Knobs, Stardust) and out-of-run upgrades persist.

**Q: Is the Skeleton Key worth 300g?**
A: Yes, if you can afford it. Using a Skeleton Key on a locked door guarantees Jeff's Shop spawns, which can be run-changing. It also opens Skull Locks.

**Q: How many Alarm Clocks do I need for The Nest?**
A: Carry at least **2-3 Alarm Clocks** to effectively distract Figure during the Door 200 Anchor activation sequence.

**Q: Can I use items during entity encounters?**
A: Yes. You can use Vitamins during a Seek chase, throw an Alarm Clock during a Figure encounter, or activate a Crucifix when an entity is about to hit you.

**Q: What is the Barrel of Starlight?**
A: It is a rare, powerful banishment item found in containers. Use it as an absolute last resort against entities that are about to kill you.

**Q: Do items stack in inventory?**
A: Consumable items like Bandages and Vitamins stack. Unique items like the Crucifix and Skeleton Key take individual inventory slots.
