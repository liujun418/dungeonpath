You're staring at the countdown. The devs just stamped a date on it: **August 28, 2026** — twelve days out as of this writing. The Archives is no longer a "someday" teaser. It's a real thing with a real launch window, and the community has spent the last few weeks tearing every leaked frame and dev reply apart to figure out what's actually inside.

Here's the honest summary of everything we know right now. I'll flag what's **confirmed**, what's **expected**, and what's **possibly** true but still rumour-level. Because a dated launch announcement is one thing — the exact behavior of seven new entities is another, and the devs can still change any of it before or after the doors open.

## The Countdown Page You're Staring At

The confirmed facts first, because they're the only things you should build a plan around.

**The Archives launches August 28, 2026.** It's free. You enter it through the Lobby, the same hub you already walk through before every run. And once it's live, the subfloors stop being a rotating tease — they're **permanently free, discover-to-unlock**. Find the entrance once, and it stays open.

"Discover-to-unlock" is the part worth reading twice. You won't buy access, and you won't wait for a limited-time window. You find the door, you unlock it, and it's yours. That's the entire monetization story: there isn't one. If you've been burned by Roblox games that lock subfloors behind premium passes, this is not that.

What we *don't* have confirmed yet: the exact unlock conditions, whether there's a secret behind a specific number of doors, and whether the entrance appears for everyone on day one or rolls out. Treat those as open questions.

## The Big One: The Rooms Is Getting Rebuilt, Not Deleted

This is the headline, and it's the part that changes how you should spend the next twelve days.

The Archives is being positioned as **a reimagined subfloor** — specifically, a full rework of **The Rooms**. Not a patch. Not a balance pass. A "we're rebuilding this from the ground up with proper lore and a coherent art direction" rework. If you've ever cleared The Rooms and felt like it didn't quite match the polish of the Hotel or The Mines, that's exactly what the devs are fixing.

Two things fall out of that.

First, the old The Rooms you've memorized — the entity timings, the room layout rhythm, the exact spots where A-90 ambushes you — is expected to change. This is the closest thing to a guaranteed "your muscle memory is about to be wrong" situation. We'll get into what that means for prep below.

Second, and this is the counterintuitive part most players miss: **the Lotus flowers you've planted in The Rooms are expected to carry over into The Archives.** The devs have indicated that planted progress migrates rather than wipes. So the "should I grind The Rooms now?" question isn't just about badges — if you've got a Lotus garden half-finished, those flowers are an investment that's about to mature, not disappear. More on that in the prep section.

## Meet the New Entities (And Which Old Ones They're Replacing)

Here's where it gets fun — and where you need to read carefully, because the most useful insight is hidden in the naming.

The community has catalogued **at least seven new entities** so far, using placeholder names like "Unnamed 1" and "Unnamed 5" (the numbering has gaps, so there are likely more we haven't seen). The big reveal: **several of them are reworks of The Rooms entities you already know.** Same threat niche, same general job — but a different look, and almost certainly different behavior.

| Entity | What it looks like | What it replaces / nods to |
|---|---|---|
| **Honcho** | Flat "employee" made of cardboard/paper — light-blue shirt, tie, dark-blue pants, brown shoes, staples holding the arms on, face blacked out in mosaic/censored rectangles | Original entity; name from "head honcho" |
| **Ransom** | Heavily pixelated, distorted monochrome face with all-black eyes | A rework of **A-90**; name from "ransomware" |
| **Unnamed 1** | Black humanoid shadow figure, colored sticky notes stuck on as eyes/nose/mouth | A pink/yellow variant is possibly a nod to Deltarune's FRIEND |
| **Unnamed 2** | Blue vine/flower creature that can **block doors**; light-blue bloom with a yellow eyeball inside | Forget-me-not flower style |
| **Unnamed 3** | Wheels for legs, right hand is a hole punch, face is a service-desk screen; dark trench coat, TV-screen face, skull jaw, wires for hair | A rework of **A-60** |
| **Unnamed 5** | Greyish-white body, claw feet, only pale legs with bone holes visible | Possibly the face painting from The Outdoors; possibly tied to a scrapped "Abraham Lincoln" entity |
| **Unnamed 6** | White TV-related entity | Details still thin |
| **Unnamed 7** | Paper/cardboard entity | Details still thin |

Read the "replaces" column like a survival cheat sheet. **Ransom is A-90's rework.** A-90 is the entity that jumpscares you off your screen and resets progress when you move at the wrong moment. Ransom keeps that punishing, "don't move" niche but comes with a pixelated, distorted face and a ransomware-inspired name — which should tell you it's still an anti-movement punisher, not a chaser. Expect the same core counter (freeze at the right instant), but don't trust the old audio timing.

Same logic for **Unnamed 3**. It's **A-60's rework** — wheels for legs, a hole-punch for a right hand, a service-desk screen for a face. A-60 in The Rooms is a fast-moving chaser you deal with by hiding at the exact right moment. Unnamed 3 is the same job with a fresh (and honestly much creepier) skin. The mechanic will feel familiar; the tell won't.

That's the recurring pattern you should internalize now: **most of these "new" entities are old entities in a new costume.** The devs aren't inventing seven brand-new threat types — they're re-skinning and re-tuning the ones The Rooms already had, which is exactly what a "reimagined subfloor" should do. Familiar mechanics, unfamiliar faces. That's the thing that'll kill veteran players on day one, and we'll cover it in the failure section.

## The New Items: Pizza Makes a Comeback

The Archives isn't just entities. The devs are bringing in **ten or more new items**, and two of them are worth knowing about before launch.

First, **Pizza.** This one has history — it's a previously *cut* item that got scrapped from an earlier build, and now it's coming back through the **Admin Panel**. If you've only ever seen Pizza referenced in old datamines or dev posts, this is its official return. What it does isn't fully confirmed, but the smart money is on a food/heal role, and the "Admin Panel" access route suggests it's a reward or unlock rather than something you'll find lying on a Hotel table.

Second, the **Paper Plane.** It's the kind of item that fits the Archives' whole paper/cardboard aesthetic — remember Honcho and Unnamed 7 are both made of the stuff. We don't have confirmed mechanics yet, but the theme is consistent: this subfloor is leaning hard into paper, cardboard, and flat cutout monsters, and the item pool matches it.

Treat every item detail as **expected, subject to change.** The devs confirmed the *update* and its date; the exact function of each item is still the part we're guessing at.

## Returning Faces You Already (Sort Of) Know

Not everything in The Archives is new, and the returning list is more interesting than a simple re-skin pass.

**Curious Light** is coming back. **Glitch** is coming back. And the **Glitch Fragment** concept is expanding into variants — specifically **Screech, Rush, and Ambush variants**. If you've fought the base versions of those three across the Hotel and The Mines, you have a head start, but "variant" is the operative word. A Rush variant isn't Rush. A Screech variant might not hide in your blind spot the way you're used to.

**World Lotus** is also returning, which is the thread that connects back to that carry-over mechanic I mentioned. If your Lotus progress migrates and World Lotus shows up as a returning entity, the implication is that the gardening loop is a core part of The Archives, not a leftover feature.

The honest framing here: none of these return dates are fully detailed yet. "Returning" is confirmed in the sense that the devs have named them. *How* they behave in a reworked subfloor is exactly the kind of thing that changes.

## Three Ways Players Will Mess This Up on Day One

A preview article's failure section is really a "don't be this person" section. Here's the version for The Archives.

**Mistake one: assuming the new entities are a whole new threat, then getting jumped by a "familiar" one.** This is the trap the rework sets for you. You'll read "seven new entities" and prep for seven unknowns — then Ransom (the A-90 rework) will hit you while you're staring at its pixelated face trying to figure out what it does. The fix is to flip your prep: *assume each new entity is a re-skinned old one until proven otherwise.* When you meet Ransom, think "A-90, but I need to re-learn the freeze timing." When you meet Unnamed 3, think "A-60, but don't trust the old audio tell." You'll die fewer times if you expect familiarity than if you expect novelty.

**Mistake two: grinding The Rooms right now for progress that's about to reset.** If you're speed-running The Rooms this week purely to "get ahead" of the update, you might be wasting effort — the subfloor is being rebuilt, and the old layout and timings won't survive. The counterintuitive fix: grind for *what carries over* (Lotus plants, badges, items already earned), not for *muscle memory* of a layout that's about to be replaced. Your Lotus garden appreciates; your memorized room order depreciates to zero.

**Mistake three: having no idea where Pizza comes from, then missing it.** Because Pizza is gated behind the **Admin Panel** rather than normal looting, a player who skips the panel — or doesn't know to look for it — simply never gets the item. This is the classic "it wasn't in a crate, so I assumed it didn't exist" miss. When you're in The Archives and something's not in the obvious loot spots, check the Admin Panel before you write the item off as absent.

## The 12-Day Decision: What to Do Now vs. What to Wait For

You have twelve days. Here's the framework for spending them well — it splits cleanly into "do this now" and "don't bother until after launch."

**Do now:**

- **Finish anything already in progress in The Rooms.** Badges, items you've collected, Lotus flowers you've planted. Anything earned stays earned; anything half-done gets harder to finish after the rework.
- **Bank your knowledge of the base entities.** Ransom and Unnamed 3 are reworks of A-90 and A-60, and the returning variants are Rush/Screech/Ambush-based. Re-learning the *base* mechanics now (through [the entities bestiary](/posts/doors/entities-bestiary/)) means you recognize the reworks faster on day one.
- **Get comfortable with the subfloor loop in general.** If you've never touched The Backdoor, run it once to internalize how DOORS subfloors differ from the Hotel — different pacing, different unlock logic, different entity density. [The Backdoor guide](/posts/doors/the-backdoor-complete-guide/) is a decent warm-up for the "this is not the Hotel" headspace.

**Wait for launch:**

- **Don't memorize The Rooms layout.** It's getting rebuilt. Any hour you spend drilling the current room order is an hour you throw away on August 28.
- **Don't theory-craft item combos yet.** We don't know what Pizza and Paper Plane actually do. Building a loadout strategy around unconfirmed item functions is speculation with extra steps.
- **Don't buy into "leak culture" certainty.** The devs confirmed a date and a free, Lobby-accessed subfloor. Everything else — entity behaviors, item effects, exact unlock conditions — is **expected** or **possibly**, and the honest label matters more than the hype.

The through-line: **invest in what carries over, hold off on what's about to change.**

## The Counterintuitive Stuff Worth Remembering

Three things, condensed, because they're the parts most players will get backwards:

1. **"New" mostly means "reworked."** The Archives isn't dropping seven never-before-seen threat types — it's re-skinning The Rooms' existing roster with proper lore and art. Expect familiar jobs, unfamiliar faces.
2. **Your The Rooms Lotus garden is an appreciating asset, not a sunk cost.** Planted progress is expected to migrate into The Archives. Finishing your garden now is productive; re-memorizing the old layout is not.
3. **Free and permanent beats "limited-time event."** The subfloors being permanently discover-to-unlock means there's no FOMO window. You can afford to wait a day or two after launch, watch the early players die for you, and learn the real entity tells from their corpses rather than from a preview article.
