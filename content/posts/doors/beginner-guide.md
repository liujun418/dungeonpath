The lights flicker once. Then twice. You are in Room 7 of the Hotel, and the grandfather clock in the corner ticks louder than it did a moment ago. A high-pitched screech tears through the hallway — the kind of sound that drops your stomach before your brain can process what it means. You freeze. Your eyes dart around looking for a closet, but you wasted those first two seconds thinking instead of moving. The screech peaks. The screen flashes red. You are dead.

This is the standard DOORS new-player experience. The game does not explain that Rush announces itself with a specific audio cue, that Ambush requires a different hiding strategy, or that the Figure in Room 50 is blind and tracks by sound. There is no tutorial. No mercy. You learn by dying, or you do not learn at all.

But here is the counter-intuitive truth: DOORS is not a hard game once you understand its systems. The monsters follow predictable rules. The audio warnings are consistent. The reaction windows are generous when you know what to listen for. This guide compresses the 50-death learning curve into pattern recognition that takes most players 20 hours to build through trial and error.

## Your First 50 Deaths Are Normal

DOORS has no tutorial. It drops you in a hotel and starts sending monsters at you. You will die. A lot. The average new player dies 30-50 times before their first Hotel clear (Room 100). This is not a reflection of your skill — the game is designed to be learned through dying.

The players who clear the Hotel fastest aren't the ones with the best reflexes. They're the ones who learn the fastest from each death. Every time you die, ask: what sound did I hear right before? What room was I in? What did I do wrong?

## The 3-Minute Drill

This is the single most efficient way to learn DOORS. It takes 30 minutes and will save you 20+ hours of frustrating deaths:

1. Start a run. Don't buy items. Don't look for loot. Sprint from room to room.
2. When you hear Rush or Ambush: don't hide. Let it kill you.
3. Restart immediately. Do it again.

After 10 runs (roughly 30 minutes), you will have heard Rush's screech 15-25 times. The sound will be burned into your brain. You will never freeze when you hear it again because your brain now processes "screech = Rush = closet" as a reflex, not a thought.

This drill works because DOORS deaths have no penalty — items don't carry over between runs. You lose nothing by dying. You gain pattern recognition that takes 50 cautious runs to build naturally.

## Failure Analysis: 5 Ways Beginners Die and the Fix for Each

Every death in DOORS is a lesson. Here are the five most common beginner deaths, the mistake that causes them, and the fix that prevents them forever.

**1. Freezing on the Rush Audio Cue**
*The scenario:* You enter a room, the lights flicker, and a rising screech begins. You stop moving to identify the sound. By the time you realize it is Rush, look for a closet, and reach it, the screech is at its peak. You die at the closet door.
*The mistake:* Your brain treats "unknown screech" as "identify threat first" rather than "move to safety first."
*The fix:* Train the flicker-closet reflex. The instant lights flicker and you hear any screech, move toward the nearest closet. Identify the closest hiding spot when you enter every room — before you need it. This habit saves the 1.5 seconds of panic-searching that causes 80% of early deaths.

**2. Looting Before Checking for Threats**
*The scenario:* You see a drawer with items near the middle of the room. You run to loot it. Rush spawns at the door behind you and travels the full room length in 2 seconds. You cancel the loot animation, but there is no closet nearby. You die holding a candle.
*The mistake:* Item greed overrides threat assessment.
*The fix:* When you enter a room, scan for the exit and the nearest closet first. Close the door behind you. Wait 3 seconds. If no flicker or screech, then loot. The items will still be there in 10 seconds. Rush will not wait.

**3. Running in the Library (Room 50)**
*The scenario:* Figure appears at the far end of the library. Your instinct says run to the opposite side. Your footsteps echo. Figure locks onto your position and crosses the room in 4 seconds. You are cornered.
*The mistake:* Running is the default response to every monster in every game. Figure specifically punishes this instinct.
*The fix:* Crouch-walk at all times in the Library. Walk (still crouched) only when Figure is more than 3 shelf-lengths away. If Figure is on your side, stay still. Use bookshelves to create sound distractions — press E to throw a book, then move while Figure investigates the noise.

**4. Exiting the Closet Too Early During Ambush**
*The scenario:* Ambush screeches, you hide in a closet. The screen shakes once. You count to 3 and exit. Ambush loops back on its second pass and kills you outside the closet.
*The mistake:* Ambush sounds similar to Rush, and beginners treat every passing entity the same.
*The fix:* Learn the sound difference. Rush has a single rising screech that peaks and fades. Ambush has a stuttering, multi-stage screech that bounces off walls. If you hear the screech fade and return, stay in the closet. Count 15 seconds of silence after the last screech before exiting. Ambush makes 2-5 passes — never assume the first pass is the last.

**5. Ignoring Screech's "Psst" in Dark Rooms**
*The scenario:* You are in a dark room without a flashlight. You hear a soft "psst" sound and keep walking. Screech latches onto your face, dealing 50 damage. You now have 50 HP remaining. One more mistake ends the run.
*The mistake:* The "psst" is subtle and easy to miss when you are focused on navigation.
*The fix:* The instant you hear any "psst" sound, spin 360 degrees. Screech spawns behind you with glowing eyes. Looking directly at it makes it disappear without attacking. This is a free save that costs nothing — no items needed, no damage taken.

## Decision Framework: Your First 150 Credits

You reach the pre-lobby shop with 150 credits from achievements. The shop offers items that change how your run plays. Here is how to decide what to buy for your first clear attempt.

**Path A: Gummy Flashlight (150 credits)**
- What you get: Infinite battery, bright light, zero darkness deaths
- The trade-off: Zero healing items. One Screech hit drops you to 50 HP. A second hit ends the run.
- Best for: Players who rarely get hit but hate battery management

**Path B: Flashlight (75 credits) + Medkit (50 credits) — 125 total**
- What you get: Good light with battery management plus one full heal
- The trade-off: Batteries run out in the late Hotel. You will navigate some rooms in dim light.
- Best for: Beginners making a first serious clear attempt

**Path C: Candle (50) + Vitamins (60) + Skull Key (100) — 210 total, needs 60 more credits**
- What you get: Speed boosts for Rush dodging, Skull Key skips a late-Hotel puzzle, minimal light
- The trade-off: Near-darkness navigation is risky. Requires grinding more credits first.
- Best for: Players who have already reached Room 50 and want to optimize their second clear

**The decision rule:** Ask one question — "Can I survive a mistake?" If the answer is no, your run ends at the first unexpected hit.

Path A fails this test. Perfect vision, but zero forgiveness. One Screech on Floor 80 and you are running on 50 HP through the hardest stretch of the Hotel.

Path B passes. You can see well enough for 80% of rooms, and the Medkit means one mistake costs health, not the run.

Path C is for second clears. The Skull Key saves time, but Candle + no healing kills beginners before they reach the locked door.

**Verdict for a first clear:** Path B. Your first clear will involve mistakes — that is guaranteed. A Medkit turns a fatal mistake into a 50-HP lesson. Buy Flashlight + Medkit, learn the entity patterns, and save the Gummy Flashlight for your next run.

## 3 Counter-Intuitive Truths About DOORS

DOORS rewards behavior that feels wrong in every other game. Here is what looks like a mistake but is actually optimal.

**1. Die on purpose to learn faster**

Every game you have ever played punishes death. DOORS does not. Items do not carry over between runs. Progress is not saved between rooms. Death resets nothing except your position.

The fastest learners exploit this. They start runs with no items, sprint through rooms, and intentionally expose themselves to entity encounters. When Rush screeches, they stand in the open and let it hit them. They die, restart, and repeat. In 30 minutes, they experience more entity encounters than a careful player sees in 20 hours of gameplay.

The counter-intuitive part: avoiding death is the slowest way to learn. Dying 50 times in 30 minutes teaches pattern recognition that dying once every 10 minutes takes 8 hours to build. Compress the deaths, compress the learning curve.

**2. Loot less to survive more**

The instinct is to pick up everything. Items make you stronger, right? In DOORS, items create a false sense of security. Beginners who load up on candles and lockpicks treat items as substitutes for pattern recognition.

The counter-intuitive approach: play 10 runs with zero items. Navigate dark rooms by memory. Learn closet positions. Recognize entity cues without a flashlight. When you finally add items, they become supplements to your skills rather than crutches. You use the Gummy Flashlight because it saves 2 seconds per room, not because you cannot navigate without it.

**3. Slow is fast in the Hotel**

Most games reward speed. Faster movement means fewer enemy encounters and better efficiency. DOORS reverses this. Rushing through rooms increases your chance of walking into an active entity spawn. The players who reach Room 100 fastest are not the sprinters — they pause at every door, scan the room, identify the closet and exit, and only then move.

This feels wrong. You want to move. The Hotel is dangerous. But the danger is triggered by doors. Every door you open is a new room with a potential spawn. Opening doors slowly and checking before committing makes your run longer in real time but shorter in attempts. One clear at 45 minutes beats 10 deaths at 5 minutes each.

## Your First Clear: The Practical Route

After the drill, here's the route to your first clear:

**Before starting:** Buy Gummy Flashlight (150 Credits) + 2 Medkits (100 Credits). No batteries to manage. No complex items to juggle. Just light and healing.

**Floors 1-10:** Practice the room protocol: enter, identify exit, note nearest closet. Screech will appear in dark rooms — spin 360° when you hear "psst."

**Floors 11-30:** Rush and Ambush are active. Closet discipline: enter immediately on screech, exit after pass for Rush, stay for all passes for Ambush.

**Floor 50 (Library):** Figure encounter. Walk only. Crouch when Figure listens. Place books when Figure is on the far side. It's a rhythm puzzle, not a stealth test.

**Floors 51-100:** Rush, Ambush, and Seek chase repeat. You've handled all of these before. The late Hotel is an endurance test, not a skill test.
