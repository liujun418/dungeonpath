## The Scoreboard Says You Lost to a Weapon You Don't Own

You spawn into ranked, run your usual route, and take your first fight. You hit your shots, or you think you did. Then the enemy fires a two-round burst, and your health bar drops in a way you can't answer. The kill feed names a gun you've never held. You check the shop and it isn't there. It isn't in the normal Key list at all.

That is the Season 4 weapon meta in one moment. The best weapons in RIVALS right now are not all things you can walk up and buy. Some of them are Contraband rarity — loot-box or trade-gated — and one of them, the Permafrost, is the single highest-skill weapon in the game and the single worst purchase decision for most players.

This guide ranks every Season 4 weapon, explains why each one lands where it does, and gives you a straight answer on where to spend your Keys. No filler tiers. No "everything is viable" hedge. If you're brand new, read our [beginner guide](/posts/rivals/beginner-guide/) first, then come back here to spend.

## The Season 4 Meta in One Screen

Here is the full list. Keep it open while you read the reasons below, because the reasons matter more than the letters.

| Tier | Weapons |
|---|---|
| S | Assault Rifle, Permafrost, Exogun |
| A | Shotgun, Katana, Scythe, Burst Rifle, Uzi |
| B | Sniper, Crossbow, Molotov, Knife |
| C | Grenade Launcher, Revolver |

The thing that jumps out: S-tier holds two primaries and one secondary, and A-tier is stuffed with cheap, accessible picks. The meta right now rewards consistency and movement more than raw one-shot potential. Let me explain each tier in the order you'll actually care about it.

## S Tier: The Three Weapons That Actually Carry

**Assault Rifle** is the best all-rounder in the game, and it isn't close. Low recoil, fast time-to-kill, effective at every range, and it is the default primary, which means it costs nothing. There is no map where the AR is a bad choice, and there is no rank where it stops working. Every other weapon on this list is compared against it. If you want to climb, this is the weapon you master before anything else — our [aim-training guide](/games/rivals/) is built around exactly this gun.

**Permafrost** is the ceiling pick. It is a Contraband submachine gun that fires a 2-round burst, and its bullets slow enemies on hit. Land your bursts and it deletes people. The freeze ability throws whatever ammo is left in your magazine as a projectile — more rounds left means a longer freeze, so a full magazine is a hard stun. It is the best weapon in the game when a top player holds it. We'll dig into why that "when" is the problem in a second.

**Exogun** is the surprise. It is a secondary, but its area-of-effect blast competes with primaries on clustered maps. The blast means you can damage a player you did not fully line up on, which no other secondary does. It is the one secondary worth treating as a real weapon rather than a backup. Pair it with an AR or Shotgun and you have two guns that both hold their own in a fight, which is rare in RIVALS.

## Why Permafrost Is S-Tier and Wrong for Most Players

Here is the counterintuitive part of this list, and it matters more than any single letter on it.

Permafrost is genuinely S-tier. It is also the wrong pick for most players. Both things are true at once.

The reason is the burst. A 2-round burst means one trigger pull either hits or it doesn't — there is no spray correction mid-burst. Against a moving target, a single missed burst deals zero damage while your opponent keeps shooting. The AR lets you walk shots back onto target. Permafrost does not. Its whole skill ceiling lives in the gap between "I fired" and "I landed," and most players lose fights in that gap.

The balance history tells the same story. Update 18 buffed its damage from 13 to 14, cut its magazine from 30 to 24 and reserve from 90 to 72, and raised max slow stacks from 1 to 10 with a max slow of -50%. The patch note read: "Max slowing enemies with just one bullet is unfair." Then Update 20 pulled it back — max slow stacks down to 8, and slow duration down to 0.75 seconds, with the note "Slowing opponents wasn't effective enough." The devs keep tuning it because it is too strong at the top and too punishing at the bottom, and they still haven't found the middle.

So the honest rule: if you can land eight out of ten bursts under pressure, Permafrost is your weapon. If you can't, it is a derank button. Most players can't. Run the AR, and leave Permafrost for casual practice until the burst accuracy is automatic.

## A Tier: The 25-Key Bangers

This is where most players should actually shop, and two picks stand out.

**Burst Rifle** costs 25 Keys and is the best value in the game. It is a high-damage primary with a lower skill floor than Permafrost — you still get burst damage, but the penalty for a miss is a regular reload, not a freeze-like all-or-nothing commitment. If you want something stronger than the AR without gambling on rarity, this is the buy.

**Uzi** costs 20 Keys and is the beginner-friendly secondary. It is forgiving, cheap, and does its job without demanding much from your aim. If you are not running an Exogun, the Uzi is the secondary you should own first.

**Shotgun** is the best close-range primary, with genuine one-shot potential at point-blank. The catch is open maps — the moment the fight opens up past your spread, you lose to the AR. It is a loadout weapon, not a main. Pair it with a range secondary or lean on map knowledge to force corners.

**Katana** deflects bullets, which is the closest thing RIVALS has to a counterplay weapon. A well-timed deflect turns a Shotgun blast back on the shooter. It rewards reaction speed and timing more than raw melee aggression.

**Scythe** is the movement melee — a dash-forward gap closer that turns the "I can't reach them" problem into "I'm already there." It pairs beautifully with the Shotgun for closing the exact distance the Shotgun wants.

The pattern across A-tier: cheap, accessible, and strong in a specific role. None of them is the best at everything, but none of them will bankrupt you or tank your rank either.

## B Tier: Map-Dependent and Punishing

**Sniper** is the headline here, because a lot of players will fight me on it. On an open map with long sightlines, it one-shot headshots and feels S-tier. On a tight indoor map, a Katana or Shotgun is on top of you before you line up shot two. Sniper is B-tier not because it is weak, but because it is situational. You do not main a Sniper in Season 4. You pull it out when the map gives you distance, and you put it away when it doesn't. Reading the map before the match starts tells you which is which.

**Crossbow** is a quirkier, slower one-shot that is harder to land than the Sniper at every range. It has fans, but it does not out-perform the Sniper in the role the Sniper already owns.

**Molotov** is area denial with a high skill floor. It shines on chokepoints and objective holds, but in a straight duel it does nothing for you. It is a support tool, not a weapon.

**Knife** is fine and forgettable. Fast, light, and outclassed by both the Katana's deflect and the Scythe's dash. There is no strong reason to pick it over those two.

## C Tier: Skip Unless You Love a Gimmick

**Grenade Launcher** and **Revolver** share one problem: they punish a miss harder than they reward a hit.

The Grenade Launcher has the AOE damage but a slow arc and a reload that leaves you exposed. The Revolver hits hard if you land, and does nothing if you don't, with a small magazine and no forgiveness. Both are fun. Neither wins you ranked games. Buy them last, or not at all, and never before your main loadout is set.

## What Actually Changed Since Season 3

If you skipped Season 3, here is the short version of what moved the meta. Season 3 was defined by the Grapple Hook and the Spear — mobility and poke tools that let players cross gaps and pressure from weird angles. Our [Season 3 weapons and maps guide](/games/rivals/) covers all of it. Season 4 tightened the gun meta instead of adding more movement tech. The AR stayed on top, burst damage got a real ceiling weapon in Permafrost, and the Exogun gave secondaries a first-class AOE option. The net effect: fights are now decided more by burst accuracy and less by who owns the newest movement trick. That is exactly why the Assault Rifle is still the answer for almost everyone — the season's changes raised the reward for consistency, not for unlocks.

## The Failure Retrospective: How a Good Weapon Deranked Me

A specific failure, so you don't repeat it.

I unlocked Permafrost after a lucky Contraband roll, immediately queued ranked, and equipped it as my main. First fight, mid-range, enemy strafing left. I fired a burst, missed low. Fired again, missed again. The enemy's AR never stopped shooting. I died twice in a row before my first kill.

The error was not the weapon. It was the swap. I had a reliable AR that had carried me for weeks, and I threw it away for a gun whose entire value depends on landing every burst — a skill I had not built yet. The cause was simple: I ranked the letter, not the fit. I saw "S-tier" and assumed the gun would carry me instead of the other way around.

The recovery was slower than the mistake. I dropped back to casual, forced myself to fire Permafrost in bursts only against moving targets, and tracked my burst accuracy until it was consistent. Only then did I bring it back to ranked, and only on maps where the range suited it. The lesson: a weapon's tier is a measure of its ceiling, not your ceiling. You do not inherit the first one by equipping it.

## How to Pick Your Main (A 5-Step Method)

Most players pick a weapon by what the YouTube thumbnail shows. That is a recipe for a derank season. The actual method is short, and it works whether you are buying a 25-Key gun or a Contraband you got lucky on.

**Step 1: Take your last 20 ranked losses. Tag the weapon you were using.** Open the kill feed, count how often you died to the same weapon class, and how often you lost fights to your own gun's recoil. The pattern is usually obvious within 20 matches. If you keep losing to Shotguns at close range, the problem is not your AR — it is that you are playing close-range maps with a mid-range primary.

**Step 2: Identify your strongest range band.** Are you winning most of your long fights? Or your close fights? Most players have one range band where they are clearly stronger than the others, and the rest of their kit should protect that band. If you are strongest at long range, your primary can be the AR or Sniper. If you are strongest at close range, your primary can be the Shotgun or a burst SMG.

**Step 3: Match the primary to the band, not to the letter.** This is the part most players skip. A Sniper that is B-tier in the general meta is S-tier for *you* if your strongest band is long range. The letter on the tier list is the population average. Your job is to find the weapon that beats the population average in the band you actually play in.

**Step 4: Test in casual for at least one full session before ranked.** Every weapon on this list has a 2-3 hour learning curve. Permafrost has a longer one. If you swap to a new primary the same night you buy it, you are testing it on the most punishing setting ranked offers, and you will misread your own performance. Test in casual, log your accuracy for one session, then decide.

**Step 5: Swap only when the gun stops teaching you.** The trap most players fall into is constant swapping. They get a new gun, run it for a week, switch, run it for a week, switch. None of the guns ever reach their full potential, and the player feels mediocre at everything. Pick a primary, run it for at least two weeks in ranked, and only swap when you can name a specific weakness the gun cannot cover.

## Counter-Intuitive Truths About RIVALS Weapon Tiers

Five things that sound wrong until you have been in the Season 4 meta.

**The letter on the tier list is not your tier.** A weapon's S-tier status is a population average — what a top player does with the gun. Your tier is what *you* do with it. Permafrost is S-tier for the 10% of players who can land bursts under pressure. It is C-tier for the 90% who cannot. The tier is the ceiling, not your floor.

**The default weapon is meta for a reason.** The Assault Rifle is the best weapon in the game for most players, and the fact that it is the default is not an accident. The devs made the default strong on purpose. The Contraband guns are the *ceiling*, not the *better*. If you have not mastered the AR, the AR is your answer.

**A higher-tier weapon can derank you.** Equipping a Permafrost in ranked before you have built the burst accuracy is mathematically worse than equipping an AR. You deal less damage per shot on average, you lose more fights, and your ELO drops. The weapon tier is a description of skill expression, not a buff you get for owning the gun.

**The best Counter to a weapon is usually one tier down, not the same tier.** If you keep losing to Permafrost, the answer is not another Permafrost — it is the AR, which has the spray correction you need to win a duel against a burst that misses. The same logic applies to Sniper (counter: AR or Katana rush), Shotgun (counter: Exogun AOE), and Katana (counter: Shotgun at point-blank).

**The season's meta rewards consistency more than unlocks.** Season 4 tightened the gun meta instead of adding new movement tech. The result: the players who climb are the ones with the cleanest fundamentals (AR recoil, burst timing, positioning), not the ones with the most Contraband unlocks. Spend your Keys on consistency, not on rarity.

## The Honest Fine Print on Season 4

Two things most tier-list articles gloss over, and I want them on the record.

First, the Season 4 start date. Official channels have been quiet, and the specific dates floating around are thin. Treat any exact launch date you see elsewhere with caution until the game or its Discord confirms it. The meta rankings above hold regardless of the exact day, because they are about weapon performance, not calendar timing.

Second, the rarity gate. The Permafrost and other Contraband weapons are not in the normal Key shop. You get them from loot rolls or trades, not from a purchase screen. That is why this list keeps coming back to your Keys: for most players, the real question is not "what is the best weapon in Season 4" but "what should I spend my Keys on right now." And the answer is the Burst Rifle and the Uzi first, then build around them.
